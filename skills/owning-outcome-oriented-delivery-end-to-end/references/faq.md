# FAQ: Owning Outcome-Oriented Delivery End to End

## How is outcome oriented delivery different from a fixed-scope statement of work?

A fixed-scope statement of work defines success as delivering listed features or components. Outcome oriented delivery defines success as a measured change in the customer's operation, and features are only means to that end. The practical difference shows up when a feature ships but the metric does not move: under an outcome contract, the work is not done. That shifts effort toward adoption, workflow fit and handover rather than feature count.

## What should an engagement milestone look like?

A good milestone has an observable exit criterion, a target date and a decision attached. Examples include first workflow live with real users, adoption stable across the target team, metric moved against baseline, and customer operating unaided. The criterion defines the milestone, and the date is a planning aid. At each one the sponsor decides whether to continue, rescope or stop.

## How do you measure ROI when the benefit is hard to price?

Start with adoption and operational KPIs, which are usually directly observable, and then agree a simple value model with the sponsor's finance contact early. For example, convert hours saved or errors avoided into cost using rates the customer already uses internally. Include the cost side, including the embedded team, so the ROI is honest. Write the formula into the outcome contract so it is not renegotiated after results appear.

## How do you stop scope creep without damaging the relationship?

Make the sponsor, not the engineer, the owner of scope decisions. Log every request that does not move the contracted metric with the requester and date, and review the log at each milestone. Requesters get a clear answer and a path, rather than a silent refusal or a quiet yes. Reusable requests can go to your platform team instead of becoming custom work.

## When is an outcome oriented engagement actually finished?

It is finished when the metric has reached its target and the customer can operate the system without your engineers. Reaching the metric alone is not enough, because a system the customer cannot maintain tends to degrade or create dependence on the vendor. A rehearsal period where customer staff run everything while your team observes is the most reliable test. If gaps surface, resolve them as handover blockers rather than extending the engagement open-endedly.
