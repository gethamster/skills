# Examples: Iterating the North Star Metric and Its Inputs

## A threshold that was set too high

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a project tool's North Star counts teams that complete five shared tasks a week. After two months, the count is small and barely moves, even though usage is growing.

**Walkthrough:**

In the review, the analyst shows that many teams complete three or four shared tasks every week and renew at similar rates to those completing five. Confidence in the threshold drops; confidence in the concept stays high.

The group lowers the threshold to three, restates the history under the new definition, and annotates the dashboard. The concept, the inputs and the bets stay the same. The change log records the reason: the original number was a guess, and renewal data showed a lower bar captured the same behavior.

## An input that no work seems to move

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a fitness app has an input called "workout variety," the number of workout types a member tries each month. Three bets aimed at it have finished with no visible effect.

**Walkthrough:**

The review asks whether the problem is the bets or the input. Customer interviews suggest members value consistency more than variety, and the input's link to the North Star, "members working out three times a week," has weakened in the data.

The group retires the input and replaces it with "members who schedule next week's workouts," which research and early data both support. The change is announced with its reasons, and the two remaining bets on variety are closed and reviewed.

## A review that triggers a revision

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a consumer app that has moved toward selling to businesses keeps growing its North Star, "weekly active creators," but revenue has been flat for two quarters.

**Walkthrough:**

The deeper review checks the North Star against business results and finds the link has broken: most new creators are individuals on the free plan, while revenue comes from team accounts. The strategy has shifted, and the North Star has not.

Instead of changing the metric in the room, the group documents the evidence and asks the sponsor to schedule a revision workshop with a broader group. Until then, the dashboard shows team-account activity next to the North Star so the gap stays visible.
