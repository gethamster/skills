# FAQ: Iterating the North Star Metric and Its Inputs

## How often should we review the North Star model?

A short review every few weeks works for most teams, with a deeper one each planning cycle. The playbook's present-thinking questions include how confidence has changed "in the last couple weeks," which suggests a similar rhythm. Changing the metric itself should be much rarer.

## What is the difference between iterating and changing the North Star?

Iterating refines the model: definitions, thresholds and inputs change as evidence arrives while the concept stays. Changing the North Star replaces the concept because the strategy, market or business has shifted. The first happens often; the second needs a full process with a broad group.

## What is the clearest sign the North Star needs to change?

The playbook's answer, from Amplitude's Ted Clark, is when the North Star stops directionally indicating where revenue is going. Other signs are a significant strategy shift, a new market or business stage, or discovering the team cannot really influence the metric.

## Should we restate history when a definition changes?

Where possible, yes, so trends remain comparable. Recalculate past values under the new definition, annotate the change on the dashboard, and keep the old series available for reference. If restating is not possible, mark a clear break in the chart.

## Who should attend the reviews?

The North Star owner, the owners of each input and an analyst who knows the data. Invite people from outside the product team to the deeper reviews, since they are less attached to the current model and more likely to question it.
