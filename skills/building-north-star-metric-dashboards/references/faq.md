# FAQ: Building North Star Metric Dashboards That Trace the Work

## What should a North Star dashboard include?

Business results, the North Star Metric with its definition, each input with its definition, a system health panel if you use a health input, and the current bets under the inputs they target. Arrange them in the order of the framework's tree so a viewer can follow the chain from work to results.

## How is this different from a normal KPI dashboard?

A KPI dashboard usually shows many metrics side by side with no stated relationship. A North Star dashboard shows a hypothesis: these bets should move these inputs, which should move the North Star, which should lead these results. That structure turns a movement in any chart into a question about a specific link.

## How often should the dashboard update?

Inputs and health measures should update at least as often as the team reviews them, which for many teams is weekly. The North Star and business results can use the same refresh but are best read over longer windows. Show a short and a long view side by side.

## Should targets appear on the dashboard?

They can, as long as they are labelled as expectations. Many North Star targets come from beliefs about how inputs relate, so a miss is information about the model. Presenting targets as verdicts encourages people to explain misses away.

## Who should own the dashboard?

One person, usually in product operations or analytics, should own its accuracy and definitions. Each input should have an owner who explains its movements in reviews. Keep ownership of the page separate from ownership of the numbers.
