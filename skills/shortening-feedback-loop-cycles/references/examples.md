# Examples: Shortening Feedback Loop Cycles for Competitive Advantage

## Support team rerouting ticket volume

**Scenario:**

A customer support lead wants to stop holding a group call every time queue volumes spike at one regional desk.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the team names one signpost, the queue at any desk exceeding 100 open tickets for more than 30 minutes. The if-then rule says that when this happens, the shift supervisor moves up to 5 agents from the quietest desk. The guardrail is that no desk may drop below 3 agents; anything beyond that escalates to the support lead. In the first month the rule fires 8 times and escalates once, when two desks spiked together.

The review adds a second rule for simultaneous spikes, and group calls for volume stop entirely.

## Product team responding to a pricing move

**Scenario:**

A product manager keeps losing a week each time a competitor changes pricing, because finance, legal and product meet from scratch to discuss it.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the manager forms a standing orientation cell of 4 people from product, finance, legal and sales, briefed once on the mission and pricing guardrails. The signpost is any competitor list price change on 3 named plans. The cell's output is a one-page assessment covering what changed, the assumptions made, what would change the view, and 2 or 3 response options. Discounts up to 10 points on existing deals are delegated to sales managers; plan restructuring escalates to the product lead.

The next competitor change gets a response in 2 days instead of 7.

## Operations team with a rule that went stale

**Scenario:**

An operations team notices escalations climbing even though its if-then rules have not changed in months.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: over 4 cycles, escalations rise from 2 per cycle to 9, while rule-driven actions fall from 20 to 11. In review, the team finds the main signpost threshold was set when daily volume was about half its current level, so normal days now look abnormal. They recalibrate the threshold, retire 2 signposts that never changed a decision, and add a premortem to the next rule change. Escalations return to 3 in the following cycle.

The lesson recorded with the rule is to review thresholds whenever baseline volume shifts noticeably.
