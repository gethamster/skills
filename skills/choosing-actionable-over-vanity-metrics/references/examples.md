# Examples: Choosing Actionable Over Vanity Metrics

## Signup growth that hid a retention problem

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B scheduling tool reports 4,000 cumulative signups, up from 2,500 last quarter, and the team plans to double its ad budget.

**Walkthrough:**

The PM names the decision: scale acquisition or fix activation first. She runs the three A test on cumulative signups and it fails actionability, since the number could not fall no matter what happened. Splitting users by signup week shows that the share of each cohort still scheduling meetings in week three has dropped from 40% to 22% since a redesign. The team sets a threshold: restore week-three activity above 35% before any budget increase.

They revert part of the redesign and track the next four cohorts. The ad budget decision waits on that cohort metric instead of the flattering total.

## Replacing a waitlist survey with preorders

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a hardware startup has 1,200 waitlist sign-ups, and 70% of surveyed members say they would pay the planned price.

**Walkthrough:**

The founders recognise the survey figure as stated intent, not behavior. They define the decision as whether to commit to a production run. They open preorders with a refundable deposit to the waitlist and set a threshold in advance: at least 8% of invited members must place a deposit within two weeks. The result comes in at 3%.

Because the threshold was fixed beforehand, the team does not argue the survey still shows demand. They interview non-buyers about price and run a second preorder test at a lower tier.

## Downloads versus core action in a mobile app

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a language-learning app celebrates 50,000 downloads after a store feature, and marketing wants to repeat the campaign.

**Walkthrough:**

The PM asks which decision downloads would inform and finds none beyond repeating the campaign. She replaces downloads with the share of new users who finish their first lesson within 24 hours, split by acquisition channel. Users from the store feature complete the first lesson at 12%, compared with 31% for users from referral links. The metric is accessible to the whole team and auditable against event logs.

The decision shifts budget toward referral incentives. Downloads move to a monthly appendix and stop appearing in weekly reviews.
