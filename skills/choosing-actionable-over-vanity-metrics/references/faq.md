# FAQ: Choosing Actionable Over Vanity Metrics

## Are vanity metrics always useless?

No. Raw counts like signups or page views can be useful inputs, for example to size a funnel or monitor system load. They become vanity when they are presented as evidence of progress without being tied to retention, conversion or a decision. Keep them in operational reports, but do not use them to judge whether a hypothesis held.

## What does actionable, accessible and auditable mean in practice?

Actionable means you can link a change in the number to a specific product change and know what to do next. Accessible means the people who act on it can read and explain it without translation. Auditable means you can trace it back to raw events or real customers and verify it. A metric that fails any of these will be ignored or misused when results are unwelcome.

## How many actionable metrics should a team track?

Track as few as your open decisions require, for example three to five per active hypothesis. Each metric should map to a decision and a threshold. More metrics increase the chance that someone finds a positive number in a failed experiment. Retire metrics once their decision has been made.

## Can a normally good metric become a vanity metric?

Yes. Retention or conversion becomes vanity if it is reported as an aggregate that mixes cohorts, or if no threshold was set before reading it. The same number can be actionable in one context and decorative in another. The test is always whether it is changing a decision right now.

## What if investors or executives ask for total users or downloads?

Give them the totals in a separate section, alongside the cohort metrics that explain what the totals mean. Keep team decision reviews focused on the actionable set. Over time, showing how cohort metrics drove specific decisions tends to shift what stakeholders ask for.
