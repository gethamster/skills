# Examples: Crafting and Communicating an Inspirational Vision

## Support team lead replacing a slogan

**Scenario:**

A newly promoted support lead inherits a team whose stated vision is 'delight every customer', which nobody uses when choosing what to work on.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the lead starts by gathering evidence and finds that most complaints involve customers being passed between agents several times. She reframes the problem as customers having to repeat themselves, and drafts a future state: customers get a working answer from the first person they speak to. She adds constraints: no new headcount this year and no drop in answer accuracy. The team revises the draft after pointing out that some issues genuinely need a specialist, so the statement becomes 'first person or one warm handoff'.

In one-to-ones, each agent writes how their work serves this, and the lead begins declining side projects that do not reduce handoffs. Within a few weeks, agents are proposing knowledge-base changes by citing the handoff goal.

## Engineering manager connecting infrastructure work

**Scenario:**

An engineering manager's platform team feels disconnected from a company vision focused on customer growth, and morale is dropping.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the manager does not invent a separate platform vision. Instead he works backward from the company goal and names the platform problem: product teams wait days for environments, which slows customer-facing releases. The future state becomes product teams can ship a tested change the same day they finish it, without weakening security review. He then sits with each engineer to connect their current work, such as build caching or access tooling, to that future state.

Two engineers realize their tasks do not connect, and one project is paused. Status updates now open with which part of the future state moved, and the team stops describing its work as invisible.

## School department head avoiding vague inspiration

**Scenario:**

A secondary school department head wants to rally teachers around improving student writing, but past initiatives faded after the launch meeting.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the head notices that previous initiatives were phrased as values, such as 'a culture of excellence', with nothing observable attached. She names the problem as students being unable to structure an argument across subjects, using samples teachers already mark. The future state is that students in every year group can write a structured paragraph that a teacher from another subject would recognize as an argument. The constraints are no additional marking load and no loss of existing curriculum time.

Teachers rewrite the statement together and each identifies one lesson type where they will practice the structure. She pairs this with a termly review date and named owners, and cites the goal when choosing training topics.
