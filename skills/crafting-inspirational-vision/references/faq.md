# FAQ: Crafting and Communicating an Inspirational Vision

## How is a vision different from a goal or an OKR?

A vision describes the future state and why it matters, while goals and OKRs describe the near-term steps toward it. The vision should explain why each goal exists, and goals should be traceable back to a part of the vision. If you have goals that no part of the vision explains, either the goals are misaligned or the vision is incomplete. Keep both, but do not confuse them.

## Can I craft a vision if I am not the most senior person?

Yes. Team leads and informal leaders can articulate a vision for their own area as long as it connects to the wider organization's direction. Work backward from the higher-level goal and describe what your part of it looks like when it is working. The same tests apply: a real problem, an observable future state and explicit constraints.

## How often should I talk about the vision?

Less in speeches and more in decisions. Refer to it whenever you prioritize, decline work or review progress, so people learn to apply it. Revisit the statement itself on a regular cadence, for example quarterly, or when the problem or constraints genuinely change. If people stop citing it, that is a signal to test whether it still describes reality.

## What if team members do not buy into the vision?

Treat resistance as information rather than disloyalty. Ask people to restate the vision and say what seems wrong or unrealistic, since objections often reveal a missing constraint or an unconvincing problem statement. Revising the vision with their input usually builds more commitment than repeating it louder. If a specific person still cannot connect their work to it, discuss that link directly in a one-to-one.

## Does a strong vision make the team too dependent on me?

It can if you remain the only person who interprets it. Reduce that risk by drafting the vision with the team, asking people to write their own role connections, and encouraging them to cite the vision in their own proposals. The aim is a vision the team can apply without you in the room.
