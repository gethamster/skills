# Examples: Now Next Later Roadmap Template

## A spreadsheet template for a small SaaS team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of five with no dedicated roadmapping tool keeps everything in a shared spreadsheet. The founder wants a Now Next Later roadmap that the team can update in the same file.

**Walkthrough:**

The product lead creates one tab with a row per initiative and columns for horizon, problem statement, objective, owner, evidence, open questions and candidate ideas. A second tab lists the objectives with a color each, and conditional formatting colors each row by objective.

A third tab is a read-only board view that groups rows into Now, Next and Later columns, shows only the problem and objective for Next and Later, and adds owner and scope for Now. The header of the board shows the last review date. The team edits only the first tab, so the board and any exported views always match.

## A whiteboard template for a workshop-driven team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a design-led team plans on a shared virtual whiteboard and runs its roadmap review as a live session. It wants a template that works during workshops.

**Walkthrough:**

The template has three wide columns with a definition sticky at the top of each, a legend of objective colors on the left and a completed area on the right. Each column has a card template that can be duplicated: Now cards have boxes for owner, scope and success measure; Next cards have boxes for evidence and open questions; Later cards have only the problem and objective.

A metadata frame in the corner shows the owner and review dates. After each session, the facilitator exports a snapshot to the team wiki so people who were not in the room can see the current board.

## A template with separate audience views

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product manager needs to show the roadmap to engineering, to the leadership team and to customers, and has been maintaining three separate slides that keep drifting apart.

**Walkthrough:**

She rebuilds the roadmap as a single source with tags for audience visibility. The engineering view shows everything, including candidate ideas and dependencies. The leadership view groups initiatives by objective and hides candidate ideas. The customer view shows only problem statements for items marked safe to share, plus a list of recently completed initiatives.

Because every view filters the same source, a change made in the review appears everywhere at once, and the drift between versions stops.
