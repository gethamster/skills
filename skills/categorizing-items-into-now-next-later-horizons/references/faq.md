# FAQ: Categorizing Items into Now, Next, and Later Horizons

## How many items should be in each horizon?

There is no universal ratio, but a healthy pattern for most teams is 3-6 items in Now, 5-15 in Next, and an uncapped Later. The Now horizon must match your team's actual delivery capacity for the current planning cycle. If Now has more items than your team can realistically complete, you have not made hard enough trade-offs. Later can be as large as you want because it functions as a curated idea backlog, not a commitment.

## How do I categorize items when certainty and urgency conflict?

When an item is urgent but uncertain (for example, a competitor just launched a threatening feature and you need to respond but have no solution designed), do not put the full initiative in Now. Instead, put a time-boxed discovery task in Now ("2-week spike to evaluate response options") and keep the actual build in Next pending the results. This acknowledges the urgency without committing to build something you do not understand. The discovery task itself has high certainty (you know exactly what you are doing: researching), high urgency (the competitive threat is real), and high alignment.

## Should I do this categorization alone or with the team?

Both. The most effective approach is a two-phase process. Phase 1: score items independently in writing before any group discussion. This prevents anchoring bias and surfaces genuine disagreement.

Phase 2: review scores as a group, focusing discussion on items where individual scores diverged significantly. Solo categorization is fine for small teams (fewer than 5 people) or fast cycles (weekly), but any categorization that affects multiple teams or stakeholders benefits from collaborative review.

## How often should I re-categorize items across horizons?

Match your re-categorization cadence to your planning cycle. If you plan in 2-week sprints, review horizon placement every 2 weeks. If you plan quarterly, do a full review monthly and a light check bi-weekly. The goal is to catch items that have changed readiness.

A Later item might gain certainty because user research completed. A Now item might lose urgency because a client extended their deadline. See [Reprioritizing and Moving Items Across Horizons](https://tryhamster.com/skills/reprioritizing-across-horizons) for the full process.

## What is the difference between categorizing items and defining graduation criteria?

Categorizing items is the act of evaluating your current backlog and placing each item in a horizon based on today's assessment. Defining graduation criteria (see [Defining Graduation Criteria Between Horizons](https://tryhamster.com/skills/defining-graduation-criteria-between-horizons)) is about establishing the conditions that must be true for an item to move from one horizon to the next. Think of categorization as the snapshot and graduation criteria as the rules that govern how the snapshot evolves. You need both: initial categorization gets you started, and graduation criteria keep the system running over time.

## How do I handle items that stakeholders insist must be in Now even when the evaluation says otherwise?

Ground the conversation in the three dimensions rather than arguing about priority. Ask the stakeholder to identify which dimension they assess differently: "Do you believe certainty is higher than we scored? Is there urgency we missed? " If they can change your assessment of a dimension with new evidence, move the item.

If they cannot, explain that placing an uncertain item in Now risks wasted cycles and delays to the items that are ready. Offering to put a discovery task in Now often satisfies the stakeholder's need to see action without committing to a premature build.

## Can an item skip Next and go directly from Later to Now?

Yes, though it is uncommon. This happens when external circumstances change rapidly, such as a regulatory deadline being announced or a major customer threatening to churn over a missing feature. If an item suddenly has high certainty, high urgency, and high strategic alignment, it can jump straight to Now regardless of where it was before. The key requirement is that certainty must be genuinely high.

If the item jumped from Later because urgency spiked but certainty is still low, you are setting your team up for a painful sprint. In that case, put discovery in Now and the build in Next.
