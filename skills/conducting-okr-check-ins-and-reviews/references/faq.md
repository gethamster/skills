# FAQ: OKR Check-In Meetings

## How often should OKR check-ins happen?

Weekly or biweekly for teams, plus one longer review mid-cycle. What Matters recommends conversation-based check-ins at least weekly, and Google's re:Work guide recommends a mid-quarter check-in before final grading. The best rhythm is one the team will actually keep.

## What is the difference between a check-in and grading?

A check-in is about progress and support: where things stand, what is blocking them, and what help is needed. Grading happens once, at the end of the cycle, and judges whether each key result was met. What Matters stresses that check-ins should be free of judgment, while grading judges the goal and not the person.

## What should happen when a key result goes off track?

Treat it as information and decide what to change: tactics, resources or, under the team's agreed rule, the key result itself. If it is a committed key result that can no longer credibly be met, escalate promptly. Google's playbook describes that escalation as required, because it gives management time to find options.

## Should we track confidence as well as progress?

Yes, if the team can keep it honest. Christina Wodtke recommends rating confidence and adjusting it every week, with a discussion of why it went up or down. Confidence often drops before the metric shows a problem, so it gives an earlier warning than the number alone.

## Who should attend the weekly check-in?

The team that owns the OKRs, with every key result owner present or represented. Invite people from other teams only when a dependency needs discussing. Keep the mid-cycle review wider, with leads from teams you depend on.
