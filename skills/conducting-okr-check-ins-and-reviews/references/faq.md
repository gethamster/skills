# FAQ: Conducting OKR Check-Ins and Progress Reviews

## How long should a weekly OKR check-in meeting take?

For a team of 5-8 people with 3-5 key results each, aim for 20-30 minutes. If your meeting consistently runs longer, you likely have too many OKRs or you are spending too much time on green items. The fix is either reducing the number of tracked key results or enforcing the rule that green items get 30 seconds maximum. If your team is larger than 8 people, consider splitting into sub-team check-ins with a summary roll-up.

## Should I run OKR check-ins weekly or bi-weekly?

Start with weekly. A two-week gap between check-ins means a problem can go undetected for nearly a quarter of the cycle. Once your team has run 2-3 quarters of weekly check-ins and consistently has short meetings with mostly green items, you can experiment with bi-weekly. Teams with fast-moving metrics (daily active users, conversion rates) benefit more from weekly cadence.

Teams with slower-moving metrics (brand awareness, enterprise deal pipeline) can often work bi-weekly. See [setting OKR cadence and cycles](https://tryhamster.com/skills/setting-okr-cadence-and-cycles) for more on finding the right rhythm.

## What tool should I use to track OKR progress for check-ins?

The tool matters less than the discipline. A shared spreadsheet with columns for key result, target value, current value, confidence score, and action items works for most teams. Dedicated OKR tools like Lattice, Weekdone, or Gtmhub add features like automated reminders and visualization, but they do not fix a broken check-in process. Choose whatever tool your team will actually update consistently.

The real requirement is that the tracker is visible to everyone and updated before the meeting.

## How do I handle a key result that has been red for three consecutive check-ins?

Three consecutive red scores signal a structural problem, not a temporary setback. First, determine if the key result is still achievable with extraordinary effort and whether that effort is worth it relative to other priorities. If yes, escalate for resources or dependency resolution outside the check-in. If no, have an honest conversation about whether to adjust the target, replace the key result, or accept the miss and redirect energy elsewhere.

Document the decision and the reasoning. This is not failure, it is responsible portfolio management. Pretending a dead KR is still alive wastes everyone's time and undermines trust in the process.

## Should the OKR check-in meeting be separate from our team standup or sprint review?

Yes, keep them separate. Standups focus on daily tasks and immediate blockers. Sprint reviews focus on what was built and demonstrated. OKR check-ins focus on whether what you are building is actually moving the needle on quarterly outcomes.

Combining them causes one purpose to crowd out the others, and it is almost always the OKR discussion that gets squeezed. A dedicated 25-minute OKR check-in meeting once a week sends a clear signal that outcome measurement is a distinct, protected priority.

## How do I prevent OKR check-ins from feeling like micromanagement?

The key is framing and facilitation. The meeting is about the metrics, not the people. " Ownership of key results should come with autonomy over how to achieve them. The check-in reviews whether the approach is working, not whether the person is working hard enough.

If your team reports feeling micromanaged, examine whether you are asking about activities rather than outcomes, and whether confidence scores are being used for judgment rather than problem-solving.

## What should I do differently in the quarterly retrospective compared to the weekly check-in?

The weekly check-in is tactical: are we on track, what is blocking us, what action do we take this week. The quarterly retrospective is strategic: did we pursue the right objectives, did our key results measure what mattered, and what systemic changes improve our next cycle. In the retrospective, you zoom out from individual metrics and examine patterns. Did the same type of blocker recur?

Were certain key results always green because they were too easy? Did any objective turn out to be irrelevant by mid-quarter? The output is 2-3 concrete process improvements, not action items on specific metrics.
