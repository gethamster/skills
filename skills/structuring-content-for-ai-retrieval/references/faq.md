# FAQ: Structuring Content for AI Retrieval

## Why does retrieval matter if GEO research shows rewrites increase visibility?

Those results measured how content was represented after it was already among the sources supplied to the engine. They did not test whether a page gets selected in the first place. If your page is not retrieved, representation tactics have nothing to act on. That is why retrieval is checked first and separately.

## How do I know whether a page is being retrieved at all?

Run a fixed set of prompts that match the page's target information needs on the engines you care about, and record whether the page is cited in each answer. Repeat each prompt several times, because outputs vary between runs. A page that is almost never cited for prompts it clearly answers likely has a retrieval problem. Check crawler access and topical scope before changing the prose.

## Can adding citations and statistics hurt my page?

It can hurt retrieval even while it helps representation. A critical review of GEO research reports that citation-oriented rewrites can impair retrieval. One practical explanation is that added material dilutes or buries the passages that answered the query. Gate every such rewrite with a before-and-after retrieval check.

## Is this just traditional SEO under a new name?

There is overlap, especially in crawler access and clear topical focus. The difference is the target: here you care whether a page is pulled into the context an AI engine uses to write an answer, and whether individual passages still make sense when extracted. The method page covers the broader comparison between SEO and GEO. This skill focuses only on getting into the candidate set.

## Should one page target many related questions or just one?

Target one primary need and a few closely related secondary questions. Blending unrelated topics weakens the match for each of them. If a secondary question keeps growing into its own section cluster, give it a dedicated page. Your headings should map cleanly onto the questions you listed.
