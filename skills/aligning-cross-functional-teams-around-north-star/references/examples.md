# Examples: Aligning Cross-Functional Teams Around a North Star

## Marketing and product stop pulling in different directions

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B software company's North Star is weekly accounts that complete a core workflow. Marketing is still rewarded on lead volume, and a recent campaign doubled signups while activation fell. Product blames marketing for bad leads; marketing blames product for weak onboarding.

**Walkthrough:**

The product lead's metric audit shows the conflict plainly: lead volume can rise while the North Star falls. In the planning session, marketing takes ownership of a new input, "new accounts that reach the first workflow within two weeks," shared with the onboarding team, and marketing is named lead. The campaign team's goal moves from lead volume to that input.

Within a cycle, marketing's targeting shifts toward segments that activate, and the two teams review the input together weekly. Disputes now start from the shared numbers instead of from each team's own report.

## Support gets a seat at the North Star

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a consumer subscription app has input metrics owned only by product squads. Support tracks ticket volume and response time and hears about the North Star only at all-hands.

**Walkthrough:**

The head of product maps support to the "returning subscribers" input, because tickets about billing and playback often precede cancellations. Support writes its own narrative: resolving those issues quickly keeps subscribers active. It adds a weekly measure of subscribers retained after a support contact.

Support's findings start feeding the squads' planning: the most common playback issue becomes a roadmap item tied to the same input. Support leads now present at the leadership review when their input shows an exception.

## Alignment drifts after a reorganization

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a year after rollout, a company reorganizes around customer segments. The quarterly alignment check finds that most people in two new teams cannot say which input they own.

**Walkthrough:**

The ownership map was drawn for the old structure, and nobody updated it. The product operations lead redraws it with the new teams, keeping each input with one owner. Function leads rewrite their narratives and present them in team meetings. New hires get the North Star section in onboarding again.

The next quarterly check shows most people can name their input. The company adds "update the ownership map" to its reorganization checklist so the gap does not recur.
