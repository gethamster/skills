# FAQ: Implementing Self-Critique and Revision in AI Outputs

## How many critique-revision iterations should I run for best results?

Most implementations see strong results with 2-3 iterations. The first pass catches obvious issues, the second catches subtleties introduced during revision. Beyond 3 iterations, you typically see diminishing returns and risk the model over-correcting into blandness. Monitor your specific use case to find the sweet spot.

## Can I use the same model for both generation and self-critique?

Yes—this is the standard approach in Constitutional AI. The same model that generates the response evaluates it. Research shows that models are often better at identifying problems in text than avoiding them during generation, making self-critique effective even without a separate evaluator model.

## How does self-critique differ from using claude ai prompts seo system instructions for safety?

System instructions set upfront constraints but can't catch every edge case during generation. Self-critique operates post-generation, evaluating the actual output against principles. The combination is more robust: system instructions reduce the frequency of issues, and self-critique catches what slips through.

## What if the self-critique loop introduces new errors during revision?

This is common and exactly why iterative passes matter. The second critique pass is specifically designed to catch issues introduced during revision. Additionally, include 'preserve accurate content' instructions in your revision prompt to minimize unnecessary changes that could introduce errors.

## How does implementing self-critique connect to RLAIF training in Constitutional AI?

The critique-revision pairs generated by this process become training data for RLAIF. The model learns to prefer revised responses over initial ones, effectively internalizing the constitutional principles. ## Can I apply self-critique to existing AI content that wasn't generated through Constitutional AI?

Absolutely. Self-critique works on any text regardless of how it was generated. You can retroactively evaluate and revise existing AI-generated content, human-written content, or outputs from any model by feeding them through your critique-revision loop with your chosen constitutional principles.
