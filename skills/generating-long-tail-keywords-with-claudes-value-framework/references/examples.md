# Examples: Claude AI Long Tail Keywords by User Need

## From support tickets to a page plan

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company selling accounting software for freelancers exports a month of support tickets and summarizes them into a list of recurring problems, such as confusion about quarterly tax estimates and tracking expenses across currencies.

**Walkthrough:**

Claude receives the problem list and a description of the readers: freelancers with no accounting background. For each problem it proposes how beginners and more experienced freelancers would search, with an intent note for each. It marks a few ideas as unlikely phrasings.

The team checks the ideas in Search Console and a keyword tool. Several match queries the site already gets impressions for without a dedicated answer. They group the ideas by intent into a handful of pages and expand two existing help articles instead of creating new pages. Each brief lists the readers' own phrasing as a guide to language.

## A list that came back too generic

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a marketer asks Claude for "long tail keywords for project management software" and gets a list of broad phrases that every competitor already targets.

**Walkthrough:**

The prompt gave Claude only a topic. The marketer rewrites it with a reader (operations leads at small construction firms), their situation (coordinating subcontractors across job sites) and a set of real questions from sales calls. The new list is specific to that situation, with phrasings like scheduling subcontractors across several sites.

Validation shows low but real volume for a few of these and none for most. The team builds one page on coordinating subcontractor schedules and uses the unrecorded phrasings as supporting sections within it.

## Catching invented numbers

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an intern's keyword spreadsheet includes a volume column filled in by asking Claude for "estimated monthly searches."

**Walkthrough:**

The SEO lead compares a sample against the keyword tool and finds the figures do not match. The team clears the column, reruns the ideas through the tool, and updates the prompt template to say that volume comes from tools and Claude should leave that column empty.

The episode becomes part of onboarding: Claude generates and explains ideas; tools supply demand data. Several ideas that looked strong on the invented figures turn out to have no recorded volume and are dropped.
