# FAQ: Writing Clarity-First Web Copy Without Jargon

## How do I remove jargon from B2B copy without sounding unsophisticated?

Keep the terms your buyers actually use and remove the ones only your team uses. Expert readers respond to precise vocabulary and concrete detail, and they lose patience with vague claims. Plain, specific copy reads as confident to them, while buzzwords read as filler.

## What is the difference between jargon and necessary technical language?

It depends on the reader. A term is necessary when most of your audience knows it and the exact word matters to their decision. A term is jargon when your readers do not know it or when a plainer word would work just as well. Decide term by term for each page's audience.

## How do I convince stakeholders to drop terms they like?

Show evidence from the audience. A five-second test or a short read-through where prospects misread the term is more persuasive than a style argument. Customer quotes that describe the same idea in other words help too, because they give the team a replacement to agree on.

## Does clarity-first copy hurt SEO?

It usually helps, as long as you keep the words people search for. Search terms often are the customer's plain words. Keep established category terms your buyers search, explain them where needed, and remove internal names nobody searches.

## Should different pages meet different clarity standards?

The standard is the same: the intended reader understands the page on one pass. What changes is the reader. A homepage serves the widest audience and needs the plainest language, while a technical documentation page can assume more shared vocabulary.

## How do I know when the page is clear enough?

When people from the audience who have not seen the page can say what it offers, who it is for and what to do next, and when their answers match your intent. Until then, keep rewriting the parts they misread or skipped.
