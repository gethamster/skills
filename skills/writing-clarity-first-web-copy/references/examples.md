# Examples: Writing Clarity-First Web Copy Without Jargon

## A logistics software homepage

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a freight software company's homepage opens with "An AI-native orchestration layer for end-to-end supply chain visibility." In a five-second test, most of the ten participants cannot say what the product does.

**Walkthrough:**

The audit marks "AI-native," "orchestration layer" and "end-to-end visibility" as terms no shipper used in sales calls. The quote file shows shippers describing the problem as not knowing where a load is until the driver calls.

The rewrite leads with what the product does in those terms: see where every shipment is and get an alert when one will be late. "Visibility" stays once in a subhead because buyers do search for it, with a plain explanation next to it. A second five-second test finds that most participants can now describe the product in a sentence.

## A pricing page full of module names

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a survey platform's pricing table lists plans by features such as "Logic Engine," "Insight Studio" and "Panel Connect." Sales reports that prospects keep asking what the modules are.

**Walkthrough:**

The writer applies the two jargon questions to each module name. Few prospects know the names, and the names themselves matter little, since buyers choose by what they need to do. Each row is rewritten as a task, for example "Show different questions based on earlier answers" in place of "Logic Engine."

The module names move to a smaller label under each task so existing customers can still find them. A read-through with three prospects shows they can now pick a plan without asking, and the pre-sales questions about module names become the first check after launch.

## A security product for expert buyers

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a network security company wants to simplify its product page. The first rewrite replaces "zero trust network access" with "safer remote access."

**Walkthrough:**

Security buyers search for and evaluate products by the established term, and the rewrite made the page less precise for them. The writer restores the term, because nearly every reader knows it and it matters to their evaluation.

The clarity work moves elsewhere: vague claims such as "unmatched protection" are replaced with the specific checks the product runs, long paragraphs are split, and headings are rewritten to say what each section covers. The page ends up both plainer and more exact for its expert audience.
