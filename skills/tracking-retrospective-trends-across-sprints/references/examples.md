# Examples: Tracking 4Ls Retrospective Trends Across Sprints

## A recurring Lacked theme

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team reviews its theme log after several sprints and sees the tag "test environment" under Lacked in almost every retrospective, despite a few completed actions.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator brings the log to the gather-data phase of the next retrospective. The team sees that each action treated a symptom, such as restarting services or adding a status page, without reaching the cause: a shared environment that several teams depend on.

The team decides the issue is outside its control and writes an escalation, with the log as evidence. The platform group agrees to provide per-team environments, and the tag stops appearing in later retrospectives.

## A Liked practice that faded

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the log shows "pairing" as a frequent Liked tag in earlier sprints, then absent for the most recent few.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator asks the team what changed. People say pairing stopped when two members moved to another project and nobody restarted it. The team agrees on a small action to schedule pairing on risky changes again.

Two sprints later, pairing is back in Liked. The team adds a note to its working agreement so the practice does not depend on particular people.

## Keeping the log light

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a facilitator started a detailed log with many tags and columns, and after a few sprints nobody was updating it.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator cuts the columns down to sprint, category, theme, tag and note count for themes, and sprint, tag, action, owner and outcome for actions. The tag list shrinks to a handful of broad tags with one-line definitions.

Logging now takes a few minutes after each retrospective and rotates with the facilitator role. The log stays current, and the periodic trend review becomes a short, useful part of every few retrospectives.
