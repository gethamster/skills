# FAQ: Comparing Transformational Leadership to Servant and Authentic Leadership

## Is servant leadership a type of transformational leadership?

They are usually treated as separate models, but they overlap. Transformational leadership's idealized influence dimension includes putting followers' and the group's needs ahead of the leader's own, which is close to servant leadership's core idea. The clearest difference is the first beneficiary: transformational leadership points at the collective goal, servant leadership at the individual's growth. In practice many leaders mix both.

## Which model has the strongest research evidence?

Transformational leadership has a large body of meta-analytic research, which the assessment skill page covers in detail. That research also has critics, including a well-known assessment arguing that the construct is poorly defined and hard to distinguish from other leadership concepts. This page does not compare evidence strength across the three models, so check the literature on servant and authentic leadership directly before claiming one is better supported.

## How does authentic leadership differ from transformational leadership?

Authentic leadership is centered on the leader acting consistently with genuine values, while transformational leadership is centered on raising followers' motivation toward a shared vision. Authenticity is a useful check on transformational influence, because inspiring language can be misused for a leader's self-interest. Many leaders use an authentic values check as a guard against that pseudo-transformational risk.

## Can I combine all three models?

Yes, and most leaders do, knowingly or not. The practical move is to choose one model as the default for hard calls and borrow a small number of behaviors from the others. Writing the blend down keeps it stable under pressure. Without a clear default, a team cannot predict how you will decide when goals and individual needs conflict.

## How do I know I chose the wrong model?

Watch the review signals tied to the risk you named. Stalled decisions when you are absent point to dependence, cynicism about your vision points to a trust problem, and missed targets despite high morale point to weak direction. Ask the team which behaviors they noticed, because their answer shows which model you are actually practicing.
