# FAQ: Writing an Internal Press Release for a Product Idea

## How long should an internal press release be?

Under one page. Colin Bryar and Bill Carr describe the press release portion of a PR/FAQ as a few paragraphs, always less than one page. If the draft runs longer, the usual cause is a list of features or a second customer, and cutting either makes the release clearer.

## Why is it called internal if it is written like a real press release?

It is written in the style of a public announcement so the author has to write for a customer, but only people inside the company read it. It is a thinking tool for deciding whether to build the product. Many internal press releases describe products that are never built, and that is expected.

## Should I write the press release before the FAQ?

Yes. The FAQ exists to answer the questions the press release raises, so it needs something to answer. In practice the two change together: writing the FAQ often reveals a problem that sends you back to rewrite the press release.

## What if I do not have data about the customer's problem?

Use what you can gather quickly, such as support tickets, reviews, sales notes or a few customer conversations, and write the problem paragraph from that. Where you are guessing, say so in the FAQ. If you cannot describe the problem credibly at all, talk to customers before writing, because the press release will not be better than your understanding of the problem.

## Can I write an internal press release for a small feature?

You can, and a short one helps when the customer value of the feature is in doubt. For a small, well-understood change that customers have asked for directly, the press release adds little. Use it when writing it would force a decision the team has not made yet.
