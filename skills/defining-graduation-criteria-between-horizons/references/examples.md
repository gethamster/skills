# Examples: Defining Graduation Criteria Between Roadmap Horizons

## A B2B team replacing gut-feel promotions

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team notices that most items reach Now because a senior stakeholder asked for them, and several have stalled mid-build because the solution was never tested. The product lead decides to write graduation criteria.

**Walkthrough:**

The team lists the evidence it actually produces: customer interviews, support ticket tags, product analytics and occasional prototype tests. Later to Next gets four questions: is there customer or data evidence the problem exists, does it serve a current objective, is the rough value worth discovery time, and is there no known blocker?

Next to Now gets five: problem validated, a solution tested with users, engineering has scoped the approach, dependencies are named, and capacity exists. Testing the checklist on the last few promotions shows that three of the stalled items would have stayed in Next. The team adopts it and cites it in every review.

## A consumer app adding demotion triggers

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a consumer app's Next column has grown to hold many items, several of which nobody is working on. The team has criteria for moving items forward but none for moving them back.

**Walkthrough:**

The team adds three demotion triggers: the linked objective has been retired, discovery shows the problem affects few users, or the leading solution failed a test and no alternative is being explored. Each demotion needs a one-line reason on the card.

In the next review, applying the triggers sends four items back to Later and removes two from the roadmap. The Next column becomes short enough that each remaining item has an owner running discovery.

## A fintech team handling a regulatory exception

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a fintech team must ship a compliance change by a date set by a regulator. The work sits in Next because the technical approach is unclear, and the Next to Now criteria are not met.

**Walkthrough:**

The criteria include an exception rule: an item with a hard external deadline may move to Now before meeting every criterion, but it is flagged, and the unmet criteria become the first tasks. The team moves the compliance item, flags it, and starts with a short technical spike to settle the approach.

Because the exception is visible on the board, stakeholders can see why a less-validated item is in Now, and the team does not treat the exception as a precedent for other requests.
