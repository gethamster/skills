# FAQ: HEART Framework Interview Answers for PM Metrics Questions

## Should I use HEART for every product manager interview metrics question?

Use it when the question is about how users experience a product or feature. For questions about pricing, market size or company-level revenue, a different structure fits better. Even then, the goal-to-signal-to-metric order is a useful habit for explaining why a metric matters.

## How do I introduce HEART if the interviewer has not heard of it?

One sentence is enough: it is a UX metrics framework from Google covering Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention and Task success, and you will move from goals to signals to metrics. Then show it working on the product. The structure of the answer carries it.

## How many HEART categories should I cover?

Usually two or three in depth. The HEART paper says it is not always appropriate to use every category, and choosing out loud shows prioritization. Mention the categories you are leaving out and why, which takes only a sentence.

## How do I pick one primary metric?

Choose the metric closest to the main goal you clarified at the start, and explain why. Then add at least one guardrail that should not get worse. Kerry Rodden and Chris Chapman both warn against relying on a single metric, so presenting a primary metric with guardrails is stronger than a lone number.

## What if the interviewer challenges one of my metrics?

Treat it as a chance to show reasoning. Restate the goal and signal behind the metric, say what could make it misleading, and offer an alternative or a second metric to cover the gap. Changing your mind for a good reason is better than defending a weak choice.
