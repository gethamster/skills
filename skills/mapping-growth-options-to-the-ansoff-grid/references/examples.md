# Examples: Mapping Growth Options to the Ansoff Grid

## A software company finds its bets clustered in one new market

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B scheduling software company sells to mid-sized clinics. Leadership asks for a map of all growth work before annual planning, suspecting the portfolio is scattered.

**Walkthrough:**

The planning owner collects ten initiatives from roadmaps and budgets. The group defines the existing market as independent clinics in its home country and the existing product as the scheduling platform with its current add-ons. Four initiatives land in market penetration (referral program, annual-plan discount, onboarding rework, win-back emails), two in product development (a billing module and a patient reminder app), and four carry the note "new market."

Reading the reasons shows that three of the four new-market cards target hospital outpatient departments, owned by three different teams. None of them knew about the others. The group merges them into one market development initiative with one owner and one research plan, which frees two people. The fourth new-market card, a product for veterinary practices, is flagged as diversification because it needs a different data model as well as a new buyer.

## A retailer's map shows no preparation for the next stage

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an online home-goods retailer has grown for several years on paid social and email to one core audience. The founder wants next year's marketing plan to support a growth target the current channels are not forecast to reach.

**Walkthrough:**

Mapping by budget shows almost all spend in market penetration: promotions, retargeting and loyalty offers to the same audience. There is one small product development idea, a line of outdoor furniture for existing customers, and nothing in market development.

The group compares the map with the forecast and agrees the gap cannot be closed from penetration alone. They fund the outdoor line properly, since existing customers already ask for it, and add one market development bet: selling the current range to small hospitality businesses through a trade account. The diversification cell stays empty on purpose, and the plan records that choice.

## A contested placement settled by the written definitions

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a data analytics company plans to sell its existing dashboard product to finance teams. Until now it has sold only to marketing teams in the same kinds of companies.

**Walkthrough:**

The sales lead calls it market penetration, since the target companies are already customers. The product lead calls it market development, since finance teams buy for a different job and through a different budget. The group returns to the definitions agreed in Step 2, which describe the market by the job the product does and the buyer who owns the budget.

Under those definitions the card goes into market development, near the border with penetration, with the note "same accounts, new buyer and job." That placement changes the plan: the initiative now gets segment research and finance-specific messaging, rather than being folded into the existing account expansion campaign.
