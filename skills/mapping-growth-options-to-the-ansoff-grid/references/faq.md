# FAQ: Mapping Growth Options to the Ansoff Grid

## How many initiatives should go on the grid?

Include every initiative that is funded, in progress or seriously proposed, however small. Leaving small bets off hides real commitments, especially when several of them point at the same new market. If the grid becomes crowded, group closely related initiatives under one card with one owner. The aim is a complete picture, and the count follows from that.

## Is there an ideal split across the four quadrants?

No single split fits every company, and Ansoff did not propose one. The right balance depends on how far the core forecast falls short of the growth target and how much risk the company can carry. Some teams use published innovation portfolio benchmarks as a reference point for discussion, but the map should be judged against your own forecast and capabilities.

## What if an initiative seems to fit two quadrants?

Go back to the written definitions of existing and new and answer the product and market questions separately. Place the card in the cell those answers point to and note which axis is only partly new. The note matters more than the cell, because it tells the owners what evidence they still need.

## Should we map by count or by budget?

Map by both if you can, and read budget and headcount first. Counting initiatives can make a portfolio look balanced when most of the money sits in one cell. A few large bets in the diversification cell can carry more risk than many small penetration tactics combined.

## How often should the map be updated?

Review it at least during annual planning and after major changes such as a new competitor, a completed launch or a failed bet. Successful market development and product development initiatives gradually become part of the core and should move on the next map. Keep dated versions so the team can see how the portfolio changed.
