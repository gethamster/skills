# FAQ: Managing a Lean Portfolio in SAFe

## What decisions does Lean Portfolio Management own in SAFe?

SAFe assigns LPM the highest level of decision-making and financial accountability for the products and solutions in a portfolio. That typically covers which epics are analyzed, which are funded and which are stopped. The exact split with finance and executive leadership is something each organization writes down when it maps existing decision rights. The key test is whether the group can actually release and withdraw funding.

## What are the Portfolio Kanban states?

SAFe material describes the Portfolio Kanban as managing flow from ideation through analysis and implementation to completion. The exact state names vary by configuration, so many portfolios use something like funnel, review, analysis, implementation and done. What matters more than the names is that each state has an exit criterion representing stronger evidence. Add or rename states to fit your governance, but keep the board short enough to scan.

## How much analysis should an epic get before funding?

Enough to decide, and no more. Start with a light check on strategic fit and size, then do deeper analysis only for epics that pass, covering expected outcomes, feasibility, risk, investment and an MVP definition. SAFe does not mandate one analysis worksheet, so choose criteria and apply them consistently. A time-box on full analysis prevents it from becoming an open-ended study.

## How is the Portfolio Kanban different from a roadmap?

A roadmap shows intended timing, while the Portfolio Kanban governs whether and when an initiative earns capacity at all. Items on the Kanban can be stopped at any state, and the board makes prioritization trade-offs visible. Approved epics then feed roadmaps and train backlogs. If your Kanban only ever shows items marching toward implementation, it is behaving like a roadmap and has lost its decision function.

## Who should sit in the Lean Portfolio Management group?

Include the people who can commit strategy, money and technical direction for the portfolio. That usually means senior business leaders, a finance partner with budget authority, and someone accountable for architecture. Keep the group small enough to decide in one session. People who sponsor individual epics present to the group but should not decide on their own proposals alone.
