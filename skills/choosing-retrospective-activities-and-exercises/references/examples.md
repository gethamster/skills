# Examples: Choosing Retrospective Activities and Exercises

## Planning for a sprint with a production incident

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of seven had a production incident mid-sprint that took three days to resolve. The retrospective is ninety minutes, in person.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator starts with the data activity. A timeline fits, because the team needs to rebuild the sequence of the incident and see what else was happening around it. She plans to bring the incident log and the deploy history to seed it.

For the check-in she chooses a one-word round, which is quick and lets her gauge how tired people are. For insights she picks a fishbone, since the incident likely had several contributing causes. For deciding, she plans dot voting followed by a control check, because some causes may sit with the platform team. She closes with a quick ROTI. She prepares a simpler backup for the insight phase, a short 5 Whys, in case the fishbone runs long.

## Refreshing a stale retrospective

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of five has used Start Stop Continue in every retrospective for months. People now write the same notes each time.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator keeps the structure and changes two activities. For the check-in he uses an anonymous ESVP poll, since he suspects some people are there only because they must be. For data he replaces Start Stop Continue with a Mad Sad Glad board, which asks about experiences rather than proposals.

He keeps dot voting in the decide phase because the team knows it and it works. The ESVP shows two vacationers and one prisoner, and he asks what would make the session worthwhile. The Mad Sad Glad board surfaces frustration about meeting load that had never appeared under Start Stop Continue.

## Adapting a plan for a remote team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a distributed team of eight across two time zones has a one-hour video retrospective. Last time, an activity that relied on moving cards around a wall confused people on the call.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator chooses activities that work on a shared digital board with little instruction. She collects data asynchronously the day before, asking people to add notes to a prepared board, so the call starts with material already in place.

In the call she uses a typed one-word check-in, clusters the notes together with the team, and runs a short 5 Whys on the top theme. For deciding she uses the board's built-in voting with votes hidden until the end. She plans a short break at the half-hour mark and keeps the close to a check-out question typed into the chat.
