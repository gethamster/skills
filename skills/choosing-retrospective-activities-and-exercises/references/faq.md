# FAQ: Choosing Retrospective Activities and Exercises

## Where can we find agile retrospective activities?

Public libraries organize activities by phase. Retromat builds random plans with one activity per phase and lets you swap each one. FunRetrospectives and Retrium's guide to the five phases also list activities with instructions. Use them for ideas, then adapt each activity to your team and your sprint.

## How often should we change activities?

Change at least one activity each retrospective, and keep the rest familiar. A completely new plan every time spends too much of the session on instructions. Running the same plan every time produces the same answers. Watch the closing feedback to see whether the balance is right.

## Are retrospective games worth the time?

Games are worth it when they serve the phase. A playful check-in can relax a tense team, and a visual metaphor can help people talk about something hard. When a game takes longer to explain than to play, or its output does not feed the next phase, pick something simpler.

## What are some scrum retrospective ideas for a short session?

Keep every phase but use the lightest activity for each: a one-word check-in, a quick prompt board for data, a short 5 Whys on the top theme, dot voting for one action, and a check-out question. Collect data before the meeting if you can. Limit the decision to a single action.

## How do we choose activities for a remote retrospective?

Choose activities that work on a shared digital board with little explanation, and test the tools in advance. Collect some data asynchronously before the call to save time. Use anonymous input and hidden voting where the tools allow it. Plan short breaks in longer sessions to manage fatigue.
