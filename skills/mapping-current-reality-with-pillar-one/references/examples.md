# Examples: Mapping SEO Reality: Pillar One of the Six Pillars

## A traffic decline with a history behind it

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a recipe site has lost visibility over the past year. The team's working explanation is a search algorithm update, and the plan on the table is to rewrite the biggest posts.

**Walkthrough:**

The shared history session puts a site redesign on the timeline, a few weeks before the decline began. Laying the data over the timeline shows the drop started with the redesign, not with the update. The crawl confirms that the redesign removed internal links from recipe pages to their category pages and left many recipes orphaned.

The futures triangle adds context. The push of the present is steady demand for the site's core topics. The weight of the past is the redesign's template and a large archive of near-duplicate recipes. The pull, from leadership, is to become known for weeknight cooking. The team agrees on a map whose first conclusion is to restore the links, and whose second is to consolidate the duplicates before any rewriting.

## A software company whose leaders want different futures

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a software company's content team runs the mapping pillar before planning the next year. The baseline shows strong visibility for developer tutorials and little for anything else.

**Walkthrough:**

Collecting the pulls reveals a conflict. The head of product wants the site known for developer tutorials. The head of sales wants content aimed at engineering managers who approve purchases. The futures landscape shows plenty of jungle items and a clear chess set, but the star is contested.

The team records both images of the future in the map instead of choosing between them. The conflict goes forward to the transforming pillar, where the group will look for a position that serves both. The map notes that the site currently has almost no content for the managers' questions.

## A small site with no written history

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a local services business has a website built over several years by different freelancers. No one has a record of what changed and when.

**Walkthrough:**

The owner and one long-time employee reconstruct the timeline from memory, old invoices and the site's archived versions. The data shows two clear changes in visibility, each matching a rebuild of the site.

The baseline is small: a few dozen pages, most of them service pages with thin descriptions. The weights of the past are the thin pages and a site template that is hard to edit. The pull is to be the known local expert for one service. The map's main conclusion is that the site needs a stable template and deeper service pages before any new content.
