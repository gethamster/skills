## Example: B2B SaaS Company with 200 Blog Posts

**Scenario:**

A project management SaaS startup has published roughly 200 blog posts over three years. The team suspects some posts are cannibalizing each other. Monthly organic traffic has plateaued at 15,000 sessions despite continued publishing. The marketing lead has 4 hours to complete the audit before a quarterly planning meeting.

**Walkthrough:**

The audit begins by exporting all 200 URLs from Search Console with impressions, clicks, position, and CTR for the trailing 90 days. Content inventory reveals that 38 posts target variations of 'project management tips,' creating severe cannibalization. 1). txt rule.

Competitive analysis shows that a rival SaaS company launched a comprehensive 'remote work hub' six months ago and now holds featured snippets for 7 of the 10 highest-volume remote work queries. Audience search behavior analysis reveals 'AI project management' queries growing 140% year over year with zero coverage on the site. The Current Reality Document's executive summary highlights three findings: cannibalization is suppressing the strongest cluster, the remote work cluster lacks the depth to compete, and AI-related demand represents an unaddressed opportunity.

## Example: Local Services Business with 30 Location Pages

**Scenario:**

A plumbing company operates in 12 cities and has 30 location-specific landing pages plus a blog with 45 how-to articles. The owner wants to understand why only 3 of 12 cities generate meaningful organic leads. Budget is limited to free tools (Search Console, Screaming Frog free tier of 500 URLs, and manual SERP checks).

**Walkthrough:**

The content inventory reveals that 9 of 12 city pages use near-identical copy with only the city name swapped, triggering thin content concerns. The 3 high-performing cities have pages with unique customer testimonials, local service area descriptions, and embedded Google Maps with reviews. The technical crawl is clean at only 75 total URLs, but 6 city pages lack LocalBusiness schema markup. Competitive positioning shows that in the underperforming cities, established local competitors have 3-5x more referring domains and Google Business Profile reviews.

Audience search behavior analysis reveals that 'emergency plumber near me' queries dominate in 8 of 12 cities, yet none of the location pages mention emergency services. The link equity assessment shows 85% of all backlinks point to the homepage, with zero external links to any city page. The Current Reality Document recommends as quick wins: rewrite the 9 thin city pages with unique content including local testimonials and service details, add LocalBusiness schema to all city pages, and create a dedicated 'Emergency Plumbing' section on each location page to match the dominant search intent.

## Example: Content Publisher Preparing for AI Search Impact

**Scenario:**

A technology news publisher with 5,000 articles and 2 million monthly organic sessions notices a 15% traffic decline over six months. The editorial director suspects AI Overviews are absorbing clicks that previously went to their articles. The team needs a current reality map that specifically accounts for AI search dynamics.

**Walkthrough:**

The content inventory segments all 5,000 articles by content type: news (2,100), tutorials (1,400), reviews (900), and opinion (600). Performance analysis shows tutorials and reviews declining 22% while opinion pieces are stable and news articles fluctuate with news cycles. Competitive analysis shows that three competitors have restructured their tutorials with FAQ schema, definition blocks, and 'key takeaway' summaries, and these competitors are cited in AI Overviews at 3x the rate. Audience search behavior analysis reveals a shift toward conversational queries ('how do I set up a VPN on my router' rather than 'VPN router setup'), which the publisher's keyword-optimized titles do not match. The Current Reality Document highlights three critical findings: bot blocking is eliminating AI search visibility, content structure does not support AI extraction, and the shift toward conversational queries requires title and heading reformulation.

Quick wins include unblocking GPTBot and PerplexityBot, adding FAQ schema and definition blocks to the top 100 tutorials, and reformulating headings on high-traffic tutorials to match natural-language query patterns.
