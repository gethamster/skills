# FAQ: Mapping SEO Reality: Pillar One of the Six Pillars

## How is this different from a normal SEO audit?

A normal audit counts pages, rankings, errors and links. This skill includes those counts and adds the site's shared history, the futures triangle and the futures landscape. The additions explain why the numbers look the way they do and which forces will shape what happens next.

## What is the futures triangle?

It is a mapping tool from Sohail Inayatullah that weighs three forces: the pull of images of the future, the pushes of the present, and the weights of the past. For a website, the pushes are measurable trends, the weights are constraints such as technical debt, and the pulls are the futures people in the organization want.

## Who should take part?

The content and SEO leads, someone who knows the site's technical history, and people from product, sales or support who hear customer questions. Including people outside the content team makes the map more accurate and easier to agree on.

## How often should the map be updated?

Refresh the data and add new events to the timeline in each quarterly review. Redo the triangle and landscape when something significant changes, such as a migration, a new product or a major shift in how search results appear for your topics.

## What if the team cannot agree on the map?

Keep reviewing until the facts are agreed, and record remaining disagreements about the future as separate pulls. Disagreements about the facts need more data. Disagreements about the future are inputs for the scenario and transforming pillars.
