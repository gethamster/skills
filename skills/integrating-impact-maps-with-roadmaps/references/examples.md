# Examples: Integrating Impact Maps with Product Roadmaps

## From map to a now, next, later roadmap

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team running an online learning product has an impact map with the goal of increasing course completions among paying learners. Three impacts are ranked: learners return within a week of starting, learners finish the first module, and team administrators assign courses to their staff.

**Walkthrough:**

The product manager writes one roadmap item per impact, each headed by the behavior change and its target range. "Learners return within a week" goes in the now column with its starting deliverable, a reminder tied to the learner's own schedule. "Learners finish the first module" goes in next, with two candidate deliverables noted but not chosen. The administrator impact goes in later.

Existing backlog items are placed under the impact they serve. A request for a new certificate design fits no ranked impact and is returned to its requester with an explanation. At the next review, the return rate has reached the lower end of its range, so the team keeps the reminder running and pulls the first-module item forward.

## Using outcome sequencing with a fixed-scope stakeholder

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a finance director has approved a large replacement of an expenses system and expects everything to be delivered together at the end of the year. The team has mapped three expected benefits: fewer late expense claims, fewer policy violations and less manual checking by the finance team.

**Walkthrough:**

The team draws the three benefits as swim-lanes on a timeline, all arriving at the end of the year. It then regroups the work so that everything needed for less manual checking comes first. The finance director agrees that getting that benefit several months early would be worth more than waiting for all three together.

The roadmap is rebuilt with the manual-checking impact first, the late-claims impact second and policy violations third. The scope is unchanged, but each impact now has its own release and its own measurement, and the director can see progress long before the end of the year.

## Re-planning when a deliverable does not move behavior

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a marketplace's roadmap has "sellers respond to buyer questions within a day" as its current item, with an in-app notification as the deliverable. After shipping, response times barely change.

**Walkthrough:**

At the planning review, the team checks the map. The notification was one of several options under the impact; another was a daily summary email, and a third was showing response time on seller profiles. Interviews suggest many sellers rarely open the app during the working day.

The team keeps the impact, replaces the deliverable with the daily email and moves the profile change to next. The roadmap item stays the same, because the impact has not changed, and only the deliverable underneath it is updated. The map records that the notification did not produce the change, so the option is not proposed again.
