# Examples: Now Next Later Framework for SaaS Product Roadmaps

## A B2B SaaS team turning requests into problems

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a project management SaaS receives feature requests through support, sales and an in-app widget. The roadmap has become a list of the most-requested features, and the team keeps building things that few customers adopt.

**Walkthrough:**

The product manager sets up a single feedback inbox and a weekly triage. Each request is linked to a problem. Requests for a Gantt view, a calendar export and deadline reminders all attach to one problem: "How can team leads see what is due and at risk this week?"

That problem already sits in Next. The new evidence, spread across several customer segments, meets the team's criteria for Now, so it is promoted at the next review. The team tests a simple at-risk digest first, and only one of the three requested features ends up being built.

## Handling a renewal that depends on a feature

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a customer success manager reports that a mid-sized customer will not renew unless single sign-on is available. The SSO initiative sits in Next.

**Walkthrough:**

The product lead checks the commitment rule the team agreed with sales and customer success. The renewal is a real commercial dependency, and SSO also serves the team's expansion objective for larger accounts, where several prospects have raised it.

The lead moves SSO to Now, marks it with the renewal date as a real deadline and tells the customer success manager what can be promised: the SSO capability itself, with no broader feature set attached. The initiative it displaced moves back to Next with a note in the change log.

## Launching a public roadmap

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a SaaS company gets repeated "is this on your roadmap?" questions in support and community channels. Leadership worries that a public roadmap will expose plans to competitors and create promises.

**Walkthrough:**

The product team publishes a view powered by the internal roadmap that shows only problem statements for items marked safe to share, a short list of recently launched work and a feedback button on each item. There are no dates and no solution details.

Customers start voting and commenting on problems. Within a few cycles, the comments surface one problem the team had in Later that far more customers care about than expected, and it moves to Next. Support can now answer roadmap questions with a link.
