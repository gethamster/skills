# FAQ: Running Outcome Review Ceremonies and Check-Ins

## How is an outcome review different from a sprint review?

A sprint review looks at what the team built in the sprint. An outcome review looks at whether the work is changing customer behavior and business results, and decides whether to continue, adjust or switch initiatives. The two can happen in the same week, but they answer different questions and usually involve different people.

## How often should outcome reviews happen?

Most teams use two rhythms: a short check-in on leading indicators weekly or every sprint, and a fuller review of outcomes and the roadmap about once a quarter. Roman Pichler recommends reviewing the roadmap and strategy at least once per quarter as a rule of thumb. Adjust the short cycle to how fast your leading indicators can move.

## Who should attend?

The product manager, the engineering and design leads, whoever owns the data, and the decision owner for the outcome. Stakeholders can join the quarterly review. Keep the weekly check-in small so it can decide quickly.

## What does pivot or persevere mean here?

It comes from the Lean Startup, which describes learning whether to pivot or persevere as a fundamental activity of a startup. In an outcome review, persevere means keep the current initiative, pivot means switch to a different initiative for the same outcome, and adjust sits in between. The outcome itself normally stays the same.

## What if the data is not ready?

Say so and decide what can be decided. A missing dataset is a finding in itself and should become an action with an owner. Avoid filling gaps with guesses presented as measurements, and postpone decisions that depend on the missing data.
