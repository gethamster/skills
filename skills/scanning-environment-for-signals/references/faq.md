# FAQ: Scanning the Environment for Relevant Signals

## What is the goal of the OODA Loop observe phase?

The goal is a current, accurate picture of what is changing, both around you and as a result of your own actions. It is not to explain the changes, which is the job of orientation. A good Observe phase gives the next step raw, timely material to work with. If the team keeps being surprised by events it could have seen, the scan is too narrow or too slow.

## How do I tell a weak signal from noise?

Often you cannot at first glance, which is why you log it instead of judging it immediately. Look for repetition across cycles, agreement between independent sources, or a link to a decision you own. A single odd data point with no connection to anything is probably noise. Several small anomalies pointing in the same direction are worth handing to Orient.

## How many sources should we scan?

There is no fixed number, and more is not automatically better. Start from the decisions your team owns and add the fewest sources that cover each one from both inside and outside the organization. If nobody reads a source, drop it or assign an owner. Coverage and attention matter more than volume.

## Are lagging indicators useless in Observe?

No. Lagging indicators are reliable confirmation that an outcome moved, and you still need them to check whether actions worked. The problem is relying on them alone, because they arrive after the window for early action. Pair each one with a leading indicator so you get both early warning and confirmation.

## How is observing different from orienting?

Observing collects what happened, where and when. Orienting interprets it against experience and mental models to decide what it means. In practice the two influence each other, since your current view affects where you look. Keeping the observation note descriptive protects the next step from inheriting a premature conclusion.
