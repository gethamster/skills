# FAQ: Generating Divergent Ideas

## How long should a design thinking brainstorm last?

The d.school bootleg deck recommends a high-engagement brainstorm of [15 to 30 minutes](https://dschool.sfo3.digitaloceanspaces.com/documents/dschool_bootleg_deck_2018_final_sm2-6.pdf). Short sessions keep energy high and discourage deliberation. If the group still has momentum at the end, run a second round with a new prompt rather than extending the first one indefinitely.

## How many ideas should a brainstorm produce?

Aim for more than feels necessary. IDEO.org reports that a good session can produce [up to 100 ideas in 60 minutes](https://designkit.org/methods/brainstorm-rules.html). Count matters less than spread, so check that ideas cover genuinely different approaches rather than variations of one.

## What is a How Might We question and why start with it?

A How Might We question reframes the challenge as an open invitation to generate solutions. The [d.school bootleg deck](https://dschool.sfo3.digitaloceanspaces.com/documents/dschool_bootleg_deck_2018_final_sm2-6.pdf) recommends it as the launch prompt for a brainstorm. It keeps the group focused on the user need while leaving room for unexpected answers.

## Should people brainstorm alone or together?

The standard rules are designed for groups, because building on others' ideas is a core part of the method. Self-posting within a group session lets individuals contribute in parallel while still reacting to each other's notes. If a few voices dominate, mix silent writing with calling ideas out.

## When do we evaluate the ideas?

After the generation round has clearly ended, in a separate activity. The Design Council describes this convergent step as selecting, testing, rejecting and improving potential solutions. Keeping it separate protects unconventional ideas long enough for the group to find what is useful in them.
