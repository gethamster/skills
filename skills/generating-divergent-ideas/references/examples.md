# Examples: Generating Divergent Ideas

## Clinic waiting room redesign

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a health-services team has interviewed patients and framed the challenge as helping first-time patients feel oriented while they wait. They want a wide set of options before choosing what to prototype.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator writes the prompt 'How might we help first-time patients know what happens next?' and posts patient quotes on the wall. Seven people from reception, nursing and IT self-post ideas for a 25-minute round. Early notes cluster around screens and signage, so the facilitator switches to a spare prompt about how an airport or theme park handles the same moment. That shift produces ideas like a printed journey map, a greeter role and a colour-coded wristband.

The wall ends with about 60 notes, which the team photographs before a separate clustering session the next morning.

## Remote onboarding for a software product

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a distributed product team wants ideas for helping new users reach their first success faster. The session runs on a shared digital board across three time zones.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator posts the rules at the top of the board and sets a visible 20-minute timer. Each participant adds headline notes directly, and the facilitator periodically asks people to pick a teammate's note and build on it. When one engineer starts writing a paragraph explaining technical constraints, the facilitator asks for a headline and moves the concern to a 'for convergence' area. By the end, the board holds around 45 ideas across tutorials, templates, human touchpoints and gamified elements.

A convergence session is already booked for the following day.

## Recovering a stalled session

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a marketing team's brainstorm on reducing event no-shows has produced twelve similar ideas in ten minutes, and a senior manager keeps asking whether each one fits the budget.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator pauses and restates the goal: quantity now, budget later. She points to the posted rule about deferring judgment and asks the manager to hold budget notes for the convergence slot. She then asks for deliberately wild ideas, such as rewarding attendees in unusual ways or making the event impossible to forget. The volume picks up and the ideas spread into new categories.

The team closes with a wall several times larger than before and a clear list of budget questions for the next step.
