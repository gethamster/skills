# FAQ: ICE Scoring: Prioritizing Product Ideas by Confidence

## Who invented ICE scoring?

Sean Ellis, the growth marketer known for coining "growth hacking". Itamar Gilad, who created GIST, credits Ellis for ICE and uses it as the default prioritization method in GIST's idea layer. Gilad's addition is the Confidence Meter, which ties the Confidence score to the type of evidence.

## Should we multiply or average the three values?

Gilad says either works: multiply them, or average them. Multiplying spreads the scores further apart and punishes a low value on any dimension more strongly. Pick one method and use it consistently so rankings stay comparable.

## What scale should we use?

Gilad scores each value from 0 to 10, and ProductPlan's description uses 1 to 10. The exact scale matters less than written definitions of what each level means for your team. Confidence, if you use the Confidence Meter, uses its own evidence-based levels.

## How is ICE different from RICE?

RICE adds Reach as a separate factor. Gilad notes that Reach is sometimes redundant with Impact, since the number of users affected is already part of how much an idea moves the metric. Either works if the team is consistent and bases Confidence on evidence.

## Why does Confidence matter so much?

Because Impact and Ease are guesses. Gilad writes that Confidence reflects how much we should trust those estimates, especially Impact. Without an honest Confidence score, the ranking rewards whoever makes the boldest claim.

## Does the top-ranked idea get built?

No. It gets tested. Gilad warns against building and launching whatever ICE says, and in GIST the top ideas go into step-projects. The results of those tests update the scores and decide what is built in full.
