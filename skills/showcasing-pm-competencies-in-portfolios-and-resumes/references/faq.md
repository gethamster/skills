# FAQ: Showcasing PM Competencies in Portfolios and Resumes

## Should I use Cabage's labels on my resume?

Use the grid to plan, and plain language to write. Hiring managers may not know the framework, so describe the work directly: "led positioning for the product against two competitors" reads better than a quadrant label. The grid's job is to make sure you pick the right evidence.

## Do I need to show every competency?

No. Cabage's framework does not expect any PM to cover every box. Show depth in the region your target role needs and a little range around it. A resume that claims everything is harder to believe.

## What if I cannot share numbers?

Use relative measures, scope or the size of the decision instead. Bock's formula needs a measure, but that can be a percentage change, a count of teams affected, or a before-and-after comparison. Be accurate about what you can share.

## How many portfolio case studies should I have?

A small number, each focused on the boxes the target role needs most. Two or three strong case studies show more judgement than many thin ones. Link them from the relevant resume bullets.

## How do I show readiness for a senior role?

Include evidence from above the band on Cabage's chart, such as discovery, roadmap or business case work, even if it was a smaller part of your job. Describe the decisions you made and their effect. Delivery evidence still belongs on the resume, but it should not be the whole story.
