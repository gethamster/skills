# FAQ: Replacing Your Product Roadmap with GIST

## Do we have to stop using roadmaps completely?

Not all at once, and perhaps not entirely. Keep real commitments with their dates, move everything uncertain into GIST, and give stakeholders the GIST board or an outcome roadmap in place of the feature list. Gilad presents GIST as a replacement for roadmaps, but a staged transition is safer.

## How long does the transition take?

Plan on a pilot quarter on one or two goals, then a quarter to move the rest if the pilot works. Teams with many external commitments may keep a delivery plan for those alongside GIST for longer.

## What happens to features already promised to customers?

They stay on a commitments list and are delivered as promised. GIST is for work where the solution is uncertain. Breaking promises to customers in the name of a new process undermines the transition.

## How do we get leadership support?

Start small and let the pilot's results make the case: ideas stopped early, evidence behind the ideas that move forward, and progress on a goal leadership cares about. Share the board regularly so leaders see the process working.

## What if leaders keep choosing the features?

Then GIST will not change much. Raise it directly, agree that leaders set and approve goals while the team chooses the ideas, and show how step results have changed decisions. Gilad's book has a chapter on adoption strategies.

## Is GIST a good fit for every team?

No. It fits teams that own an outcome and face uncertainty about solutions. Critics such as ProductPlan note it handles long, multi-phase initiatives and capacity planning poorly, so teams dominated by fixed-scope work may get less from it.
