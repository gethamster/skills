# Examples: Replacing Your Product Roadmap with GIST

## Sorting a roadmap before the switch

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team has a roadmap of a dozen features for the next two quarters. The head of product wants to move to GIST but knows sales has shown the roadmap to customers.

**Walkthrough:**

The product manager lists every item and asks each item's requester what was promised and to whom. Two items are tied to signed customer contracts and one to a regulatory deadline. Those stay as tracked commitments with their dates.

The rest are rewritten as ideas. Four of them turn out to be different answers to one unwritten goal: getting new customers to value faster. That becomes the first GIST goal, with a baseline and target, and the four features go into its idea bank with ICE scores. Two items with no clear purpose are parked with a note.

## A pilot quarter on one goal

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the team pilots GIST on the onboarding goal for one quarter while the rest of the roadmap continues. Leadership has agreed to review the pilot at the end.

**Walkthrough:**

The top-scored idea, a guided setup flow, gets a first step: a clickable prototype tested with a few new customers. It shows that customers get stuck on data import, which the flow did not address. The team stops the flow idea and brings in a simpler import improvement, which a smoke test and then a small release support.

At the review, the team shows the board: one idea stopped after a short test, one moving to a full build with evidence behind it, and progress on the onboarding metric. Leadership agrees to move the remaining goals to GIST next quarter.

## Handling a stakeholder who wants dates

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a sales leader objects to the change because customers ask when features will arrive. The old roadmap gave him dates to share.

**Walkthrough:**

The product manager shows him the commitments list, which keeps the contracted items and their dates. For everything else, she offers two things: decision dates for each step on the GIST board, and an outcome roadmap that shows discovery and delivery on a timeline.

They agree that sales will share decision dates with customers who ask, and that the product manager will send the board to the sales team every week. The sales leader asks for the ideas his customers requested to be added to the bank, where they are scored like everything else.
