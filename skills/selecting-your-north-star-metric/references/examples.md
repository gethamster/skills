# Examples: Selecting Your North Star Metric

## A team collaboration tool chooses between activity and completion

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a project tool for small teams tracks weekly active users, tasks created, paid seats and NPS. Leadership wants one North Star before planning next year, and the product lead is asked to propose it.

**Walkthrough:**

The lead writes the value sentence first: "A team finishes shared work without chasing each other for status." The product plays the productivity game, so candidates should count finished work rather than time spent. The list includes weekly active users, tasks created, tasks completed, projects with activity from more than one member, and projects that reach done.

Screening removes weekly active users and tasks created, which can rise while no work gets finished. Two finalists remain: tasks completed and weekly projects with completed work from at least two members. The historical check shows that the second one moves a few weeks before retention changes, and it cannot be inflated by one person closing trivial tasks. The team names it "Weekly Shared Projects" and writes the definition with its threshold, its window and the events that feed it.

## A marketplace resists choosing revenue

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a marketplace that connects homeowners with local tradespeople has used gross bookings revenue as its headline number. Revenue swings with job size and seasonal pricing, and teams cannot tell whether their work helped.

**Walkthrough:**

The value sentence is: "A homeowner gets a job done by a trusted tradesperson, and the tradesperson gets paid." That is the transaction game, so the candidates count completed jobs rather than money. The team compares jobs booked, jobs completed, and jobs completed with no dispute or reschedule.

Jobs booked fails the value test because cancelled jobs count. Jobs completed with no issues fits the value sentence for both sides of the market and moves earlier than revenue in the historical data. Revenue stays on the dashboard as the business result. The North Star becomes "Clean Jobs Completed," and the team notes that it does not capture job size, which finance will keep tracking.

## A learning app with two tempting candidates

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a language learning app is split between "daily active learners" and "lessons completed per week." Marketing prefers the first because it is large and familiar. Product prefers the second.

**Walkthrough:**

The team runs both through the checklist. Daily active learners is understandable and measurable, but it counts anyone who opens the app, so it fails the vanity test. Lessons completed expresses value better, but one power user can inflate it.

They write a third candidate that combines the strengths: learners who complete lessons on several days in a week. It counts people rather than events, reflects a habit, and still reads plainly to non-technical colleagues. The historical check shows it tracks next-month retention more closely than either original. They adopt it and record the threshold as a first guess to revisit after a quarter.
