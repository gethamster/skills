# FAQ: Scaling Agile with Spotify Squads and Tribes

## When should we split a tribe?

When it passes your size cap, when its gathering no longer serves most attendees, or when dependency data shows two groups inside it with few links between them. The original Spotify paper designed tribes to stay smaller than about a hundred people. Split along the weakest dependency line and give each new tribe a mission and a lead.

## Is the Spotify model a scaling framework?

Its authors said it was not intended as a generic framework, only a description of how one company worked at a point in time. It offers structures and ideas for scaling, but no prescribed cadence or roles for coordinating many teams. Organizations that need that often compare it with SAFe or LeSS.

## How many tribes can an organization have?

There is no fixed limit, but coordination between tribes becomes the main challenge as their number grows. Use alliances for goals that span tribes, keep company priorities visible, and review whether some tribes should be grouped under a shared cadence.

## What happens to guilds and chapters as we grow?

They need adjustment. Research on Spotify's guilds found that growth across locations caused detachment and fragmentation, and recommended regional plus cross-site activities. Chapters should split when they outgrow what one lead can support.

## Should we restructure before or after the architecture changes?

Plan them together. New squads and tribes work best when their boundaries match parts of the system they can own and release. Where a split would cut through tightly coupled code, schedule the technical work alongside the organizational change.
