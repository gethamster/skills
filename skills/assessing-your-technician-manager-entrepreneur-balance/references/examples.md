# Examples: E-Myth Self-Assessment

## A physiotherapy clinic owner who thought she was a strategist

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the owner of a small physiotherapy clinic believes she spends a good share of her week on planning. She runs the assessment using her booking system and calendar.

**Walkthrough:**

The log shows most of her hours are patient sessions, and the "planning" she remembered was mostly rota fixes and answering staff questions, which she tags as Manager work. Entrepreneur time barely registers. Her energy ratings show she enjoys thinking about new services, and the quiz shows she agrees strongly with the Entrepreneur statements.

Because energy and comfort are high but time is low, she names the gap as a scheduling problem in the Entrepreneur role. Her next step is the time allocation skill, starting with one protected planning block. She sets a review date for next quarter.

## Co-founders who each assumed the other covered operations

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: two co-founders of a small e-commerce brand run the assessment separately. One handles product and suppliers, the other marketing.

**Walkthrough:**

Each scorecard shows heavy Technician time in their own area and some Entrepreneur time. Neither shows much Manager time, and both rate Manager work as draining. When they combine the scorecards, the team gap is obvious: nobody owns order handling, returns or supplier processes as systems.

They name the Manager role as the shared gap. Rather than splitting it vaguely, they agree that one founder will build the first two systems, returns and supplier ordering, and that they will discuss hiring an operations lead at the next review.

## A consultant with no clear gap

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a solo management consultant with no staff runs the assessment and finds that almost all of her time is Technician work, with a little of each other role.

**Walkthrough:**

She places the business in Gerber's infancy stage and notes that she has no plans to hire this year. For her, a large Technician share is appropriate. The gap she names is small but specific: she has not written down how she runs a diagnostic engagement, so every new project starts from scratch.

She hands that gap to the systems skill and sets a lighter goal than a founder with a team would: one documented engagement process by the next review. The assessment stops her from feeling guilty about a Technician-heavy week that fits her stage.
