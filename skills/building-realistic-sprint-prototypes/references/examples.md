# Examples: Building a Realistic Sprint Prototype in One Day

## A mobile app flow built in slides

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team at a small bank is prototyping a savings feature for its mobile app. The storyboard has nine frames, from a push notification to a confirmation screen. The team has two designers, an engineer, a marketer, the Decider and a Facilitator.

**Walkthrough:**

The team chooses a slide tool with clickable links because both designers know it well, and agrees to show the prototype on a phone. The designers are Makers, splitting the frames at the savings goal screen. The marketer is the Writer, the engineer is the Asset Collector and pulls realistic account balances and a believable transaction history, and the Facilitator acts as Stitcher. The Decider answers two questions in the morning about fees.

At the afternoon trial, the Stitcher finds that one designer called the feature "Goals" and the other "Pots." The team picks one name and fixes it in a few minutes. The Interviewer notices that the confirmation screen does not show the date the first transfer happens, which is one of the sprint questions, and the Writer adds it before the prototype is frozen.

## A service prototype with no screens

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a clinic wants to test a new check-in process in which patients are greeted by a staff member with a tablet instead of a front desk. The sprint question is whether patients will trust sharing their details this way.

**Walkthrough:**

The team builds a role-play. One Maker creates the tablet screens in a slide tool, another writes a greeter script and sets up a corner of a meeting room to look like a waiting area, and the Writer prepares the forms and a printed welcome card. The Stitcher runs the whole scene twice with colleagues playing patients, timing each step. On Friday, customers walk through the check-in with a team member playing the greeter, and the team watches their reactions to the tablet step.

## Cutting scope at lunchtime

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: by lunchtime, one Maker has built three polished screens of a settings area that appears in only one storyboard frame, and the frames that carry the main sprint question are still empty.

**Walkthrough:**

The Stitcher spots the imbalance at the midday check-in and raises it with the Facilitator. The team reduces the settings area to a single static screen and moves that Maker onto the unbuilt frames. The Asset Collector supplies the missing content so the Maker can start immediately. The prototype is complete in time for the trial, with the most care spent on the frames Friday's customers will study.
