# FAQ: User Story Mapping Template: Build One Your Team Reuses

## What should a story map template include?

A place for the brief and users, a row for activities, a row for high-level tasks, open space below for details, release lanes, and an outcome card beside each lane. Jeff Patton's quick reference covers each of these parts. Add a small legend for colors and markers, and leave the rest empty.

## Is a Miro or Mural template better than a wall?

Each works. Walls make moving cards effortless and suit co-located teams. Whiteboard tools suit remote and hybrid groups, and Miro's User Story Map app can link cards to Jira issues. Choose the medium that makes rearranging easiest for the people in the session.

## Can I use Google Sheets as a story mapping template?

Yes, especially as a record. Put backbone tasks in columns, activities in a header row, details in the rows below, and release lanes as shaded bands. Moving cards in a spreadsheet is slower than on a board, so it often works best to run the session on a whiteboard and keep the spreadsheet as the shared record.

## Should the template include example cards?

Keep examples out of the working template. Sample content anchors the group on someone else's product and often gets copied. If new facilitators need an example, keep a separate filled-in map for reference.

## How often should the template change?

Change it when your conventions change, such as adding a risk marker or a new lane label. Review it after several sessions and fold in anything facilitators keep adding by hand. Note each change so older maps remain easy to read.
