# Examples: User Story Mapping Template: Build One Your Team Reuses

## A shared whiteboard template for several product teams

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company has a few product teams, each mapping in its own style on a whiteboard tool. Maps use different colors for the same thing, and managers find them hard to compare.

**Walkthrough:**

A product operations lead builds one template. The top left holds the brief (what, who, why) and a persona panel. Below come a labelled activity row in one color and a task row in another, a line under the backbone, and two release lanes, each with an empty outcome card at the left edge. A small legend shows the card colors plus a red dot for risk and a question tag.

The lead tests it by rebuilding a recent map. The facilitator ignores a field for story estimates on every card, so it is removed. After the change, the teams adopt the template and their maps become readable to each other at a glance.

## A spreadsheet record for a team that maps on a wall

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a co-located team maps with sticky notes on a wall and wants a record it can share with remote stakeholders.

**Walkthrough:**

The team builds a spreadsheet template in Google Sheets. Each column is a backbone task, with its activity in a merged header row above. Rows below hold details, and shaded bands mark release lanes, with an outcome cell at the left of each band. After each session, someone photographs the wall and updates the sheet. Sessions stay on the wall, where moving cards is easy, and the sheet becomes the shared record.

## Slimming down an over-built template

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team's template has pre-drawn cells for every story, sample cards from an old project, and a long page of instructions. Sessions start slowly and participants copy the sample cards.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator removes the sample cards and the pre-drawn cells, leaving open space under the backbone. The instructions shrink to a one-line note and a legend. In the next session, the group starts writing cards within the first few minutes, and the columns grow to different depths as the story needs.
