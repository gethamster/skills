# Examples: Identifying Your Product's North Star Metric

## Example: B2B Project Management SaaS

**Scenario:**

A 50-person SaaS company builds project management software for mid-market teams. They've been tracking MAU, NPS, and MRR, but teams are pulling in different directions—marketing optimizes for signups, product optimizes for feature adoption, and customer success optimizes for NPS. The CEO asks the product team to implement the North Star Framework to create alignment. The product manager needs to figure out what is a product manager's most important contribution here: identifying the metric that unites all these efforts.

**Walkthrough:**

The PM interviews 12 customers and discovers the consistent 'aha moment' is when a team completes their first project on time using the tool. She generates five candidate metrics: MAU, weekly active projects, projects completed, teams with 3+ active members, and weekly projects completed on time by teams with 3+ members. She pulls 12 months of cohort data and finds that 'teams that complete at least one project per month' has a 0.78 correlation with 12-month retention and a 0.65 correlation with expansion revenue. She applies the six criteria test: it expresses value (projects completed = work delivered), represents vision (more teams succeeding), is leading (predicts retention 3 months out), is actionable (product improvements in templates, collaboration, and reminders can all move it), is understandable ('monthly active teams completing projects'), and is measurable (they already track project status). The final NSM becomes: 'Number of teams that complete at least one project per month.' She presents it with the data and gets leadership buy-in for a 9-month commitment.

## Example: Consumer Fitness App

**Scenario:**

A consumer fitness app has 2 million downloads but struggles with retention—only 15% of users are active after 30 days. The product team suspects they're measuring the wrong things. Currently they track daily active users (DAU) and workouts started, but these metrics haven't helped them improve retention. The head of product asks a senior PM to identify a proper North Star Metric.

**Walkthrough:**

The PM starts by analyzing the behavioral difference between users who retain past 30 days and those who don't. The data reveals that users who complete 3+ workouts per week in their first two weeks have a 72% chance of being active at 90 days, versus 11% for users who complete fewer. He generates candidates: workouts started, workouts completed, weekly active users completing 3+ workouts, and total workout minutes. He eliminates 'workouts started' because users often start and quit mid-workout (activity without value). 'Total workout minutes' fails the gaming test—the team could just make workouts longer. 'Weekly active users completing 3+ workouts' scores highest across all criteria, but after stress-testing, the team adds a quality qualifier: workouts of at least 15 minutes that reach the cool-down phase. The final NSM is: 'Weekly users completing 3+ full workouts (15+ minutes reaching cool-down).' This metric immediately reframes product priorities—instead of adding more workout types, the team focuses on reducing mid-workout dropout and building habit-formation features like scheduling and streak tracking.

## Example: Two-Sided Marketplace for Freelancers

**Scenario:**

A freelance marketplace connects designers with small businesses. They have 20,000 freelancers and 8,000 clients on the platform. The company has been measuring GMV (gross merchandise value) as their primary metric, but the product team feels this overweights a small number of high-value contracts and doesn't capture the health of the marketplace for the average user.

**Walkthrough:**

The PM maps both sides of the marketplace. For clients, the value moment is receiving deliverables they're satisfied with. For freelancers, it's getting paid for work that matches their skills. She realizes the metric must capture both sides. Candidates include: monthly transactions completed, projects with 4+ star ratings, freelancers earning $500+/month, and repeat client engagements. She tests correlations and finds that 'projects completed with both parties rating 4+ stars' is the strongest predictor of both freelancer retention (they stay on the platform) and client repeat purchase behavior. The stress test reveals a risk: teams might optimize for easy, low-value projects that are easy to rate well. She refines to 'monthly projects completed with mutual 4+ star ratings and minimum $200 project value.' This captures quality, satisfaction, and meaningful economic exchange. The metric is 3,400 currently, and she works with the data team to build a daily tracking dashboard—which feeds directly into the [building North Star Metric dashboards](https://tryhamster.com/skills/building-north-star-metric-dashboards) skill.
