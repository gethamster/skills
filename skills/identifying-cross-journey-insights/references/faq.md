# FAQ: Identifying Cross-Journey Insights and Patterns

## What is a cross-journey insight?

It is a finding that only appears when several journeys are compared, such as a pain point that recurs in many journeys, a failure at the handoff between two journeys, a promise made in one journey and broken in another, or a process duplicated across journeys. A single journey map cannot show it. Its value is that one fix can improve several journeys at once.

## How many journeys do we need before this is worthwhile?

There is no fixed threshold, but the analysis needs at least a few researched journeys that share stage names and a similar level of detail. With only one or two maps, most patterns are guesses. The analysis becomes more useful as the portfolio grows and more journeys have research behind them.

## How do we know a pattern is really systemic?

Trace every pain point in the cluster back to its evidence and confirm they share a cause, such as one system, policy, or process. Ask the owners of the affected journeys to challenge the explanation. Patterns supported by several data types, such as customer feedback plus operational data, are more reliable than those from one source.

## Who owns a cross-journey insight?

Usually the owner of the shared cause, such as the team that runs a common touchpoint or system. If the insight spans several lifecycle stages with no single cause owner, the stage or lifecycle owner takes it. Every insight in the register needs one named owner, otherwise it will not reach a roadmap.

## How often should cross-journey analysis be done?

Many teams run it once per prioritization cycle, so fresh insights feed each round of ranking. Add an extra pass after major research or a big change to a shared system. Keep the register live between cycles, adding insights as journey owners report them.
