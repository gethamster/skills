# FAQ: Measuring Customer Journey Metrics and KPIs by Stage

## How many metrics should each stage have?

One primary metric per stage, plus the conversion into the next stage. Keep a few diagnostic measures underneath for investigation, but do not report them routinely. A scorecard that fits on one page gets read. Longer ones get skimmed.

## Is Net Promoter Score a good advocacy metric?

It is a useful signal of advocacy potential if you follow the standard definition: promoters minus detractors on a zero-to-ten likelihood-to-recommend question. It measures stated intent, not behavior. Pair it with a behavioral measure such as referrals or reviews. If the two disagree, trust the behavior.

## Should I use industry benchmarks as targets?

Use them as rough context at most. Published benchmarks rarely share your definitions, segment or business model, so matching them tells you little. Set targets as improvements over your own baseline once you have a few periods of consistent data.

## How do I measure awareness when attribution is unreliable?

Use measures that do not depend on perfect attribution, such as branded search volume, direct traffic from the target segment or self-reported "how did you hear about us" answers. Track the trend rather than the absolute number. Combine two measures if one is noisy.

## How often should the scorecard be reviewed?

Monthly for fast-moving self-serve businesses and quarterly for longer B2B cycles is a common starting point. Keep the cadence fixed and have every stage owner present. Reviews that happen only when something breaks miss slow declines.
