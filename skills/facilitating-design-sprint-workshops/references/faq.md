# FAQ: Design Sprint Facilitator: How to Facilitate a Sprint

## What does a design sprint facilitator do?

The Facilitator manages time, conversations and the overall sprint process, according to the checklist from Jake Knapp and John Zeratsky ([Design Sprint guide](https://www.character.vc/guide/design-sprint)). In practice that means running each exercise, keeping the timers, capturing discussion on the whiteboard, calling breaks and sending decisions to the Decider. The Facilitator does not decide what the team builds.

## Can I facilitate a design sprint without experience?

Yes. Knapp and Zeratsky's guide says that with the book and their template you have everything you need, and that you will be better prepared than they were when they started. It helps to follow the checklist closely the first time, to practice the exercises beforehand, and to ask an experienced facilitator to review your plan.

## Should the facilitator also participate in the sprint?

It is usually better not to. A Facilitator who sketches and votes has a stake in the outcome, which makes it harder to stay neutral when handing decisions to the Decider. If the team is very small and you must participate, be explicit about when you are facilitating and when you are contributing.

## What is the difference between the facilitator and the Decider?

The Facilitator runs the process; the Decider makes the calls. The Decider chooses the target on Monday, the solutions to prototype on Wednesday and the verdict on each question on Friday. The Facilitator makes sure those decisions happen on time, with the team's input in front of the Decider.

## How is facilitating a remote design sprint different?

It takes more preparation and more attention to people. The Remote Design Sprint Guide recommends a co-facilitator who focuses on engagement, keeping video on, a turn-taking routine for discussions, and extra breaks ([Remote Design Sprint Guide](https://www.jackiecolburn.com/remote-design-sprint-guide)). It also reports that remote sprints take longer, so plan the schedule with more slack.
