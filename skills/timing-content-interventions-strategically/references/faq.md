# FAQ: Timing Content Interventions for SEO Pillars

## What counts as a content intervention?

Any deliberate change to a page or topic: publishing a new page, substantively refreshing an existing one, consolidating several pages, or retiring a page with a redirect. Leaving a page alone on purpose is also a decision the timing note should record.

## Can I use Google Trends to estimate search volume?

No. Google Trends scales interest on a relative index, which is good for comparing shapes over time and between terms. For volumes, use your Search Console data for queries you already get, or a keyword research tool.

## How far ahead should a seasonal page be ready?

Work backward from when demand starts rising, not from the peak, and allow for the time it takes to write, review and get the page indexed. Your own Search Console history for the previous season shows when demand began to rise for your pages.

## When is a refresh worth doing?

When there is something new to say: changed facts, a better answer, new examples, or a shift in what searchers want. Changing only the date or a few words does not help readers, and Google lists it as a warning sign.

## How does timing relate to scenario planning?

Timing reads patterns in current demand. Scenario planning describes different futures and the indicators that would signal them. When a scenario indicator fires, it becomes a trigger that can change the timing calendar mid-cycle.
