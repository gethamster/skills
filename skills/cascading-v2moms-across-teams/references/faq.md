# FAQ: Cascading V2MOMs from Leadership to Individual Contributors

## How long does a full cascade take?

It depends on the size of the organization and how many levels it has. The first cascade usually takes several weeks, because each level waits for the one above to settle. Later cycles go faster once people know the template and the code system. Set a timeline per level and publish it when the company V2MOM goes out.

## Does every employee need a V2MOM?

At Salesforce every employee writes one, and that is where much of the method's value comes from. Smaller organizations sometimes start with company and team V2MOMs and add individual ones later. If you skip individual documents, check more carefully that team methods are translated into people's actual work.

## What should a team do if its work does not map to any company method?

First check whether the work is still a priority. If it is, raise it with the owner of the parent V2MOM, because the company document may be missing something. Teams can also label one method as serving their own health, such as reliability or hiring, without a parent code.

## Should managers edit their reports' V2MOMs?

Managers should review and comment, but the author should do the writing. The understanding the cascade builds comes from translating the parent document into your own plan. A manager who rewrites the document gets a neater page and a report who understands the plan less.

## How do we handle a change to the company V2MOM mid-year?

Update the company document, keep its method codes stable where possible, and tell the owners of affected documents which items changed. Ask them to update their V2MOMs at the next review rather than immediately, unless the change is large. Track which documents have been updated so stale ones are visible.
