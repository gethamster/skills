# Examples: Building Minimum Viable Products \(MVPs\)

## Concierge MVP for a meal-planning service

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a two-person team believes busy parents will pay for weekly meal plans tailored to their kids' preferences, but has no product yet.

**Walkthrough:**

The riskiest assumption is willingness to pay, not whether software can generate plans. The team writes a hypothesis that parents recruited from a local school group will pay for a second week after a paid first week. Instead of building a planner, they collect preferences through a short form and write plans by hand. They set an example threshold before launch: at least 8 of 20 paying families renew.

They log each order, delivery and renewal in a spreadsheet. The made-up result of 11 renewals clears the bar, so they persevere and start automating the most repetitive part of their manual work.

## Cutting scope on a B2B reporting tool

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product manager's draft MVP for a finance-reporting tool lists 14 features, including user roles, exports and a custom dashboard builder.

**Walkthrough:**

The team names the hypothesis: finance leads at small firms will connect their accounting data and view a monthly summary at least twice in a month. They walk the feature list and ask of each item whether removing it makes the result unreadable. Roles, exports and the dashboard builder go to the parking lot, leaving data connection, one fixed report and usage tracking. They instrument connection success and report views per account.

The trimmed build ships in a fraction of the planned time, and the invented data shows most accounts connect but few return, pointing the next test at report content rather than setup.

## Landing page that exposed a targeting error

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a founder tests demand for a language-tutoring subscription with a landing page offering a paid waitlist spot.

**Walkthrough:**

The hypothesis names working professionals preparing for relocation abroad. The founder sets an example threshold of a 5% paid-waitlist conversion before launch and tracks visits and payments by traffic source. Early traffic comes mostly from the founder's own network, and conversion looks strong. When she separates cohorts, the paid ads aimed at the named segment convert far below the bar.

She concludes the positive signal came from the wrong audience, rewrites the value proposition for the target segment, and reruns the page with ads only.
