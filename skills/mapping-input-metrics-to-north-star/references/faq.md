# FAQ: Mapping Input Metrics That Drive Your North Star

## How many input metrics should I map to my North Star Metric?

Aim for 3-5 input metrics. Fewer than three usually means you're missing an important driver—your model is incomplete and your product manager roadmap will have blind spots. More than five creates focus problems: teams can't meaningfully prioritize across too many competing inputs, weekly reviews become unfocused, and it becomes difficult to attribute which input is actually driving North Star changes. If you have six strong candidates, force-rank them and either drop or combine the weakest. You can always rotate inputs quarterly as your product matures.

## How do I tell the difference between a correlated metric and a causal input metric?

Correlation means two metrics move together; causation means changing one reliably changes the other. The simplest test is a cohort comparison: find users who started performing the candidate behavior in a specific period, compare them against similar users who didn't, and track whether the behavior group's North Star contribution increased afterward. If you can run an A/B experiment that encourages the candidate behavior and measure the North Star impact, that's even stronger evidence. Be skeptical of metrics where the causal direction could be reversed—high-value users often perform many behaviors that look like inputs but are actually side effects of engagement.

## How often should I revisit and update my input metric map?

Review your input metrics quarterly, ideally as part of your quarterly roadmap planning cycle. Check three things: (1) Does each input still show a causal relationship to the North Star in recent data? (2) Has any input hit a ceiling where further improvement yields diminishing returns? (3) Have new user behaviors or product changes introduced a potential new driver that isn't being tracked? Treat the input metric map as a living document on your product manager roadmap, not a one-time exercise. Most mature product teams evolve at least one input metric per year.

## Can two teams share ownership of the same input metric?

Avoid shared ownership whenever possible. When two teams share an input metric, accountability diffuses—each team assumes the other will take the lead, and neither feels the urgency to run experiments or prioritize initiatives against it. If a metric genuinely spans two teams' domains, either redefine it at a more specific level where ownership is clear, or assign a primary owner with a secondary contributor. The primary team leads the metric in weekly reviews and has final say on prioritization decisions related to it.

## What if my North Star Metric moves but none of my input metrics changed?

This signals that your input metric map is incomplete—there's a driver you haven't identified. First, check for external factors (seasonality, marketing campaigns, press coverage) that might explain the North Star movement. If those don't explain it, dig into your analytics to find which user cohorts or behaviors drove the change. This investigation often reveals a new candidate input metric you should add to your map. It's also possible that your North Star is defined too broadly, capturing value drivers that your current inputs don't represent. Use this as a trigger for a mapping refresh.

## How do input metrics connect to my product manager roadmap and sprint planning?

Every initiative on your product manager roadmap should be tagged with the input metric it's designed to move. During sprint planning, teams prioritize work that moves their owned input metric, not just work that ships features. In weekly reviews, teams report on their input metric's trajectory and what they're doing to move it. This creates a direct chain from daily work to sprint goals to input metrics to North Star. If a proposed feature or project can't be connected to any input metric, it either reveals a gap in your input map or it's work that doesn't align with your current strategy—both are important signals.
