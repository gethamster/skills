# Examples: Mapping Input Metrics From the North Star to the Work

## A meal-kit service maps down to the sprint

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a meal-kit subscription has settled on "households cooking three kits a week" as its North Star. Teams agree with it but still cannot say how their sprint work relates to it.

**Walkthrough:**

The team writes the function in words: the North Star depends on its ability to get new households cooking in their first week, keep menus appealing, and deliver boxes complete and on time. A mind-mapping session adds a fourth cluster about recipe difficulty, which merges into "menus people can cook on a weeknight."

Under "menus people can cook on a weeknight," the team lists two opportunities: fewer steps per recipe and clearer prep guidance. Current roadmap items sit under them, including a recipe card redesign and a test of shorter recipes. A loyalty points feature fits nowhere on the map, so it goes on the discussion list and is later dropped.

## A developer platform adds a health input

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an API platform's North Star counts active integrations that send traffic every week. Engineering keeps losing prioritization debates because reliability work never appears on the map.

**Walkthrough:**

The team adds a system health input built from two measures: API availability and time to deploy a fix. Reliability projects now sit under opportunities for that input, next to customer-facing work.

In the next planning round, a spike in failed deploys shows up on the health input before customers complain. The team can point to the map when it pauses a feature to fix the deploy pipeline, and product leadership sees the trade-off in the same terms as everything else.

## An input that turns out to be the North Star in disguise

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a note-taking app lists "weekly active note writers" as an input to its North Star, "weekly writers who share a note." The input rises and falls exactly with the North Star.

**Walkthrough:**

When the team tries to map opportunities under the input, every idea also appears under the North Star itself, so the input adds no separate lever. The team breaks it into two narrower inputs: new users who write a second note in their first week, and existing users who return to edit a note.

Each new input gets its own opportunities and interventions, and the two move separately in the following weeks. The map now tells the onboarding team and the editing team different things, which is the point of having inputs.
