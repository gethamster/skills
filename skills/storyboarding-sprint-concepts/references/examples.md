# Examples: Sprint Storyboarding: Plan the Prototype Step by Step

## A storyboard that starts before the product

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team at a home insurance startup has chosen, by supervote, a sketch of a claims page that shows photos of the damage and a live estimate. The target moment is a homeowner filing a first claim after a storm.

**Walkthrough:**

The team's first instinct is to start the storyboard on the claims page. The Facilitator asks how a homeowner would actually get there, and the group agrees it would start with a text message from the insurer after a storm warning. The first square becomes that text message, with the exact wording drafted on the board. The second square is the login screen, kept deliberately simple, and the third is the claims page from the winning sketch.

The rest of the story follows the sketch panels: uploading photos, seeing the estimate, confirming a repair visit. The team ends at the confirmation screen, which answers the sprint question "Will homeowners trust an instant estimate?" The finished story has eight steps, and Thursday's Makers can split it at the photo upload.

## Cutting a storyboard that grew too long

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team building a tool for school administrators fills all fifteen squares by mid-afternoon and is still adding frames for settings screens and an admin dashboard.

**Walkthrough:**

The Facilitator stops the group and reads the sprint questions aloud. None of them are about settings or the dashboard. The team removes those frames and merges two onboarding screens into one. The storyboard drops to eleven steps, all of which lead to or through the target moment: an administrator scheduling a parent meeting for the first time. The removed frames go on a "later" list next to the board so nobody feels their idea was lost.

## Keeping the storyboard on the voted ideas

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: halfway through the storyboard, a team member proposes a new pricing model that nobody sketched on Tuesday, and the room starts discussing it.

**Walkthrough:**

The Facilitator writes the idea on a sticky note and puts it on the maybe-later wall. They remind the group that the storyboard uses the sketches already chosen, and that a new idea without a sketch or a vote would undo the morning's decision. The Decider agrees that pricing could be a future sprint. The team goes back to the next empty square and finishes the story before the end of the afternoon.
