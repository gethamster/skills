# Examples: Agile Coaching: Guiding a Team Through Adoption

## A skeptical team that starts with a board

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of seven has been told by leadership to "go agile." Several members have been through a failed Scrum rollout elsewhere and expect more meetings with no benefit.

**Walkthrough:**

The coach spends two weeks observing and talking to each person. The most common complaint is that work gets lost between people and nobody knows what anyone else is doing. The coach proposes one experiment: a visible board with columns for ready, in progress, review and done, reviewed for ten minutes each morning. The team agrees to try it for three weeks.

At the review, the team keeps the board and asks to add a limit on items in review, which keep piling up. The coach introduces WIP limits as the next experiment. The team's view of agile shifts from "more meetings" to "fixing our own problems."

## A team that depends on its coach

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a coach has run a team's planning, standups and retrospectives for several months. The sessions go well, but when the coach is away for a week, the team skips all of them.

**Walkthrough:**

The coach raises it at the next retrospective, and the team admits the practices feel like the coach's meetings. They agree a handover plan: each practice gets a rotating facilitator from the team, with the coach present but silent for the first round and absent after that.

After a month, the team runs every practice without the coach and changes the retrospective format on its own initiative. The coach moves to supporting the team's manager on removing organizational obstacles.

## Coaching beyond the ceremonies

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team has run Scrum events faithfully for months, but each sprint delivers less and releases often break.

**Walkthrough:**

The coach looks beyond the meetings and finds manual testing, rare integration and a growing list of known defects. At a retrospective, the coach shares the idea behind Flaccid Scrum and asks what the team wants to change. The team chooses automated tests for new code and a shared definition of done that includes them.

The coach pairs with developers in the first sprints to build the habit. Over the following sprints, releases break less often, and the health check rating for "Easy to release" moves from red to yellow.
