# FAQ: Coaching Teams Through Agile Adoption and Transformation

## How long does it take to coach a team through full agile adoption?

Typical adoption cycles run 6-12 weeks for a single team. The first practice can be introduced in week 1-2, but sustainable adoption, where the team runs ceremonies independently and self-corrects through retrospectives, usually requires 8-10 weeks. Teams with prior negative agile experiences take longer because you need to rebuild trust before introducing any process changes. Plan for 12 weeks in those cases.

## How do I handle a team member who actively resists every change I propose?

Individual resistance almost always has a specific root cause: a bad past experience, fear of exposure, or a legitimate concern that the proposed change will make their job harder. Schedule a private one-on-one and ask directly what concerns them. Listen without defending agile. Often, addressing their specific concern, such as capping stand-up duration at 10 minutes or ensuring retro feedback stays anonymous, converts resisters into participants.

If they remain opposed after their concerns are addressed, focus on the willing majority and let the resister observe positive results before re-engaging.

## Should I coach agile adoption before or after the team selects which agile framework to use?

Start coaching before the framework selection. Your diagnostic from Step 1 will reveal which framework fits best, and prescribing Scrum or Kanban before understanding the team's pain points leads to mismatches. For example, a team whose main issue is unpredictable work may be better served by Kanban than Scrum, and you will only know that after diagnosis. See [choosing between Scrum and Kanban](https://tryhamster.com/skills/choosing-between-scrum-and-kanban) for the framework selection process.

## How do I measure whether my coaching is actually working?

Track three categories of evidence. Behavioral signals: does the team run ceremonies without your prompting, and do they modify their own process through retrospectives? Quantitative metrics: has cycle time, blocker resolution speed, or delivery predictability improved? Team self-assessment: do the health snapshot scores trend upward over 3-4 measurement cycles?

If all three categories show improvement, coaching is working. If the metrics improve but the team cannot run independently, you have built a dependency, not a capability.

## What do I do when leadership mandates agile but refuses to change their own behavior?

This is the most common failure mode for agile adoptions. If leadership demands fixed scope, fixed deadlines, and detailed upfront plans while expecting the team to be 'agile,' the team is trapped. Address this directly with leadership by showing the specific contradiction: 'You have asked the team to deliver iteratively, but the quarterly planning process still requires committing to a fixed feature list 3 months out. ' Propose a concrete experiment for leadership too, such as reviewing scope at the end of each sprint rather than quarterly.

If leadership will not adjust, be transparent with the team about the constraint and focus on the agile practices that work within it.

## Why does my team keep reverting to old habits after I step back?

Reversion happens when the team adopted practices because the coach was present, not because they experienced the value themselves. The diagnostic signal is whether the team ever self-modified a practice during a retrospective. If they followed your prescription exactly without any team-initiated adjustments, they were complying rather than internalizing. To fix this, return to active coaching but shift your approach: instead of facilitating ceremonies, ask the team to run them and debrief afterward.

Focus retros on concrete incidents where the new practices helped or failed. Tie every practice back to a specific pain point the team identified themselves.

## Can I use this coaching approach for teams that are partially agile but stuck in bad habits?

Yes, and this is actually the most common coaching scenario. The approach is identical: diagnose first, then address the highest-impact problem. The difference is that your diagnostic will focus on where the existing agile implementation has gone wrong rather than mapping a pre-agile workflow. Common findings include retrospectives that produce no action items, sprint planning that ignores velocity data, or stand-ups that run 30+ minutes.

Treat each broken practice as its own mini-adoption cycle: propose a specific experiment to fix it, run it for 2-3 weeks, and evaluate.
