# FAQ: Agile Coaching: Guiding a Team Through Adoption

## What is the difference between an agile coach and a Scrum Master?

A Scrum Master is an accountability defined by the Scrum Guide, which includes coaching the team and the organization in Scrum. An agile coach is a broader role that may work with several teams, with leaders, and with frameworks other than Scrum. In practice the two overlap, and many Scrum Masters do the coaching this skill describes.

## How do I handle a team member who refuses to take part?

Talk to them privately and ask what concerns them. Resistance often reflects past experience of process imposed without benefit. Frame the next practice as an experiment the whole team will judge, and give their concern a place in the review.

## How long does it take a team to adopt agile?

It varies with the team, the organization and the starting point. Adoption is a series of small changes, each tested over a few weeks, rather than a single switch. Judge progress by whether the team runs and improves its own practices.

## Should the coach report to management on the team's progress?

Share goals and progress in terms the team has agreed to, such as which practices are in place and what obstacles need management's help. Do not share retrospective content or health check results without the team's consent. Reporting on the team undermines the trust coaching depends on.

## What if leadership wants a specific framework adopted by a deadline?

Explain the risk of imposed process and propose a staged adoption that starts with the team's biggest problem. Agree which outcomes leadership actually needs, such as predictable delivery, and show how each practice contributes. Help leadership see its own role in removing obstacles.
