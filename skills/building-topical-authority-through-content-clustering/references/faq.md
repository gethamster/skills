# FAQ: Building Topical Authority With Content Clustering

## What is topical authority?

It is a site being recognized by search engines as an expert source across the range of queries within a subject. It comes from covering the subject completely and connecting the pages so their relationships are clear. It is different from domain-level link strength: a focused site can compete on its topic with much larger sites.

## How many cluster pages does a topic need?

As many as the completeness checklist says, and no more. The number depends on how many distinct questions and use cases the topic has once keywords with the same search results are grouped together. Writing pages to reach a target count usually produces overlapping pages that compete with each other.

## How is this skill different from designing a cluster architecture?

The architecture skill produces the blueprint for one cluster: pages, URLs and links. This skill runs the program around it: the boundary, the completeness checklist, the publishing order, the depth work and the quarterly decisions about what to fill, merge or extend. Use the architecture skill inside steps three and four of this one.

## When should I start a second topic?

When the first topic's checklist is complete, pages no longer compete with each other, and visibility across the topic has been stable for a review or two. Starting earlier splits a small team's attention and leaves both topics thin.

## How do I know the program is working?

Topic-level visibility grows across many queries, including ones no page was written for. Fewer queries land on the wrong page, and competing pages disappear. Check whether new cluster pages start earning impressions sooner than the early ones did.
