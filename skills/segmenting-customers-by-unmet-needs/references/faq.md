# FAQ: Segmenting Customers by Unmet Needs

## How many survey responses do I need?

Enough that segments stay stable when you re-run the analysis on different halves of the data. Strategyn asks for "a statistically valid sample" before clustering ([outcome-based segmentation](https://strategyn.com/outcome-based-segmentation/)). Plan the sample size with whoever will run the analysis, and plan for segmentation before you field the survey.

## Can I segment by unmet needs using interviews only?

You can form hypotheses. Interview patterns often suggest groups with different struggles, and those are worth writing down. Treat them as candidate segments to test with survey data, since a handful of interviews cannot tell you how large each group is.

## Why use factor analysis before clustering?

Long outcome lists contain many items that move together. Reducing them to factors removes noise and makes the resulting segments easier to interpret. Strategyn describes using factor analysis and cluster analysis together for this reason ([outcome-based segmentation](https://strategyn.com/outcome-based-segmentation/)).

## What if the result is one big segment and several tiny ones?

It usually means the sample is too small, the inputs are noisy or the clustering is picking up how people answer surveys rather than what they need. Clean the data, cluster on factors rather than raw items, and test stability. If the pattern persists, report market-wide results and gather more data.

## How do I present segments to people used to personas?

Lead with the unmet needs that define each segment, then show the profile. Point out where a segment cuts across familiar personas, since that is the new information. Keep persona names out of segment names so the two views do not get merged.
