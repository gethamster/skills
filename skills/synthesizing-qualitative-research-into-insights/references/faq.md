# FAQ: Synthesizing Qualitative Research into Insights

## What is the difference between a theme and an insight?

A theme describes what happens, such as a group of notes about long waits. An insight explains why it matters by linking the behavior to an underlying need or motivation, often with the tension that makes the need hard to meet. Themes are an intermediate product of clustering. Insights are what the team designs against.

## How many people should take part in synthesis?

A small group works best, typically the researchers who were in the field plus one or two people who will build or run the solution. Too few and interpretation rests on one perspective. Too many and sorting slows into debate. If a wider audience needs to be involved, bring them in for a review of the clustered board rather than the sorting itself.

## Can synthesis be done alone?

It can, but it is riskier. A solo synthesizer has no one to challenge placements or catch the influence of a memorable participant. If you must work alone, keep observation and interpretation strictly separate and ask a colleague to review the clusters and insights against the raw notes before handoff.

## How do I know when synthesis is finished?

You are done when new notes stop changing the clusters, each insight links a behavior to a need, and every insight has a confidence label and evidence trail. If key insights rest on only a few participants or one context, synthesis may be finished for this round while research is not. Record those gaps and plan the next round around them.

## Should I use software or physical sticky notes?

Either works as long as everyone can see all the notes and move them. Physical walls encourage hands-on participation and are easy to set up with notes, markers and a flip chart. Digital boards suit remote teams and make it easier to keep participant tags and export the evidence trail. Pick based on where your team works, not on features.
