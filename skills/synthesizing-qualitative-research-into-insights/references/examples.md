# Examples: Synthesizing Qualitative Research into Insights

## Clinic check-in synthesis

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a service design team has completed interviews and observation at several outpatient clinics about how patients check in, and has a folder of notes, photos and quotes.

**Walkthrough:**

The team sets the unit of analysis as the check-in moment, from arriving at the building to being seated. They split notes into single observations, one color per participant, and cluster them in silence on a wall. Clusters emerge around wayfinding, paperwork and waiting anxiety. Comparing clusters, they spot a tension: patients say they prefer to check in themselves, yet many were observed queuing for the front desk.

The insight they write is that patients want reassurance a person knows they have arrived, and self-service kiosks do not provide it. They label it well supported for the clinics visited but note that evening appointments were not observed.

## Internal tool adoption

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an operations team interviewed and shadowed warehouse supervisors to understand why a new planning tool is barely used.

**Walkthrough:**

Notes are broken into observations, and the first clustering pass produces a large group called 'tool problems'. The facilitator splits it and asks for sentence names, which surfaces separate clusters about data entry time and about trust in the tool's forecasts. Looking across them, the team notices supervisors keep a paper backup because they are accountable for shortfalls the tool does not explain. The insight becomes: supervisors need to defend their plan to their manager, and a forecast they cannot explain is a risk rather than a help.

The team checks it against support tickets, which mention 'why did it suggest this' repeatedly, and marks the insight as corroborated.

## Catching a biased sample

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team synthesizing interviews about a budgeting app finds a strong pattern that users enjoy detailed category tracking.

**Walkthrough:**

During the evidence check, the team reviews participant codes on the supporting notes. Nearly all of them come from people recruited through the app's power-user forum. The team relabels the insight as a hypothesis limited to highly engaged users. They add lapsed and new users to the 'who we did not hear from' list.

The next research round targets those groups, and the handoff document explains why the finding is provisional.
