# FAQ: Reprioritizing and Moving Items Across Horizons

## How often should I review each horizon in a now next later agile roadmap?

Review Now every sprint or every two weeks because these are your active execution commitments and misalignment here has immediate cost. Review Next monthly because these items change at a moderate pace as evidence accumulates. Review Later quarterly or when a significant strategic event occurs, such as a pivot, a major customer win or loss, or annual planning. If you are just starting out, review all three together in a single session every two weeks until the team builds confidence with the process, then separate the cadences.

## What should I do when a stakeholder demands an item be promoted to Now without evidence?

Redirect the conversation to your graduation criteria. Ask the stakeholder to help you evaluate the item against the criteria dimensions: customer demand evidence, strategic alignment, technical feasibility, and resource availability. If the item does not meet the criteria, explain what evidence would need to exist for it to earn promotion. ").

Document the conversation so it does not repeat at the next review.

## Should I reprioritize across horizons before or after defining graduation criteria?

Define [graduation criteria](https://tryhamster.com/skills/defining-graduation-criteria-between-horizons) first, even if the definitions are rough and preliminary. Without criteria, your reprioritization review becomes a subjective debate that resolves based on whoever argues most persuasively. Having even imperfect criteria gives the team a shared reference point. You can refine the criteria after 2-3 review cycles based on what you learn about which criteria actually predict success after promotion.

## How do I handle items that keep bouncing between horizons?

An item that has been promoted and demoted more than twice is a signal that either the graduation criteria are poorly calibrated, the item's scope is unclear, or there is unresolved disagreement about its strategic value. Pull the item out of the regular review and run a dedicated 15-minute discussion focused exclusively on it. Clarify what would need to be true for this item to stay in Now permanently. If you cannot articulate clear conditions, the item likely belongs in Later until the strategic picture clarifies, or it should be removed entirely.

## How do I keep the Later horizon from becoming a dumping ground for ideas nobody will ever build?

Apply a maximum age rule. Any item that has been in Later for more than two quarterly reviews (roughly 6 months) without accumulating new supporting evidence should be archived. This is not the same as rejecting the idea. Archiving preserves the item in your decision log so it can be retrieved if circumstances change.

But it removes it from the active roadmap, which keeps Later focused on ideas with genuine strategic potential rather than aspirational clutter. A healthy Later column contains 8-15 items, not 50.

## Can I use this reprioritization process with a Kanban board instead of a dedicated roadmap tool?

Yes. Map the three horizons to three columns or swimlanes on your board. The review process is identical regardless of tooling: walk through each column, evaluate items against criteria, move cards between columns, and document the rationale. Some teams use labels or tags instead of separate columns, which works as long as you can filter the view to see one horizon at a time during the review.

The process is tool-agnostic. What matters is the cadence, the criteria, and the documentation habit.

## Why does my roadmap keep drifting back to looking like a fixed timeline even after adopting now next later agile?

This usually happens when stakeholders or leadership pressure the team for delivery dates on Next and Later items. The team responds by attaching implicit timelines to horizons ("Next means next quarter"), which defeats the purpose of the framework. Combat this by reinforcing that horizons represent certainty and readiness, not calendar dates. " for Next and Later items.
