# FAQ: Reprioritizing Roadmap Items Across Now, Next and Later

## How often should we review the roadmap?

Tie the regular review to your planning cycle, and add unscheduled reviews when a significant event changes the evidence, such as a major experiment result, a customer loss or a strategy change. ProdPad's guidance is to review every planning cycle and whenever new evidence shifts confidence. A roadmap that has gone a whole quarter without changes usually signals stalled discovery.

## Who should attend the review?

The core product trio of product, engineering and design, plus anyone who owns evidence for the items being discussed. Stakeholders can attend occasionally, but the review works best as a working session. Share the outcome with everyone afterwards.

## What should we do when a stakeholder objects to a demotion?

Show the reason and the evidence behind it, and ask whether they have evidence the team lacks. If they do, reconsider. If they do not, explain what would bring the item back, using the graduation criteria. Talking to them before the change is published avoids most of the friction.

## Should finished items stay on the roadmap?

Move them off the active columns, but keep a completed view. A record of recently finished initiatives shows progress, and ProdPad recommends including recently launched items on public roadmaps for the same reason. It also gives the team a basis for checking whether shipped work moved its objectives.

## How is this different from backlog grooming?

Backlog grooming refines delivery tickets for upcoming sprints. Reprioritizing works one level up, on problem-framed initiatives and the horizons they sit in. The two connect when an initiative reaches Now and its validated ideas become backlog items.
