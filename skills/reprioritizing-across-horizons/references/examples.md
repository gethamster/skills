# Examples: Reprioritizing Roadmap Items Across Now, Next and Later

## A routine review that promotes and demotes

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team reviews its roadmap at the end of each two-week cycle. Since the last review, a prototype test for a Next item on bulk editing went well, and discovery on a Later bet about a partner marketplace found little customer interest.

**Walkthrough:**

In Now, one initiative finished and one is blocked waiting on a design hire. The team moves the blocked item back to Next with the note "waiting on capacity, evidence unchanged," which frees room. Bulk editing meets the Next to Now criteria after the prototype test, so it moves into Now.

In Later, the partner marketplace bet is removed with the reason "interviews found few customers who would use it; revisit if partner requests grow." The change note to stakeholders lists the three moves and their reasons, and the sales lead who had asked about the marketplace gets a direct message.

## An event-triggered review after a customer loss

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B product loses a large customer, and the exit interview names poor audit logging as the main reason. The next scheduled review is a few weeks away.

**Walkthrough:**

The product manager calls a short triggered review. Audit logging has been sitting in Later as a vague compliance bet. The exit interview, combined with a search of support tickets that turns up similar complaints from other accounts, is enough to meet the Later to Next criteria.

The team moves it to Next and starts discovery on what customers need logged. Nothing in Now is bumped yet, because the solution is still unclear. The change note explains the trigger and says the item will be reconsidered for Now at the next regular review.

## Clearing backlog gravity from Next

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team notices that several items have sat in Next for many cycles. They were promised to stakeholders long ago, and nobody wants to be the one to remove them.

**Walkthrough:**

The product lead asks, for each long-standing item, which current objective it serves and what evidence has changed since it arrived. Two items serve retired objectives and have no new evidence; they move to Later with a note. One has quietly become irrelevant after a product change and is removed.

The lead meets the stakeholders who originally asked for the items before publishing the change, explains the reasons, and asks whether they have evidence the team missed. One stakeholder does, and that item stays in Next with a named owner for discovery.
