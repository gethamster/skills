# FAQ: Shifting from Working In to Working On Your Business

## What counts as working on the business?

Work that changes how the business runs or where it is going. Documenting a process, redesigning how work flows, hiring, pricing, choosing which customers to serve and deciding what to stop selling all count. Doing the delivery work yourself, even very well, is working in the business. A useful test is whether the result will still help after you stop doing the task.

## How much time should I move?

There is no fixed number. Start with one recurring block you can protect every week and grow it as handoffs hold. Birkinshaw and Cohen's research for Harvard Business Review suggests many people could free a large share of their week, but your first target should be a block you will actually keep.

## What if nobody on my team can take the work?

Sort the task as long-term redesign and use your first blocks on the business to write the process, so that a new hire, a contractor or a service could take it later. Some tasks can also be dropped outright once you ask who would notice. If there is truly no one, the first thing to work on may be the hiring plan.

## Clients insist on working with me. What do I do?

Introduce the person taking over while you are still involved, and tell the client what stays with you. Keep a visible role at the start of the relationship and at key decisions, and let the delegate own the day-to-day contact. Most clients care that the work is good and that someone responsive owns it.

## How do I stop tasks from creeping back?

Run the weekly review and look at every task that returned to you. The cause is usually a missing standard, unclear authority or a delegate who does not know what to do next. Fix the cause in writing so the same task does not come back the following week.
