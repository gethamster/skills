# Examples: Presenting GIST Plans to Stakeholders

## A quarterly review with the leadership team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product manager presents the team's plan at a quarterly leadership review. The previous quarter's reviews were feature roadmaps, and one executive has asked when a specific integration will ship.

**Walkthrough:**

She opens with the team's goal: raising the share of new accounts that reach first value in their first week, with the baseline and target. She shows the three ideas in the working set with their ICE scores and names the evidence behind each. The integration is one of them, with low Confidence because the only support so far is a few sales requests.

She then describes the current step for the integration: a round of customer interviews and a mock-up test that end in three weeks and will decide whether a build is worth it. When the executive asks for a ship date, she gives the date of that decision instead and promises to share the result. She closes with an idea stopped last quarter and the effort it saved.

## A kickoff with engineering and design

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team lead introduces GIST to the engineers and designers who will run the first step-projects. They are used to receiving specs with fixed scope.

**Walkthrough:**

The lead keeps the goal short and spends most of the time on steps and tasks. She shows the first step-project brief: its question, its threshold, its end date and its owner. She shows the few tasks it needs, including the instrumentation, and explains that polish waits until the idea earns a larger step.

The engineers ask what happens to their work if the idea fails. The lead explains that the step was designed to be small for exactly that reason, and that the result will be recorded and the next idea brought in. The GIST board goes up next to the team's task board.

## A product manager interview

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a candidate is asked in an interview how they would plan the next quarter for a product whose retention is falling.

**Walkthrough:**

The candidate sketches four layers. For the goal, they propose a retention metric with a baseline to be measured and a target to be set with the team. For ideas, they name three possible causes and a solution for each, and explain how they would rank them by impact, confidence and ease, with confidence low until there is data.

For the first step, they describe a cheap test for the top idea: analyzing where retained and churned users differ, then interviewing a few churned users. They finish with the first tasks: pulling the cohort data and scheduling the interviews. When asked what happens if the test fails, they move to the next idea and keep the goal.
