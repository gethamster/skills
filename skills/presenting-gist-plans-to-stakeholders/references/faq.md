# FAQ: Presenting GIST Plans to Stakeholders

## How do I answer "when will it ship"?

Give the date of the next decision instead of a ship date. Explain which step is running, when it ends, and what result would lead to a full build. If a timeline is truly required, use an outcome roadmap that shows discovery and delivery honestly.

## Should I show the whole GIST board?

For team meetings, yes. For executives, follow one goal from top to bottom and summarize the others. Gilad suggests stakeholders see the board regularly, so a shared board between meetings reduces the need for detail in the room.

## How do I present an idea with low confidence?

State the confidence level and the evidence behind it, then explain the test that will raise or lower it. Presenting uncertainty with a plan to resolve it builds more trust than a confident promise that later slips.

## Do stakeholders accept GIST instead of a roadmap?

Gilad reports that coworkers and board members readily understand the language of goals, ideas and step-projects and appreciate its realism. Some stakeholders still want dates. Give them decision dates and, when needed, an outcome roadmap.

## How should I use GIST in a product manager interview?

Use it as a structure for your reasoning. Pick one concrete goal, name a few ideas and how you would rank them, describe a cheap first test, and list the first tasks. Explain your choices at each layer, since the interviewer is judging how you think.
