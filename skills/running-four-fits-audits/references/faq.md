# FAQ: Running Periodic Four Fits Audits

## How often should we run the audit?

Match the cadence to how fast your market and channels change, and add event triggers. Early-stage companies changing quickly may review more often than established ones. Whatever the schedule, run an extra audit after a pricing change, a new primary channel, a platform rule change, or a sudden move in any indicator.

## Who should attend?

A small group that covers the product, the primary channel, pricing or finance, and customer insight, plus a facilitator. Each fit needs someone who knows its evidence. Keep the group small enough to reach decisions in one meeting.

## What scale should we use for scoring?

A simple scale such as strong, moderate, and weak works well, as long as each level has a written definition for each fit. The definitions keep scores consistent across audits and people. Numeric scales add false precision unless they are tied to specific thresholds.

## What if we lack data for a fit?

Score it as unknown and make gathering the evidence an action. Missing data is itself a finding, because a fit without evidence cannot be defended when it matters. Avoid filling gaps with estimates presented as measurements.

## How do we keep audits from becoming routine box-ticking?

Spend most of each meeting on the weakest or fastest-declining fit, and open each audit by checking whether the last audit's actions happened. If actions keep slipping, reduce their number. The audit is only useful if it changes what the team does.
