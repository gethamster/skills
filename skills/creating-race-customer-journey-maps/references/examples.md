# Examples: RACE Framework Journey Map

## An online course provider finds the Act question nobody answered

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an online course provider sells professional certificates. Traffic is healthy, but few visitors enroll. The team maps the journey for one persona: a mid-career professional paying for the course personally.

**Walkthrough:**

Interviews with people who browsed but did not enroll raise the same question again and again: whether employers would recognize the certificate. The Act column shows that no page answers it. The analytics numbers under the column confirm the drop: many course page views, few syllabus downloads.

The top opportunity sits in Act: a short page on how past learners used the certificate at work, with an option to download a letter for employers. The owner is the content lead, and the KPI is syllabus downloads per course page view.

## A B2B supplier maps the wait between Act and Convert

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an industrial parts supplier generates quote requests through its website. Sales says lead quality is poor, and marketing says sales is slow.

**Walkthrough:**

The map for the purchasing-manager persona puts quote requests in Act and signed orders in Convert. Interviews with buyers who went elsewhere reveal the friction sits at the transition: they waited days for a quote and bought from a competitor who answered first. The CRM data under the columns shows the same gap.

The opportunity is owned jointly: marketing adds instant indicative pricing for standard parts, and sales commits to a response time for custom quotes. The map's transition marker is updated to show what now moves the buyer on.

## A subscription box maps Engage for the first time

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a coffee subscription company has mapped Reach to Convert before, but never Engage. Cancellations after the second delivery are rising.

**Walkthrough:**

The team splits Engage into sub-stages: first delivery, second delivery, and ongoing. Interviews with customers who cancelled show that many did not know they could change grind or pause deliveries, and felt stuck with coffee they did not like.

Opportunities include a short note in the first box explaining how to adjust the subscription, and an email before the second delivery asking whether to change anything. Each is tied to the Engage KPI of active subscribers after the third delivery, with the customer experience lead as owner.
