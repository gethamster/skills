# Examples: Designing Content Cluster Architectures

## A new cluster on a small site

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a bookkeeping firm wants a cluster on month-end close for small businesses. The keyword map has one broad group for the pillar and eight narrower groups. Two old blog posts touch the topic.

**Walkthrough:**

The pillar page gets one section per major subtopic: preparing, reconciling, reviewing and reporting. Each section summarizes the step and links to a supporting page. The eight groups become eight supporting pages, one of them a rewrite of an old post. The second old post overlaps with the reconciliation page, so the blueprint records a merge and a redirect.

All new pages go under one resources path. The pillar is linked from the site's resources menu and from the service page for bookkeeping. Cross-links follow the order of the close: preparing links to reconciling, reconciling links to reviewing. The pillar launches with four supporting pages and the other four follow over the next two months.

## A pillar page that was too narrow

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a developer tools company has a long page on configuring one build setting and calls it the pillar for its build performance cluster.

**Walkthrough:**

Applying the scope test shows the page answers one narrow question in depth, which makes it a supporting page. The team writes a new pillar page on build performance that summarizes caching, parallelism, dependencies and monitoring, and links to a supporting page for each.

The old page stays as a supporting page under the caching section. Its anchor text in the pillar is updated to describe exactly what it covers. The blueprint lists two planned supporting pages for subtopics that have little demand yet.

## Restructuring a tangled archive

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a travel publisher has many posts about packing, spread across several folders, with links added over the years in no particular pattern.

**Walkthrough:**

The keyword map shows several posts serving the same intent. The blueprint picks one page per intent, records merges and redirects for the rest, and names a new pillar page for packing guides.

The team decides not to move every surviving post into a new folder, because the posts already rank and redirects add risk. Instead they fix the linking: every packing post now links to the pillar with a consistent anchor, the pillar links to each post, and cross-links follow common trip types. The blueprint is dated and stored with the team's content plans.
