# FAQ: Comparing Agile and Waterfall for Project Selection

## Is waterfall ever the better choice?

Yes. When requirements are fixed and well understood, stakeholders can only engage at defined points, and a regulator requires formal evidence at each stage, a plan-driven approach can cost less than repeated iteration. The scoring in this skill is designed to show when that is the case. Even then, building an early version of the riskiest part is worth doing.

## Did Royce invent waterfall?

His 1970 paper drew the sequential phases that later became known as waterfall, but he called that single-pass version risky and recommended doing the job twice. Larman and Basili describe the common reading of the paper as a misunderstanding. The word "waterfall" does not appear in the paper itself.

## Can a regulated project be agile?

Often, yes. Regulation usually constrains what evidence you must produce and who must approve it, and those can be produced within iterative delivery. The compliance score in this skill captures how much formal gating the project needs, and a low score usually points to a hybrid rather than ruling iteration out.

## What should a hybrid look like?

Name which decisions are made at gates and which are made in cycles. A common pattern is fixed funding and compliance gates, with delivery inside each stage run in short cycles that end in working software. Write it down so each group knows which rules apply to them.

## Who should make the final call?

The sponsor or product owner makes the call, informed by the group's scores. The value of the workshop is that the decision and its accepted risks are visible to everyone who will be asked to deliver under it.
