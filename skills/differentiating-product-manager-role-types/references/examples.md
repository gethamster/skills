# Examples: Differentiating PM Roles with the Competency Framework

## Separating a growth PM from a core consumer PM

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a consumer subscription product has a core PM and a newly hired growth PM. Both are writing specs for the onboarding flow, and the team is confused about who decides.

**Walkthrough:**

The head of product places both roles on the grid. The core PM's core boxes are Jobs to be Done, User Interviews, Product Roadmap and Feature Prioritization. The growth PM's core boxes are Conversion Optimization, A/B Testing and Product-Market Fit, with Opportunity Discovery as an occasional box.

The boundary rules say the core PM owns what onboarding must accomplish for the user, and the growth PM owns experiments on how the flow converts. Feature Prioritization is shared through a joint weekly review. The duplicate specs stop.

## Defining a platform PM role

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an engineering organization wants its first platform PM to own shared services used by several product teams. The draft job ad reads like a consumer PM role.

**Walkthrough:**

The hiring manager places the role on the right of the chart. Its core boxes are Technical Partnerships, Build vs Buy, System Analysis and Internal Tools Requirements, with Portfolio Planning at the senior level. Following Cagan's point about platform PMs serving several constituencies, User Requests from the internal teams that use the platform is added as a core box.

Market Positioning and Conversion Optimization are marked not expected. The rewritten profile attracts candidates with engineering and internal tools backgrounds instead of consumer marketers.

## Growth PM vs technical PM on the same team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a data product team has a growth PM and a technical PM, and leadership wonders whether one of them is redundant.

**Walkthrough:**

Side by side on the chart, the growth PM sits left of center, weighted toward Market boxes. The technical PM sits on the right, weighted toward System Analysis, Data Queries and Model Evals. Their only shared boxes are in Core Product.

Leadership keeps both roles and writes boundary rules for the Core Product boxes. The chart makes it clear that the two roles cover different parts of the grid.
