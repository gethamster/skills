# Examples: Keyword Research for Conversational AI Queries

## A project management vendor rebuilding its keyword list

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a project management software company targets a list of short keywords such as "project management software" and "task tracker". Its sales team reports that prospects arrive with long, specific questions they asked an assistant first, and the company's pages do not answer them.

**Walkthrough:**

The marketing team pulls a quarter of sales call notes and support tickets and extracts every question in the customer's words. Questions like "Which project tool handles client approvals for a small design agency?" show context the keyword list never captured: agency work, client approvals, small teams.

The team adds long question-shaped queries from Search Console and grounding queries from Bing Webmaster Tools, then puts a sample of questions to several assistants and notes the sub-topics each answer covers. Clusters emerge around client approvals, billing, and moving from spreadsheets.

Each cluster maps to one page. The client approvals cluster becomes a single guide with sections for the sub-questions and links to the billing page, because billing is the usual follow-up.

## A local accounting firm and spoken questions

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an accounting firm serving small businesses in one city finds that its service pages use professional terms, while clients ask questions in everyday language, often by voice or through an assistant.

**Walkthrough:**

The partners list the questions clients ask in first meetings and by email, such as "Do I need an accountant if I just started freelancing?" and "What happens if I miss the quarterly tax deadline?". They group the questions by job: getting started, deadlines, deductions and switching accountants.

Asking assistants these questions shows that answers cite national guides and government pages, with no local firms. The firm decides its best targets are questions where local context matters, such as city-specific registration steps, and where it can add practical experience.

The firm rewrites its service pages to answer those questions directly and keeps its business profile details current, since local answers draw on business listings as well as web pages.

## Consolidating variant pages into one

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a software company's earlier SEO work produced several near-identical pages for variations of one question, such as "how to reduce churn", "reducing customer churn" and "churn reduction strategies". None of them is cited in AI answers.

**Walkthrough:**

The question map groups all the variants into one cluster with one main question. The team compares the pages, keeps the strongest URL, merges the best material from the others into it and redirects the rest.

The merged page gets sections for the sub-questions the assistants' answers covered, such as measuring churn, early warning signs and retention tactics by customer segment. The team adds the company's own anonymized retention findings with the method explained.

The variants stay in the tracking set so the team can see whether the single page is retrieved for them over the following months.
