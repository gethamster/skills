# Examples: Running Sprint Planning and Agile Sprint Execution

## Turning a ticket list into a sprint goal

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of five arrives at planning with the top ten backlog items already refined. The items span billing, search and account settings, and the Product Owner wants "as many as possible."

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator asks what would make this sprint valuable to customers. The discussion settles on search, because support reports that customers cannot find past invoices. The goal becomes "Customers can find any past invoice by date or amount." Four of the ten items serve it; two billing items that do not serve it move down.

The team's recent sprints finished around 20 points of work, and one person is out for three days, so the team plans about 16 points and keeps one small item as a stretch. Everyone can state the goal before the meeting ends.

## A mid-sprint request from sales

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: halfway through a two-week sprint, a sales lead asks the team to add a custom export for a prospect. The sprint goal is about invoice search.

**Walkthrough:**

The request goes to the Product Owner, who asks whether it serves the goal. It does not. The Product Owner checks with the sales lead and learns the prospect's decision is a month away, so the export goes to the top of the backlog for the next sprint.

The team notes the request on its board as a deferred change. In the retrospective, the team sees that three mid-sprint requests in the last month came from sales and agrees to invite the sales lead to the next sprint review.

## A sprint that misses its goal

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team plans an integration with a payment provider. On day four, it discovers the provider's test environment does not support the needed feature.

**Walkthrough:**

At the next Daily Scrum, the team flags the goal as at risk and the Product Owner joins the discussion. They agree to renegotiate scope: the team builds and tests everything on its side against a stub and escalates the provider issue.

At the review, the team shows the working parts, explains the blocker and what it learned, and stakeholders agree to a new date. The retrospective produces one change: test third-party dependencies before planning a sprint around them.
