# FAQ: Running Sprint Planning and Agile Sprint Execution

## How long should sprint planning take?

The Scrum Guide sets a maximum of eight hours for a one-month sprint, and shorter sprints usually need less. If planning regularly runs long, the backlog is probably not refined enough beforehand. Fix refinement before cutting the planning meeting.

## Should we plan using story points or hours?

Either can work. Story points and relative sizing avoid arguments about exact hours and tend to be quicker, while task hours can help a team check a plan against the time people actually have. Many teams size stories in points and break only the first few days of work into tasks.

## What happens to unfinished work at the end of a sprint?

It goes back to the product backlog, where the Product Owner re-orders it with everything else. It does not automatically start the next sprint. Look at why it was not finished in the retrospective.

## Can the sprint goal change during the sprint?

Scope can be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as the team learns, but changes that endanger the goal should not be made. If the goal itself becomes obsolete, the Product Owner can cancel the sprint and the team plans a new one.

## How do we handle support work alongside sprint work?

Reserve capacity for it during planning based on how much support work recent sprints absorbed. If support dominates, consider whether a flow-based approach fits the team better, as covered in the Scrum and Kanban skill.
