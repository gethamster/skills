# Examples: Tracking Your Visibility in AI Search Results

## Setting up tracking for a software company

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B software company has finished its first brand audit and wants a monthly view of AI visibility without buying a tool yet. The team has a question set of 40 questions in four groups.

**Walkthrough:**

The team exports the Search Console Generative AI performance report and the Bing AI Performance report on the first working day of each month and stores both in a shared folder. It runs the question set in three assistants in fresh sessions, twice per question, and logs each answer in a sheet.

Illustrative scenario: first-month results from the log for one question group.

| Question group | Answers logged | Answers mentioning the brand | Answers citing the site |
|---|---|---|---|
| Category questions | 60 | 12 | 6 |
| Comparison questions | 60 | 30 | 15 |

The mention rate for category questions is 20% (12 of 60), and the citation rate is 10% (6 of 60). The team marks category questions as the priority and records the pages it plans to change, with dates, in a change log next to the sheet.

## Comparing against competitors in one category

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an ecommerce brand selling running shoes wants to know how often it appears next to its main competitors when people ask assistants for shoe recommendations.

**Walkthrough:**

The brand adds comparison and recommendation questions to its set and logs every brand named and every URL cited in each answer. After a few runs, it counts mentions and citations for itself and each competitor by question group.

The log shows that for trail running questions, answers cite independent review sites and a competitor's buying guide, while the brand's own product pages are rarely cited. The brand's Bing citation share for related grounding queries confirms low presence.

The team plans a detailed trail running guide with test notes from its product team and pitches its products to independent reviewers. It keeps the trail running group as a named line in the monthly report.

## Connecting AI visibility to signups

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a leadership team asks whether AI visibility work is worth continuing, because organic sessions have fallen while the AI visibility numbers have risen.

**Walkthrough:**

The analyst segments sessions referred by AI assistant domains in the analytics tool and compares their engagement and signup rate with other organic sessions. She plots branded search impressions from Search Console alongside the AI visibility trend.

The report shows AI citations, AI-referred sessions and their conversion rate side by side, with the change log marking when major pages were updated. It states plainly which movements line up with content changes and which do not.

The leadership team decides based on signups and engaged sessions from AI referrals, with visibility numbers as the leading indicator, rather than on organic sessions alone.
