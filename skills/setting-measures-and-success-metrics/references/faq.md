# FAQ: Setting V2MOM Measures and Success Metrics

## What makes a good V2MOM measure?

A good measure names a metric, a baseline, a target and a date, and it describes an outcome rather than an activity. It is tied to a method in the same V2MOM and has one owner who reports on it. Anyone reading it should be able to tell at a review whether it is on track.

## How many measures should a V2MOM have?

At least one per ranked method, plus leading or counter measures where they add real information. The limit is attention: every measure needs to be reviewed. If the list grows long, keep the measures for the top methods and drop the rest.

## What is the difference between leading and lagging measures?

A lagging measure shows the final outcome, such as renewals or revenue, and often moves slowly. A leading measure moves earlier and predicts the outcome, such as product usage in the first weeks after a sale. Pairing them lets a team see trouble in time to act instead of learning the result at year end.

## Should V2MOM measures be tied to compensation?

Be cautious. Tying measures to pay tends to push people toward safe targets and away from reporting problems early. OKR practitioners such as John Doerr's What Matters recommend keeping OKRs separate from compensation for similar reasons. If measures do feed into reviews, look at how the person handled the work, not only whether the number was hit.

## What if we do not have data for a measure yet?

Make getting a baseline the first action under the method, and set the target once the first data arrives. Committing to a target for a metric nobody can measure turns every review into an argument about the numbers. A short delay to get real data is better than a target that cannot be checked.
