# FAQ: Conducting Weekly Customer Interviews

## How many continuous customer interviews should we do each week?

Sources vary. One review describes the benchmark as at least one customer per week, while a book summary describes three to five per week for a trio. Start with one real story-based conversation every week and add more only when your team can still write snapshots and act on them. Consistency over months matters more than volume in any single week.

## What makes an interview story-based?

A story-based interview asks the customer to recount a specific past event, usually starting with "Tell me about the last time you". The interviewer then helps them reconstruct what happened step by step, including obstacles, workarounds and feelings. It avoids asking customers to predict future behavior or react to your ideas, because those answers are unreliable. The result is evidence grounded in what people actually did.

## What goes into an interview snapshot?

A snapshot is a one-page summary written right after the interview. It typically includes the participant's context, a memorable quote, a sketch of the story's key steps, the opportunities heard framed as customer needs or pains, and open questions. Writing it together as a trio aligns everyone on what was heard. Over time, the collection of snapshots becomes the evidence base for mapping opportunities.

## Can we keep a weekly cadence in a regulated or enterprise B2B setting?

It is harder, and one review names regulated industries, enterprise B2B with limited user access and intense delivery pressure as the toughest settings. Adapt the tactics rather than dropping the habit: shorter calls, introductions through account or support teams, user groups and internal staff who do similar work. Count any genuine story-based conversation with a user as the week's touchpoint. The goal is steady contact, not a perfect research protocol.

## What if our engineers say they have no time for interviews?

Frame it as a short, fixed commitment with no preparation or follow-up work beyond the snapshot debrief. Engineers who hear a customer describe a workaround often spot a fix or a risk that never appears in a written ticket. If full attendance is impossible, rotate which engineer joins each week. The aim is that every trio member hears customers directly on a regular basis.
