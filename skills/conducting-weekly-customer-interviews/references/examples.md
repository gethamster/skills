# Examples: Conducting Weekly Customer Interviews

## Early-Stage SaaS Team Starting from Scratch

**Scenario:**

A small startup building a task-sharing tool has never interviewed customers systematically and wants to improve how many new users become weekly regulars.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the team of four books one interview every Tuesday afternoon and sends an automatic invitation to anyone who signed up in the past two weeks and logged in at least twice. Their single opening prompt is "Tell me about your first day using the tool." In week one, a customer describes trying to share a project with a colleague and giving up. In week two, another explains exporting tasks to a spreadsheet so a manager could see progress.

By week four, three of the four snapshots mention sharing as the point where they stalled, so the team moves that opportunity ahead of the reporting work it had planned.

## Enterprise HR Platform with Limited Access

**Scenario:**

A product trio at an HR software company serves large enterprise customers whose HR managers are hard to reach, and leadership is pushing a specific matching feature.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the trio asks account managers to introduce one HR manager per week who posted a job recently, and offers a short call instead of a long study. The prompt is "Walk me through the last role you hired for, from the moment you decided to hire." Over the first three weeks, three of the four HR managers describe long waits for internal approvals before the job was ever posted. Their snapshots show the delay sits upstream of the matching feature leadership wants.

The trio brings the customer quotes to leadership, and the conversation shifts to the approval workflow as the bigger opportunity.

## Consumer Fitness App Investigating Churn

**Scenario:**

A fitness app team can see in analytics when users stop opening the app but has no idea why, and wants to reduce early churn.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the team alternates weeks, inviting active users one week and recently cancelled subscribers the next. Active users are asked to describe their last workout, and cancelled users are asked about the last time they opened the app. By week six, four of the six cancelled users describe missing two days in a row, seeing a broken-streak message and feeling discouraged. Several active users describe building their own rest-day routine that the app does not support.

The engineer, who heard these stories firsthand, proposes treating rest days as part of the plan, an idea the funnel charts alone had never suggested.
