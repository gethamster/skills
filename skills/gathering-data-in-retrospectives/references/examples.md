# Examples: Gathering Data in Retrospectives

## A timeline for a sprint with a late release

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of seven shipped its release three days after the planned date. People remember the sprint differently, and a few already have strong views about why.

**Walkthrough:**

Before the meeting, the facilitator prepares a one-page sheet: the planned and actual release dates, the stories added mid-sprint, and the two actions from the previous retrospective with their status. In the meeting she draws a line across the board for the sprint's ten working days.

Each person spends a few quiet minutes writing events on notes and placing them on the line, with a mark above or below it for how they felt. The board fills up around day four, when a dependency slipped, and again at the end. Day two has almost nothing on it, so she asks what happened then. A tester remembers that the test environment was down that morning, which nobody else knew.

She reads the notes back and groups duplicates. The group agrees the picture is complete, and she moves to the insight phase with two visible clusters: the dependency slip and the late test environment.

## Anonymous input after a tense sprint

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a sprint ended with an argument between two developers during a code review. The team of five knows about it, and the facilitator expects people to avoid the topic in open discussion.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator runs a Mad Sad Glad board on a digital tool with anonymous notes. He asks for observations about how work moved through review, not about individuals, and reminds the team of its agreement to discuss the process.

Several notes mention review delays and unclear expectations for what a review should check. Two mention feeling uncomfortable raising disagreements. He reads all notes aloud without asking who wrote them, groups them into review process and disagreement norms, and asks whether anything is missing. One person adds that reviews pile up on the last two days. The team goes into the insight phase with the process issue in front of it, and the argument itself is not rehashed.

## Short data gathering for a weekly retrospective

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of four runs a short retrospective every week. Last week's action was to cut the time pull requests wait for review.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator starts with the action. She shows how many pull requests waited more than a day this week compared with the week before, taken from the team's repository. The number went down.

She then asks one open question: "What else stood out this week?" Each person writes two notes. The notes point to a flaky test suite and a quiet week for support tickets. With the action's result and two new observations on the board, the team moves on.
