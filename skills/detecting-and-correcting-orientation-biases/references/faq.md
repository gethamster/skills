# FAQ: Detecting and Correcting Cognitive Biases in Orientation

## Why focus on the Orient phase rather than the whole OODA loop?

Orient is where observations pass through your experience, culture and mental models, so it is where interpretation can be distorted. Because orientation also shapes what you observe next, a bias there spreads to every other phase. Fixing Orient therefore protects the decisions and actions that follow. Biases can appear elsewhere, but Orient is the highest-leverage place to check.

## What is incestuous amplification in the OODA loop?

It is the feedback trap where your current orientation drives observation toward data that confirm it, and the confirming data then strengthen the orientation. An analysis of Boyd's loop uses the term for exactly this pattern. The loop feels like growing evidence while actually being a closed circle. A disconfirmation search is the most direct way to break it.

## How is anchoring different from confirmation bias?

Confirmation bias affects what you search for: you look for support and downplay conflict. Anchoring affects how you weigh what you find: later evidence is judged relative to the first datum instead of on its own merits. They often travel together, since the first report usually becomes the belief you then try to confirm. The controls differ, with disconfirmation targeting the search and independent assessment targeting the weighting.

## Can a checklist eliminate bias from decision making?

No. The sources describe biases as vulnerabilities in the OODA process and offer controls for detecting and correcting them, not for removing them. A checklist makes bias easier to catch and harder to ignore. Expect some distortion to survive, which is why the handoff should list signals that would reopen the interpretation.

## How long should a bias check take?

Long enough to state the explanation, define and search for a disconfirmer, and challenge the anchor, and no longer. Practitioner guidance recommends time-boxing Orient to the stakes rather than using one fixed duration. A reversible call might need a quick pass, while a hard-to-reverse one justifies a red team. When the time runs out, hand off what you have along with its open questions.
