# FAQ: Balancing Desirability, Feasibility, and Viability

## Which lens should come first?

None should come first in the sense of acting as a gate the others wait behind. The point of the framework is to find the overlap, so gather readings on all three before deciding. In practice desirability evidence often exists earliest because it comes from research, but feasibility and viability owners should see concepts at the same time. Sequencing them usually means the last lens gets overruled.

## What if a concept fails one lens badly?

First ask whether a change to the concept would let that lens pass without breaking the others. Scope cuts, phased releases or a different delivery channel often rescue a strong idea. If nothing works, park it with a note on what would need to change, such as new technology or a different business model. Do not average it into the middle of the ranking.

## How do we score desirability without real users?

You can record a reading, but mark its evidence as assumed and treat it as the first thing to test. Research insights give reported evidence, and watching people use a rough prototype gives observed evidence. A concept advancing on assumed desirability should come with a scheduled user test in its action plan. That keeps the evaluation honest about where confidence comes from.

## Does this apply outside commercial products?

Yes, with viability read as whether the organization can sustain the solution. For a public service or nonprofit program that means budget, staffing and mandate rather than revenue. The questions change but the logic of checking all three lenses together stays the same. Name a viability owner who is accountable for those organizational constraints.

## How often should we re-run the evaluation?

Re-run it whenever new evidence changes a lens reading, typically after a prototype test or a technical spike. Because the process is iterative, the first evaluation is a checkpoint, not a verdict. Update the decision record each time so the team can see how confidence in each lens has moved. A concept whose weakest lens never improves across rounds is a signal to stop.
