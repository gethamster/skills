# FAQ: Managing Shared Task State

## Why is a task still blocked when its prerequisite looks finished?

The most common cause is that the prerequisite was never marked complete. Anthropic documents that teammates sometimes fail to mark tasks complete, which leaves dependents blocked. Check the prerequisite's output, and if it meets the definition of done, update its status or ask the owning teammate to. Dependents should then unblock.

## Should the lead assign tasks or let teammates claim them?

Both patterns work with a shared list, and practitioners describe teammates claiming work directly from it. Assignment gives the lead more control when roles and file ownership are fixed. Self-claiming suits pools of similar, independent tasks. Whichever you use, ensure each task has one visible owner before work begins.

## How often should I check the task list?

Check whenever a teammate reports in and whenever a teammate goes idle, since those are the moments status and reality are most likely to diverge. Also check if a chain of blocked tasks has not moved for a while. Waiting until the whole team is quiet usually means several dependents have been stuck for some time.

## What happens to claimed tasks when I resume a session?

In-process teammates are not restored by resume or rewind, so their claims point to sessions that no longer exist. The lead may even try to message them. Audit open claims after resuming, inspect any partial output, and reassign or reset those tasks before spawning new teammates.

## Can I mark a task complete myself to keep the team moving?

Yes, but only after verifying the output exists and passes the task's check. Marking completion releases every dependent task, so a false completion spreads the problem. If the output is missing or incomplete, leave the task open and message its owner with what is needed.
