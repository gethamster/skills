# FAQ: Setting Metrics for GO Roadmap Goals

## How many metrics should each goal have?

As few as you need to tell whether the goal was met, usually one or two. Pichler advises limiting the number of KPIs and warns that measuring more than necessary wastes time. A compound goal with a user part and a business part often needs one measure for each.

## What is the difference between roadmap metrics and KPIs?

Roadmap metrics tell you whether a specific goal was met, usually within a timeframe. KPIs track the product's overall performance and health on an ongoing basis. In a reply to a reader, Pichler describes roadmap metrics as a subset of the KPIs and recommends discovering them from the roadmap goals rather than from a general list.

## What if I cannot measure a goal yet?

Make establishing the measurement part of the work, and state in the metrics row when the baseline will be known. Pichler accepts that later goals on a roadmap may not be measurable yet, as long as at least the first goal can be judged and the rest are refined at reviews. If a goal can never be measured, it is probably too vague.

## Why does the checklist ask when the result will be known?

Because many outcomes lag behind the release that should cause them. Pichler notes that for an acquisition goal you may have to wait days or weeks after release before the data shows whether it worked. Writing down the reading date prevents premature conclusions and makes sure the result is available at the review.

## Should metrics appear on an external roadmap?

Often not. Targets can be commercially sensitive, and customers care more about the benefit than the number. Many teams hide the metrics row on external views while keeping it on the internal roadmap that guides the team and stakeholders.
