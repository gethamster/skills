# Examples: Converging on Final Solutions in the Deliver Phase

## Choosing a way to remind tenants about repairs

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a housing association's Develop phase produced three concepts for helping tenants keep repair appointments: text reminders with a rebooking link, a named repair coordinator per estate, and a shared calendar in the tenant portal. The team has a few weeks before it must choose.

**Walkthrough:**

Criteria are agreed first: tenants must be able to use it without the portal, it must work for tenants who do not read English well, and it must fit within existing staffing.

Each concept's riskiest assumption is written down. For text reminders, it is that tenants will trust a link in a text message. For the coordinator, it is that staff time can be found. For the calendar, it is that tenants use the portal at all.

The first round is small. Five tenants react to mock-up text messages, a coordinator role is role-played with two housing officers, and portal usage figures are checked. The calendar fails its riskiest assumption straight away, because few tenants log in.

Two more rounds refine the text messages (plain wording, a phone number as well as a link) and test a lighter coordinator model. The team combines them: texts for most tenants, with a coordinator for tenants who have missed an appointment before.

The decision is recorded against the criteria, with the rejected calendar and the reason. A pilot on one estate is planned with two measures: missed appointments and rebooking calls.

## Converging when every concept fails

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a software team tested four onboarding concepts. In every one, new users stalled at the same point: importing their existing data.

**Walkthrough:**

Instead of picking the least-bad concept, the team stops and reviews the pattern. The shared failure suggests the problem statement, which focused on explaining features, missed the real obstacle.

The team loops back to Define with the test evidence. The revised problem statement centres on getting existing data in quickly.

A short Develop round produces two import-focused concepts, and Deliver resumes with those. The loop costs a couple of weeks and prevents shipping a polished tour that would not have fixed the drop-off.

## A structured vote without letting it decide

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a museum's visitor experience team has two tested concepts for a wayfinding redesign, and the group is split.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator runs a silent dot vote, with dots colour-coded by criterion: ease for first-time visitors, accessibility, and cost to maintain. The vote shows one concept ahead on ease and the other ahead on maintenance cost.

Rather than counting total dots, the group discusses the split against the criteria. The accessibility results from testing, where wheelchair users found one concept's signs hard to read, settle the question.

The director records the decision, the vote and the deciding evidence. The unchosen concept's lower-cost signage idea is kept as an improvement for a later phase.
