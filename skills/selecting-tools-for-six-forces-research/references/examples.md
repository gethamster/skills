# Examples: Six Forces Analysis Tools and Scoring Templates

## A lean toolkit for a small strategy team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of 3 at a mid-sized software company runs a six forces analysis each year and wants the results to be comparable from year to year.

**Walkthrough:**

The team lists questions and evidence types per force, then maps them to sources: company filings and full-text search for rivals, patent search for entrants, government business statistics for market size, interview notes for substitutes and complements. It builds one spreadsheet template with a section per force, a row per factor, and fields for rating, evidence, source link, date and confidence. Rating definitions sit at the top of each section.

The visual format is a single page with the six forces around the industry, each with a rating, an arrow for direction and the main driver. A test run on rivalry shows that the template has no place for interview quotes, so the team adds one.

## Deciding whether to buy a database

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a strategy lead is asked to approve a subscription to a market-share database.

**Walkthrough:**

She checks the question list. Market shares are needed for rivalry concentration and are not available publicly for private competitors. The database answers that one question well and nothing else. She approves it with a note in the toolkit stating which question it answers, so the next team knows why it exists and can drop it if the question changes.

## Standardizing ratings across analysts

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: two analysts rate supplier power for the same industry and reach different results.

**Walkthrough:**

Comparing their templates shows that one read "high" as "suppliers could raise prices" and the other as "suppliers have raised prices." The team writes explicit definitions for each level of each factor into the template. On a second pass, the analysts agree on most factors and record their remaining disagreement with the evidence behind each view.
