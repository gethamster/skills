# FAQ: Six Forces Analysis Tools and Scoring Templates

## Do I need special software for a six forces analysis?

No. A shared spreadsheet or document with a good template covers most needs. Specialist tools and paid databases help with specific questions, such as private-company market shares, but consistency matters more than the platform.

## What should a six forces scoring template contain?

A section per force, a row per factor, and fields for the rating, the evidence, the source, the date and a confidence grade. Include written definitions of each rating level, a summary row per force and a field for direction. Keep forces separate rather than averaging them.

## Which free sources are most useful?

Company filings through [EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search-and-access), patent records through the USPTO's [Patent Public Search](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/search/patent-public-search), and government statistics such as the [Census Business Builder](https://www.census.gov/data/data-tools/cbb.html). Trade associations and regulators often publish useful industry material too.

## How should search trend data be used?

As a signal of relative interest, for spotting a rising substitute or a shift in demand. Google Trends scales values from 0 to 100 relative to all searches in a place and time, as its [help page](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533) explains, so it should not be reported as search volume.

## How do I get a team to use the toolkit?

Keep it small, document it on one page, and test it on one force before rolling it out. Put the rating definitions and the citation rule in the template itself so analysts see them while they work.
