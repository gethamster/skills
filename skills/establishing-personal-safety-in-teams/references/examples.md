# Examples: Establishing Personal Safety for Honest Team Collaboration

## Surfacing a hidden integration slip

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A team of six keeps discovering integration problems on the day before each delivery, even though daily status always sounds fine.

**Walkthrough:**

The lead reviews the last three slips and finds that one developer knew about each problem several days earlier but did not mention it. In a one-to-one, the developer explains that a previous lead had publicly criticized people for reporting delays. The lead openly admits a recent estimation error of their own in the next team meeting and proposes an agreement that any reported problem starts with thanks and a question about what is needed. Two cycles later, the same developer flags a schema conflict four days before delivery, and the team resolves it without a last-minute scramble.

The lead notes this in the next reflection session as evidence the agreement is working.

## Getting the quiet voices into design reviews

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: Design reviews on a small product team always end with the tech lead's proposal accepted, while two junior developers later build workarounds.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator changes the review format so each person names their biggest concern before the lead speaks, starting with the most junior. In the first session, one junior developer points out that the proposed caching approach breaks an offline use case the lead had not considered. The team records the objection next to the decision and revises the design. The lead thanks the developer in front of the team and adopts the objections-first round as a standing part of design reviews.

Over the next month, workarounds stop appearing because concerns are now raised before code is written.

## Balancing openness and privacy in a shared room

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A co-located team sits together to benefit from overheard conversation, but a conflict between two members goes unaddressed for weeks.

**Walkthrough:**

The team lead notices tension but realizes there is no private room nearby, so any conversation would happen in front of everyone. The lead books a small meeting room for regular one-to-ones and tells the team it is available for any conversation that should not happen at the shared desks. One of the two members uses it to raise the conflict, and the lead facilitates a private conversation between them. The shared space stays open for everyday questions, while sensitive issues now have a place to go.

In the next reflection session, the team adds "private room available on request" to its working conventions.
