# FAQ: Establishing Personal Safety for Honest Team Collaboration

## What does personal safety mean in Crystal?

Personal safety is one of the seven properties Crystal Clear lists, and Cockburn describes it as the first step in trust. In practice it means people can report problems, admit mistakes and disagree without fearing that it will be held against them. It is a starting condition rather than the full extent of trust. Once people feel safe to speak, deeper reliance on each other's judgment can develop.

## How is personal safety different from trust?

Safety is the narrower, earlier condition: confidence that speaking up will not cause harm. Trust goes further, to relying on colleagues' competence, commitments and intentions. Crystal frames safety as the first step, which suggests you work on it before expecting broader trust to form. A team can be safe but still building trust, but it is hard to build trust on a team where people are afraid to be honest.

## Can a team have personal safety in an open-plan workspace?

Yes, but only if openness is balanced with privacy. Shared space helps information flow through overheard conversation, yet it can also make people feel exposed. Guidance drawn from Crystal Clear stresses providing private space and not letting open communication erode privacy or safety. A nearby room for private conversations and regular one-to-ones usually covers the gap.

## How do I know if my team lacks personal safety?

Look at behavior rather than asking directly. Common signals include problems surfacing only at the last minute, the same few people doing all the talking, disagreements appearing only after decisions are made, and reflection sessions that produce only trivial items. If several of these show up together, safety is likely low. Trace a recent slip back to when someone first knew about it to find the specific cause.

## Who is responsible for personal safety on the team?

Everyone contributes, but the person with the most authority has the largest effect. How a lead reacts to bad news, disagreement and mistakes sets the price of honesty for the whole team. Leads should model admitting errors and keep the agreed response to problems even under deadline pressure. Team members reinforce safety by critiquing ideas rather than people and by supporting colleagues who raise concerns.
