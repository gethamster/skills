# Examples: Designing Customer Retention Strategies for Loyalty

## Finding the first value moment

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small invoicing tool loses many customers in their first two months. Onboarding is a setup checklist that most customers complete.

**Walkthrough:**

The team compares customers who stayed with those who left. Both groups complete setup, but the ones who stayed sent their first invoice and received a payment through the tool within the first couple of weeks. That becomes the first value moment. Onboarding is rebuilt around it: the checklist ends with sending a real invoice, and a reminder goes out to customers who set up but have not sent one. The team tracks the share of new customers who receive a first payment and how long it takes.

## Removing effort from support

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a home internet provider sees customers leave after contacting support about the same billing confusion.

**Walkthrough:**

Grouping recent contacts by cause shows that many are about a prorated first bill that looks like an overcharge. The team rewrites the first bill to explain the proration in one line and adds a short explanation to the welcome email. Agents are given a way to see previous contacts so customers do not repeat themselves. A one-question effort survey after each contact tracks whether resolution feels easier.

## Early warning for a B2B account

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a project software vendor loses an account without warning at renewal. Looking back, the champion had left the customer months earlier.

**Walkthrough:**

The team adds two churn signals to its account alerts: a drop in weekly active users and a change of admin on the account. Each alert goes to the account manager, who reaches out to find the new decision-maker and offers a short session on what the team has achieved with the product. Renewal conversations now start earlier and include a summary of usage and outcomes.
