# FAQ: Designing Customer Retention Strategies for Loyalty

## Where should a retention strategy start?

With the first value moment. Customers who get what they came for early are more likely to stay, and onboarding is where most businesses can improve fastest. Measure how many new customers reach that moment and how long it takes. Work on support effort and churn signals next.

## Are loyalty programs worth it?

They can be, once the basics work. Rewards help retain customers who already get value and can nudge repeat behavior. They rarely save customers who struggle with the product or with support. Test a program against a comparable group without it before scaling.

## How do I tell voluntary from involuntary churn?

Voluntary churn is a customer choosing to leave. Involuntary churn comes from failed payments, expired cards and similar problems the customer did not intend. Tag cancellations by cause in your billing system. Handle involuntary churn with payment retries and reminders, and keep it out of the analysis of why customers choose to leave.

## What should I ask customers who cancel?

Ask why they are leaving and what they will use instead, and record the answers in consistent categories. Keep it short so people answer. Review the patterns regularly and share them with the owners of awareness and consideration, because some churn starts with the wrong customers or the wrong promise.

## How early should renewal conversations begin?

Early enough to show value and address concerns before the customer is deciding under time pressure. For annual B2B contracts that often means a few months ahead. Bring evidence of use and outcomes, and confirm who will make the decision this time.
