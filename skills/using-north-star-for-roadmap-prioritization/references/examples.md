# Examples: Using the North Star Framework for Roadmap Prioritization

## Two strong requests, one slot

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a scheduling app for clinics has room for one major item next cycle. Sales wants a calendar integration a large prospect asked for. Product wants to rework appointment reminders. The North Star is "appointments attended," and the inputs are clinics onboarded, appointments booked per clinic, and attendance rate.

**Walkthrough:**

The team rates attendance rate as the most influential input, because past data shows missed appointments are the main gap between bookings and the North Star. The reminder rework targets attendance, and a small test last quarter showed attendance rising for clinics that used text reminders, so its likelihood is rated high. The integration targets clinics onboarded, a lower-rated input this cycle, with evidence from one prospect.

The reminder rework ranks first. The team splits it into two bets, starting with the clinics that already use text reminders. Sales gets a clear explanation of the ranking and the evidence that would move the integration up, such as several more prospects asking for it.

## A large platform rebuild that cannot be split

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a data platform team proposes rebuilding its ingestion pipeline. It will take most of the year and cannot ship in parts.

**Walkthrough:**

The item scores well on influence through the system health input, since slow ingestion drags on every customer-facing input. Its likelihood is uncertain, and it cannot produce a signal until the end. The team flags it as a large batch.

Instead of approving it as one item, the group asks what smaller bets would reduce the uncertainty first. The platform team proposes a prototype that moves one data source to the new design. That bet ranks high on its own, and the full rebuild is re-scored after the prototype's review.

## Reviews that change the model

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a recipe app has shipped four bets aimed at its "saved recipes cooked" input over two cycles. Each was rated highly likely to work.

**Walkthrough:**

The reviews show one clear win, two inconclusive results and one loss. The win came from reducing steps in recipes; the others added social features. The team lowers likelihood ratings for social features on this input and raises them for simplification work.

The next ranking looks different. Two social feature ideas drop down the list, and a new bet on shorter shopping lists moves up. The team notes the change in reasoning on each item so stakeholders can see why the ranking shifted.
