# FAQ: Using the North Star Metric to Prioritize Your Product Roadmap

## How many input metrics should I score each initiative against?

Most North Star implementations have 3-5 input metrics, and you should score every initiative against all of them. Resist the temptation to only score against the 'obvious' input—surprising secondary effects often emerge. An initiative designed for retention might also meaningfully impact activation if it generates shareable content. Scoring all inputs takes minutes per initiative and frequently surfaces unexpected connections that change the ranking.

## What if my team doesn't have enough data to estimate impact with confidence?

That's exactly what the confidence score captures. Low-confidence, high-potential initiatives shouldn't be thrown out—they should be routed to validation first. Design a lightweight experiment, prototype test, or customer research sprint to increase your confidence before committing full engineering capacity. Many teams reserve 20-30% of their roadmap capacity specifically for these validation activities, which continuously feeds higher-quality scores into future prioritization cycles.

## How often should I re-score and re-rank the product manager roadmap?

Score formally once per planning cycle (typically quarterly) and do a lightweight review monthly. The monthly check isn't about re-scoring everything—it's about asking whether any assumptions have changed materially. Did a competitor launch something that shifts urgency? Did last month's shipped initiative move a metric more or less than expected? If assumptions hold, the ranking holds. If something material changed, re-score the affected initiatives only.

## Can I use this alongside RICE or other prioritization frameworks?

Yes, and many product managers do. The North Star scoring model is essentially a more structured version of the 'Impact' component in RICE. You can replace RICE's generic impact score with your weighted North Star impact score while keeping the Confidence and Effort components. This gives you the best of both worlds: RICE's simplicity and the North Star Framework's strategic alignment. The key addition is that your impact estimate is now decomposed into specific, measurable input metrics rather than a vague 1-3 rating.

## What do I do when the scoring model produces a result that feels wrong?

That tension is the most valuable moment in the process. Don't override the model silently—investigate the gap. Either the model is missing information (an input metric isn't weighted correctly, an impact score doesn't reflect new data) or your intuition is anchored on something non-metric-related like stakeholder politics or sunk cost. Ask: 'What would need to be true for the model to match my intuition?' If you can identify a legitimate factor, update the model. If you can't, trust the model over your gut—that's the whole point of having one.

## How do I handle initiatives that affect the North Star Metric but not through any defined input metric?

This usually signals a gap in your input metric map rather than a flaw in the initiative. If an initiative clearly impacts your North Star but you can't trace it through existing inputs, you've likely discovered a missing input metric. Go back to the mapping step (see Mapping Input Metrics That Drive Your North Star) and evaluate whether to add it. If the pathway is genuinely indirect or speculative, capture it as a 'secondary effect' note rather than inflating scores on loosely-related inputs.
