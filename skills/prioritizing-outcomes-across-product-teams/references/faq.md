# FAQ: Prioritizing Outcomes Across Product Teams

## Should we use a scoring formula for product outcome ranking?

A formula helps teams compare outcomes consistently and makes assumptions visible, which is why models such as RICE are popular. Use it to structure the discussion and record the judgment that follows. Rough estimates multiplied together can look more precise than they are.

## Who makes the final call?

Name one decision owner before the session, usually the head of product for the area. Aim for consent, where nobody has a meaningful objection, and let the owner decide if that fails. Roman Pichler describes the person in charge of the product as first among equals in outcome workshops, able to make the final call.

## How many outcomes should each team own?

Few. Pichler recommends one goal at a time for a product where possible, and treats several at once as the exception. Across teams, it is better to have some outcomes waiting below the line than to spread every team across every priority.

## How do we handle an outcome that needs several teams?

Give it one lead team that owns the metric and name the contributing teams and what each provides. Plan the dependency explicitly, with dates where one team's work unblocks another. Marty Cagan notes that much of the best work requires collaboration between teams, so shared outcomes are normal.

## How often should we re-prioritize?

At a fixed cadence tied to the strategy review, often quarterly, plus an extra round when a major assumption breaks. Re-ranking too often unsettles teams mid-work; too rarely lets the list drift from reality.
