# FAQ: OKR Cadence and Planning Cycles

## Should OKRs be annual or quarterly?

Most organizations use both. Google's re:Work guide says Google sets annual and quarterly OKRs. Annual objectives give direction, and quarterly key results give a short enough horizon to measure progress and correct course. What Matters notes that quarterly rather than annual goals were one of the ways OKRs departed from Management by Objectives.

## How long should an OKR cycle be?

A quarter is the usual default. It is long enough to move an outcome and short enough to adjust several times a year. Shorter cycles can suit very young companies whose priorities change often, at the cost of more planning time. Whatever you choose, keep it consistent long enough to learn from it.

## When should company OKRs be ready?

Before teams need to draft. In John Doerr's typical OKR cycle, company OKRs are finalized and communicated about two weeks before the quarter, after senior leaders start brainstorming several weeks earlier. That leaves teams time to draft, align and publish by the start of the quarter.

## Can we change OKRs in the middle of a cycle?

Practitioners disagree. Christina Wodtke advises against changing OKRs halfway through the quarter, while What Matters says it is reasonable to drop an OKR mid-cycle if it is not producing value. A common compromise is to allow changes only at a scheduled mid-cycle review, with a recorded reason, and to keep the original for the end-of-cycle reflection.

## How often should we check in on OKRs?

Weekly or biweekly at team level, plus a fuller mid-cycle review. What Matters recommends conversation-based check-ins at least weekly, and Google's re:Work guide recommends a mid-quarter check-in for all levels before grading. Choose a rhythm the team will actually keep.
