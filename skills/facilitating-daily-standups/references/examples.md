# Examples: Facilitating the Daily Standup Meeting

## A standup that had become a status report

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of seven holds a daily standup that regularly runs twice its timebox. Each person faces the team lead and lists everything they did yesterday. Blockers are rarely mentioned.

**Walkthrough:**

The team agrees in a retrospective to try walking the board for two weeks. The facilitator opens each standup with the sprint goal, then goes through the items from the right-hand column leftward, asking what each needs to move. The team lead stands at the back and stays quiet.

Within a few days the meeting fits its timebox. An item stuck in review for three days surfaces on the first morning and two developers pair on it that afternoon. The team keeps the new format.

## A distributed team across time zones

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of six is split between two regions with only a short overlap in working hours. The live standup uses most of that overlap.

**Walkthrough:**

The team moves routine updates to a written check-in each morning, posted against the board. It keeps a short live call in the overlap focused only on blockers and anything someone flagged in the written updates.

The overlap time is freed for pairing and questions. After a month, the team reviews the setup in a retrospective and agrees to keep a full live standup once a week so people still hear each other.

## Blockers that never went away

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a developer has mentioned the same blocker, waiting for access to a partner's test environment, at five standups in a row.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator notices the repetition and changes the close of the standup: every blocker is written on the board with an owner and a date. The Scrum Master takes the access request and escalates it to the partner manager that day.

Access arrives two days later. The team adds a column for open blockers to the board so repeated blockers are visible to everyone.
