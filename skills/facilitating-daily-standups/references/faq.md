# FAQ: Facilitating the Daily Standup Meeting

## How long should a daily standup be?

The Scrum Guide sets the Daily Scrum at fifteen minutes, and the Agile Alliance describes the same maximum, noting larger teams may need to adjust. If a standup regularly overruns, the usual causes are problem-solving during the meeting and too many people. Move discussions to afterward, and consider whether the team is too large.

## Do we have to use the three questions?

No. The Scrum Guide leaves the structure to the Developers. The three questions work for small teams with few items in progress. Walking the board often works better when many items are in progress, because it keeps attention on the work.

## Should the manager attend the standup?

They can, but the standup is for the team to coordinate. If a manager attends, agree that they listen and pick up blockers that need their help. If people start addressing updates to the manager, move the manager out of the circle or out of the meeting.

## Can we replace the standup with written updates?

Some teams do, especially across time zones. Written updates save time but lose the quick exchange where one person's update prompts another to help. A common compromise is written updates plus a short live call for blockers.

## What if nobody ever raises a blocker?

It usually means raising one does not feel safe or useful. Thank people when they raise blockers, act on them visibly, and ask directly about items that have not moved for several days.
