# Examples: How to Translate Features into Benefits

## A backup feature for small law firms

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a document management product lists "automatic versioned backups every hour" on its feature page. The page is aimed at office managers at small law firms.

**Walkthrough:**

The writer runs the so-what chain. Versioned backups mean any document can be restored to an earlier state. That means an overwritten brief or a deleted folder can be recovered by the office manager without calling IT. For a small firm, it means a mistake late at night does not turn into a missed filing.

Checking the customer quote file, the writer finds office managers talking about "the time someone saved over the final version." The benefit line becomes "Restore any file to how it looked an hour ago, without calling IT," with the hourly versioning named in the next sentence as the mechanism. The phrase "enterprise-grade resilience" is removed because no customer used anything like it.

## One feature, two audiences

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an expense tool has a feature that reads receipts from a phone photo. The company has one page for employees who submit expenses and one for finance teams who approve them.

**Walkthrough:**

For employees, the chain ends at "no more saving paper receipts in a wallet until month end," which matches how employees describe the chore. For finance teams, the same feature leads somewhere else: every expense arrives with a legible receipt attached, so approvers stop chasing missing paperwork.

The writer keeps one feature row in the table with two benefit columns, one per audience, and uses each on its own page. The finance page also names the formats the feature reads, because finance reviewers asked about it in sales calls.

## A developer tool where the feature leads

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a database hosting company is rewriting its product page for backend engineers. Early drafts opened with "Ship faster and sleep better."

**Walkthrough:**

The writer notes that the audience evaluates hosting by specific properties and treats generic benefit lines with suspicion. The feature table is kept, but the order on the page is reversed: each block leads with the precise capability, such as point-in-time recovery to any second within the retention window, followed by one sentence on what it saves the reader.

The headline states the product category and the main capability in plain terms. The benefit sentences stay, but they sit under the facts that support them, which matches what expert readers look for.
