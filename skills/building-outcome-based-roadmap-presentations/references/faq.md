# FAQ: Building Outcome-Based Roadmap Presentations

## How do I present a roadmap to executives who want dates?

Give them the dates that are real. Separate the small number of true commitments, which have dates, from the rest of the plan, which is shown by time horizon and confidence. Marty Cagan calls the first kind high-integrity commitments. Explaining which is which usually satisfies the need behind the date request.

## Should an external roadmap show outcomes?

Show the problems you are solving in customer terms, and avoid specific dates. Roman Pichler recommends broad timeframes such as "this year" on external roadmaps, because customers and sales teams tend to read target dates as promises. Internal metrics and targets normally stay internal.

## How long should a roadmap presentation be?

Long enough to cover objectives, outcomes, bets and commitments, and to leave most of the time for discussion of the outcome ranking. If the deck needs many slides of features, it is probably not yet an outcome roadmap. Send detailed material ahead as a pre-read.

## What if a key stakeholder disagrees in the meeting?

Acknowledge the disagreement, ask what evidence would change their view, and agree a follow-up. Pichler recommends making the level of agreement visible, for example with an agreement scale, and letting the person in charge decide when repeated discussion has not produced consent. Speaking with key stakeholders before the meeting prevents most public disagreements.

## How often should the roadmap be presented?

At the cadence of the outcome review, often quarterly for the full stakeholder group, with lighter updates in between. Pichler suggests reviewing the roadmap with the team and stakeholders at least once every quarter as a rule of thumb.
