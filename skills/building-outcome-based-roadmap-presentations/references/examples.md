# Examples: Building Outcome-Based Roadmap Presentations

## A quarterly executive review

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product director presents the next quarter's roadmap to the executive team for the first time in outcome format. The previous quarter's roadmap was a list of features with dates, and two of them slipped.

**Walkthrough:**

Before the meeting, the director walks the heads of sales and finance through the draft. Sales objects that a partner integration is missing; the director shows where it sits below the line and what evidence would move it up, and adds a note that discovery on it starts this quarter.

In the meeting, the deck opens with the company's two objectives for the year, then shows three outcomes with their baselines, targets and latest readings. Under each outcome are the current bets and the reserves. A separate slide lists the two dated commitments: a regulatory change and a contracted data export. The executives spend most of the time debating whether the ranking of outcomes is right, which is the conversation the director wanted. The meeting ends with a dot vote showing consent, and one open question assigned to finance.

## The same roadmap for engineers and for sales

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: after the executive review, the same product director needs to brief the engineering teams and then the sales team on the agreed roadmap.

**Walkthrough:**

For engineers, the view drops the revenue context to a single line and adds the leading indicator and decision thresholds for each outcome. It states which parts of each solution are open for the team to decide and which constraints are fixed. Engineers ask mostly about the indicators and how they will be measured.

For sales, the view uses the Now-Next-Later format with no specific dates beyond the two commitments. Each item is described as a customer problem the product is working on. The director explains what sales can say to customers ("we are working on reducing setup time for new teams") and what they should not promise. Both views come from the same roadmap file, so they cannot disagree.

## Handling a date demand in the room

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: during a roadmap presentation, a senior stakeholder asks when a specific reporting feature will ship, because a customer has been asking.

**Walkthrough:**

The product manager maps the request on the spot: the reporting feature is a candidate initiative under the outcome of reducing time spent on manual exports. It is not the current bet, because interviews showed a scheduled export would help more customers sooner.

The product manager offers to review the customer's situation after the meeting and, if the account is at risk, to raise it at the next outcome review. The stakeholder gets a clear answer and a path, and the group returns to the outcomes. Afterward, the product manager follows up in writing with the reasoning and the review date.
