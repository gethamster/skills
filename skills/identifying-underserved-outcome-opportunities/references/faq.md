# FAQ: Identifying Underserved Outcome Opportunities

## How are importance and satisfaction scores calculated?

Each is the share of respondents who answered 4 or 5 on a 1-to-5 scale, expressed on a scale of ten. The Product Compass masterclass with Ulwick gives the example of a 31% top-two-box becoming an importance score of 3.1 ([Product Compass](https://www.productcompass.pm/p/jobs-to-be-done-masterclass-with)). The opportunity score then adds importance to the gap between importance and satisfaction, when that gap is positive.

## What score counts as underserved?

The same masterclass describes scores above 10 as a solid opportunity, above 12 as high and above 15 as extreme, with 10 or below appropriately served or overserved ([Product Compass](https://www.productcompass.pm/p/jobs-to-be-done-masterclass-with)). Treat these as bands rather than cutoffs. Outcomes just either side of a line deserve the same scrutiny.

## Should I run the survey before or after interviews?

After. Interviews produce and test the outcome statements the survey asks about. A survey on untested statements measures how respondents read the team's wording.

## Are there criticisms of the opportunity score?

Yes. Braden Buchanan argues that the score largely follows importance, gives satisfaction no weight once it exceeds importance, and asks a lot of respondents who rate long lists twice ([Buchanan's critique](https://bradenbuchanan.substack.com/p/outcome-driven-innovation-a-critique)). Report importance and satisfaction next to the score and confirm top outcomes with other evidence.

## How often should I re-run the survey?

When something has changed that could move satisfaction: a major competitor launch, your own release aimed at a top outcome, or a shift in who your customers are. Re-running on a fixed schedule without such a change mostly reproduces the last result.
