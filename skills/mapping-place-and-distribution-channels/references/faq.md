# FAQ: Place in the Marketing Mix

## What does Place mean in the marketing mix?

Place covers where and how customers find, buy and receive the offer: direct and indirect channels, locations, coverage, logistics and fulfilment. For services, it is mainly about access, such as locations, opening hours, booking and response times.

## How do I choose distribution channels?

Start from how your target customers already find and buy similar offers. List the realistic channels at each stage, then score them on reach, cost to serve, control over the experience and fit with the price and positioning. Test uncertain channels on a small scale before committing.

## Should a service use direct or indirect channels?

Direct channels give control over the experience and the relationship, which matters when people and process are central to the service. Indirect channels such as agents, brokers and resellers give reach. Many services use both, with indirect partners trained and equipped to deliver the same standard.

## How does Place connect to the other Ps?

Coverage signals positioning, so it must match price and promotion. Every channel also has to deliver the people, process and physical evidence of the service. A channel that cannot do that weakens the whole mix, however many customers it brings.

## What is an omnichannel approach?

It means designing the channels so customers can move between them without friction, with the same prices, information and service standards everywhere. The practical work is in the handoffs, for example from a website enquiry to a sales call, or from a partner to your onboarding.
