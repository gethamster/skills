# Examples: Place in the Marketing Mix

## A dental practice treats booking as a channel

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a dental practice has a good location and strong reviews, but new patient numbers are flat. Bookings are taken by phone during office hours only.

**Walkthrough:**

Mapping the path shows that most prospective patients find the practice through search and reviews in the evening, when the phone is not answered. Many never call back. The team treats access as the Place problem it is.

It adds online booking for new patient appointments, a callback promise for messages left after hours, and one early morning slot a week. It checks the handoff: online bookings flow into the same system the front desk uses, so patients are not asked for the same details twice. The practice then tracks new bookings by channel.

## A software company adds a reseller without losing quality

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company sells accounting software directly to small businesses. A regional accounting firm offers to resell it to its clients.

**Walkthrough:**

The team scores the reseller on reach, cost, control and fit. Reach into the target segment is strong, and clients already trust their accountant. Control is the weak point, because the firm's staff would handle setup.

The company agrees to a selective arrangement with this one firm first. It trains the firm's staff, shares its onboarding checklist and setup guides, and keeps the same list price on both channels. It agrees how support requests will be routed and reviews satisfaction scores for reseller customers against direct customers after the first few months.

## A cafe chain chooses between delivery apps

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small cafe chain wants to add delivery. Several delivery apps operate in its city.

**Walkthrough:**

The team maps what customers value: coffee that arrives hot and the chain's own packaging. It scores each app on reach in the neighbourhoods it serves, commission cost, delivery times and how the order appears to the customer.

It chooses one app with shorter delivery radii that fit its locations, rather than listing on all of them. It sets the same menu and a small delivery price difference that customers can see, and redesigns packaging so drinks travel well. The team reviews complaints about delivered orders separately from in-store feedback.
