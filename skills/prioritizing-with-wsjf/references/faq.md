# FAQ: Prioritizing Work Using WSJF

## What is the formula for weighted shortest job first?

WSJF equals cost of delay divided by job size. Cost of delay is built by adding relative scores for value to users and the business, time sensitivity, and risk reduction or opportunity enablement. Job size is a relative estimate of how long the item will occupy capacity. The item with the highest ratio is scheduled first.

## Why use relative estimates instead of real money and days?

Most teams cannot estimate revenue impact or duration accurately for items that have not been analyzed yet. Relative estimates only require judging whether one item is bigger or more urgent than another, which groups do far more reliably. The ratio still produces a sensible sequence because every item is scored against the same anchors. If you do have trustworthy financial data, use it to inform the value column.

## Should WSJF be used for user stories?

It is designed for items that compete for significant capacity, such as features at the ART level and epics at the portfolio level. Stories are usually sequenced by the team within an iteration, where the overhead of formal scoring outweighs the benefit. Teams can still borrow the thinking informally, asking which small, urgent item unblocks the most. Keep formal scoring for the levels where trade-offs are expensive.

## How often should we re-score the backlog?

Re-score before each planning cycle, and whenever a major assumption changes, such as a moved deadline or a lost customer. Start with the time-sensitivity column, since it changes fastest, then check whether any new items need scoring. A full re-score of every column is rarely needed if the rationale sheet is kept current.

## What if WSJF produces a ranking stakeholders reject?

Treat the rejection as a signal that one or more scores encode a wrong assumption. Walk through the rationale sheet with the objecting stakeholders and find the specific score they disagree with. Correct it with a documented reason and recalculate, rather than reordering the list by hand. If the disagreement is about strategy itself, that belongs in portfolio decision-making, not in the scoring session.
