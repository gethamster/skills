# Examples: Backlog Grooming and Product Backlog Refinement

## Splitting a large feature into Sprint-sized slices

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the top item in a team's backlog reads "Customers can manage their subscriptions." In refinement, the Developers say it would take several Sprints and involves billing, email and the account page.

**Walkthrough:**

The Product Owner explains the outcome: most support tickets are customers asking to pause or cancel. The team splits the item by user value. The first slice lets a customer cancel from the account page with a confirmation email. The second lets them pause for a month. Plan changes and refunds become later items lower in the backlog.

Each slice gets acceptance criteria written together, including what happens to an active trial. The Developers size the first slice as fitting comfortably in a Sprint and the second as larger but still possible. The Product Owner keeps both at the top, because together they address the most common support requests.

## Refinement that changes the order

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the Product Owner has ranked a reporting export above a search improvement. In refinement, the Developers point out that the export depends on a data service another team is rebuilding.

**Walkthrough:**

The team records the dependency and asks the other team for a date. Meanwhile, the search improvement turns out to be smaller than expected, because an existing library covers most of it. The Product Owner moves search above the export for the next Sprint and adds a note to revisit the export once the data service is ready.

The session ends with fewer items refined than planned, but with a clearer order. The Product Owner explains the change to stakeholders at the next Sprint Review.

## Introducing a light readiness check

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: Sprint Planning keeps running long because items arrive without acceptance criteria, and the Developers spend the meeting working out what they mean.

**Walkthrough:**

At a retrospective the team agrees a three-question readiness check: does the item have acceptance criteria, has at least one Developer looked at it, and do we think it fits in a Sprint? The Scrum Master adds a short weekly refinement session, and the Product Owner brings items to it a day early.

After a few Sprints, planning is shorter and most selected items are finished. The team deliberately keeps the check at three questions. When someone proposes adding more, the team asks whether a missing item has actually caused a problem before adding it.
