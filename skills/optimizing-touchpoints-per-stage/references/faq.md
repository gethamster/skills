# FAQ: Touchpoint Optimization for Each Journey Stage

## What counts as a touchpoint?

Any point where a buyer meets information about your brand or category: advertising, websites, reviews, comparison sites, friends, stores, salespeople, forms and emails. Include touchpoints you do not control, because in a long purchase they often carry more weight than your own.

## How do I measure a touchpoint's influence?

Combine what buyers say with what they do. Ask buyers which sources mattered at each stage, and use analytics, attribution or modeling to see which touchpoints precede a brand staying in consideration. Where the two disagree, treat it as a question to investigate rather than picking one.

## Why not just use attribution data?

Attribution sees only tracked clicks, and last-click models give all credit to the final channel. In a long planned journey, that undervalues the latent and early evaluation touchpoints that put the brand in the set. Use attribution as one input, next to buyer research.

## How do I reduce customer journey friction?

Look for points where buyers who would otherwise continue slow down or stop: missing information, slow responses, confusing forms, surprise costs. Buyer interviews and form or funnel analytics reveal them. Fix the ones at the transition where your brand loses the most buyers first.

## How many touchpoints should I track?

Enough to cover what buyers actually use at each stage, and few enough to score properly. SKIM suggests a list of around fifteen as a good starting point for a decision journey map, and it can grow as buyer accounts reveal new sources.

## How long before I see results?

It depends on the length of the purchase journey. A fix at the buying stage can show results quickly. A fix at the latent stage may take months to show up in consideration, which is why a change log and repeated measurement matter.
