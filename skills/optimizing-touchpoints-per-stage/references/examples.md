# Examples: Optimizing Touchpoints at Each Journey Stage

## Example: Mid-Size Auto Dealership Group Optimizing the Evaluation Stage

**Scenario:**

A regional auto dealership group with 8 locations notices that website visitors spend significant time on the inventory search and vehicle detail pages but rarely submit a lead form or schedule a test drive. The evaluation stage has been defined as the period from active vehicle research through test drive scheduling. The group has Google Analytics data, a CRM with lead records, and informal feedback from sales managers. Budget for optimization is modest, about $5,000 per quarter for content and UX improvements.

**Walkthrough:**

The team inventoried 18 touchpoints in the evaluation stage, including organic search listings, inventory search filters, vehicle detail pages, third-party listings on Autotrader and CarGurus, Google reviews, the 'Schedule Test Drive' form, and sales team email responses. They gathered friction evidence from analytics (72% bounce rate on vehicle detail pages viewed on mobile, 89% abandonment on the test drive scheduling form) and from sales manager interviews ('customers keep saying they could not find the car they saw online when they arrive'). Scoring revealed three critical touchpoints: the mobile vehicle detail page (Friction 5, Influence 4, Frequency 5 = index 100), the test drive scheduling form (Friction 4, Influence 5, Frequency 3 = index 60), and the inventory sync between website and lot (Friction 4, Influence 4, Frequency 4 = index 64). Root cause analysis showed the mobile page was slow to load and lacked key comparison information, the scheduling form required 11 fields including information customers considered invasive at that stage, and the inventory feed updated only nightly so cars sold during the day remained visible online.

The team reduced the scheduling form to 4 fields, added a mobile-optimized comparison view to vehicle pages, and switched to a real-time inventory feed. 2x, and the 'car not on lot' complaint disappeared from sales manager feedback.

## Example: B2B SaaS Company Fixing Latent-Stage Friction

**Scenario:**

A B2B SaaS company selling supply chain management software has mapped its latent stage as the period when logistics managers are experiencing supply chain problems but have not yet started evaluating solutions. The company publishes thought leadership content (blog posts, white papers, webinars) targeting this stage. Content gets traffic but almost nobody transitions from content consumption to evaluation activities like requesting a demo or visiting the product pages. The team has 14 months of content analytics, marketing automation data, and quarterly customer surveys with a sample of 200+ respondents.

**Walkthrough:**

The inventory identified 12 latent-stage touchpoints including LinkedIn ads, blog posts, industry conference presentations, a monthly newsletter, white papers behind a lead capture form, and a Gartner analyst report mentioning the company. Friction evidence from the survey showed that 67% of respondents who read the blog described the content as 'interesting but too general to act on,' and that the lead capture form for white papers was the most-cited reason for not downloading. Scoring flagged three priorities: the white paper gate (Friction 5, Influence 3, Frequency 4 = 60), the blog-to-product-page transition (Friction 3, Influence 5, Frequency 5 = 75), and the newsletter-to-blog click path (Friction 4, Influence 3, Frequency 4 = 48). The team ungated the white papers and added a contextual 'See how this applies to your operation' CTA within each blog post that linked to a relevant product use case page rather than a generic demo request.

They also redesigned the newsletter to lead with a specific supply chain problem and link directly to the most relevant blog post rather than listing five links with equal prominence. 4%, white paper downloads increased 4x (though email captures dropped by 60%, which was accepted as a tradeoff), and demo requests attributed to content increased by 40%.

## Example: Financial Services Firm Smoothing the Buying Stage

**Scenario:**

A national mortgage lender finds that customers who complete the online pre-approval process and receive a conditional offer have a 35% drop-off rate before closing. The buying stage spans from conditional offer acceptance through document submission, underwriting, and closing. The company has detailed funnel data from its loan origination system, recorded calls with loan officers, and a post-closing survey with NPS and open-ended questions. The customer experience team has three people and a mandate to improve close rates by 5 percentage points within two quarters.

**Walkthrough:**

The buying-stage inventory contained 22 touchpoints including the conditional offer email, the document upload portal, automated status update emails, loan officer phone calls, third-party appraisal scheduling, title company coordination, and the closing document signing. 3 incomplete upload attempts before abandoning. Call recordings revealed that loan officers spent 30% of call time re-explaining what documents were needed because the automated email was unclear. Post-closing surveys cited 'not knowing where I stood in the process' as the top frustration.

The three priority touchpoints were: the document upload portal (Friction 5, Influence 5, Frequency 5 = 125), the automated status update emails (Friction 4, Influence 4, Frequency 5 = 80), and the initial document request email (Friction 4, Influence 4, Frequency 5 = 80). Root cause analysis revealed the upload portal rejected files over 5MB with a generic error message, the status emails used internal jargon ('file in UW queue'), and the initial document request listed 14 possible documents without indicating which ones applied to the customer's specific loan type. The team increased the upload limit to 25MB with clear error messaging, rewrote status emails in plain language with a visual progress bar, and created conditional document checklists that showed only the 4-6 documents relevant to each customer's loan type. Within one quarter, document upload completion rate increased from 59% to 84%, loan officer call duration decreased by 22%, and close rates improved by 7 percentage points, exceeding the target.

## Example: D2C Consumer Electronics Brand Addressing Third-Party Touchpoints

**Scenario:**

A direct-to-consumer electronics brand selling premium headphones sees strong brand awareness and consideration in the latent and early evaluation stages, but loses a disproportionate share of customers to competitors in the late evaluation stage. Internal analytics look healthy: the product pages perform well, the comparison page is thorough, and the checkout flow has a low abandonment rate. A small marketing team of four people suspects the problem is happening off-site but has limited visibility into third-party touchpoints.

**Walkthrough:**

The team expanded their evaluation-stage touchpoint inventory beyond owned channels, adding YouTube review videos, Reddit threads in r/headphones, Amazon reviews, and three prominent tech blog comparison articles. They searched for their brand name alongside competitor names and read the top 20 results across Google, YouTube, and Reddit. Friction evidence was striking: two of the three tech blog comparisons contained outdated pricing (listing the brand at launch price, which was $50 higher than the current price), the most-viewed YouTube comparison video noted that the brand's website did not publish detailed frequency response data while competitors did, and Reddit threads consistently mentioned difficulty finding warranty information. Scoring these third-party touchpoints revealed that the outdated tech blog comparisons had the highest priority index (Friction 4, Influence 5, Frequency 4 = 80) because they reached thousands of prospective customers monthly with inaccurate information.

The team contacted the blog authors with updated pricing and offered product samples for re-review, added a detailed specifications page with frequency response charts and downloadable data, created a dedicated warranty page with plain-language coverage details and linked it prominently from every product page, and published their own comparison content targeting the same keywords as the inaccurate third-party articles. Within three months, two of three blog articles were updated, the brand's own comparison page ranked on page one for key comparison queries, and the ratio of customers who visited a competitor's product page after viewing the brand's page (tracked via retargeting pixel data) decreased by 18%.
