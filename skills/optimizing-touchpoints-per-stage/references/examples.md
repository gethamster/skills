# Examples: Touchpoint Optimization for Each Journey Stage

## A car brand's test-drive booking

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: consideration tracking shows a car brand losing many buyers between evaluation and buying. The team inventories touchpoints around that transition: the model configurator, the test-drive booking form, dealer follow-up calls and third-party reviews.

**Walkthrough:**

Reach is high for the configurator and the booking form. Buyer interviews reveal that the form asks for a preferred dealer and date but then waits for a dealer to call back, often days later. Several lost buyers booked a rival's test drive in the meantime.

The team ranks the booking form first: high reach, high influence at the losing transition, poor performance. The fix is instant online confirmation of a slot. The retail team owns it, and the measure is the share of evaluation-stage buyers who keep the brand into buying.

## A pension provider's comparison content

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a pension provider is often in buyers' latent consideration but is dropped early in evaluation. The team scores its evaluation touchpoints against the decision aids buyers use when comparing.

**Walkthrough:**

The product pages list features in detail but give no simple summary of fees, no independent ratings and no customer reviews. Buyers comparing providers describe the pages as hard to compare. Competitors' pages show a short summary and an independent rating near the top.

The team adds a plain summary of fees and features, links to independent ratings and adds reviews. It tests the new page against the old one for buyers arriving from comparison sites and re-measures evaluation-stage consideration in the next wave.

## A latent-stage touchpoint judged on the wrong measure

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a furniture brand is about to cut an outdoor advertising campaign because it drives few website visits. The journey team asks to score it against the latent stage's job first.

**Walkthrough:**

The team adds a question to its consideration study asking which brands come to mind when people think about replacing a sofa, and whether they recall the brand's advertising. Buyers who recall the campaign are more likely to include the brand in their first consideration set than those who do not.

The team reports that the campaign is doing a latent-stage job and should be judged on consideration. Website visits measure an evaluation-stage job. The campaign continues, and the team adds a note to its measurement plan so latent touchpoints are not judged on click metrics again.
