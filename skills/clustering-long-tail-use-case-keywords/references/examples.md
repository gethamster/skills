# Examples: Clustering Long-Tail Use-Case Keywords into Content Silos

## Invoicing software splitting one bloated page

**Scenario:**

A small invoicing app has one long page targeting every invoice-related query and it ranks for almost none of them.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the team exports 300 invoice-related queries and tags intent first, finding three groups: learn, compare and do. Within the do group, use-case tagging separates freelancers sending a first invoice, agencies setting up recurring billing and anyone chasing a late payment. Reading each group by hand, they notice the tool had merged late-payment reminders with payment terms explanations, which are different intents, so they split them. The result is 12 clusters, each mapped to one URL, grouped into three silos: getting paid, recurring billing and invoice basics.

The original long page becomes the hub for invoice basics, and the other clusters get focused pages linked from it.

## Garden supplier discovering use cases in forums

**Scenario:**

An online garden supplier's keyword exports only show generic product terms with heavy competition.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the team reads 50 threads in two gardening communities and copies the thread titles into the query sheet. Titles such as growing tomatoes on a north-facing balcony describe a situation and a goal that no export had surfaced. Tagging these gives a new silo, small-space growing, with 8 clusters split by intent into how-to guides and product comparisons. They validate each cluster against their product range and drop two that concern plants they do not sell.

The remaining clusters go into the brief list with the forum phrasing kept as the working titles.

## Consolidating overlapping pages in an HR software blog

**Scenario:**

An HR software blog has published separate posts for many phrasings of the same onboarding question over several years.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: clustering the blog's own ranking queries shows 6 existing posts all mapped to one use case, a manager onboarding a remote hire. Because every query in the cluster shares the same do intent, the team assigns one target URL, the post with the most impressions. The other five are merged into it and redirected, with their unique sections kept as subsections. A separate compare cluster about onboarding checklists versus software stays its own page, since it serves a different intent.

The silo now reads as one strong page per job instead of a pile of overlapping posts.
