# FAQ: Aligning Stakeholders with a GO Product Roadmap

## Who should take part in a roadmap workshop?

The key stakeholders, meaning the people who are both interested in the product and have power over its success, plus development team representatives. Pichler recommends identifying them with a power-interest grid. Other stakeholders are kept informed through reviews or one-to-one conversations instead of joining the workshop.

## Do all stakeholders need to agree?

For roadmap decisions, Pichler suggests consent: nobody has a meaningful objection. Unanimity, where everyone actively endorses the plan, he reserves for new or significantly changed strategies. Consent lets the group move forward even when some people have reservations.

## What if agreement cannot be reached?

The person in charge of the product makes the final decision and explains it. Pichler describes this role as first among equals: empowered to decide when no agreement can be reached. Collaborative goal-setting does not mean everyone gets their way.

## Why use a separate facilitator?

A facilitator makes sure everybody is heard and nobody dominates, which frees the product person to focus on the content of the roadmap. Pichler suggests a team coach or Scrum Master. It also reduces the chance that the most senior person's opinion wins by default.

## How do I say no to a stakeholder's feature request?

Listen first to understand the need behind the request, then reframe it around the user problem and the current roadmap goal. If the request does not help meet the goal, decline it and explain why. Pichler advises not rushing the decision and bringing bigger requests to the next roadmap workshop.
