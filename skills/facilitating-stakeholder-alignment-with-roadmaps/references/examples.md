# Examples: Aligning Stakeholders with a GO Product Roadmap

## Replacing a sign-off meeting with a workshop

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product manager for a field-service app has presented a finished roadmap to sales, support, and operations leads each quarter. Each time, the leads accept it in the meeting and then lobby for changes over the following weeks.

**Walkthrough:**

This quarter she runs a half-day workshop instead, with a team coach facilitating. The strategy and KPIs go out a week ahead. In the room, each lead writes the outcomes they think matter most. The clusters reveal that sales and support both care about technicians finishing jobs on the first visit, for different reasons, which becomes a shared goal.

The group orders three goals, checks feasibility with two engineers present, and dot-votes on an agreement scale. Operations marks one goal with a reservation, which is discussed and resolved by moving it a quarter later. Nobody lobbies afterwards, because the leads helped make the decisions.

## Handling a feature promised to a customer

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: two weeks after a roadmap workshop, the product lead of a data-export tool learns that a sales representative who took part has promised a large prospect a custom connector that is not on the roadmap.

**Walkthrough:**

The product lead does not add the connector quietly. She meets the representative, starts by asking about the deal and the pressure behind the promise, and then describes what happened and why it matters to the rest of the group. Together they look at whether the prospect's need fits the current goal of reducing setup time for new customers.

It partly does: a generic import improvement already on the roadmap covers most of the need. The representative goes back to the prospect with that, and the two agree that future requests of this kind go to the next roadmap workshop. The roadmap stays intact and the working relationship survives.

## When the group cannot agree

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: in a roadmap workshop for a hotel booking platform, marketing wants the next goal to be acquiring new hotels, while engineering wants to reduce technical debt that is slowing every release.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator asks each side to describe the cost of delaying the other's goal. Engineering shows that the debt has made the last three releases late. Marketing shows a partner event where new hotels could be signed. The agreement scale shows serious disagreement on both orderings.

After a second round of discussion without movement, the product manager uses her final say. She sets a six-week debt goal first, followed by the acquisition goal timed to the partner event, and explains the reasoning. Marketing still has reservations but no objection that blocks the plan, which meets the consent standard the group agreed at the start.
