# FAQ: Executing Actions with Implicit Guidance and Control

## What does implicit guidance and control mean in the Act phase?

It means a well-developed orientation can trigger action directly, without a separate deliberate Decide step. For familiar situations, your understanding recognises what is happening and a prepared response follows. The explicit Decide route is still there for novel or high-stakes cases. The skill lies in building the understanding and repertoire that make the implicit route safe.

## Is skipping the Decide step reckless?

It is reckless only when orientation is weak or the situation is new. The safeguard is a rule that limits the implicit path to recognised, reversible situations inside your stated intent. Everything else goes through explicit deciding. Regular reviews of implicit actions catch cases where a play fired when it should not have.

## Why treat an action as a test rather than a decision carried out?

Because the environment's response is the most reliable evidence of whether your orientation was right. Treating the action as a test forces you to state an expected result and look for it. That makes surprises visible early, when they are still cheap to act on. It also keeps you from defending a choice just because you made it.

## How is this different from simply acting fast?

Fast action without prepared understanding is guessing, and fast action without feedback teaches you nothing. This skill pairs speed with preparation, variation and deliberate reading of results. The aim is to shape the situation and learn from it each cycle. Speed is a consequence of preparation, not a goal on its own.

## Does irregularity matter if we have no direct opponent?

Mostly not. Variation is useful when someone can observe and anticipate your moves, such as competitors, counterparts in a negotiation or adversaries in security work. For internal operations with no one watching, consistent and well-rehearsed plays usually serve better. Decide based on whether anyone gains from reading your pattern.
