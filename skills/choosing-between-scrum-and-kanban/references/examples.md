# Examples: Choosing Between Scrum, Kanban, and Scrumban

## A platform team drowning in interrupts

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a platform team of six runs two-week Sprints. Its history shows that of the last 40 items it finished, 26 were not in the Sprint plan when the Sprint started. Most came from other teams asking for help with infrastructure.

**Walkthrough:**

The arrival data settles the question: Sprint plans are being replaced by unplanned work every Sprint. The team moves to Kanban, maps its workflow as ready, in progress, review and done, and sets a WIP limit on in progress. It keeps a fortnightly retrospective and a short weekly review with the teams it serves.

After the trial, cycle times are easier to predict and requesting teams see where their items sit on the board. The team keeps Kanban and schedules another check in a quarter.

## A new product team that needs a rhythm

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a newly formed team of five is building the first version of a product. Most work is planned feature development, sizes are fairly consistent once split, and the sponsor wants to see progress every two weeks.

**Walkthrough:**

The history is short, but what exists is almost all planned work. The sponsor's need for regular reviews and the team's inexperience with agile both favor structure. The team picks Scrum with two-week Sprints and runs all four events.

During the trial the team struggles to finish what it plans in the first Sprint and plans less in the second. The retrospective focuses on splitting work smaller. Scrum stays.

## A Scrum team with a support rotation

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team does planned feature work but also handles customer escalations. About one item in four is an escalation, and escalations have broken the Sprint Goal in each of the last three Sprints.

**Walkthrough:**

The signals are mixed, so the team chooses Scrumban. It keeps Sprint Planning, the Sprint Review and the retrospective, and adds a separate expedite lane on its board with a WIP limit of one escalation at a time. Capacity for escalations is set aside during planning.

The next two Sprint Goals are met. The team records the setup and agrees to revisit it if escalations grow.
