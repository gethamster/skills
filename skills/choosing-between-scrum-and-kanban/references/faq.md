# FAQ: Choosing Between Scrum, Kanban, and Scrumban

## Is Kanban agile?

Yes. Kanban puts several agile principles into practice, especially continuous delivery and reflecting on the process, through a visualized workflow and explicit limits on work in progress. It differs from Scrum in having no prescribed roles or Sprints. The underlying values are the same.

## Can a team switch from Scrum to Kanban partway through a project?

Yes, and it is common when the work changes character, for example after a product launches and maintenance work grows. Run the switch as a trial with baseline measures so the team can tell whether it helped. Tell stakeholders how planning and reviews will change.

## Do Kanban teams need a Scrum Master or Product Owner?

The Kanban Guide does not prescribe roles. Most Kanban teams still need someone who orders the queue and someone who looks after the flow and the team's improvement, but those can be existing roles.

## What is the difference between Scrumban and just doing both?

Scrumban is a deliberate combination: specific Scrum events kept for cadence, plus Kanban's WIP limits and flow measures. Doing both without choosing tends to keep the meetings of Scrum and drop the discipline of either. Write down which parts you use.

## How long should the trial be?

Long enough to see a pattern: several Sprints for Scrum, or enough finished items for cycle times to settle in Kanban. For most teams that means a few weeks to a month. Decide the length and the success criteria before starting.
