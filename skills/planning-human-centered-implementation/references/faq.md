# FAQ: Planning Human-Centered Implementation

## What is the difference between desirability, feasibility and viability?

Desirability asks whether people want, trust and find the solution useful. Feasibility asks whether the organization can build and deliver it with its capabilities, technology and partners. Viability asks whether it can be sustained economically and operationally over time. A concept needs to pass all three, because strength on one does not compensate for failure on another.

## When should stakeholders get involved in implementation planning?

As early as possible, ideally while the concept is still being refined. Sponsors, operators, frontline leaders, technology and compliance each hold evidence the plan needs. Involving them late usually means objections surface at sign-off, when changes are most expensive. Sharing prototypes early also lets disagreements be settled through testing rather than opinion.

## How big should a pilot be?

Small enough to reverse cheaply and large enough to expose real operating conditions such as peak load, staff turnover and edge cases. For example, a few sites for one quarter often works for service changes, but the right size depends on how quickly your metrics move. Define the pilot scope alongside the success metrics so you know it can produce a clear answer.

## What should we do if a concept fails the feasibility or viability gate?

Send it back for revision rather than forcing it through. Treat the failed gate as a new design constraint and generate options that respect it, then retest with users. Sometimes the right answer is to acquire a missing capability or to drop the concept, and recording why is useful for the next project.

## Is user feedback enough to justify scaling a solution?

No. User feedback shows desirability, but scaling also depends on operational data, frontline input, resource and readiness assessments, and measured impact from a pilot. You also need to check for unintended consequences on people outside the target group. Scaling on user enthusiasm alone is one of the most common implementation failures.
