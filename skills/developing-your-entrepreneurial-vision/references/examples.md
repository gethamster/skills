# Examples: Developing Your Entrepreneurial Vision

## A web developer decides what the business is for

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a freelance web developer has grown to three contractors and takes any project that comes in. She feels busy and directionless, and her team does not know which work to prioritize.

**Walkthrough:**

Her primary aim surprises her: she wants to stop coding client projects within a few years and spend more time with her family. Her strategic objective sets a profit target that supports that life and names the product in Gerber's sense: clients feel their website finally works for their business, rather than just receiving a site.

The canvas narrows the customers to local service firms, where her team already does its best work, and sets a goal of running a maintenance-and-growth service for them. Her decision criteria lead her to turn down the next one-off e-commerce build, which would have filled the calendar without moving toward the goal.

## A bakery owner tests a bold idea

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the owner of a single busy bakery wants to open more locations. His draft canvas describes a regional chain.

**Walkthrough:**

He shares the draft with his accountant, who asks what would have to be true. The numbers show the current shop depends on the owner's own early-morning baking, which cannot be copied. Instead of dropping the goal, he adds a prerequisite to the canvas: a documented baking process that a head baker can run.

The strategic objective now includes a standard for consistency across batches. The vision still points toward more locations, but the first year's work is systems in the existing shop, which he hands to the Manager-role skill.

## A consultancy writes decision criteria

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a four-person strategy consultancy has a vision document that nobody reads. Partners argue about every new opportunity.

**Walkthrough:**

The partners rewrite the vision as a one-page canvas and derive three decision questions from it: does the client fit the sectors we chose, does the work build a repeatable offer, and can someone other than a partner deliver most of it. They test the questions on the last few deals and find two they would have declined.

They put the questions at the top of their pipeline review. The arguments do not disappear, but they now happen about specific criteria, and the vision gets reviewed each quarter because the partners use it every week.
