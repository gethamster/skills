# FAQ: Developing Your Entrepreneurial Vision

## What is the Entrepreneur role in the E-Myth?

It is the part of the owner that looks to the future, imagines what the business could become and pushes for change. In Gerber's model it sits alongside the Technician, who does the work, and the Manager, who builds order. Most owners underuse it because it has no deadlines and no immediate output.

## What is the difference between the primary aim and the strategic objective?

The primary aim is personal: what you value, the life you want and who you want to become. The strategic objective is for the business: the standards it must meet, starting with money, so that it serves the primary aim. Write the aim first, because the objective should follow from it.

## How far ahead should the vision look?

Choose a horizon that fits your business and say it explicitly. Collins and Porras describe big goals that take many years, while a small service business may be better served by a few years. The part of the vision that describes what the business stands for should hold much longer than any goal.

## What if I do not feel like a visionary?

Treat vision work as a task with a schedule and a deliverable. The steps in this skill, from the primary aim to decision criteria, produce a usable vision without needing a sudden insight. Talking to your best customers is often the most productive starting point.

## How do I get the team to use the vision?

Turn it into a few decision questions and use them in real decisions where people can see it. Refer to it when you say yes or no to opportunities. A vision the owner visibly uses gets adopted; one that sits in a folder does not.
