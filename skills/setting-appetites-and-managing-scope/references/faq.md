# FAQ: Setting Appetites and Managing Scope in Shape Up

## What is an appetite in Shape Up?

The appetite is the amount of time the team wants to spend on a project, decided before the solution is designed. The book contrasts it with an estimate: estimates start with a design and end with a number, while appetites start with a number and end with a design. It works as a creative constraint on the shaping.

## What appetite sizes does the book use?

Two. A small batch is a project a designer and one or two programmers can build in one or two weeks, and several of these are grouped into one cycle. A big batch takes the same team the whole six-week cycle. Anything larger is narrowed or split into a meaningful piece that fits a cycle.

## What does fixed time, variable scope mean?

The time box does not move, and the scope of the solution adjusts to fit it. The book applies the principle at every stage: the appetite constrains the design during shaping, and the fixed deadline pushes the team to decide what is core during the build.

## What is scope hammering?

Forcefully questioning a design, implementation or use case to cut scope and finish inside the time box. The book uses the word hammering because it takes repeated force to make scope fit. Teams ask whether each item is a must-have, whether they could ship without it, and who would actually be affected.

## Does cutting scope mean lower quality?

No. The book says variable scope is not about sacrificing quality, and Basecamp was picky about code, design, copy and performance. The cuts are to features, edge cases and polish on peripheral parts, so the core that ships is done well.

## What if the appetite turns out to be wrong?

If the project cannot ship in time, the circuit breaker applies: no extension by default, and the problem goes back to shaping. The shaper can then pitch a different approach, a smaller version, or a larger appetite with better evidence. A missed appetite is information about the shaping, and it should feed the next pitch.
