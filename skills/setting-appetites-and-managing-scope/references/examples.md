# Examples: Setting Appetites and Managing Scope in Shape Up

## A big request with a small appetite

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: customers of a project tool ask for Gantt charts. A full Gantt feature with dependencies, drag-to-reschedule and critical path would take a team months.

**Walkthrough:**

Before sizing anything, the product lead calls three customers who asked and asks when they wanted a Gantt chart. Each describes the same moment: a client asks whether the project will finish on time, and they need a picture of the milestones to answer. None of them mention dependencies.

The lead sets a two-week appetite for that narrow problem: a read-only timeline of milestones that can be shared with a client. Dependencies, dragging and critical path are listed as no-gos. The small batch team ships the timeline inside its two weeks, and the requests slow down. If dependencies still matter later, the idea will come back with its own evidence.

## Hammering scope in the last two weeks

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team is in the fifth week of a six-week cycle building team calendars. Four scopes are done; the Recurring Events scope has six open tasks.

**Walkthrough:**

The team runs each open task through the hammering questions. Weekly and monthly repeats are must-haves; without them, the feature is worse than the current workaround. Custom patterns such as the last Friday of each month are rare, and customers already manage without them, so they get a tilde. Editing a single occurrence of a series is common, so it stays. Time zone labels on repeats are nice but not needed for the core use case.

Four tasks remain as must-haves, and the team finishes them in the final week. The two tilde items are never built, and nobody asks for them in the first month after launch.

## An appetite that was really a no

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: at the betting table, a shaper pitches six weeks for a new import tool. The CEO says six weeks is too much.

**Walkthrough:**

The shaper asks whether two weeks would be acceptable. The CEO says no: the real concern is that importing from a competitor's format invites a stream of support cases, whatever the size. The appetite discussion surfaced a different objection.

The shaper does not shrink the pitch to force it through. They go back to the problem, talk to support about what customers actually do when they switch, and find that most only need their contact list moved. A one-week pitch for a contact import, with other data types as no-gos, is bet on at the next table.
