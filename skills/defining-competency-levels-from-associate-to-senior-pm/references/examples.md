# Examples: Defining PM Competency Levels from Associate to Senior

## A three-level ladder for a consumer team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a consumer app company has associate PMs, PMs and senior PMs, and promotions have been argued case by case. The head of product builds a matrix on Cabage's grid.

**Walkthrough:**

Associate PMs own Backlog Grooming, Product Requirements, UAT Testing and User Requests, and contribute to Prototyping and A/B Testing. PMs own those plus Feature Prioritization, Conversion Optimization and User Interviews. Senior PMs own Opportunity Discovery, Market Positioning, Product Roadmap and Jobs to be Done, and keep the lower boxes as a foundation.

The promotion criteria from PM to senior PM list the boxes that move from contributes to owns. When the next promotion case comes up, the panel asks for examples in those boxes, and the discussion is shorter and less personal than before.

## A second path for platform PMs

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the same company adds an internal platform team. Its PMs worry they can never reach senior level because they rarely do market work.

**Walkthrough:**

The head of product adds a right-side path. Senior platform PMs own Build vs Buy, Technical Partnerships and Portfolio Planning, and contribute to Product Roadmap. Market Positioning and Jobs to be Done are marked not expected on this path.

Both paths share the same level names and pay bands. The platform PMs can now see what senior looks like for their kind of product, which matches Cabage's reading that technical PMs on internal tools work on the right side of the chart.

## A matrix that fails its test

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a draft matrix is tested against current PMs. A PM everyone regards as senior meets only half of the senior criteria.

**Walkthrough:**

The leaders look at the unmet criteria. Most are Business boxes such as Product Council and Portfolio Planning, which the company does not actually run at the product-area level. The draft had copied the full strategic half of the chart into the senior column.

They move those boxes to the group PM level and retest. The PM now meets the senior criteria, and the matrix matches how leaders already judge the team.
