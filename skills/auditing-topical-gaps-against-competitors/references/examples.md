# Examples: Auditing Topical Gaps Against Competitor Sites

## B2B software vendor finds its real rivals

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A project-management software company assumed its three product competitors were its SEO rivals. Its content team of 2 people had 40 blog posts and wanted a plan for the next quarter.

**Walkthrough:**

The strategist searched 20 core queries and found that a publisher and a community forum appeared more often than any product rival. She added both to the competitor set and dropped one product rival that only ranked for its own brand. Clustering competitor URLs showed the publisher had 30 pages on team workflows where the vendor had 2. The keyword gap confirmed dozens of workflow queries with no vendor page ranking.

After filtering, 6 workflow topics went into the plan as new coverage, and 3 thin existing posts were marked for expansion.

## Publisher separates weak pages from missing ones

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A personal-finance site with 200 articles saw traffic flatten. Its editor suspected missing topics, but had not checked its own performance data.

**Walkthrough:**

The team exported Search Console data for every page and tagged each with a category. Of the budgeting cluster's 25 pages, 10 had strong impressions but low clicks, and 4 had declined steadily. Competitor comparison showed rivals had updated equivalent pages more recently and added calculator-style comparison pages. The audit assigned updates to the 10 low-click pages, merged the 4 declining pages into 2 stronger ones, and added a single missing comparison format.

Only 2 genuinely new topics made the list, which kept the quarter's workload realistic.

## Agency audit that avoids one page per keyword

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: An agency ran a keyword gap for a garden-supplies retailer against 3 competitors and got a raw export of 500 queries the client did not rank for.

**Walkthrough:**

The analyst first removed branded competitor terms, which cut the list to 400. An automated cluster produced 60 groups, but manual review merged many that shared an intent and split a few that mixed buying and how-to queries, leaving 25 topics. Each topic was tested for fit with the retailer's subject area, traffic potential and seasonal priorities, and 8 passed. Reading the ranking pages showed 3 of those needed buying guides rather than product category pages.

The client received 8 actions instead of 500 keywords, each with a format and an owner.
