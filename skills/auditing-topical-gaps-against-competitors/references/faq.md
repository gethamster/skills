# FAQ: Auditing Topical Gaps Against Competitor Sites

## How many competitors should I include in a content gap audit?

For a keyword-gap comparison, [The SEO Handbook suggests two or three direct competitors](https://seohandbook.co.uk/content-strategy/content-gap-analysis). Some tools let you compare against up to four in one run, according to [Semrush](https://semrush.com/blog/content-gap-analysis). What matters more than the count is that the set includes the sites that actually rank for your queries, not only the ones that sell similar products.

## What is the difference between a keyword gap and a topical gap?

A keyword gap is a single query a competitor ranks for and you do not. A topical gap is a whole subject area missing from your site, usually visible once you group related keywords together. Topical gaps are more useful for planning because they map to clusters of pages rather than isolated posts.

## Should I always create new content to close a gap?

No. Many gaps are weak depth, a missing format, a stale page or a declining page, and those are better closed by updating, expanding or consolidating existing URLs. [Andava recommends auditing underperforming pages](https://andava.com/learn/content-gap-analysis) rather than defaulting to new content. New pages are the right call for genuinely missing topics or formats.

## How do AI answer engines change a content gap audit?

They add a citation pass. [Stridec includes citation comparison](https://stridec.com/blog/competitor-content-analysis) to find topics where competitors are cited in AI Overviews or answer engines and you are not. Treat those as gaps even when your rankings look fine, and inspect what the cited pages do differently in structure and depth.

## How often should I repeat the audit?

Repeat it whenever your subject area, competitor set or priorities change, and on a regular cadence your team can sustain, for example once a quarter. Keep the previous coverage matrix and deprioritised list so each run shows what moved. Freshness gaps in particular reappear as competitors update their pages.
