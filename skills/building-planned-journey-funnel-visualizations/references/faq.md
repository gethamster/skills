# FAQ: Building a Planned Journey Funnel Diagram

## What is a planned journey funnel diagram?

It is a chart showing how many buyers keep your brand in consideration at each stage of a long, deliberate purchase, with the losses between stages. It is usually paired with a flow diagram showing where lost buyers went and a journey map explaining why.

## How is it different from an ecommerce conversion funnel?

An ecommerce funnel tracks a sequence of site events for your own visitors. A planned journey funnel tracks buyer states across a much longer period, often using research as well as analytics, and it shows flows that ecommerce funnels ignore, such as brands being added late in evaluation and buyers dropping back to latent.

## Should I use a funnel or a Sankey diagram?

Use both for different audiences. A proportional funnel gives executives the headline in seconds. A Sankey diagram shows where buyers went and is better for working sessions with stage owners and analysts.

## How do I show drop-off rates clearly?

Label each stage with the absolute number and the share kept from the previous stage, and make bar widths proportional. Highlight the single largest loss. Add a short annotation with the cause so the rate is not read without its explanation.

## How do I handle uncertain numbers?

Say where the numbers come from and how confident you are, especially for stage volumes estimated from recall surveys. Flag small bases. Credible labelling protects the diagram when later waves shift the numbers.

## How often should the diagram be updated?

Update it whenever new consideration data arrives. Keep the stages, colors and layout fixed so readers can compare versions, and note any change in definitions or data sources.
