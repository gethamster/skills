# Examples: Building a Planned Journey Funnel Diagram

## A funnel for a quarterly leadership review

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a motorcycle brand's insight team has consideration data from a recall survey of recent buyers. The leadership team wants one slide on where the brand loses buyers.

**Walkthrough:**

The team draws three proportional bars for the latent, evaluation and buying stages, each labelled with the number of surveyed buyers who had the brand in consideration and the share kept from the previous stage. Out of every 100 surveyed buyers, 60 considered the brand at latent, 45 kept it into evaluation and 12 chose it.

The drop from evaluation to buying is highlighted in the accent color, with a one-line annotation naming the main rival and the leading reason, financing terms. A footnote says the numbers come from a recall survey and are estimates. Leadership asks for the financing comparison as a follow-up.

## A Sankey diagram that changes the discussion

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a kitchen appliance brand's retail team believes buyers are lost to discount brands on price. The analyst builds a flow diagram from the latest consideration study to test that view.

**Walkthrough:**

The flow diagram shows the widths of each flow out of the brand's evaluation set. The widest outgoing flow goes to a premium rival, and the reasons attached are design and a longer warranty. Only a thin flow goes to discount brands.

Seeing the flows side by side moves the discussion from price cuts to the warranty offer. The team keeps the diagram as the standard view for future waves, with competitors in the same order each time.

## A journey map under the funnel

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a private school's admissions team has a funnel showing many families drop out between visiting and applying. It adds a journey map to explain the drop.

**Walkthrough:**

The map uses the three stages as columns and rows for family actions, questions, touchpoints and mood, drawn from interviews with families who applied and families who did not. In the evaluation column, families' questions focus on fees and bursaries, and their mood drops after the open day, where fees were not discussed.

The annotation under the largest drop reads that families left evaluation without fee information and did not ask. The admissions team adds a clear fees and bursaries session to open days and repeats the diagram after the next admissions cycle.
