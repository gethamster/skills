# Examples: Customer Journey Touchpoints Mapping Across Five Stages

## Finding the empty retention stage

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a meal-kit company inventories touchpoints for first-time subscribers. Marketing expects the list to be dominated by acquisition channels, and it is.

**Walkthrough:**

The team exports ads, landing pages and welcome emails, then asks support and operations to add theirs. Awareness and consideration fill up quickly. Retention holds only the weekly delivery, a shipping notification and a skip-a-week page. Nothing speaks to the customer between the third and sixth delivery, which is when support sees cancellation requests start.

The finding goes to the lifecycle marketing owner with the retention metric attached. The team also notices that the skip-a-week page has no owner at all, so it is assigned to the product manager for the account area.

## Surfacing a touchpoint nobody knew about

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B analytics vendor interviews a few recently won customers while building its inventory.

**Walkthrough:**

Several customers mention the same independent comparison article and a thread in a practitioner community where users compared tools. Neither appears in any internal system. The team adds both as external consideration touchpoints and assigns a product marketer to monitor them.

The walk-through in step 6 adds another finding. The comparison article links to a pricing page that no longer exists, so readers land on an error. The fix is a redirect, owned by the web team, and it is added to the row as a note.

## Splitting a shared touchpoint by stage

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a software company's help center is used by trial users evaluating the product and by paying customers troubleshooting it.

**Walkthrough:**

The first draft lists the help center once under retention. During review, the sales lead points out that prospects read setup guides to judge how hard the product is to adopt. The team lists the help center twice: once under consideration, with the purpose "judge effort to get started", and once under retention, with the purpose "fix a problem without contacting support". Each row gets its own signal, so the documentation team can see that the consideration use needs clearer getting-started pages.
