# FAQ: Customer Journey Touchpoints Mapping Across Five Stages

## What counts as a touchpoint?

Any moment where the customer meets your company or forms an opinion about it. That includes owned interactions such as pages, emails and calls, and external ones such as reviews, community threads and a colleague's recommendation. If the moment could change what the customer thinks or does next, it belongs on the list. A channel on its own, such as "social media", is too broad to be a touchpoint.

## How detailed should the inventory be?

Detailed enough that each row has one owner, one purpose and one signal. If a row needs several owners, split it. If a group of rows always changes together, you can merge them. Aim for a list a stage owner can read in one sitting.

## How do I handle touchpoints I cannot control?

List them, mark them as external and decide how to monitor and influence them. For review sites that might mean responding to reviews and asking satisfied customers to post. For communities it might mean having someone read and answer threads. The goal is awareness of what customers see, not control of it.

## How is a touchpoint inventory different from a journey map?

The inventory is a structured list of interactions sorted by stage. The journey map adds the customer's actions, thoughts and emotions and shows where the experience breaks. Most teams build the map first to set the frame, then use the inventory to fill each stage in detail. Link the two so updates in one are visible from the other.

## How often should the inventory be updated?

Review it whenever the journey map is reviewed, usually each planning cycle, and add rows whenever a campaign, feature or channel launches. Put a review date on each row so stale entries are easy to spot. Retire rows for touchpoints that no longer exist rather than leaving them in place.
