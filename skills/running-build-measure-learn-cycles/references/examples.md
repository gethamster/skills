# Examples: Running the Build-Measure-Learn Loop

## A two-week cycle on onboarding

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team running a budgeting app sees that many new users never connect a bank account. The top hypothesis on their list is that users drop off because they do not trust the app with their bank login.

**Walkthrough:**

The team plans backward. They need to learn whether trust is the barrier. They will measure the share of new users who connect an account in their first session. The smallest build is a short explanation of how the connection works and who can see the data, shown to half of new users in a split test.

After two weeks, the connection rate is higher in the group that saw the explanation, and above the pass mark. Calls with a few users who still did not connect show that some had no online banking at all. The team records the verdict, adds a new assumption about users without online banking, and starts the next cycle on the second step of onboarding.

## Using manual work to shorten a cycle

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team wants to know whether small online shops will act on weekly reports that point out slow-selling stock. Building the automated report would take most of a month.

**Walkthrough:**

The team asks what they need to learn: whether shop owners change their stock after reading the report. The measure is the share of recipients who discount or reorder a flagged item within a week. Instead of building the report engine, an analyst assembles reports by hand from exported sales data for a small group of shops.

The cycle takes days instead of weeks. Most owners open the report, but few act on it, which misses the pass mark. Follow-up calls show owners want a suggested discount, not only a list. The next cycle tests reports with suggested actions, still assembled by hand.

## Escalating after repeated misses

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team has run several cycles trying to raise the share of trial users of its meeting-notes product who invite a colleague. Each cycle tested a different prompt or incentive.

**Walkthrough:**

Reviewing the cycle record, the team sees that none of the changes moved the invite rate away from its baseline, even though each was well designed. Interviews repeatedly show that users treat their notes as private.

Rather than run another prompt test, the team brings the record to its pivot or persevere meeting. The assumption that the product spreads through sharing is marked as contradicted, and the group discusses whether a different engine of growth fits the product better.
