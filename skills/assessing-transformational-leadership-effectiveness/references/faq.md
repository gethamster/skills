# FAQ: Assessing Transformational Leadership Effectiveness

## What is the Multifactor Leadership Questionnaire?

The Multifactor Leadership Questionnaire, or MLQ, is the survey instrument most associated with measuring transformational leadership, with raters scoring how often a leader shows the relevant behaviors. A [review of conceptual weaknesses](https://dl.icdst.org/pdfs/files4/707d99a3fac5a0d94c58794462f64f75.pdf) notes that most survey studies using the MLQ and similar questionnaires find positive links to subordinate satisfaction, motivation and performance. Its value for practitioners is that results can be read against that body of research. Its main limitation is that survey correlations alone do not prove the leader caused those outcomes.

## What does a validity of .44 for transformational leadership mean?

It is the overall correlation between transformational leadership scores and effectiveness criteria reported by the [Judge and Piccolo meta-analysis](https://my.carolinau.edu/ICS/icsfs/1_Judge___Piccolo_2004_Transformational_and_Transa.pdf?target=9fd48bd2-ed80-416f-b340-cf1b5a545655), pooled from 626 correlations across 87 ([source](https://my.carolinau.edu/ICS/icsfs/1_Judge___Piccolo_2004_Transformational_and_Transa.pdf?target=9fd48bd2-ed80-416f-b340-cf1b5a545655)) sources. A correlation of that size indicates a meaningful association across many leaders. It does not mean any individual leader's score explains a fixed share of their team's results. The same study found the result held in longitudinal and multisource designs, which strengthens it.

## Why do reported effect sizes for transformational leadership differ so much?

Different meta-analyses use different samples, outcome criteria and statistics. For example, the [2023 evidence review](https://tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13678868.2022.2135938) reports ρ = .79 from Hoch et al. and 8.4% of variance explained from DeRue et al., and it lists .64 for Judge and Piccolo, whose own abstract gives [an overall validity of .44](https://my.carolinau.edu/ICS/icsfs/1_Judge___Piccolo_2004_Transformational_and_Transa.pdf?target=9fd48bd2-ed80-416f-b340-cf1b5a545655). These are not contradictions so much as answers to different questions. Always check which criterion and statistic a number refers to before comparing it with another.

## Can a transformational leadership questionnaire reliably separate the Four I's?

Not as cleanly as the model suggests. A [2013 critical assessment](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/19416520.2013.759433) found that commonly used tools fail to reproduce the proposed dimensions and do not distinguish them empirically from other leadership concepts. Other critics point to [limited evidence distinguishing the four factors](https://thescopes.org/assets/Uploads/SCOPE-LT-10-Chaplin-Cheyne-v2.pdf). Use the overall score for decisions and dimension scores for coaching conversations.

## If a leader scores high on transformational leadership, does transactional leadership stop mattering?

No. Judge and Piccolo found [contingent-reward transactional leadership had a validity of .39, close to the .44 for transformational leadership](https://my.carolinau.edu/ICS/icsfs/1_Judge___Piccolo_2004_Transformational_and_Transa.pdf?target=9fd48bd2-ed80-416f-b340-cf1b5a545655). The [2025 public administration meta-analysis](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02750740241290810?icid=int.sj-abstract.citing-articles.14) also found the two styles positively related, so they often appear together. Measure both, so you can see what each contributes.
