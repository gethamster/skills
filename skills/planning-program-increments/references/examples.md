# Examples: Planning Program Increments \(PI Planning\)

## First PI planning for a newly launched train

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a new ART of six teams is running its first PI planning, and several teams have never planned beyond a single sprint.

**Walkthrough:**

The Release Train Engineer circulates a planning pack two weeks early with the top ten features and a capacity template, and runs a short dry run with product owners. During the first breakout, two teams fill every iteration to full capacity, and the facilitator points out that nothing is left for defects. At the draft plan review, leaders drop the two lowest-priority features to relieve the most loaded team. On day two, teams rewrite objectives from story titles into outcomes after business owners ask what customers will notice.

The confidence vote comes back mixed, so the train spends a short session moving one feature to stretch. The second vote passes, and the retrospective notes that capacity planning needs more coaching next time.

## Resolving a dependency chain on a shared service

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: three teams each need changes to a payments service owned by a fourth team, all targeted for the third of five iterations.

**Walkthrough:**

The dependency board shows three lines converging on the payments team in the same iteration, which the facilitator flags during the first breakout. The payments team reviews its capacity and says it can absorb only one of the changes in that iteration. The four teams meet at the board and agree to sequence the changes across iterations two, three and four. One consuming team replans its feature so its dependent slice lands later, while shipping an independent slice earlier.

The risk of the payments team becoming a bottleneck is recorded with an owner who will watch its load. The final plan shows three separate dependency lines with agreed iterations instead of one overloaded one.

## Handling a low confidence vote

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: at the end of day two, a train's confidence vote shows many low votes, mainly from two teams working on a new integration.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator asks low voters to explain their concerns rather than calling a revote. The two teams say the integration relies on a vendor API whose documentation is incomplete. Leaders agree to move the integration objective from committed to stretch and add an early spike to test the vendor API in the first iteration. The teams rework their plans in a short breakout, pulling a lower-risk objective forward to fill the capacity.

A second vote shows broad confidence. The risk about the vendor API is marked as mitigated by the spike, with a named owner reporting the result after the first iteration.
