# FAQ: Iterating PR/FAQ Documents Through Feedback

## How many drafts does a PR/FAQ need?

There is no fixed number. Colin Bryar and Bill Carr write that it is not unusual for an Amazon team to write ten drafts or more and to meet senior leaders five times or more for a PR/FAQ under serious consideration. Small ideas in small teams may need far fewer, and ideas that stop early may need only one or two.

## How do I handle reviewers who contradict each other?

Record both views in the feedback log, then look for the assumption behind the disagreement. Often the two reviewers are picturing different customers or different constraints. Resolve the assumption in the FAQ and, if it is a judgment call, flag it for the decision maker.

## When is the document ready for the executive review?

When the small-group decision maker is satisfied and recent reviews produce refinements instead of structural changes. The customer, the problem and the economics should be settled, and remaining unknowns should be listed with plans to resolve them. Taking it earlier spends executive time on issues peers would have caught.

## What if a senior leader asks for a fundamental change late in the process?

Treat it like any structural feedback: understand the reason, and write the changed version if the reason holds. Late changes are expensive, which is one reason to involve senior reviewers in earlier rounds. If you disagree, say so in writing with your reasoning, and let the decision maker decide.

## Should the PR/FAQ change after the project is approved?

Yes. Bryar and Carr describe it as a living document that will almost certainly still be edited after approval, with changes directed by or reviewed with the leadership team. Keeping it current keeps it useful as the shared description of what is being built.
