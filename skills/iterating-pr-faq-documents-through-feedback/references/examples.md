# Examples: Iterating PR/FAQ Documents Through Feedback

## A new integration that changes customer in round two

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product manager at a B2B software company drafts a PR/FAQ for a two-way sync with a popular project tracker. Her first draft names engineering managers as the customer.

**Walkthrough:**

In the manager and peer round, a customer success lead points out that the people asking for the sync are project coordinators, who live in the tracker all day. The product manager rewrites the press release around coordinators before the small-group review, and the feedback log records the change and its reason.

The small-group review focuses on the FAQ: what happens when both tools edit the same item, and what the partner's API allows. Two more drafts resolve these, and the feedback in the third review is about wording. She takes the document to the executive review with a one-paragraph summary of how the conflict question was settled.

## A startup that learns to stop

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a three-person startup writes a PR/FAQ for a second product line, a paid template marketplace, to sit next to its core app.

**Walkthrough:**

The founders review each other's drafts and ask two advisers to read the document. Each round surfaces the same issue from a different angle: the internal FAQ cannot say why template creators would sell there rather than on existing marketplaces, and the market estimate keeps shrinking as the assumptions are written down.

After the third draft the founders agree the document does not support a go. They record the reasons in the FAQ, including the question that would need a good answer to reopen it, and put their time back into the core app.

## An enterprise platform change with many stakeholders

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a platform team at a large company proposes replacing a shared internal login system. Many teams depend on it, and several leaders have strong views.

**Walkthrough:**

The author runs the small-group rounds with representatives of the most affected teams and keeps a detailed feedback log. Security raises a requirement in the second round that changes the migration plan, and the press release is updated so it no longer promises a single-day switchover.

Before the executive review, the author circulates a summary of the log so leaders can see their teams' concerns and the answers. The executives approve a phased version and ask that the PR/FAQ be updated at each phase, which becomes part of the project plan.
