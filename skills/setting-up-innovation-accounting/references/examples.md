# Examples: Setting Up Innovation Accounting

## B2B scheduling tool finds its weak link

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A four-person team has a scheduling MVP for small clinics. Their business plan assumes 40% of trial clinics convert to paid, and the board wants a progress update in six weeks.

**Walkthrough:**

The team maps the chain as trial started, first appointment booked, used in week three, converted to paid. After three weekly cohorts, the frozen baseline shows 55% book a first appointment, 20% are active in week three and 6% convert. The plan's 40% conversion target stays in the report, but the gap now points to week-three usage as the weakest link. The team runs an experiment that auto-imports existing patient lists for a new cohort, predicting week-three usage will rise above 30%.

That cohort reaches 31%, and conversion in the cohort rises to 11%. The board report shows baseline, target, latest cohort and the one experiment, and the team perseveres with the next experiment aimed at conversion.

## Consumer app stops celebrating downloads

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A habit-tracking app reports 50,000 cumulative downloads and rising monthly totals to its investors. The product lead suspects the growth is masking poor retention.

**Walkthrough:**

She rebuilds the report around cohorts by install week and a single retention metric: share of users logging a habit on day 14. The baseline across recent cohorts is 8%, and the team sets a target of 20% based on what their subscription model needs. They agree in advance that if four tuning experiments fail to lift any cohort above 12%, they will reconsider the core value proposition. Onboarding, reminder timing and streak experiments each move day-14 retention by a point or less.

After the fourth flat result, the pre-set criterion triggers a pivot review rather than another round of feature ideas. Investors see the same table every month, so the pivot reads as disciplined learning rather than a surprise.

## Internal innovation team reporting to a steering committee

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A corporate team is testing a self-service quoting portal for existing insurance customers. The steering committee is used to milestone reports showing features delivered on schedule.

**Walkthrough:**

The team proposes swapping the milestone report for an innovation accounting table with three metrics: portal visits that start a quote, quotes completed, and completed quotes that bind a policy. They run a concierge version where staff complete quotes behind the scenes, and the first two cohorts set a baseline of 12% start-to-bind. The committee agrees a target of 25% and a pivot rule of no cohort above 15% after three experiments. A simplified form lifts one cohort to 19%, so the team perseveres and automates that path.

The committee now asks which experiment moved which number, a question the old milestone report could never answer.
