# FAQ: Setting Up Innovation Accounting

## How is innovation accounting different from regular product analytics?

Regular analytics describes what happened across the whole product. Innovation accounting selects a few metrics tied to the business model's riskiest assumptions, records a baseline, sets targets and pre-commits to pivot criteria. The analytics tool can be the same; the difference is that every number is attached to a decision. If a dashboard metric would not change what you do next, it does not belong in the accounting.

## How many metrics should we track?

Track only the behaviors your riskiest assumptions depend on, which for most early products is a handful. For example, three to five cohort metrics is usually enough for a stakeholder report to stay readable. More metrics make it easier to find one that went up by chance. If you are unsure whether a metric belongs, ask what you would do differently if it halved.

## How long should we wait before trusting the baseline?

Wait until at least two or three cohorts have moved through the chain far enough to produce similar readings. A baseline from a single cohort can reflect an unusual launch week or a burst of friendly early users. Record the date range and cohorts behind the baseline so stakeholders know what it rests on. If later cohorts diverge sharply before any tuning, investigate the instrumentation or customer mix before resetting.

## What should we do when tuning results are inconclusive?

First check whether the cohort was large enough and whether only one variable changed. If the test was sound but the metric barely moved, log it as a flat result, since that counts toward your pre-set pivot criteria. Design the next experiment with a bigger expected effect rather than rerunning a small tweak. Repeated flat results on the weakest link are information, not bad luck.

## How do we get stakeholders to accept this instead of milestone reports?

Agree the metrics, baseline, targets and pivot rules with them before tuning starts, so the report reflects commitments they made. Keep the format identical every period so trends are obvious at a glance. Pair each table with a short note on what was learned and what you will test next. Stakeholders usually accept the change once they can see which experiment moved which number.
