# FAQ: Building Guilds as Cross-Cutting Communities of Practice

## How is a guild different from a chapter in the Spotify model?

A chapter groups people with the same discipline inside one tribe and is led by a line manager. A guild is an informal, voluntary community of interest that can span the whole organization. Chapters handle people management and discipline coordination, while guilds exist for knowledge sharing. If your topic lives entirely inside one tribe's discipline, it may belong in a chapter instead.

## How often should a guild meet?

The original material does not set a cadence, so choose one your members can sustain. A common starting pattern is a monthly session paired with an always-open chat channel. Increase frequency only when the topic backlog consistently outgrows the time available. Cancel sessions when there is nothing worth discussing rather than filling the slot.

## Who should lead a guild?

No formal role is prescribed, so a volunteer convener, or a pair, usually works best. The convener manages the backlog, schedules sessions and publishes notes rather than directing anyone's work. Rotating the role on a fixed term, for example each quarter, spreads the effort and keeps the guild from depending on one person.

## Can a guild set standards for squads?

A guild can recommend practices and produce examples, templates and write-ups. Binding decisions about tools and methods are better routed to the relevant chapters or squads, which own delivery and discipline coordination. Keeping guilds advisory protects squad autonomy and keeps participation voluntary in spirit, not just on paper.

## How do I know if a guild is working?

Look for reuse rather than attendance alone: guild material cited in squad wikis, answers in the channel that save people time, and new presenters from different teams. Warning signs include the same few people presenting every time, managers assigning attendance, and an empty topic backlog. Review these signals on a regular interval and reshape or close the guild when they turn negative.
