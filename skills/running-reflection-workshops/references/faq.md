# FAQ: Running Reflective Improvement Workshops in Crystal

## How long should a Crystal reflection workshop be?

Cockburn's Crystal presentation states that the reflection workshop takes an hour a month. That is a good default for a small team. If you consistently run out of time, the usual cause is too many topics rather than too little time, so narrow the agenda before extending the session.

## How is a reflection workshop different from a Scrum retrospective?

Both look back at how the team worked and aim to improve it. The Crystal version is tied to the team's own delivery rhythm rather than a fixed sprint, and it is meant to change the team's working conventions directly, which is part of how Crystal teams adapt their methodology over time. In practice, a good retrospective and a good reflection workshop look similar; what matters is that the output is changed behaviour, not a list.

## What if nothing changed after the last workshop?

Make that the main topic of the session. Ask for each unused change whether it was unclear, lacked an owner, conflicted with delivery pressure, or simply was not important. Reduce the number of new changes until follow-through recovers, because a workshop that never changes anything erodes trust in the whole process.

## Who should attend the reflection workshop?

Invite everyone who works in the delivery cycle, since the workshop is about how the whole team collaborates. Include people who work closely with expert users so that feedback problems are visible. Managers can attend if the team is comfortable speaking openly in front of them; if not, that discomfort is itself worth addressing.

## Should we hold workshops more often than monthly?

Tie the frequency to your delivery rhythm and your rate of change. A team in a difficult period, such as a new project or a major change in staffing, may benefit from shorter, more frequent sessions for a while. Once things stabilise, return to a regular cadence that the team can sustain without the workshop feeling like overhead.
