# FAQ: Adapting GEO Tactics Across Domains

## Why can't I reuse the tactics that worked in the original GEO study?

The original study reported gains that [varied by domain](https://geo.wiki/papers/aggarwal-geo-benchmark-2024), and a later review found generic heuristics [transfer poorly across settings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14035). The study's tactic list is a useful menu of hypotheses. Whether a given tactic helps your pages depends on your domain, engine and language, so it needs a local test.

## Which GEO levers are most likely to work across domains?

The 2026 critical review identified [topical relevance and context position](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14035) as the most reproducible levers. In practice that means making sure each page directly answers its target prompts and placing the core answer early. Start there before testing stylistic or evidence-type tactics.

## How many engines and languages should I test?

Test the engines and languages your audience actually uses, because services [differ in domain diversity, freshness and cross-language stability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919). Rank combinations by audience value and begin with the highest few rather than the full grid. Add cells when usage data shows a new engine or language matters.

## Do niche organizations need a different approach from large brands?

Often, yes. The 2025 study identifies [big-brand bias as a limitation for niche organizations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919) and recommends earned-media coverage alongside engine-specific and language-aware work. If engines in your domain rarely cite brand-owned pages, on-page tactics alone may have limited reach.

## How do I know a tactic truly works in a cell and is not just noise?

Compare the tactic against a baseline measured with repeated runs, because audits show [substantial run-to-run variability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14035). Look for a gain that clearly exceeds the baseline's normal spread across several prompt phrasings. If the difference disappears on a re-check, treat the result as neutral.
