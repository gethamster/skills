# FAQ: Designing Assumption Tests for OST Solutions

## What is the difference between an assumption test and an experiment?

Teresa Torres uses assumption tests for discovery, when a team is deciding between ideas, and experiments for measuring the impact of something already built. Assumption tests evaluate a single underlying assumption rather than a whole idea, so they are faster and take less work. A large A/B test comes after building, and only if there is enough traffic.

## Do we need to test every assumption?

No. Torres says that if you engage with customers regularly, most of your assumptions will carry little risk. Test only the riskiest ones, meaning those that are critical to the idea and have little evidence behind them. Assumption mapping helps you find them.

## What types of assumptions should we look for?

Torres groups them into five categories: desirability, viability, feasibility, usability, and ethical. She says it does not matter much which category a specific assumption belongs to. The categories exist to help you generate assumptions across all kinds of risk so you do not miss the riskiest ones.

## What kinds of tests should we run?

Most assumption tests fall into four types: prototype tests that simulate a moment and observe behavior, one-question surveys, data mining on existing data, and research spikes for feasibility. Start with the smallest test that gives an early signal, and move to larger tests only when the risk justifies it.

## Why write success criteria before the test?

Criteria written afterward tend to bend toward what the team hoped to see. Torres's guidance is to make criteria specific enough that someone else could call the win or the loss. Agreeing them with stakeholders in advance also makes the result easier to accept.

## Why test three solutions instead of one?

Testing one idea encourages confirmation bias: the team looks for evidence that its favorite works. Testing assumptions across three candidates gives a compare-and-contrast view and makes it easier to drop weak ideas early.
