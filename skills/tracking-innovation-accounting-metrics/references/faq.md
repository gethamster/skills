# FAQ: Innovation Accounting Metrics: Tracking Real Progress

## What is innovation accounting?

It is the lean startup's system for measuring progress on a new product, where normal financial accounting has little to measure. It sets a baseline with an MVP, runs experiments to move key metrics toward what the business model needs and uses the results to decide whether to pivot or persevere. Eric Ries lists it as one of the principles of the lean startup.

## What is the difference between actionable metrics and vanity metrics?

Actionable metrics show cause and effect, so they tell you what to do next. Vanity metrics look good but do not guide decisions, such as total page views or cumulative sign-ups. A metric can be actionable for one business and vanity for another, depending on whether it drives that business's results.

## What goes on an innovation accounting scorecard?

A few metrics tied to your engine of growth, each with a baseline, a target taken from the business model and a trend by cohort. Typical entries include activation, retention, conversion to paid and referral rates. Keep it to one page and use it at every decision meeting.

## Why use cohorts instead of totals?

Totals rise over time almost regardless of what you change, so they cannot show whether the product is improving. Cohorts compare groups of customers who joined at different times, so you can see whether newer customers behave better after a change. That makes the effect of each experiment visible.

## How many metrics should we track?

As few as can capture the key steps of your growth model, often a handful. Ries advises measuring what matters and focusing on a few metrics. A long dashboard makes it easy to find a number that went up and hard to agree on progress.
