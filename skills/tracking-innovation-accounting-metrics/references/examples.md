# Examples: Innovation Accounting Metrics: Tracking Real Progress

## Replacing a sign-up chart with a cohort scorecard

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team running a habit-tracking app reports its cumulative sign-ups every week, and the line keeps rising. Investors are pleased, but revenue is flat.

**Walkthrough:**

The team rebuilds its reporting around the sticky engine it is betting on. The scorecard has four rates by weekly cohort: activation (tracked a habit on day one), week-one retention, week-four retention and conversion to paid. They measure the last several cohorts to set a baseline.

The baseline shows week-four retention falling slightly with each new cohort, which the cumulative chart had hidden. The team sets a retention target based on what paid acquisition would need to break even, and aims its next experiments at the first week of use.

## Tuning the engine with before and after cohorts

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team selling scheduling software to salons has a baseline for trial-to-paid conversion and a target from its business model. It runs one onboarding change per cycle.

**Walkthrough:**

For each change, the team compares cohorts that joined after the change with those that joined before, rather than looking at all customers at once. The first two changes move conversion up. The third moves nothing, and the team records that result on the scorecard as well.

At the next decision meeting, the scorecard shows conversion about halfway from the baseline to the target, with most of the gain from the first two changes. The group decides to persevere and focuses the next cycle on the step with the largest remaining gap.

## Choosing metrics for a viral engine

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team building a shared shopping list app believes it will grow mainly through invitations between household members and friends.

**Walkthrough:**

The team picks metrics that govern a viral engine: the share of new users who send an invite, the share of invites accepted and the resulting number of new users each user brings in. They trace each number back to individual accounts so any surprise can be checked.

The baseline shows that users invite their household but rarely anyone outside it, so each user brings in fewer than one new user. The team records that the viral engine is not running on its own and designs experiments to test whether sharing a list with friends can change that.
