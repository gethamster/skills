# Examples: Creating SEO Strategy Templates for Full Topical Coverage

## Rebuilding a stalled blog plan

**Scenario:**

A B2B software team has published steadily for two years but cannot say which topics are covered or why traffic has flattened.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the team maps 120 existing posts into 8 clusters and three funnel stages. The matrix shows 7 of the 8 clusters have awareness content, but only 2 have anything at the decision stage. It also reveals 15 posts sharing near-identical intent across three clusters. The team marks those 15 for consolidation into 5 stronger pages and adds 10 decision-stage rows as planned gaps.

The first quarterly review targets those rows before any new awareness content.

## Absorbing a competitor gap audit

**Scenario:**

An SEO lead receives a competitor gap report with a long list of queries and needs to turn it into planned work without flooding the backlog.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the report lists 200 queries. The lead groups them into 18 topics, discards 4 that fall outside the owned subject area, and checks the remaining 14 against the register. Six already have pages with weak performance, so they get an update action rather than new rows. Eight become new planned rows tagged with the audit as their source.

The matrix now shows exactly where the competitor findings change coverage.

## Setting a cadence for a small team

**Scenario:**

A two-person content team keeps building ambitious plans that go stale within weeks.

**Walkthrough:**

Illustrative scenario: the team sets a 30-minute monthly status pass and a half-day quarterly review. Monthly, they update statuses and move shipped rows to published. Quarterly, they refresh performance columns, sort by last updated date and flag the 10 oldest pages. Each flagged row leaves with one action.

After two quarters, the saved matrix snapshots show which clusters moved and which stalled, and the team cuts the next plan to match what they actually shipped.
