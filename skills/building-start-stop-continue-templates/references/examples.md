# Examples: Building a Start Stop Continue Retrospective Template

## An online whiteboard template for a distributed team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team spread across several time zones runs a retrospective every two weeks on an online whiteboard. Each facilitator has been drawing the board from scratch, and sessions start late.

**Walkthrough:**

One team member builds a master board. The header has fields for sprint name, date and facilitator. Below it sits a "Last time" frame with the previous actions and a status sticker for each.

The three columns each have a one-line definition and a sprint-specific prompt, plus one example note in grey. Notes are set to stay hidden until the facilitator reveals them. To the right is an empty grouping area, a reminder that each person gets a fixed number of votes, and an action table with only a few rows. A small "Anything else?" frame sits at the bottom.

Each facilitator now duplicates the master board the day before. After two sessions, the team moves the example notes above the prompts because people were copying them, and saves the revised board as the new master.

## A shared document template for asynchronous input

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team with little meeting overlap wants people to add their items before a short call. They use a shared document.

**Walkthrough:**

The template starts with a table of last session's actions: action, owner, review date and status. Below it is a section for each team member, with Start, Stop and Continue headings and a prompt under each. People fill in their own section during the two days before the call.

Before the call, the facilitator copies all items into a grouping table with a theme column and a votes column. During the call, the team labels themes, adds votes and fills in the action table at the bottom. The free text section catches two comments about the release calendar that fit no column, which the team decides to raise with another group.

## A printed worksheet for self-reflection

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a manager wants each report to reflect before their quarterly review and designs a one-page worksheet.

**Walkthrough:**

The worksheet has three boxes, each with a short prompt: what would help your work if you started it, what takes more of your time than it should, and what you do that is working and should continue. Under each prompt is a line asking for one recent example.

A fourth box asks what the manager could start, stop or continue. A final box says "Anything else you want to raise." Reports fill it in before the meeting and bring it along, and the manager keeps copies with the review notes.
