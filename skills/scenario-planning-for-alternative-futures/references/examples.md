# Examples: SEO Scenario Planning for Alternative Futures

## A how-to publisher facing answer panels

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a home repair site earns most of its search traffic from how-to queries. The team worries that more answers will appear directly on the results page, and its plan for next year is simply more how-to articles.

**Walkthrough:**

The team lists the plan's assumptions and picks two uncertainties: how much how-to traffic still reaches websites, and whether people start searching for repair help through video platforms more than web search. Crossing them gives four scenarios, which the team names so they are easy to discuss.

Testing the plan shows that short how-to articles pay off in only one scenario. Detailed guides with original photos, tool lists and troubleshooting pay off in three. A video series pays off in two. The move that works everywhere is to deepen the most important guides with original material. The short articles become a bet tied to an indicator: the team's share of clicks on its core how-to queries, checked monthly.

## A B2B company in a fast-growing category

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company sells software in a category that is growing quickly. The plan assumes the category name will stick and that buyers will search for it by name.

**Walkthrough:**

The deepening session showed that buyers describe the problem in several different ways. The team chooses two uncertainties: whether the category name becomes standard, and whether a large platform adds a competing feature. The resulting scenarios include one where the category name fades and buyers search by problem instead.

The plan is re-sorted. Content organized around the problem pays off in every scenario and moves first. A glossary built around the category name becomes a bet tied to an indicator: how often the category term appears in customer conversations and in search results. A worst-case backcast from the platform scenario adds a comparison page the team had not planned.

## Reviewing scenarios a year later

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a travel content team built four scenarios last year. At the quarterly review, one indicator has fired: a competitor has launched a large set of destination guides.

**Walkthrough:**

The team updates the narrative for that scenario with what has happened and starts the bet it had prepared, a set of deeper guides for the destinations where it already has strong visibility. Two other scenarios still look plausible. One now seems unlikely, so the team retires it and keeps the old version on file.

The review also surfaces a new uncertainty about travel regulations, which goes onto the emerging issues list for the next full pass.
