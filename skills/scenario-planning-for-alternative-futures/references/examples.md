# Examples: Scenario Planning for Alternative SEO and Content Futures

## Example: B2B SaaS company planning for AI search disruption

**Scenario:**

A project management SaaS with 500 blog posts, 40,000 monthly organic sessions, and 70% of traffic from informational queries like "what is agile methodology" and "how to run a sprint retrospective." The team has 2 content writers and an SEO lead. They have noticed AI Overviews appearing on 35% of their top queries and their CTR on informational queries dropping 12% year-over-year.

**Walkthrough:**

The team runs the scenario exercise in a 4-hour offsite. Their two critical uncertainties are: (1) pace of AI Overview expansion on their query set (slow: stays at 35% vs. fast: reaches 75% within 12 months) and (2) competitive content investment (stable vs. two competitors launching programmatic comparison pages at scale).

They name the four scenarios: "Comfortable Cruise" (slow AI, stable competition), "AI Squeeze" (fast AI, stable competition), "Content War" (slow AI, aggressive competition), and "Perfect Storm" (fast AI, aggressive competition). Impact assessment reveals that 60% of their current content calendar targets informational queries that score poorly in both AI-heavy scenarios. Their no-regret moves become: improving comparison and product-led content (scores 4+ in all scenarios), adding structured data to all pages (helps everywhere), and building FAQ sections with AI-extractable answer blocks. txt file.

Their scenario-specific bet is a 10% allocation to programmatic comparison pages, triggered when competitor programmatic page counts exceed 500. They set monthly monitoring of AI Overview prevalence via manual checks of their top 50 queries and quarterly competitor content audits. Within the first quarter, the team shifts 8 planned informational blog posts to comparison-format content with structured data, a move that performs well regardless of which scenario materializes.

## Example: E-commerce brand hedging against zero-click search growth

**Scenario:**

A direct-to-consumer skincare brand generating 120,000 monthly organic sessions, with 45% from product-related queries and 55% from educational content about skincare routines. The brand has a small marketing team of 3 people and a content budget of $8,000/month. They have seen featured snippets appearing on many of their educational queries and are concerned about the combined effect of AI Overviews and Google Shopping integration reducing their organic click-through.

**Walkthrough:**

The team identifies two critical uncertainties: (1) zero-click behavior on their educational queries (moderate growth vs. dramatic acceleration where 50%+ of educational queries produce zero clicks) and (2) Google Shopping integration depth (stays as-is vs. Google deeply integrates product comparisons into AI Overviews, reducing product query clicks). They construct four scenarios and name them: "Status Quo Plus" (moderate zero-click, current Shopping), "Knowledge Drain" (high zero-click, current Shopping), "Shopping Takeover" (moderate zero-click, deep Shopping integration), and "Full Squeeze" (both).

Their impact assessment shows that their educational blog content scores 4 in Status Quo Plus but drops to 1-2 in both high zero-click scenarios. Their product pages score reasonably across all scenarios because product queries still drive purchase intent. No-regret moves: invest in product page SEO with rich schema markup (Product, Review, AggregateRating), build an email capture funnel so educational content converts visitors before they disappear, and add comparison tables to product pages. Hedged bet (25% of budget): create YouTube skincare tutorials targeting the same educational queries, since video content gets cited in AI Overviews and builds brand presence on a platform AI systems reference heavily.

Scenario-specific bet (10%): build a skincare ingredient directory with programmatic pages for each ingredient, triggered only if zero-click rates on educational queries exceed 40% (as measured by their CTR in Search Console). 5%. 5% combined with a 15%+ traffic decline on educational pages.

## Example: Content agency preparing for market consolidation

**Scenario:**

A 15-person content marketing agency serving mid-market B2B companies. They produce blog content, whitepapers, and case studies for 12 retainer clients. Their own website generates 25,000 monthly organic sessions, primarily from "content marketing" and "B2B content strategy" terms. They are seeing AI writing tools reduce client willingness to pay premium rates for standard blog content, and they are worried about AI-generated content flooding the SERPs in their clients' industries.

**Walkthrough:**

The agency's two critical uncertainties are: (1) AI content quality trajectory (AI-generated content remains identifiable and lower quality vs. AI content becomes indistinguishable from expert human content within 18 months) and (2) client budget direction (B2B content budgets grow as companies double down on content vs. budgets shrink as companies automate content production with AI tools). The four scenarios: "Golden Age" (AI stays mediocre, budgets grow), "Automation Wave" (AI gets good, budgets grow but shift to AI-assisted production), "Budget Crunch" (AI stays mediocre, budgets shrink due to economic pressure), and "Existential Shift" (AI gets good AND budgets shrink).

For their own site, the team identifies no-regret moves: publish original research and proprietary data from their client work (anonymized) that AI cannot replicate, create detailed case studies with specific metrics, and build comparison content around content strategy methodologies. These score well across all four scenarios because original data and demonstrated results maintain value regardless of AI content quality. Hedged bet: invest 20% of their own marketing time into building an AI-assisted content production workflow they can offer to clients, positioning the agency as an AI-augmented partner rather than a pure human-content provider. This scores highest in "Automation Wave" but also adds value in "Golden Age" as an efficiency gain.

Scenario-specific bet: develop a programmatic SEO offering for clients using the templates and directory playbooks, triggered when 3+ clients ask about programmatic content in a single quarter. Trigger indicators include: monitoring AI content detection rates in their clients' industries (using manual review of top 20 SERPs quarterly), tracking client budget conversations in their CRM, and surveying clients semi-annually about their AI tool usage. At their first quarterly review, they find that 4 of 12 clients have started using AI drafting tools internally, pushing their probability estimate for "Automation Wave" from 25% to 40% and leading them to accelerate the AI-assisted workflow development.

## Example: Local services business planning for search landscape shifts

**Scenario:**

A multi-location dental practice group with 8 locations across Texas. They generate 60% of new patient inquiries through local SEO, ranking in the map pack and organic results for "dentist in [city]" and "dental implants near me" queries. They have a part-time marketing coordinator and use an SEO agency. Their concern is that Google's AI features and competitive aggregator sites might reduce the visibility and click-through of traditional local search results.

**Walkthrough:**

The practice group's two critical uncertainties are: (1) local search result format changes (Google maintains current map pack and organic structure vs. Google integrates AI-powered local recommendations that consolidate listings and reduce visibility for individual practices) and (2) aggregator competition (HealthGrades, Zocdoc, and similar platforms maintain current market share vs. aggregators dominate local health search with massive programmatic SEO and ad spend). They build four scenarios: "Familiar Territory" (current local search, stable aggregators), "AI Concierge" (AI-powered local recommendations, stable aggregators), "Platform Power" (current local search, dominant aggregators), and "Double Shift" (AI recommendations AND dominant aggregators).

No-regret moves include: optimizing Google Business Profiles across all 8 locations with complete, accurate, and regularly updated information (photos, posts, Q&A, reviews); building location-specific landing pages with unique content per city including patient testimonials, doctor bios, and community involvement; and adding LocalBusiness and Dentist schema markup to all location pages. These score 4+ across every scenario. Hedged bet (25% of marketing budget): invest in building the practice's presence on the aggregator platforms (claim and optimize Zocdoc, HealthGrades, Yelp profiles), pursue patient reviews systematically, and create video testimonials for YouTube. This pays off especially in "Platform Power" and "Double Shift" scenarios but also helps in all scenarios by building third-party presence that AI systems reference.

Scenario-specific bet: develop a patient referral program with dedicated landing pages and email nurture sequences, triggered when direct organic traffic from local queries drops by 20% or more over two consecutive quarters. This reduces dependence on search entirely. The marketing coordinator monitors: percentage of queries where AI-generated results appear instead of traditional map packs (monthly manual check for top 15 queries), Google Business Profile impression and click trends (monthly via GBP dashboard), and referral traffic from aggregator platforms (monthly via GA4). Their yellow alert is a 10% decline in GBP clicks over a rolling 3-month period.
