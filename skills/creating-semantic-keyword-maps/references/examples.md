# Examples: Semantic Keyword Mapping for Pillar Content

## Two phrases that look different and share results

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a project software company is mapping its pillar on team capacity planning. The list includes "resource planning template" and "team capacity spreadsheet", and the writer assumes they need two pages.

**Walkthrough:**

The team compares the search results for both. Most of the top results are the same template pages, so searchers want the same thing. The two phrases go into one group with a single target page: a capacity planning template guide.

The writer uses the secondary keywords to decide what the page covers, including a downloadable sheet and instructions for adapting it. No phrase is repeated for its own sake.

## Two phrases that look alike and need different pages

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the same company has "capacity planning" and "capacity planning software" in one group because a tool matched them on shared words.

**Walkthrough:**

Reading the results shows a split. The first query returns guides and definitions. The second returns product pages and comparison lists. The team splits the group. The broad query becomes the pillar page, which explains the practice. The software query becomes a cluster page comparing approaches and tools, linked from the pillar.

The map records the intent for each group in plain words, so writers do not drift back into mixing them.

## Mapping onto an existing archive

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a marketing agency has several years of blog posts on email marketing and wants to turn them into a cluster.

**Walkthrough:**

The team exports the queries each post receives from Search Console and adds them to the keyword list before clustering. After grouping, three posts turn out to target the same group about welcome emails. The map keeps the one that ranks best as the target page, records merges and redirects for the other two, and notes which sections to bring across.

Several groups have no page at all, including one about email list cleaning. Those go into the publishing plan. The map is dated and shared with the writers and editors.
