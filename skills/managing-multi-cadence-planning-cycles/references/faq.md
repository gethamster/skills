# FAQ: GIST Planning Cadence: Managing Multi-Cadence Cycles

## What cadence does each GIST layer use?

In Itamar Gilad's original article, goals look a year or more ahead and are reviewed every quarter, ideas are collected and prioritized continuously, step-projects are chosen each quarter and reprioritized every one to two weeks, and tasks are planned in sprints and adjusted daily.

## Why not plan everything at once?

Because the layers change at different speeds. Planning all of them in one event either freezes the fast layers for too long or unsettles the slow ones. Separate cadences let the team act on new evidence quickly while keeping goals stable.

## How long should the board review take?

As short as the decisions allow. It walks the board from goals to ideas to steps, updates scores from results and plans the next steps. If it regularly runs long, the board may carry too many goals or ideas at once.

## Can goals change mid-quarter?

Only when something significant happens, such as a strategy change. Write down in advance what triggers an unscheduled goal review. Otherwise, collect the evidence and bring it to the quarterly review.

## Does the cadence work for Kanban teams?

Yes. The task layer runs on whatever flow the team uses. Kanban teams hold the board review on a fixed weekly or bi-weekly rhythm and pull tasks from the current steps continuously.

## How do stakeholders fit into the cadence?

Share the board on a regular rhythm and invite key stakeholders to the quarterly goal review. Gilad suggests stakeholders see the board regularly, even if that means sending photos weekly, so they follow progress without needing a separate roadmap.
