# Examples: GIST Planning Cadence: Managing Multi-Cadence Cycles

## Replacing a quarterly planning marathon

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team spends most of a week each quarter in planning meetings that set goals, pick features and estimate every ticket for the next three months. By the middle of the quarter the plan is out of date.

**Walkthrough:**

The team splits the marathon into GIST cadences. Goal setting stays quarterly but shrinks to one session focused only on goals. A new idea review before the quarter picks a working set for each goal. Step-projects are planned only for the first few weeks, and a board review every two weeks, just before sprint planning, decides the next steps from results.

By the end of the first quarter, planning time is spread across the weeks instead of concentrated in one, and two ideas that would have consumed half the quarter were stopped after their first steps.

## A task surprise that does not reopen a goal

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: during a sprint, an engineer finds that the data needed for a planned recommendation step is much less complete than expected.

**Walkthrough:**

The engineer raises it at standup, and the step's owner hears about it the same day. The step cannot answer its question as designed, so the owner pauses it and brings it to the next board review.

At the review, the team replaces the step with a cheaper one: interviews to check whether users want recommendations at all while the data is fixed. The idea's Confidence stays where it was, and the activation goal above it is untouched. The change stays inside the layers it affects.

## An unscheduled goal review

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: halfway through a quarter, the company changes its target market after a strategic review. One of the product team's goals was about a segment the company is no longer pursuing.

**Walkthrough:**

The team's cadence rules list a strategy change as a trigger for an unscheduled goal review. The team holds a short session, retires the old goal, and writes a new one for the new segment with a baseline and target.

The ideas linked to the retired goal are parked with a note, and the idea bank is searched for ideas that fit the new goal. The next board review picks a working set, and step-projects resume within two weeks.
