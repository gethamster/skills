# FAQ: Optimizing Moment-of-Purchase Triggers

## What counts as the moment of purchase?

It is the final step where the buyer commits, and it looks different by business. For an online store it is the cart and checkout. For a retailer it includes the shelf and the conversation with staff. For a B2B deal it includes the proposal, security review and contract. Define it explicitly so measurements stay consistent.

## Are urgency and scarcity tactics acceptable?

Only when they are true. A real delivery cutoff or genuinely limited stock is useful information for a buyer. Invented timers and fake stock counts can lift short-term conversion but damage trust, which hurts repeat purchase and word of mouth later in the journey.

## How do I know if abandonment is a problem or normal browsing?

Look at where and when buyers leave. Abandonment spread evenly across sessions, with items saved for later, usually reflects browsing and comparison. Abandonment concentrated right after a specific screen or event, such as shipping costs or account creation, points to a fixable cause.

## What if I do not have enough traffic for A/B tests?

Test larger changes, run tests longer, or compare equal periods before and after a change while tracking the reasons buyers give. In sales-led businesses, lost-deal reviews and stage timing often tell you more than conversion experiments could.

## How is this different from conversion rate optimization?

The techniques overlap, but this skill places the final step inside the whole journey. It asks what buyers bring from evaluation, and it checks whether a change helps or hurts the postpurchase phase. A lift that increases refunds or cancellations is counted as a loss.
