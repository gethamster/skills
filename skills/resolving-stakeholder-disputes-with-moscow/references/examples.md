# Examples: Resolving Stakeholder Priority Conflicts with MoSCoW

## Three departments, one timebox

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: sales, support and finance each bring requirements to the planning session for a customer portal, and each marks its own items as Must. Together the claimed Musts would fill the whole timebox.

**Walkthrough:**

The product owner shows the capacity and the claimed Must effort side by side. The gap is plain before anyone argues about a single item.

She starts with the largest item, support's "full ticket history in the portal". Split into parts, only "customers can see open tickets" passes the consequence test; history older than the current year has a workaround through the support team. Sales' "quote builder" splits the same way, with only the basic quote path passing.

Finance's "invoice download" passes all three tests: customers are contractually entitled to their invoices. After the splits the Must effort is within the agreed level. Each stakeholder keeps the part they need on the date, and the rest is recorded as Should or Could with reasons.

## A Must that depended on a Could

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a marketing lead wins agreement that "personalised welcome email" is a Must for a product launch. A developer then points out that it depends on "customer preference centre", which was placed as a Could.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator reads out the DSDM rule that a Must cannot depend on anything other than a Must. The group has three options: promote the preference centre, remove the dependency, or demote the email. Promoting the preference centre would push the Must effort past the agreed level.

The marketing lead agrees to a welcome email that uses only data collected at sign-up, which removes the dependency. The email stays a Must and the preference centre stays a Could. The decision and the dependency check are recorded on the list.

## Escalating a genuine deadlock

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the operations director and the head of product disagree about whether audit logging is a Must for a first release. Both have tested the item and reached different answers.

**Walkthrough:**

After five minutes without agreement the facilitator parks the item. The scribe writes down both positions: operations says the release is not allowed to go live without the log, and product says the log is only needed once external customers join. The escalation route agreed at the start sends it to the sponsor.

The sponsor checks with the compliance officer, who confirms the log is required from the first day of use. The item becomes a Must, and a lower item moves to Could to keep the balance. Everyone involved receives the decision and the reason the same day.
