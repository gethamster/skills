# Examples: Tracking Developer Sentiment Across Advisory Sessions

## Following one member's priorities over three calls

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a startup building a local development environment tool tracks a backend developer through her first three TAB calls.

**Walkthrough:**

In the first call, she names onboarding new engineers as her main problem and describes a recent hire who took a week to get a working setup. The entry records her words, marks the strength as "described in detail with a recent story," and notes her why-now answer: the team is growing fast.

In the second call, she agrees with the synthesis but ranks onboarding lower than before. The interviewer reads her earlier entry, asks what changed, and learns her team adopted container-based dev setups since the first call. By the third call her top problem is keeping those containers in sync with production. The record shows the shift clearly, and the monthly review finds two other members describing the same move.

## Replacing scattered notes with one record

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: three founders each run TAB calls and keep notes in their own documents. After a couple of months, nobody can answer which problems the security persona cares about.

**Walkthrough:**

They create one shared spreadsheet with member and call tabs and agree on fixed call fields. Each founder moves their existing notes into it, linking every entry to its recording. Several gaps appear: some calls have no why-now answer recorded, and a few recordings are missing.

For the next round, each founder fills the entry on the day of the call. At the monthly review, the security persona's problems are visible in one filtered view, and the founders notice that two members described the same audit problem in almost identical words.

## Closing out a member at the end of their term

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a platform engineer finishes his sixth monthly call with a startup building deployment tooling.

**Walkthrough:**

The interviewer writes a closing note: at the start, his main problem was slow rollbacks; by the end, it was approvals from the security team. His view of the startup's problem statement moved from skeptical to interested. He says he would like to hear from the team when there is something to try.

The record marks him as alumni with those notes. Some months later, when the startup has an early version addressing rollbacks and approvals, the founder reads the closing note before reaching out, and opens by referring to the approvals problem he described.
