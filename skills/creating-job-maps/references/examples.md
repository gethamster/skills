# Examples: Creating Job Maps

## Paying supplier invoices at a small business

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team building accounts payable software has the job statement "pay suppliers the correct amount on time", with the office manager as executor. Its first draft map lists the product's screens: upload invoice, approve, schedule payment.

**Walkthrough:**

The team sorts interview evidence into the eight stages. Define gets "determine which invoices are due this week". Locate gets "gather invoices and supporting documents". Prepare gets "match each invoice to what was ordered and received". Confirm gets "verify the amount and payee details". Execute gets "send the payment". Monitor gets "check that the supplier received it". Modify gets "resolve disputed or partial payments". Conclude gets "record the payment and file the documents".

Walking office managers through the draft, the team learns that checking payee bank details is a separate worry after a fraud scare, so it adds a confirm step. The first draft had nothing before upload or after scheduling, which is where most of the struggles turned out to be.

## Preparing a home-cooked weeknight meal

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a recipe app team wants to understand the job "prepare a weeknight dinner for the household" with the person who usually cooks as executor.

**Walkthrough:**

The team's first map is detailed about cooking and silent about everything else. Using the stages as prompts, it adds define ("decide what to cook given who is eating"), locate ("gather the ingredients"), prepare ("prep ingredients and equipment") and confirm ("check that everything will be ready at the same time"). After execute, monitor ("check whether dishes are done") and modify ("adjust seasoning or timing") come out of interviews, and conclude ("serve and store leftovers") completes it.

Several cooks say deciding what to cook is the hardest part of the evening. The team marks that step for dense outcome writing, since the app currently starts after that decision is made.

## Releasing a code change at a software company

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an internal platform team maps the job "release a code change to production safely" for product engineers.

**Walkthrough:**

The draft lists the pipeline stages: build, test, deploy. The team rewrites each as a goal and fills in the stages around them: determine what the change must not break, locate the owners of affected services, prepare the change for review, confirm it is safe to release, release it, monitor its effect, roll back or adjust if needed and close out the change.

Engineers point out that finding the owners of affected services is slow and often skipped. That step had no tool at all, which is why it never appeared on the platform team's backlog.
