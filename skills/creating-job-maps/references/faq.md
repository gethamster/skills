# FAQ: Creating Job Maps

## How is a job map different from a customer journey map?

A journey map follows a customer's experience with a company across touchpoints. A job map describes what the executor is trying to get done, step by step, regardless of which company or tool they use. Strategyn's whitepaper calls this a needs view as opposed to a solution view ([ODI whitepaper](https://innovationroundtable.com/summit/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Strategyn_what_is_Outcome_Driven_Innovation.pdf)). Use a journey map to improve your own experience and a job map to find unmet needs.

## Do I need all eight stages?

No. The stages are prompts to make sure you have looked before and after the obvious part of the job. Some jobs have nothing meaningful in a stage, and some stages hold several steps. Leave a stage empty only after checking that the evidence supports it.

## How many interviews do I need to build a map?

Enough that new conversations stop adding or correcting steps. A first draft can come from a handful of interviews and observation. Walking further executors through the draft is usually the fastest way to find what is missing.

## Should the map come before or after outcome statements?

Before. Outcome statements are written per step, so the map defines their structure. Writing outcomes first tends to cluster them around the steps the team already knows.

## How long does a job map stay valid?

A long time, if it is written without solutions, because the job changes much more slowly than the tools used for it. Revisit it when you study a new executor, when the market changes in a way that adds or removes steps, or when interviews keep surfacing a step that is not on the map.
