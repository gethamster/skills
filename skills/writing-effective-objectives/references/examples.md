# Examples: How to Write OKR Objectives

## A support team rewrites a maintenance goal

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a customer support team's first draft objective for the quarter is "Maintain high customer satisfaction." Satisfaction scores have been flat for a year, and the team lead wants the quarter to change something.

**Walkthrough:**

The team runs the maintenance test and the draft fails: "maintain" describes the status quo. They ask what would need to be true for customers to notice a difference, and the answer that keeps coming up is waiting time on complex tickets. The rewritten objective is "Resolve complex customer problems on first contact."

The satisfaction score stays on the team's dashboard as a KPI. The new objective leaves room for key results about first-contact resolution and reopened tickets, which the team drafts next.

## A platform team finds the benefit behind a migration

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a platform team proposes "Migrate all services to the new deployment pipeline" as its main objective. Leadership asks why a product manager outside the team should care.

**Walkthrough:**

The team applies the value test: could this score 1.0 without anyone outside the team benefiting? Yes, a complete migration could still leave release times unchanged. They ask what gets better if the migration succeeds, and the answer is that product teams could ship changes the same day without waiting for a release window.

The objective becomes "Let every product team ship to production on the day a change is ready." The migration moves into the plan underneath it, where it belongs, and one key result measures how many teams actually deploy on the same day.

## A growth team splits a double objective

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a growth team drafts "Improve activation and expand into the mid-market segment." The two halves involve different customers, different work and different people.

**Walkthrough:**

The "and" test flags the draft. The team splits it and discusses each half on its own. Activation is urgent and the team has a clear idea of what to try, so "Get new sign-ups to their first useful result on day one" is labelled committed.

Mid-market expansion is uncertain; nobody knows yet what those buyers need. Rather than invent targets, the team writes a learning objective, "Learn what mid-market buyers need before they will trial us," and plans interviews as its key results. Each objective now has a clear owner and a clear meaning of success.
