# Examples: Double Diamond vs Design Thinking

## A public service with an unclear problem

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a regional transport authority wants to "improve accessibility" of its bus network. Nobody agrees what the main barriers are, the budget is approved in stages, and the board wants a decision point before any build money is spent.

**Walkthrough:**

The team rates the problem as unclear: it has complaint data but no direct research with disabled passengers.

It maps the funding: a small research budget now, a larger delivery budget only after board approval. That lines up with the end of the first diamond.

The team chooses the Double Diamond as its backbone. It borrows design thinking methods (accompanied journeys, empathy interviews, rough prototypes of timetable formats) inside each phase.

The choice is explained to the board as "we will bring you an agreed problem, backed by evidence, before asking for delivery budget." The review checkpoint is set at the end of Define.

## A product team already shipping weekly

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a software team releases every week and has a well-researched problem: new users do not complete their first import. The question is which of several fixes works.

**Walkthrough:**

The team rates the problem as clear, backed by recent research and funnel data.

It works as a standing team with a quarterly roadmap, so there are no stage gates to align with.

It chooses Lean UX as the backbone, writing each fix as a hypothesis with a measurable outcome and running small experiments in production. It borrows the Double Diamond's idea of a problem checkpoint: if several experiments fail for the same reason, the team will stop and revisit the problem.

## A mixed team new to structured design

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small nonprofit forms its first cross-functional project team, with a designer, a developer and two programme staff. None of them has used a design framework before.

**Walkthrough:**

The team compares the frameworks on one page. Design thinking offers the most methods but the least structure. Lean UX assumes an existing product. The Double Diamond is the easiest to explain to trustees.

The team chooses the Double Diamond for its simple picture and clear outputs, and picks a few design thinking methods to learn during the project.

At the end of the first diamond, the team reviews the choice. It keeps the backbone and adds a short weekly retrospective to decide which methods to try next.
