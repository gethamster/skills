# FAQ: Organizing Squads into Tribes

## How big should a tribe be?

One later description of Spotify's structure puts tribes at roughly 100 ([source](https://stratrix.com/vault/spotify-squad-model)) people, chosen to preserve communication. Treat that as a reference point rather than a rule. The practical test is whether members can still know each other and coordinate informally. If they cannot, the tribe is too large for your context.

## Should tribes be organized by product area or by technology?

Either can work. The creators' examples include a user-facing area like the music player and a platform area like backend infrastructure. Choose the grouping that keeps squads that ship together in the same tribe. If a draft tribe produces many cross-tribe dependencies, the axis is probably wrong.

## Who coordinates work at tribe level?

The dossier sources describe coordination forums rather than a single fixed role. One case study describes product owners, chapter leads and agile coaches meeting to solve high-impact problems, and chapters within a tribe meeting regularly. Name the people and the forum explicitly, and limit the forum to problems squads cannot resolve alone.

## How often should tribe boundaries change?

Review them on a regular cadence and whenever a tribe nears its cap or strategy creates a new product area. Small, frequent adjustments are less disruptive than rare large reorganizations. Spotify itself changed its structure over time, so expect your tribe map to evolve too.

## What if a squad does not fit any tribe?

First check whether its mission is clear; an orphan squad often has a vague or split scope. If the mission is clear and still unrelated to other squads, it may belong in a small tribe of its own or signal a new product area. Avoid parking it in the nearest tribe, because that creates dependencies and dilutes the tribe's focus.
