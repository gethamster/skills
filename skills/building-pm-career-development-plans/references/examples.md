# Examples: Building PM Career Development Plans from Competencies

## A PM heading toward senior consumer work

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a mid-level PM on a consumer app wants to become a senior PM. Her assessment shows strong tactical Market and Core Product boxes and little work in the strategic Users and Market boxes.

**Walkthrough:**

Her direction is the upper-left of the chart. The gap list against the senior expectations is led by Opportunity Discovery, Market Positioning and Jobs to be Done. The team assessment also shows the strategic Users and Market region as thin, so all three boxes serve both her and the team.

Her plan names three focus boxes. For Jobs to be Done, she runs a round of customer interviews for the next major feature, reviewed by the team's research lead. For Opportunity Discovery, she writes the opportunity assessment for a new segment. For Market Positioning, she drafts the positioning for the next launch with the product marketing lead. Monthly reviews track each output.

## A PM moving toward platform leadership

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a PM on an internal data platform wants to lead the platform group. His ratings are strong in System Analysis and Data Queries and thin in Build vs Buy and Portfolio Planning.

**Walkthrough:**

His direction is the upper-right. The plan gives him ownership of a pending decision on whether to build or license a data catalog, with a senior engineer as reviewer. It also adds him to the quarterly portfolio planning discussion with a contribution to prepare.

One short book on vendor evaluation supports the build-versus-buy assignment. Nothing else is on the learning list. When the decision ships, Build vs Buy comes off the plan and Technical Partnerships goes on.

## A plan that stalls and gets fixed

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: two monthly reviews in a row show no progress on a PM's Business Case focus box.

**Walkthrough:**

The manager asks what happened. The assignment was to write a business case for a project that was later postponed, so there was no real work to do. The PM had been reading about financial modeling instead.

They swap the assignment for a smaller business case on an existing pricing question, due within the month and reviewed by someone in finance. The next review shows a draft, feedback, and a revised version.
