# FAQ: Building PM Career Development Plans from Competencies

## How many focus areas should a plan have?

A few at a time. Cagan suggests focusing on the top 3 areas and moving to the next ones after progress. More focus boxes than the PM can work on at once usually means none of them improve.

## What if the PM's goals do not match the team's needs?

Look for boxes that serve both, since those get real assignments fastest. If there is no overlap, say so plainly and agree on a balance. A plan the PM does not believe in will not be followed.

## How much should be courses and reading?

Keep formal learning small and tied to an assignment. The Center for Creative Leadership's 70-20-10 rule puts most leadership learning in challenging assignments and relationships, with coursework amplifying the rest. For a PM, that means the assignment comes first.

## How often should we review the plan?

At least monthly, which matches Cagan's advice. Short reviews that check the assignment, the feedback and the rating are enough. Replace boxes as they reach the target.

## Can this plan be used for a PM who is struggling?

It can, but keep the purpose clear. Cagan notes that the term development plan is sometimes used for performance problems, while he intends it as ongoing development for every PM. If the plan is part of a performance process, say so openly.
