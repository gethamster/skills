# Facilitating V2MOM Planning Sessions

> Run V2MOM workshops that move a leadership team or department from blank page to an agreed, ranked draft, with every voice heard before debate.

## Overview

A V2MOM can be written by one person, but at the company and department level it is usually drafted in a workshop with the people who will own it. The session has a specific job: get the group to agree on a vision, a ranked set of values, a ranked set of methods, honest obstacles and measurable results. Marc Benioff describes the V2MOM as a way to get everyone aligned on the same priorities in [his write-up of the practice](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/how-to-create-alignment-within-your-company/), and the workshop is where that alignment first forms among leaders.

These sessions fail in predictable ways. The most senior person states their view first and everyone else adjusts to it. The group spends hours on the wording of the vision and runs out of time before ranking anything. Obstacles come out sanitized because nobody wants to say the uncomfortable thing in front of the boss. The session ends with a draft nobody owns and no date for finishing it. Good facilitation is mostly about preventing these four failures.

The output of this skill is a session design and a finished workshop that leaves the group with a ranked draft V2MOM, a list of open questions with owners, and a date for the final version. The [V2MOM Framework](https://tryhamster.com/methods/v2mom-framework) method page explains the format; the other skills in this method cover how to write each section well. This one covers how to run the room.

The facilitator is ideally someone other than the V2MOM owner, so the owner can take part as a participant and still make the final call where the group splits. When that is not possible, the owner should say at the start which decisions are theirs and hold back their own view until others have spoken.

## How It Works

The session follows the order of the document: vision, then values, then methods, then obstacles, then measures. Each section builds on the one before it, so skipping ahead usually means coming back. For each section the pattern is the same: individual thinking first, then small-group comparison, then whole-group discussion and decision.

That pattern matters because group discussion rewards speed and seniority. Liberating Structures' [1-2-4-All](http://www.liberatingstructures.com/1-2-4-all) is a well-known version of it: people reflect silently, then share in pairs, then in groups of four, then with everyone, which draws out ideas from the whole group and reduces the influence of rank. Using a pattern like this for each V2MOM section gives quieter participants a real voice and produces more options before the group narrows down.

Ranking is where most of the time goes, and it should. The facilitator's job is to keep the group ranking against concrete scenarios rather than abstract preferences. "If we can only do one of these next quarter, which one?" produces a decision. "Which is more important?" produces a debate about definitions. Where the group cannot agree, the facilitator names the disagreement clearly and hands the decision to the owner, instead of letting the group settle on a tie.

Obstacles need a different kind of safety. A premortem, described by Gary Klein in [Performing a Project Premortem](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem), asks people to assume the plan has failed and list why. The framing makes it easier to raise concerns, including concerns about leadership's own assumptions. Running it as silent individual writing first increases the effect.

Timeboxing keeps the session from stalling on wording. The facilitator agrees in advance that the session produces a draft and that wordsmithing happens afterward, by one person. The group agrees on substance and order in the room. Polished language comes later.

The session ends by recording decisions, open questions and owners. A parking lot collects issues that matter but belong elsewhere. Every open question gets an owner and a date, and the V2MOM owner commits to a date for the final version.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Prepare the inputs

Confirm who owns the V2MOM and who will attend, keeping the group small enough for real discussion. Send the parent V2MOM, last period's V2MOM if one exists and relevant data a few days ahead. Ask participants to come with a draft vision sentence and their top concerns written down. Set up the shared template so everyone works in the same document.

### Step 2: Open with the rules of the session

Explain the order of sections, the timebox for each and the thinking pattern the group will use. State who makes the final call where the group cannot agree. Agree that the session produces a draft and that wording will be polished afterward. Introduce the parking lot for issues that belong elsewhere.

### Step 3: Converge on the vision

Have each person write a vision sentence silently, then compare in pairs and small groups before sharing with the room. Look for the destination most drafts share and combine the strongest elements. Test the result: could the group tell at year end whether it came true? Move on once the substance is agreed, even if the wording is rough.

### Step 4: Rank the values

Collect candidate values individually, merge duplicates and drop ones that could never conflict with anything. Rank them by comparing pairs against real past decisions. When the group splits, name the disagreement and let the owner decide. Write a behavior sentence for at least the top two values before moving on.

### Step 5: List and rank the methods

Brainstorm methods individually, then group them and rewrite activities as initiatives. Rank them by contribution to the vision, using the ranked values to break ties. Name an owner for each method in the room. Move lower-ranked items to a visible "not this period" list.

### Step 6: Run a premortem for obstacles

Ask everyone to imagine the period is over and the plan has failed, and to write reasons silently. Collect the reasons, ask the most senior people to share last, and group them by method. Rank them roughly by likelihood and impact and assign owners to the top few. Put anything outside the V2MOM's scope in the parking lot.

### Step 7: Draft the measures and close

For each top method, agree on an outcome measure and who will find the baseline. Do not set precise targets without data in the room; assign someone to propose them. Review the parking lot, give every open question an owner and a date, and confirm when the final version will be published. Send the draft out within a day while the reasoning is fresh.

## Best Practices

- Keep individual writing before group discussion in every section. It gathers more options and reduces anchoring on the most senior voice.
- Separate substance from wording. Agree on meaning and order in the room, and let one person polish the language afterward.
- Rank against scenarios drawn from the organization's recent history. Real cases turn abstract debates into decisions.
- Make the decision rule explicit at the start. People accept a call they disagree with more easily when they knew in advance who would make it.
- Close with owners and dates for every open item. A session that ends without them tends to produce a draft that never gets finished.

## Common Mistakes

- **Letting the leader speak first**: the group converges on the first strong view it hears. Ask senior people to share last, or use silent writing so everyone commits before hearing it.
- **Wordsmithing the vision for hours**: the group runs out of time before ranking anything. Agree on the substance and hand the wording to one person.
- **Accepting a tie in the rankings**: ties defeat the purpose of ranking. Name the disagreement and let the owner decide.
- **Skipping or softening obstacles**: a quick "any risks?" gets polite answers. Use a premortem with silent writing to get the real ones.
- **Ending without next steps**: without owners and dates, the draft stalls. Close by assigning every open question and setting a publish date.
