# FAQ: Writing Benefit-Driven Headlines That Convert

## What is the difference between a feature headline and a benefit headline?

A feature headline names what the product is or does, such as a category or a capability. A benefit headline names what changes for the reader, such as a task that gets easier or a problem that stops happening. Both can work. Benefit headlines usually give new visitors a reason to read on, while feature headlines can win when the reader is already searching for that category.

## How long should a headline be?

Long enough to state the benefit clearly and no longer. Put the most informative words first, because many readers only take in the start of a line. If the line keeps growing, move the detail into the subhead.

## Should I use headline formulas?

Formulas are useful prompts for generating options, such as an outcome without a pain or a question about the main problem. They are not a guarantee. Fill them with your customers' words and filter the results for clarity, and discard any formula result that sounds like a template.

## Do benefit-driven headlines work for technical audiences?

They work when the benefit is specific and credible. Technical readers tend to distrust broad promises and look for precise capabilities. For those audiences, a headline that names the capability and the concrete result together often reads best.

## How many headline options should I write?

Write enough that the filter has real choices, usually well over a handful, and then cut hard. Keep the two or three strongest with a note on why each works. Those become your test queue.

## How do I improve headlines with no customer research?

Start by gathering some. Reviews of competing products and a few short conversations with buyers will give you phrases quickly. Until then, write the plainest accurate headline you can and treat anything more ambitious as a hypothesis to test.
