# Examples: Synthesizing User Insights

## Missed clinic appointments

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a clinic team interviews patients and front-desk staff to understand why many booked appointments go unattended, assuming the cause is forgetfulness.

**Walkthrough:**

The team writes one observation per note and separates evidence from interpretation, moving the forgetfulness assumption to the interpretation column. Clustering reveals a pattern the team did not expect: several patients knew about the appointment but could not reach anyone to reschedule, so they simply stayed away. A journey map shows the pain concentrated at the rescheduling step, not the reminder step. Asking what would make skipping sensible, the team proposes that patients see a missed appointment as less awkward than a hard-to-complete phone call.

The insight reads: patients who need to change plans avoid the clinic rather than face an unreliable rescheduling process, so reminders alone will not help. The team notes it can check this by asking recent no-show patients whether they tried to reschedule.

## Finance staff and spreadsheets

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team studying an expense tool notices that finance staff export nearly every report into spreadsheets, and leadership reads this as a sign the tool's reports are poor.

**Walkthrough:**

Observations show staff adding comments, color codes and extra columns to the exported files. The team clusters these under a user-voice label: I need to explain my decisions to the auditor. Two competing explanations are written, one that staff distrust the tool's numbers and one that they need a place to annotate reasoning. The evidence supports annotation far better, since no participant questioned the figures themselves.

The resulting insight states that finance staff export not to fix the data but to attach justifications the tool cannot hold. It is handed to problem framing with links to the supporting notes.

## Reading app with analytics and interviews

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a library app team has usage analytics showing that many users abandon the holds feature, plus a handful of interviews with active borrowers.

**Walkthrough:**

The team treats the analytics as a signal of how widespread the drop-off is, then turns to interviews to explain it. Clustering interview notes surfaces a pattern of borrowers placing holds in person because they want to know when a book will realistically arrive. A two-by-two of urgency against flexibility shows two distinct groups with different needs, which a single funnel view had blurred together. The team writes an insight for the urgent group: borrowers with a deadline abandon holds when they cannot see an expected wait.

They flag it as provisional because interviews covered few urgent borrowers, and add a follow-up question to the next research round.
