# FAQ: Synthesizing User Insights

## What is the difference between a finding and an insight?

A finding reports what happened, such as people exporting reports or skipping a step. An insight explains why it happens in a way that is not obvious from the data and that changes what the team would do. Findings are the evidence underneath insights. If a teammate who skipped the research would nod rather than be surprised, you probably have a finding.

## How much research do I need before synthesizing?

Enough that patterns start to repeat across participants rather than appearing once. There is no fixed count, and a sensible approach is to synthesize after each small batch of interviews, for example a handful at a time, so you can adjust later sessions. If every cluster rests on a single person, gather more data before drawing conclusions.

## Should one researcher synthesize alone or should the team do it together?

Doing it together is usually better, because shared synthesis builds shared understanding and exposes individual blind spots. A lone researcher can prepare by unpacking notes onto the wall beforehand to save group time. Include at least one person who was not in the field, since they question assumptions that interviewers take for granted.

## What does abductive reasoning look like in practice?

It starts with a surprising pattern and asks what would make that behavior sensible. The team writes several candidate explanations, then checks each against the evidence to see which accounts for the most observations with the fewest extra assumptions. The chosen explanation becomes a hypothesis, not a conclusion, and later testing decides whether it holds.

## How do I know my synthesis is good?

Each insight should trace to specific observations, surprise someone who was not in the research, and imply a decision or a test. The problem statement written from it should name a specific user and need rather than a generic customer. If later prototype tests keep contradicting your insights, revisit the evidence wall and check whether interpretations slipped in as evidence.
