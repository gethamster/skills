# Examples: Customer Journey Content Strategy by Stage

## A blog that drew traffic and no trials

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small HR software company publishes a steady stream of articles about hiring. Traffic grows, but trial signups from content stay flat.

**Walkthrough:**

The content audit shows that nearly every article is awareness content and nearly every one ends with a demo request. Consideration questions from sales calls, such as how the tool compares with a spreadsheet and how long setup takes, have no content at all. The team changes the call to action on the top articles to point to a new comparison page and a setup guide, and briefs both as the first consideration assets.

The awareness articles are now measured by engaged reading and clicks to the comparison page rather than by demos. That removes the pressure to add sales language to them.

## Retention content hiding in the help center

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a design tool's marketing team believes it has no retention content. The support team maintains a large help center that marketing has never audited.

**Walkthrough:**

Tagging the help center by stage shows that it is almost all retention content, but it answers "how do I fix this" and rarely "how do I do more". The team adds a short series of in-product tips for features that retained customers use and newer customers do not. Each tip ends with a link to a longer help article.

The lifecycle email owner takes over the series, and the tips are measured by adoption of the featured capability. The help center gets a named owner for the first time.

## Giving advocates something to share

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a consulting firm has many satisfied clients who say they would recommend it, but referrals are rare.

**Walkthrough:**

The audit finds no advocacy content: no short case summaries, no one-page overview a client could forward. The team writes a one-page summary for each service line in plain language, using phrases clients used in feedback. Account managers send it after a successful project with a note that it is there if a colleague asks. The firm tracks forwards and referral mentions in the CRM to see whether the asset gets used.
