# FAQ: Balancing Squad Autonomy with Organizational Alignment

## Is autonomy the opposite of alignment?

No. Kniberg's matrix treats them as two separate dimensions, so an organization can raise both. Alignment without autonomy tends toward micromanagement, and autonomy without alignment lets squads drift in different directions. The aim is clear direction from leaders and freedom for squads in how to meet it.

## Do we need OKRs to align squads?

No particular format is required. Spotify itself tried OKRs and another model before moving to ranked company bets on shared boards. Whatever the format, keep priorities few, ranked, visible and connected from company to squad level.

## How many constraints should squads have?

As few as protect real shared risks, such as security, privacy and key interfaces. Each constraint should have a written reason and an owner who can change it. If squads regularly work around a rule, review whether it still earns its place.

## How do we tell whether alignment is working?

Ask squads. Health checks that cover mission clarity and whether squads feel like pawns or players show problems early. Also watch for duplicated work, colliding changes and a growing number of coordination meetings, which all suggest direction or boundaries are unclear.

## What should squads standardize if they choose their own process?

Keep internal process free and standardize how squads work together: how to ask another squad for something, how to raise a dependency, and how joint projects are coordinated. Critics of the original model point to the lack of such shared practice as a main source of friction at Spotify.
