# FAQ: Balancing Squad Autonomy with Organizational Alignment

## What is the difference between alignment and control?

Alignment shapes what squads aim for and which limits they respect, while control decides individual choices on their behalf. Shared outcomes and testable guardrails are alignment, because the squad still decides how to meet them. Approval of each design or release is control, because the decision moves out of the squad. Most teams need some of both, but the balance should lean heavily toward alignment.

## How many approval gates should an autonomous squad have?

There is no figure in the source material, so treat any number as a local choice. A useful rule is that every gate should protect against a named, serious risk that no guardrail already covers. If you cannot state the risk in one sentence, the gate is probably approval creep. Review the list on a fixed cadence and expect it to shrink as guardrails mature.

## Who should own alignment in a Spotify-style organization?

Tribe leadership usually owns shared direction, because the tribe groups related squads while leaving delivery to them. Chapters own technical consistency within a discipline, and guilds spread knowledge voluntarily across the organization. Squads own how they deliver against both. Problems start when one role, often a manager or chapter lead, quietly takes on all three.

## How do I know if squads have too much autonomy?

Watch for squads shipping features that contradict each other, duplicating work other squads already did, or being unable to explain how their work serves a shared outcome. These are signs that direction is missing or unclear, not that autonomy itself is wrong. The fix is usually sharper tribe outcomes and clearer mission boundaries rather than new approval steps. Adding reviews treats the symptom and slows everyone down.

## Does the original Spotify material explain how to create alignment?

Only in outline. For example, the 2012 report describes the structures, including tribes, chapters and guilds, but gives little detail on the mechanics of alignment between squads. One academic study noted there were no guidelines for building and maintaining that alignment. You will need to design your own mechanisms and adjust them as you learn what works.
