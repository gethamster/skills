# FAQ: Framing Human-Centered Problems

## What is the difference between a POV statement and a How Might We question?

The POV is the frame: it names the user, the need, the insight and what would be game-changing. How Might We questions are prompts derived from the POV to launch brainstorming. You usually write several prompts from one POV, each opening a different angle. Writing prompts before the POV is settled tends to produce questions that drift from the user.

## How many problem statements should a team write?

Write as many as there are genuinely distinct users or insights worth pursuing, then choose where to start. Many teams draft a few candidates and compare them with a quick smoke test. Writing only one early can lock in a weak frame. Writing too many spreads ideation thin, so pick one or two to take forward.

## Can a problem statement change after prototyping and testing?

Yes, and it often should. Test results frequently reveal that the insight was wrong or that the real need sits elsewhere. When that happens, revise the relevant component instead of patching the wording. Keeping earlier drafts makes these rewrites faster.

## How do I know if my insight is strong enough?

A strong insight changes how the team sees the problem and rests on something users said or did. Ask what the team believed before research that this insight contradicts. If nobody can name anything, the insight is likely restating common knowledge. Also check that it explains why the need is unmet, not just that it exists.

## Should the problem statement include business goals?

Keep the POV focused on the user so ideation starts from their situation. Business and technical constraints matter, but they are better applied when evaluating ideas later. Folding them into the frame too early tends to rule out options before anyone has explored them.
