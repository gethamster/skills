# Examples: Facilitating Participatory Ideation

## Redesigning a benefits application with applicants

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a city team wants to reduce abandoned benefits applications and has research showing applicants give up when asked for documents they do not have on hand.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator frames the prompt "How might we help applicants finish even when they are missing a document?" and posts applicant quotes beside it. The room includes applicants, caseworkers and a policy lead, each with the same notes and markers. Silent generation produces ideas ranging from save-and-return links to caseworkers accepting photos from a phone. During clustering, caseworkers worry that photo uploads create fraud risk, while applicants say travel to an office is the real blocker.

The facilitator writes the tension down and converts it into a question: would photos plus a later verification step satisfy both needs? The group selects three concepts, including the photo idea, for paper prototypes the following week.

## Improving discharge instructions on a hospital ward

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a ward team learns from interviews that patients forget their medication schedule within days of going home.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator invites former patients, family caregivers, nurses and a pharmacist. A pre-brief tells the ward manager to contribute ideas but vote last. Silent writing surfaces ideas nurses had not considered, such as caregivers receiving the instructions directly. When the pharmacist objects that simplified schedules could omit safety warnings, the facilitator asks which patient need each position serves and records both as design constraints.

Patient votes are marked in a different color and cluster around caregiver involvement, while staff votes favor a redesigned printed sheet. The team keeps both concepts alive and plans to test them side by side.

## Remote ideation for an internal tool

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team is replacing a scheduling tool used by field technicians spread across several regions.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator runs the session on a shared digital board, with one area per prompt and technician quotes pinned to each. Access is tested with every participant the day before, since technicians join from vans on tablets. Silent generation happens in private areas of the board, then everyone moves notes into the shared space at once. A conflict appears between technicians wanting schedule flexibility and dispatchers wanting predictability, and the facilitator frames it as a question for a prototype test.

Voting uses criteria agreed at the start: fit with the technician need and ease of testing. The summary shared afterward credits which ideas came from technicians and invites them to the first test.
