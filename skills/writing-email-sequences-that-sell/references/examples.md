# Examples: Email Copywriting: Writing Sequences That Drive Action

## A trial welcome sequence with one job per email

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a survey tool's trial users receive one long welcome email listing every feature, followed by silence until the trial ends. Few trial users create a second survey.

**Walkthrough:**

The writer sets the sequence goal as sending a first survey to real respondents, the step that research links to upgrades. The trigger is trial start, and the exit is either sending a survey or the trial ending.

The plan has four emails. The first delivers login details and one link to a template for the use case the user chose at sign-up. The second, sent after two days to users who have not sent a survey, addresses the most common worry from support tickets, that respondents will not answer. The third shows how to read results. The fourth, near the end of the trial, explains what happens when the trial ends and links to plans. Each has one button that names its step, such as "Open the customer feedback template."

## Rewriting a single sales email

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B analytics company sends a nurture email with the subject line "Exciting news from our team," a paragraph about a funding round, three feature announcements and four links.

**Walkthrough:**

The writer asks what one job this email should do for the reader. Sales notes show that prospects in this segment keep asking whether the product connects to their data warehouse, and a new connector just shipped.

The rewrite has one job: get prospects who use that warehouse to see the connector. The subject line names the warehouse and the benefit plainly. The body opens with the problem as prospects describe it, explains in two short paragraphs what the connector does, and ends with one button, "See the connector setup." The funding news and the other features move to separate emails or the newsletter.

## A re-engagement sequence that respects the exit

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a recipe newsletter has many subscribers who have not opened an email in months. The team wants to win some back and stop mailing the rest.

**Walkthrough:**

The writer plans three emails with a clear exit: anyone who clicks stays on the main list, and anyone who does not is removed after the last email. The first email offers the most-saved recipes of the season with a subject line that says exactly that. The second asks one question, what kind of recipes they want, with a few one-click answers.

The third says plainly that the subscriber will stop receiving emails unless they click to stay, with the button "Keep sending me recipes." Every email includes the postal address and a working unsubscribe link, and the removals happen as promised.
