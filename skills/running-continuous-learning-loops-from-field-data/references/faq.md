# FAQ: Continuous Learning Loops from Field Deployments

## What should go into a field log entry?

Date, customer, what happened, whether you saw it or were told about it, evidence, a type and a scope tag, and a status. Evidence is the most important field, because the product team was not there. Keep entries short enough to write on the day. A rough entry written immediately is better than a polished one written weeks later.

## How often should field learnings reach the product team?

On a fixed rhythm that both sides can keep, such as a short weekly or fortnightly review with a named product counterpart. OpenAI's FDE team, for example, pairs regular sessions with research and readouts with product leadership with an always-open field notes channel. Urgent items, such as a blocking bug, should go through a faster path. The rhythm matters more than the exact interval.

## How do I tell a one-off request from a recurring need?

Search the shared log for similar entries at other customers. One customer asking is a data point. Several customers showing the same behavior, especially unprompted, is a pattern. Until the evidence is there, tag the entry as "possibly recurring" and revisit it.

## What if the product team never acts on field learnings?

First check the quality of what you bring: grouped patterns with evidence and customer names get more traction than raw requests. Then agree a response for every item, even if it is "declined, because...". If nothing changes, raise it with leadership, because the method page lists the absence of a path from field to roadmap as a reason the FDE model breaks down.

## Does this apply to people who are not FDEs?

Yes. Solutions engineers, customer success engineers and analytics engineers on customer deployments see the same kind of field evidence. The log, the rhythm and the closed loop work the same way. A shared log across all customer-facing roles gives the product team the widest view.
