# FAQ: Ecosystem Journey Framework Levels: L0 to L3 Hierarchy

## What do L0, L1, L2, and L3 mean in a journey framework?

They are zoom levels from the broadest view to the most detailed. In the convention this method uses, L0 is the ecosystem view of journeys, teams, partners, and systems, L1 is the customer lifecycle, L2 holds specific journeys toward a customer goal, and L3 holds detailed interactions within those journeys. Other sources define the levels differently, so write down one set of definitions for your organization and use it consistently.

## Do we need all four levels?

No. Many organizations start with three levels, and TheyDo's own help center says most frameworks start with three. Add a fourth only if people need a view the other levels do not provide. Within any branch, add the finest level only where a decision needs it.

## How do I tell whether a journey is L2 or L3?

Ask whether it is a complete attempt at a customer goal, with its own trigger and outcome, or one interaction inside such an attempt. A complete attempt is L2. A single interaction, such as filling in a form or resetting a password, is L3 and should attach to a step of its parent L2 journey. If one team could own it end to end and it has a clear start and finish, it is probably L2.

## Should different customer segments get separate hierarchies?

Usually no. Keep one hierarchy and use segment tags, and split a journey into segment-specific versions only when the experiences differ fundamentally. Smaply suggests one to three lifecycle maps, one per major segment or product line, as a typical range. Separate hierarchies make cross-segment comparison harder.

## How often should the level definitions change?

Rarely. Review them after the first few months of use, when real classification questions reveal gaps, and then only when the business changes shape, such as adding a new product line. Frequent changes break comparisons and confuse owners. Changes to which journeys sit where are normal and happen much more often than changes to the definitions.
