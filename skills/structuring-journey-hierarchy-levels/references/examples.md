# Examples: Ecosystem Journey Framework Levels: L0 to L3 Hierarchy

## An insurer settles what "claims" means

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an insurer's leadership talks about "the claims journey," its digital team talks about "the claims journey," and each means something different. The CX team is asked to build a hierarchy that ends the confusion.

**Walkthrough:**

The team writes level definitions first. L1 becomes the customer lifecycle, with a stage called "Get help when something goes wrong." Under it, L2 journeys include "Make a car claim" and "Make a home claim," each with a trigger (the incident) and an outcome (payment or a decision).

The digital team's "claims journey" turns out to be an L3 interaction, "Upload photos of the damage," attached to one step of each L2 claim journey. Once the hierarchy is published, leadership reviews the L2 journeys and the digital team owns the L3 upload flow, and both groups use the same names.

## A software company removes an overgrown lifecycle

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a software company's lifecycle diagram has grown to include stages like "Password reset" and "Invoice download" alongside "Evaluate" and "Adopt." Nobody uses it any more.

**Walkthrough:**

The team applies the written tests to each stage. Most of the added stages fail the L1 test because each describes a single interaction inside a phase of the relationship. They move down to L3 entries under the relevant L2 journeys.

The cleaned-up lifecycle fits on one screen again, and each stage now has a small set of L2 journeys under it. The team records a rule that new L1 stages need approval from the lifecycle owner.

## A bank chooses where to start its numbering

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a bank's CX team reads several outside sources and finds that some start the hierarchy at L0 and others at L1. Two internal teams have already adopted different conventions.

**Walkthrough:**

The team decides that L0 will be an ecosystem view of the bank's journeys, partners, and systems, and that the customer lifecycle will be L1. They write a short note mapping each internal team's old labels to the new ones.

Existing maps are relabelled in one pass, and the note is attached to the published hierarchy. When new staff read outside articles that number levels differently, they check the note instead of debating which convention is right.
