# FAQ: Using Working Backwards in PM Interviews

## Does Amazon ask product sense questions?

A co-author of the book Working Backwards, writing on the Working Backwards site, describes Amazon interviews as behavioral and built on the Leadership Principles, and contrasts that with hypothetical questions such as how would you improve a product. Prepare stories from your own work first. The customer-first structure still helps if a hypothetical question does come up.

## How long should I spend on the customer and problem?

Long enough to settle them clearly, which is usually a short part of the answer but a deliberate one. The mistake is skipping them. If the interviewer pushes you toward the solution, give a one-sentence summary of customer and problem and move on.

## Should I actually write a press release in the interview?

Usually not in full, but framing your solution as a headline and a one-sentence subheading is a quick way to state it clearly. It also gives the interviewer a crisp summary to react to.

## How is this different from other PM interview frameworks?

Many PM interview frameworks also start from the customer. What Working Backwards adds is the PR/FAQ's pressure test: asking why customers would switch, what the hard problem is, and what would make the product fail, before the interviewer does.

## How should I practice?

Take a few product questions and answer them out loud with a partner, recording yourself. For behavioral questions, write out stories for the Leadership Principles, with measurable results, and practice telling each in one to two minutes. Using the company's products before the interview also gives you real problems to reason from.
