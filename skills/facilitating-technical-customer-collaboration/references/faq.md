# FAQ: Facilitating Technical Customer Collaboration

## How much time should a forward deployed engineer spend with the customer?

There is no standard figure. [IIT's overview of the role](https://iit.edu/blog/forward-deployed-engineer) reports Palantir historically expecting around 25 percent of an FDE's time with customers, while Commure has estimated up to 50 percent ([source](https://iit.edu/blog/forward-deployed-engineer)). Set the number per engagement based on how ambiguous the problem is and how low trust starts, and front-load presence early.

## What is the trust and validation gap?

It is the distance between a system that works technically and one the customer believes is correct enough to rely on. One practitioner quoted in [the forward-deployed buyer's field guide](https://magmainc.ca/guides/forward-deployed-engineering-model) estimates it consumes seventy-five percent or more of an engagement, though that is an individual estimate. Closing it depends on joint validation with the customer's experts and visible responsiveness to their corrections.

## Should I attend the customer's standups even if I have little to report?

Yes. The main value is hearing their problems, priorities and vocabulary firsthand, not delivering updates. [Alephic's description of embedded engineering](https://alephic.com/forward-deployed-engineering) includes attending standups as part of embedding. Keep your own contributions short and use the time to listen.

## How do I handle stakeholders who want conflicting things?

Do not try to satisfy both quietly, because each group will assume its preference won. Bring the trade-off to a check-in with the sponsor, explain the consequences of each option and ask for an explicit decision. Record it in writing and share it with everyone affected the same day.

## What makes someone good at this skill?

[Perspective AI's summary of Palantir's playbook](https://getperspective.ai/blog/palantir-forward-deployed-engineering-playbook-anthropic-openai-copying) describes the ideal as an engineer-diplomat. In practice that means someone who can build credibly while listening patiently, admit when the system is wrong, and deliver unwelcome trade-offs to senior stakeholders without losing the room. Technical skill earns the first meeting, but these habits earn continued access.
