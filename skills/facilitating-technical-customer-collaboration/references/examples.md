# Examples: Facilitating Technical Customer Collaboration

## Winning over a skeptical operations lead

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an engineer is embedded with a logistics team whose operations lead has seen two previous tools fail and openly doubts the new system's routing suggestions.

**Walkthrough:**

The engineer joins the lead's daily standup for two weeks and mostly listens, noting the three routing exceptions that come up every day. Instead of defending the system, the engineer asks the lead to pick twenty past shipments where the right answer is known. They run the system on those cases side by side, and it gets several wrong because of an unwritten rule about weekend depots. The engineer encodes the rule and reruns the set with the lead watching.

The lead then starts forwarding new exception cases directly, which the engineer treats as the sign that trust has turned.

## Resolving a sponsor and user conflict

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: at a claims-processing customer, the sponsor wants the system to auto-close simple claims, while frontline adjusters want it only to suggest decisions they approve.

**Walkthrough:**

The engineer hears both positions separately in working sessions and realizes each group assumes its version is the plan. At the next alignment check-in, the engineer lays out both options with their risks and asks the sponsor to decide. The sponsor chooses suggestions first, with auto-close for one narrow claim type after a review period. The engineer sends a short written note of the decision to the sponsor and adjuster leads that afternoon.

When an adjuster later objects to auto-close, the note settles the question in minutes.

## Adjusting on-site time mid-engagement

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an engineer planned to spend two days a week at a hospital customer, but after a month users are still routing requests through their department head.

**Walkthrough:**

The engineer reads the indirect routing as a sign that trust with users has not formed. They agree with their manager and the customer sponsor to increase presence to four days a week for three weeks, pairing with nurses on real shifts. Users begin raising issues directly during those sessions, and demo feedback turns from polite approval into specific corrections. Once validation disputes stop recurring, the engineer steps back to two days, as agreed in advance.

The explicit plan meant neither side was surprised by the change.
