# FAQ: Planned Journey Framework for B2B Buying Committees

## Does the planned journey framework apply to B2B?

Partly. B2B purchases are infrequent and heavily researched, which fits the planned journey. SKIM, which published the framework, suggests a separate ecosystem journey for B2B decisions with many stakeholders. This skill combines the two: each stakeholder role gets its own path through the latent, evaluation and buying stages.

## What is a buying committee?

It is the group of people in an organization involved in a purchase, also called the buying center or decision-making unit. Members take different roles, such as user, technical reviewer, budget holder and procurement. The roles matter more for the journey than job titles.

## What are the B2B buying process stages in this model?

The same three stages as the consumer version, applied per role: latent, when a role is not looking; evaluation, when it researches and compares; and buying, when it takes part in the final choice. The B2B version adds consensus points where the committee must agree to move forward.

## Why do B2B deals stall?

Often because roles are out of step. Users may be ready while the budget holder is still latent, or a technical reviewer may join late with new requirements. Consensus points where these roles must agree are where stalls happen most, which is why the map marks them.

## How do I find roles that are not in the CRM?

Interview your sales staff about who influenced recent deals, and interview buyers about who was involved on their side. Late-appearing roles, such as security, legal or facilities, often decide deals without appearing as contacts.

## How should marketing handle the long latent stage?

Treat it as the normal state of most accounts. Build familiarity with the roles that will lead the next evaluation, and watch for organizational triggers such as renewals, budget cycles and incidents that move the whole committee into evaluation.
