# Examples: Planned Journey Framework for B2B Buying Committees

## A security review that arrives too late

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a software vendor selling to mid-sized companies loses several deals late in the cycle. It reviews recent won and lost deals and interviews its sales team and a few buyers.

**Walkthrough:**

The role list shows users, a team lead, an IT security reviewer, a finance approver and procurement. The multi-lane map shows users and the team lead deep in evaluation for weeks while the security reviewer is still latent. The reviewer enters at the buying stage with a long questionnaire, and in lost deals the questionnaire stalls the purchase until the budget window closes.

The vendor marks the security review as a consensus point and moves its security documentation into the evaluation stage, offering it to the team lead to share early. Sales starts asking about security review requirements in the first meeting.

## A renewal as an organizational trigger

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a payroll services provider wants to win clients from competitors. Most target accounts are latent, locked into multi-year contracts.

**Walkthrough:**

Interviews show the committee leaves latent together when the incumbent's contract comes up for renewal, and that the finance lead and HR lead drive the evaluation, with IT joining late. Buyers say they shortlist the providers they already know when the renewal notice arrives.

The provider maps renewal timing as the main organizational trigger and focuses latent-stage effort on being known to finance and HR leads well before renewals. It also prepares integration material for IT, which usually joins late in evaluation.

## A committee split at the shortlist

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a laboratory equipment maker sells to research institutes. Deals often stall after a successful demo, and sales staff cannot say why.

**Walkthrough:**

Interviews with members of lost buying committees show that scientists prefer the maker's instrument, while the facilities manager, who is responsible for installation and servicing, prefers a rival with a local service team. The shortlist decision is a consensus point, and the maker had never engaged facilities.

The map adds a facilities lane, and the maker prepares service and installation material for that role. Sales begins identifying the facilities contact in the evaluation stage, and the team adds the shortlist decision to its win-loss questions to track whether the change helps.
