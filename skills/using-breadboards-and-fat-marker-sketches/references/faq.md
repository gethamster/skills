# FAQ: Breadboarding Product Design with Fat Marker Sketches

## What is a breadboard in Shape Up?

A breadboard is a UI concept that defines affordances and their connections without visual styling. It borrows the name from electrical engineering, where a breadboard has all the components and wiring of a device but no industrial design. In Shape Up, it is drawn with three things: places, affordances and connection lines, all written in words.

## What is a fat marker sketch?

A sketch of a UI concept drawn with a line so thick that adding detail is difficult or impossible. Basecamp originally used large markers on paper and later iPads with a large pen size. It is for ideas where the visual arrangement is the problem.

## When should I use a breadboard instead of a fat marker sketch?

Use a breadboard when the hard part is the flow: which places exist, what the user can do there and where it leads. Use a fat marker sketch when the hard part is how elements are arranged on a screen. Many pitches combine a breadboard for the flow with one fat marker sketch for the key screen.

## Why not just draw wireframes?

Wireframes commit to layout and detail too early. The book says they leave designers no room, hide complexity that makes estimates wrong, and bias whoever works on the design next, especially when they come from someone senior. Rough sketches let the shaper explore more options quickly and keep those decisions open.

## Can I use a digital tool?

Yes. The technique matters more than the medium. Pick a tool that makes fine detail hard, such as a thick pen size on a tablet or a whiteboard, and use plain text or simple boxes and arrows for breadboards.

## Do breadboards help during the build as well?

Yes. The book notes that a designer's first working interface can look more like a breadboard than a polished mock-up, with just enough affordances to wire up and test. Ryan Singer's recent case study also uses the shaped breadboard at kickoff to find the vertical slices the team will build.
