# FAQ: Planning a Market Development Strategy for New Segments

## What is the difference between market development and market penetration?

Market penetration sells more of your product to the market you already serve. Market development takes the same product to a market you do not serve yet, such as a new region, industry, customer size or use. The difference matters because a new market needs new research, positioning and often new channels, while penetration works with what you already know.

## Does the product have to stay exactly the same?

No. Ansoff's definition expects the product to be adapted, generally with some modification, to the new market. Typical changes include language, compliance features, integrations, packaging and pricing. If the product needs a new core capability, the move is closer to product development or diversification.

## Which segmentation bases should I use?

Use the bases that explain why customers buy your product. Business products are often segmented by industry, company size and region, and consumer products by geography, demographics, lifestyle and behavior. Try more than one base, since a segment that looks weak on one base can look strong on another.

## How many new markets should we enter at once?

Usually one at a time for a small or mid-sized team. Each new segment needs its own learning about buyers, channels and messaging, and splitting effort slows all of them. Move to the next segment once the first shows a repeatable way to win customers.

## When should a market development initiative be stopped?

When it misses the stop conditions agreed in the business case, such as no repeatable sales motion by a set date or acquisition costs well above what the segment can support. Write these conditions before launch and review them on schedule. Stopping one segment frees resources for the next candidate on the shortlist.
