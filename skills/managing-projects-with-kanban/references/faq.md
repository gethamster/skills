# FAQ: Managing Projects with Kanban

## How do we handle deadlines without sprints?

Give items with a real deadline a fixed-date class of service and pull them early enough to finish in time, using your cycle time history as a guide. For the project as a whole, forecast completion from throughput and compare the range with the deadline. If they do not match, decide early whether to cut scope or move the date.

## How long does it take to set up kanban project management?

A board, initial WIP limits and basic pull policies can be ready in a day. Reliable forecasts take longer, because you need a few weeks of throughput data. Until then, share a rough estimate and say when a data-based forecast will be available.

## How do we manage dependencies between items?

Show them on the card and use a visible blocked marker when an item is waiting on another team or item. Order options so that items others depend on are pulled early. Track how long items stay blocked, which shows how much the dependencies cost the project.

## What if team members split their time across several projects?

Put all of their work on the board, or use a per-person WIP limit that covers every project they serve. Otherwise the board shows capacity that does not exist. If people are split across many projects, that fragmentation is often the biggest cause of delay and worth raising with leadership.

## Can we run a kanban project alongside Scrum?

Yes. A Scrum team can use WIP limits, pull policies and throughput forecasting inside its Sprints. The Kanban Guide describes Kanban as something that can augment other delivery approaches. Write down which rules apply so the team is not following two systems at once.
