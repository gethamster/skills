# Examples: Managing Projects with Kanban

## A small team launches a feature

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a four-person team must launch a customer onboarding flow. The sponsor wants a launch date on the first day, and the scope is still fuzzy.

**Walkthrough:**

The team breaks the goal into about thirty small items and puts them in Options, ordering the riskiest ones, such as an integration with the identity provider, near the top. The board has Committed, Building, Review, Testing and Released, with WIP limits on the three middle columns. They tell the sponsor they will give a forecast after three weeks of data.

After three weeks, the team has finished between four and seven items each week. With about twenty items left, they forecast a range of three to five more weeks and show the sponsor which low-value options could be dropped to hit the earlier end. The sponsor cuts four items, and the team updates the forecast weekly until launch.

## A cross-functional marketing campaign

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a campaign project involves copywriters, designers, a web developer and a legal reviewer. Past campaigns ran late because each group worked in its own list and handoffs were invisible.

**Walkthrough:**

The team puts the whole campaign on one board with Brief, Copy, Design, Build, Legal and Scheduled, and gives the Legal column a small WIP limit because only one reviewer is available. Assets with a fixed launch date get a fixed-date marker and are pulled first.

Within two weeks, the board shows items piling up before Legal. The team agrees to send legal the checklist earlier, as an entry criterion for Design, so fewer assets come back with changes. Delivery planning before launch groups finished assets into a release, and the campaign goes out on the planned date.

## A two-person founding team builds an MVP

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: two founders are building a minimum viable product while talking to customers. Every conversation adds new ideas, and they keep starting features they never finish.

**Walkthrough:**

They keep all ideas in Options and allow only two items in progress at once, one each. At a short weekly replenishment meeting they pick the next items based on what customers said that week, and anything not picked stays in Options.

After a month, Options has grown long, but finished work has become steady. The founders prune Options every week, dropping ideas nobody has asked for again. Their throughput history lets them tell early customers roughly when the next capability will be ready.
