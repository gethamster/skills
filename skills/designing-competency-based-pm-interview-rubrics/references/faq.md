# FAQ: Designing Competency-Based PM Interview Rubrics

## How many boxes should one interviewer assess?

Usually a small group of related boxes from the same region of the chart, which fit naturally into one conversation. Too many boxes per interviewer leaves no time for follow-ups. Make sure every core box is covered by someone.

## Should we use behavioral or hypothetical questions?

Both. Behavioral questions test boxes the candidate should already have owned. Hypothetical questions test boxes the role will stretch them into and show how they reason in new situations. Google's re:Work guide describes both types.

## What rating scale should the rubric use?

A four-level scale of poor, borderline, solid and outstanding, as in the re:Work guide, works well. Define solid as meeting the level you are hiring for and outstanding as showing the next level. Written descriptions matter more than the labels.

## How do we stop interviewers from influencing each other?

Ask for written evidence and ratings before the debrief, and have interviewers submit them without seeing others' scorecards. In the debrief, read evidence box by box before anyone gives an overall recommendation.

## How often should questions change?

Keep questions fixed within a hiring cycle so candidates are comparable. Between cycles, refresh questions that produced weak signal or may have been shared by past candidates. Record every change in the kit.
