# Examples: Scaling Agile Across Teams with SAFe, LeSS and More

## Three teams that only needed a shared cadence

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: three teams build different parts of one web product. Each runs two-week sprints, but on different start days, and cross-team features keep slipping by a sprint because one team plans its part after the others have finished.

**Walkthrough:**

The dependency map shows only a handful of recurring dependencies, all between the same two teams. Rather than adopting a framework, the teams align their sprint start days, hold the first half hour of sprint planning together, and add a short cross-team sync twice a week.

After two sprints, cross-team features ship in the sprint they were planned for. The teams keep the setup and add a joint retrospective once a month.

## Layer teams redesigned before scaling

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product has five teams organized as front end, back end, data, mobile and quality assurance. Almost every feature needs four of them, and leadership proposes adopting a full scaling framework.

**Walkthrough:**

The dependency map shows that nearly every delay comes from the layer split. Before choosing a framework, the group reorganizes into four cross-functional teams, each owning a customer journey end to end, with one shared product backlog and one product owner.

Most features now need one team. The few remaining dependencies are handled in a joint sprint planning session. Leadership drops the plan for a heavier framework and agrees to revisit the question if the product grows.

## A pilot train in a larger organization

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company runs a dozen teams across two related products that share a platform. Releases need coordination across most teams, and the platform team is a constant bottleneck.

**Walkthrough:**

The group chooses SAFe for its portfolio layer and starts with one Agile Release Train around the first product, including the platform team. It runs one Planning Interval as a pilot, with a joint planning event, a system demo at the end of each iteration and an Inspect and Adapt session.

The retrospective shows fewer surprise dependencies but too many meetings for the platform team. The group cuts two recurring events, keeps joint planning, and plans a second train for the other product.
