# FAQ: Scaling Agile Across Teams with SAFe, LeSS and More

## When do we actually need a scaling framework?

When several teams work on one product and dependencies between them regularly delay delivery, even after team boundaries have been redrawn to reduce them. Two or three teams with few dependencies can usually coordinate with a shared cadence and a joint planning session. Start with the dependency map and choose a framework afterward.

## What is the difference between SAFe and LeSS?

SAFe adds structure: Agile Release Trains, Planning Intervals, a portfolio layer and roles such as the Release Train Engineer. LeSS keeps Scrum's structure and scales it with one Product Owner, one backlog and a common sprint for up to eight teams. SAFe tends to suit organizations with many products and formal portfolio governance, while LeSS suits a single product built by several teams.

## Should we copy the Spotify model?

The people who described it advise against copying it. Kniberg called it an example of how one company works, and a Spotify chapter lead said the model helps you understand Spotify but is not something you should copy. Borrow ideas that address your own dependencies.

## How do we keep teams autonomous at scale?

Let teams own how they work, and limit coordination roles to making dependencies visible and removing obstacles. Keep decisions about priority with one accountable person or group, and decisions about how to build with the teams. Revisit any process that requires teams to ask permission for routine work.

## How long should a scaling pilot run?

At least one full planning cycle, so the teams go through planning, delivery, integration and a joint retrospective. A shorter pilot tests the kickoff rather than the coordination.
