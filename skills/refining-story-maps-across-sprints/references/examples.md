# Examples: Refining a Story Map Across Sprints

## Marking progress reveals a lagging activity

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team is a few sprints into the first release of a learning platform. The map's backbone is: find a course, enroll, learn, check progress, get a certificate.

**Walkthrough:**

After each sprint, the product owner marks done stories in green. Reading across the map, the team sees that "find a course," "enroll" and "learn" have working basics, but "get a certificate" has nothing done, and it is part of the first slice's outcome. In refinement, the team pulls the top card from that column, "download a simple completion certificate," into the next sprint, ahead of richer learning features. The release still works end to end when it ships.

## Feedback moves a release line

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: after a sprint review of a household budgeting app, test users say they will not keep using it unless they can import bank transactions. Manual entry was above the first line and import was in the second slice.

**Walkthrough:**

The team walks the map in refinement and agrees the first slice no longer meets its outcome, which was that users keep tracking their spending. They split import into a crude version, uploading a file exported from the bank, and a richer automatic connection. The crude version moves above the first line, and a lower-value reporting card moves down to make room. The product owner updates the outcome card and tells stakeholders what moved and why.

## Reconnecting a drifted map and backlog

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team realizes its map has not been touched for several sprints. The backlog tool has many stories that do not appear on the map, and some map cards have been dropped from the backlog.

**Walkthrough:**

The map owner runs a session to reconcile the two. Each backlog story is placed under the activity it serves, or flagged if it serves none. Map cards with no backlog item are either confirmed and added or removed. The team then agrees a rule: every story in the current slice links to its place on the map, and refinement starts with a walk along the backbone.
