# FAQ: Refining a Story Map Across Sprints

## How often should we update the story map?

Update progress after every sprint and walk the map at the start of backlog refinement. Review the whole map with the wider team after a release or when goals shift, which Atlassian's guide also recommends. The exact rhythm matters less than never letting the map fall several sprints behind the backlog.

## Who should maintain the story map?

One named owner, often the product owner or product manager, should make sure it stays current. Changes come from the whole team, and the owner makes sure they land on the map and in the backlog. Without a named owner, maps tend to go stale.

## Does the story map replace the sprint backlog?

No. Jeff Patton describes using the story map on the planning wall and a separate task wall for the stories being worked in an iteration. The map shows the whole journey and the release plan, while the backlog and task board track detailed work. Link the two so each story can be traced to its place on the map.

## When should a release line move?

When evidence says the slice will not meet its outcome, or when it is much larger or smaller than the team can deliver. User feedback, measured outcomes and new technical knowledge are all good reasons. Tell stakeholders what moved and why, and update the outcome card beside the slice.

## What do we do with cards that no longer make sense?

Remove them or move them to an archive area once the team agrees. A map that keeps every idea forever becomes hard to read. Keep a short change log so anyone can see why a card disappeared.
