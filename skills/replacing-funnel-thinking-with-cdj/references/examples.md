# Examples: Replacing Funnel Thinking with the Decision Journey

## A consumer electronics brand remaps its plan

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a headphone brand plans its marketing in three funnel stages: awareness, consideration and conversion. Almost nothing in the plan covers what happens after purchase, and repeat purchase is rare.

**Walkthrough:**

The team audits the plan and remaps every activity to the four CDJ phases.

Illustrative scenario: the remapped budget, in units of 100, looks like this.

| CDJ phase | Budget before | Budget after first shift |
|-----------|---------------|--------------------------|
| Initial consideration | 50 | 45 |
| Active evaluation | 15 | 20 |
| Moment of purchase | 30 | 25 |
| Postpurchase | 5 | 10 |

Buyer interviews show that reviews and comparison videos decide most purchases and that owners rarely hear from the brand after buying. The first shift moves part of the budget to review programs and to a setup and care series for new owners. The team keeps its old funnel metrics beside the new phase metrics for two quarters before deciding on the next shift.

## A software company assigns an owner to the loop

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: at a software company, marketing owns leads, sales owns deals and customer success owns renewals. Each team hits its targets, yet growth from expansion and referrals is weak.

**Walkthrough:**

The leadership team remaps activities to CDJ phases and sees that no one owns the handoff between purchase and the first months of use, which interviews show is when customers decide whether to recommend the product.

The company names a journey owner with a mandate across the three teams and sets a phase metric for the share of customers who are active loyalists. Marketing's briefs now state the phase they target, and customer success joins the quarterly planning review. The first joint project is an onboarding change owned by customer success and supported by marketing content.

## A retailer shifts focus to the moment of purchase

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a beauty brand sold in pharmacies has strong recall but loses at the shelf to competitors with more visible packaging and promotions.

**Walkthrough:**

The remapped plan shows most spending on brand advertising for initial consideration, which research shows the brand already wins. Little is spent at the shelf, where shoppers make the final choice.

The brand redesigns its shelf display and packaging messages, tests them in a small number of stores, and compares sales against similar stores without the change. Brand advertising is reduced slightly to fund the test. The team tracks both recall and shelf conversion so it can confirm that the shift does not weaken the phase it already wins.
