# Examples: Story Map Backbone: Identifying User Activities

## A backbone for a home-cleaning booking app

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small team is building an app that lets households book a cleaner. The first draft of the backbone, written before the session, reads: Home Screen, Search, Booking Engine, Payments, Notifications.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator points out that every card names a part of the product. The group starts again from the household's side and narrates a first booking: describe my home, pick a date, choose a cleaner, pay, let the cleaner in, rate the clean. Those tasks go on cards left to right. Two activities emerge above them: "Arrange a clean" and "Get the clean done." "Rate the clean" starts a third activity, "Look after my home over time," which also collects rebooking and changing the regular cleaner.

When the group walks the backbone with a customer support lead, she points out that households often need to give access instructions such as a key safe code. "Share access details" is added between "pay" and "let the cleaner in." The team now has a backbone every person can read aloud as one story.

## Reordering a backbone for an expense tool

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a finance tool team argues about whether "submit an expense" or "set spending policy" comes first. Employees submit expenses daily, while an administrator sets policy once.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator asks the product manager to explain the product to a new hire. She starts with the administrator setting up the company and its policy, then moves to employees submitting and managers approving. The team uses that order. They also notice that the administrator and the employee are different users, so they mark where the story changes hands, which later helps them plan a release that serves each role at a basic level.

## Fixing uneven goal levels

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team's backbone for a recipe app contains "Plan my week of meals," "Tap the heart icon," "Shop for ingredients," and "Adjust servings."

**Walkthrough:**

The team applies the "before taking a break" test. "Plan my week of meals" is a summary-level goal, so it becomes an activity with tasks such as "choose recipes" and "build a shopping list" below it. "Tap the heart icon" is sub-functional, so it is rewritten as the user task "save a recipe I like" and placed under discovery. "Adjust servings" moves below the backbone as a detail of "follow a recipe." The revised row reads as a steady sequence of tasks of similar size.
