# FAQ: Research-Intensive Purchase Mapping

## What is a high-involvement purchase journey?

It is the path a buyer takes to a purchase they research and deliberate over, usually because it is expensive, infrequent or hard to reverse. Cars, mortgages, insurance, education and premium electronics are typical. These journeys have a long latent period, active comparison and a considered final choice.

## How is this different from a general customer journey map?

A general journey map can cover any process, such as onboarding or support. A planned purchase map covers the decision to buy and adds layers a general map usually lacks: the triggers between stages, the consideration set at each stage and the information sources buyers rely on.

## Who should I interview?

Recent buyers of your brand, recent buyers of competitors and people who started looking and stopped. Each group shows different parts of the journey. Recruit for variety in triggers and outcomes rather than a large number of similar buyers.

## How do I handle buyers' faulty memories?

Anchor interviews to specific events and dates, move forward in time, and ask about concrete actions. Check timing against behavioral data where you have it. Mark cells that rest on thin evidence so readers know how much weight to give them.

## Should I make one map or several?

Make as many as there are genuinely different journeys that matter to the business, and no more. If two groups of buyers have different triggers and paths, separate maps are clearer than one averaged map.

## How long does a map stay valid?

It stays useful while the category's triggers, sources and competitors stay similar. Refresh it when new channels, products or competitors appear, or on a regular schedule if the category moves quickly.
