# Examples: Research-Intensive Purchase Mapping

## Mapping first-time mortgage journeys

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a lender wants a journey map for first-time buyers taking out a mortgage. It interviews recent borrowers of its own, borrowers from other lenders and a few people who started looking at mortgages and put their plans on hold.

**Walkthrough:**

The interviews show two distinct groups. One starts from a rent increase and spends a long latent period reading about saving for a deposit. The other starts from a sudden opportunity, such as a family gift, and moves quickly into evaluation. The team builds a separate map for each.

On the second map, the moment that matters is the agreement in principle: buyers who got one quickly from a rival often stopped considering other lenders. The mindset row records buyers' reluctance to start again with another lender once one had said yes. The opportunities row records faster agreements in principle as the lender's main opening.

## Mapping a premium television purchase

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an electronics maker wants to understand how buyers choose a premium television. It interviews recent buyers and pairs the interviews with site analytics and search trend data for the same months.

**Walkthrough:**

The latent row shows buyers noticing picture quality at friends' homes and in stores. Evaluation is dominated by review sites and video reviews, and analytics confirms a spike in visits to comparison pages in the weeks before purchase. Buying centers on price tracking across retailers and waiting for a sale.

The team marks two moments that matter: the review site verdict in evaluation and the sale timing in buying. Cells about in-store influence are marked as assumptions, because few interviewees mentioned it and no data confirmed it.

## Mapping a care home decision

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a care home operator wants to understand how families choose a home for a relative. It interviews families who chose one of its homes, families who chose elsewhere and families still deciding.

**Walkthrough:**

The trigger is almost always a health event, and the latent stage is short or absent. Evaluation is intense and emotional: families visit several homes, ask friends and read inspection reports. The mood row shows guilt and time pressure throughout.

The map shows the visit as the moment that matters, with families judging homes on staff warmth and cleanliness in the first minutes. The operator shares the map with home managers and schedules a refresh after a year of changes to its visit process.
