# Examples: Assessing Threats of New Entrants and Substitutes

## An adjacent company as the main entrant

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company sells invoicing software to freelancers. The team lists candidate entrants and finds 3 startups and one large accounting platform that already serves small businesses.

**Walkthrough:**

Testing the barriers shows that switching costs are low and capital needs are modest, so the startups could enter. The accounting platform matters more: it already has distribution to the target customers, a trusted brand and existing payment integrations. It gets past every barrier that would slow a startup. A patent search and the platform's recent announcements show it building invoicing features. The team rates the threat of new entrants high and names the platform as the main candidate, with its next product release as a signal to watch.

## Finding substitutes by talking to lost customers

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a meal-kit company interviews 10 customers who cancelled in the past quarter.

**Walkthrough:**

Four moved to a competing meal kit, which is rivalry. The other six left the category: some returned to grocery delivery with saved recipes, some to prepared-meal services, one to cooking less. The team groups these into two substitutes and compares price and convenience with its own product. Prepared-meal services are improving quickly and getting cheaper in the team's region, so they get the higher rating. A short survey of current customers then checks how many also use prepared meals.

## A regulatory change that lowers a barrier

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company operates in a licensed field where a new rule will simplify licensing for new providers.

**Walkthrough:**

The team rates the government policy barrier as falling. It lists firms in neighboring regions that could now enter and checks their capacity. The assessment rates the entrant threat as rising from low to medium over the horizon, names the rule's start date as a trigger, and suggests preparing retention offers for the customer segments most likely to be targeted first.
