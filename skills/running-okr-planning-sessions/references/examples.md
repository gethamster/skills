# Examples: How to Run an OKR Planning Session

## A first session for a new OKR team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a data team of six is setting OKRs for the first time. Company OKRs arrived last week, and the team lead has a long list of requests from other departments.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator, a product manager from another team, sends the company OKRs and the request list as a pre-read. In the session, each person silently writes the three things they think would help the company most. Clustering shows that most requests trace back to one problem: other teams cannot trust the numbers in shared dashboards.

The team writes one committed objective about trustworthy core metrics and one aspirational objective about self-serve analysis, and deliberately parks the rest of the request list. They leave with owners for each key result and two dependencies to raise with the engineering teams that produce the raw data.

## Calibrating a set that is too safe

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a mature sales operations team drafts four OKRs in its planning session. When the facilitator asks for confidence ratings, everyone rates every key result near the top of the scale.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator points out that, by the playbook's capacity test, the team could meet the whole set without its full capacity, which the playbook calls sandbagging. The group agrees that two OKRs are really business as usual and moves them to its KPI dashboard.

For the remaining two, the team raises one target to a level where confidence drops to roughly even odds and labels that OKR aspirational. The other stays committed. The final set is shorter and has one goal the team is genuinely unsure it can reach.

## A session that runs out of time

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a marketing team spends most of its planning session debating which channels to prioritize. With little time left, it has objectives but no key results or owners.

**Walkthrough:**

Instead of rushing metrics in the last minutes, the facilitator ends by assigning each objective to a pair who will draft key results with baselines within two days. The team books a short follow-up to test the drafts together.

At the follow-up, the drafts are tested against the sufficiency and outcome checks, owners are confirmed, and dependencies are listed. For the next cycle, the facilitator timeboxes each phase of the agenda and sends the channel data as a pre-read so the debate starts from shared numbers.
