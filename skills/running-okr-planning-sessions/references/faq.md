# FAQ: How to Run an OKR Planning Session

## When should a team hold its OKR planning session?

After the company OKRs are known and before the quarter starts. In the typical OKR cycle John Doerr describes, company OKRs are communicated two weeks before the quarter and teams share their own OKRs at its start. The team session fits in that window, with time left for a cross-team alignment review.

## Who should attend?

Everyone on the team who will own a key result, plus a facilitator. It helps to invite one person from each team you depend on heavily for part of the session, so dependencies are raised early. Keep the group small enough that everyone can speak during drafting.

## Should the team lead write the OKRs before the session?

The lead should bring company context and constraints and leave the team OKRs for the session to write. Google's re:Work guide describes good OKRs as a mix of top-down and bottom-up suggestions. If the lead has strong views, share them as candidate priorities that go through the same tests as everyone else's.

## How do we know if the OKRs are ambitious enough?

Use an agreed check. Google's playbook tests ambition against capacity: committed OKRs should use most but not all of the team's resources, and committed plus aspirational should use somewhat more than it has. Christina Wodtke suggests rating confidence and treating roughly even odds as the right level of stretch for aspirational goals.

## What if the team cannot agree on priorities?

Make the disagreement explicit and tie it back to the company OKRs. Ask which priority does more for the company objective and what each would displace. If the choice depends on strategy the team does not own, escalate it to the leader who does, and record the decision so it is not reopened every cycle.
