# FAQ: North Star Metric Cross-Functional Alignment That Sticks

## Why does alignment fade after a North Star workshop?

Because nothing in the organization's routine changed. Existing targets, meetings and approval processes keep pulling people back to their old metrics. The playbook's list of what successful teams had, including a sponsor, leadership buy-in, change management, onboarding and approval processes, is a list of routines to change.

## Is the North Star Framework a way of cascading metrics across teams?

No. John Cutler writes that models like the North Star Framework are not cascades, because there is no hierarchical relationship between the metric and its inputs. Teams choose how to move inputs, and goals map their work to those inputs. Handing each team a slice of the North Star as a target misreads the framework.

## How do we align product and engineering teams in particular?

Give engineering a clear view of which inputs its work affects, including a system health input for reliability and tooling if you use one. Put engineering leads in the workshop and in the reviews where inputs are discussed. When platform work competes with features, compare both against the same inputs.

## What if leadership will not change existing targets?

Start small. Build a North Star for a product area your team can influence, use its language in your own reviews, and gather evidence over a few quarters. Results in one area are often more persuasive than a proposal for the whole company.

## How do we know alignment is working?

Use the playbook's signals: people can explain how their work connects to the North Star, non-product colleagues use words like "inputs," the metric is mentioned in large company meetings, and saying "no" becomes easier and better supported. Check them each quarter and act on the weakest.
