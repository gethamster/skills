# FAQ: Implementing Internal Linking Structures Across Topic Clusters

## Is internal linking really part of an SEO link building strategy?

Yes. Link building usually refers to earning links from other sites, but internal links are the half you fully control. They decide how readers and crawlers move through your content and whether new pages get discovered. A strong internal structure also makes any external links you earn more useful, because visitors can move on from the page they land on.

## How many internal links should a page in a topic cluster have?

There is no single correct number, so decide by role rather than by count. A hub should link to every spoke in its cluster. A supporting page needs a link to its hub plus a small number of siblings that answer the reader's next questions, for example two to four. Add cross-cluster links only where a clear next question lives elsewhere.

## Should supporting pages link to each other or only to the hub?

Both. The hub link gives every page a route to the broader context, while sibling links let readers follow a natural sequence without returning to the hub each time. Choose siblings by the journey, such as setup leading to troubleshooting. Avoid linking every sibling to every other sibling, which turns the cluster into a flat list.

## What anchor text works best for cluster links?

Anchors that name the destination's topic or the question it answers, phrased to fit the sentence. Vary wording naturally rather than repeating one keyword across the site. Avoid vague anchors like "click here", which tell neither readers nor crawlers what the page covers.

## How do I know whether the internal linking is working?

Check structure first: a crawl should show no orphaned pages, every hub linking to all its spokes, and no broken internal links. Then look at indexing coverage for the cluster's pages. Query-level click data is useful but noisy for long-tail pages, so treat it as one signal among several rather than the only measure.
