# Examples: Identifying Customer Opportunities from Research

## A travel app turns one story into several opportunities

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a trio at a trip-planning app owns the outcome "more travelers finish a full itinerary before departure." It interviews a customer about the last trip she planned.

**Walkthrough:**

The interviewer asks her to walk through the last trip from the moment she decided to go. She describes collecting ideas in a notes app, losing track of which friend recommended which restaurant, arguing with her partner about how full each day should be, and giving up on planning the last two days because she ran out of time.

In the snapshot, the trio writes these opportunities in her voice: "I lose track of where recommendations came from," "We disagree about how packed each day should be," and "I run out of steam before the plan is finished." It also notes a memorable quote about the notes app. All three relate to finishing an itinerary, so all three become candidates for the tree.

## A request that turns out to be a solution

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team on an invoicing tool hears the same line in several support tickets and one interview: "I need a bulk edit button."

**Walkthrough:**

The trio applies the one-way test. A bulk edit button is one specific solution, so it asks the interviewed customer why she wanted it. Her story is about month-end, when she has to fix the tax rate on many invoices after a client changes status.

The opportunity becomes "When a client's tax status changes, I have to fix every invoice one by one." That statement has many possible solutions: bulk edit, a client-level default that updates open invoices, or a warning before invoices are sent. The trio adds the reframed opportunity to its candidate list and keeps the support tickets as a prompt to ask about month-end in future interviews.

## Filtering by outcome

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team focused on new customer activation keeps hearing from long-time customers about slow reporting.

**Walkthrough:**

The complaints are real, but they do not relate to activation. The trio records them in the interview snapshots with the customer's own wording and leaves them off the current tree. When the company later schedules a maintenance cycle, the product manager pulls every reporting opportunity from the snapshots in a few minutes and hands the list to the team doing that work.
