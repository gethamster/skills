# Examples: Maintaining a Living Opportunity Solution Tree

## A routine opportunity update

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a trio at a note-taking app has run four interviews since its last update. Its outcome is "more new users create a second note within their first week."

**Walkthrough:**

The product manager pulls the opportunities from the four interview snapshots before the session. Six relate to the outcome. Three duplicate existing nodes, so the trio adds a tally mark to each. Two fit under existing parents as new siblings. One, "I don't trust that my notes are backed up," does not fit anywhere, so it goes into a parking area with a note to listen for it.

Four other opportunities, about sharing notebooks with a team, have nothing to do with first-week behavior. They stay in the snapshots. The session is short, and the tree now reflects the latest interviews.

## Updating after a round of tests

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the same trio has been testing three solutions for its target opportunity, "I don't know what to write in my first note."

**Walkthrough:**

Two tests come back. The template gallery's key assumption, that new users will browse templates, is refuted: almost nobody in the prototype test scrolled past the first row. The prompt-of-the-day idea passes its test.

The trio marks the template gallery with an x and archives it with a one-line reason. It marks the prompt idea with a check and adds its next riskiest assumptions as new test nodes. The third solution is still in testing and keeps its circle. A stakeholder glancing at the tree can now see which idea is ahead and why.

## Deciding whether a new tree is needed

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: at quarterly planning, leadership raises the team's target for second-note creation and also asks another team to take over acquisition.

**Walkthrough:**

The direction of the trio's outcome has not changed, only the target. It updates the number on the root node and keeps the tree, including all the opportunity structure it built over the quarter.

The acquisition team starts its own tree. The trio shares its archived notes on the opportunities it heard from people who never signed up, which were parked in interview snapshots because they were outside its outcome.
