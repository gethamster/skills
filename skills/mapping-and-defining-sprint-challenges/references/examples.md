# Examples: Design Sprint Day 1: Map the Problem and Pick a Target

## Narrowing a broad challenge to one moment

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team at a small accounting software company arrives on Monday with the challenge "get more small businesses to switch to us." The Decider is the head of product, and the team has booked three experts for the afternoon: a support lead, a salesperson and an engineer who built the data import tool.

**Walkthrough:**

The long-term goal the team writes is "Small businesses switch to us without fear of losing their records." The sprint questions include "Will owners trust that their old data comes across intact?" and "Will they understand the price before they start?" The map runs from the business owner on the left, through hearing about the product, comparing plans, starting a trial and importing records, to a completed first month on the right.

In the afternoon the support lead describes owners abandoning trials halfway through importing, and the salesperson says prospects ask about data safety on nearly every call. Most of the How Might We votes land on notes about the import step. The Decider circles the business owner and the moment they decide whether to start importing. The team now has a target it can prototype and test, instead of the whole switching journey.

## A target that fails the prototype check

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a hospital operations team is sprinting on how nurses hand over patients between shifts. By late afternoon the votes point at the whole handover conversation.

**Walkthrough:**

Before the Decider circles anything, the Facilitator asks whether the team could build something on Thursday that a nurse could react to in an interview. A full handover involves two people, a ward and live patient data, which a one-day facade cannot recreate. The team looks again at the map and the sprint questions, and sees that the riskiest question is whether an incoming nurse can find the three most urgent items quickly. The Decider narrows the target to that moment: the incoming nurse reading the handover summary. The target still addresses the riskiest question, and it can be shown as a printed or on-screen summary.

## Expert interviews that change the map

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a consumer app team drew its map in the morning assuming users find the app through app store search. The first expert of the afternoon runs partnerships.

**Walkthrough:**

The partnerships lead explains that most new users arrive through a referral from an existing user. The Facilitator stops, redraws the start of the map to begin with a referral message, and asks the team to write How Might We notes about that moment. Later experts confirm the pattern. When the votes come in, several of the top notes sit on the new referral step. The Decider picks the referred new user and the moment they open the invitation. Without the interview the team would have spent the week on a step few users experience.
