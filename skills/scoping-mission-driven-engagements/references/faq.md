# FAQ: FDE Engagement Scoping

## How is a mission brief different from a statement of work?

A statement of work is a legal and commercial document, and it is often written broadly to give both sides room. A mission brief is an operating agreement that names one outcome, an end date, exit criteria and a handoff plan. The two can coexist: the contract sets the commercial frame and the brief sets what this specific engagement is trying to achieve. Keep the brief short enough that the sponsor can restate it from memory.

## How long should an FDE mission be?

There is no standard length in the sources on the role. Choose a time box short enough that the outcome stays urgent and long enough to ship something real in the customer's environment. OpenAI's FDE process separates a short on-site scoping phase and a validation phase from delivery, which is a useful pattern: commit to the full mission only after validation. If the outcome cannot plausibly be reached in the time box, narrow the outcome.

## What if the customer refuses to narrow the scope?

Offer a sequence of missions. Agree the first mission around the most valuable single outcome and put the other requests into a backlog that feeds follow-on missions. Show the sponsor how a broad mandate makes it impossible to say whether the engagement worked. If the customer still insists on an open mandate, escalate to your own leadership, because that engagement is closer to open-ended services.

## Who should sign off the brief?

Three parties: the FDE, the FDE's own leadership, and the customer's sponsor who owns the outcome. The FDE's leadership signs because it is committing a person for a bounded period. The sponsor signs because the outcome is theirs to judge. Add the operational owner who will run the system after handoff if that is a different person.

## How do I handle a request that is valuable but out of scope?

Log it with who asked, what problem it solves and why it is out of scope, and tell the requester where it went. Review the backlog with the sponsor at set checkpoints. Recurring requests across customers are useful evidence for the product team. If a request turns out to be necessary for the outcome, amend the brief explicitly instead of absorbing it silently.
