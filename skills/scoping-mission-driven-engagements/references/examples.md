# Examples: FDE Engagement Scoping

## Compressing a platform wish list into one mission

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a solo FDE joins a mid-size logistics company that bought an analytics product and asked for "a better data platform." The sponsor, a VP of operations, has a list of eight requests. The FDE has been committed for about three months.

**Walkthrough:**

The FDE interviews the VP, a dispatch lead and a data engineer separately. Two of the three name the same pain: the morning delivery plan is built from data that arrives late, so dispatchers start the day with yesterday's picture. The outcome statement becomes "Dispatchers start each shift with a delivery plan built from same-day data by the end of the quarter." Six of the eight requests go into a visible backlog; one (a data quality check on the carrier feed) stays in scope because the outcome depends on it. The exit criteria include a stop condition: if the carrier feed cannot be accessed within the first two weeks, the mission is rescoped with the sponsor.

## Rescoping after the ground truth changes

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an FDE team of two is scoped to automate claim triage for an insurer using a language model. The brief assumes historical claims can be used for evaluation. In the first week on site, the security team rules that historical claims cannot leave a restricted environment where the team has no model access.

**Walkthrough:**

The FDEs do not quietly switch to a different project. They document the constraint, then meet the sponsor with two options: narrow the mission to the claim types whose data is already approved, or pause while access is negotiated. The sponsor picks the narrower mission. The brief is amended with the new outcome, the reason for the change and the date both sides agreed. The original, broader outcome goes into the backlog as a follow-on mission once access is resolved.

## Using the brief as a filter mid-engagement

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: halfway through a mission to cut the time a hospital's pharmacy team spends reconciling orders, a department head asks the FDE to build a staffing dashboard. The request is sensible and the FDE could build it in a few days.

**Walkthrough:**

The FDE checks the request against the outcome statement. The dashboard does not affect reconciliation time, so it is out of scope. The FDE logs it in the backlog with the requester's name and the problem it would solve, tells the department head where it went and when the backlog will be reviewed with the sponsor, and notes it as a possible pattern for the product team because two other hospital customers have asked for similar views. The mission stays on its end date.
