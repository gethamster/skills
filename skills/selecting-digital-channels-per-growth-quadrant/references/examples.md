# Examples: Digital Marketing Channels for Each Ansoff Matrix Quadrant

## One software company, three quadrants, three channel plans

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an HR software company has three initiatives on its Ansoff map: raising annual-plan adoption among current customers (penetration), entering the non-profit sector (market development), and launching a payroll add-on (product development).

**Walkthrough:**

For penetration, the team uses email to monthly-plan customers, an in-product prompt at renewal time and a referral offer, measured on annual-plan conversions. For the non-profit sector, a keyword check shows little search demand for HR software in non-profit language, so the team tests partnerships with non-profit associations and sponsored content in two sector newsletters, measured on cost per qualified demo.

For the payroll add-on, the launch goes first to the most active customers through the customer community and a webinar, then to all customers by email and in-product announcement, measured on add-on adoption. The budget follows the portfolio agreed in planning, with part of the non-profit budget held back until the first tests report.

## A consumer brand stops using one mix for everything

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a skincare brand runs all marketing through paid social aimed at its core audience. It now also sells a new body-care line and has started shipping to a neighboring country.

**Walkthrough:**

Reporting all spend together had hidden the problem: the new line was being advertised to strangers, while existing customers, the likeliest buyers, heard about it only by chance. The team moves the body-care launch to email and post-purchase messages for current customers and measures uptake among them.

For the new country, the team sets up local-language landing pages and runs small paid social tests with local creators, reporting them separately from the home market. Core-market paid social continues for penetration, now judged only on home-market results.

## Diversification channels judged on learning

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company that makes accounting software for restaurants is testing a new staff scheduling product for gyms and fitness studios, a diversification move.

**Walkthrough:**

The team has no audience, brand or search presence in fitness. It sets learning goals for the first quarter: which of three problem statements draws the most response, and which channel produces conversations with studio owners. It runs small tests through a fitness industry newsletter, a partnership with a studio booking platform, and short paid social campaigns.

The partnership produces the most conversations and the clearest message. The team shifts the next quarter's small budget toward the partner channel and sets its first revenue target only after a repeatable way to reach studio owners is proven.
