# Examples: Writing Effective Start Stop Continue Feedback

## Rewriting vague retrospective items

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: after a sprint, a developer drafts three items: "Start communicating better," "Stop the chaos in planning," and "Continue the good teamwork." The facilitator asks everyone to check their items against the skill before posting.

**Walkthrough:**

The developer adds a situation, a behavior and an effect to each. "Start communicating better" becomes "Start posting a short note in the team channel when a story is blocked for more than half a day, so others can help before the daily meeting."

"Stop the chaos in planning" becomes "Stop pulling stories into the sprint that have no acceptance criteria, which caused rework on two stories this sprint."

"Continue the good teamwork" becomes "Continue pairing on unfamiliar parts of the codebase, which got the payment change reviewed in one day." Each item now names something the team could adopt, drop or protect.

## Feedback for a colleague in a 1-on-1 setting

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team lead is preparing start stop continue feedback for a designer before their monthly 1-on-1. The lead's first draft says "Stop being defensive in reviews."

**Walkthrough:**

The lead recognizes a judgment and rewrites it as a behavior with a situation: "In Tuesday's design review, when engineers raised concerns about the table layout, you explained the reasoning before asking what their concern was, and the discussion ran out of time before we reached a decision."

The lead adds a Start item that offers an alternative: "Start design reviews by asking reviewers for their biggest concern first." And a Continue item with evidence: "Continue sharing early sketches in the channel, which caught the navigation problem a week before build." In the meeting, the designer agrees with the situation and suggests the Start item herself.

## Items outside the team's control

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: several team members write Stop items about the same problem: "Stop the sales team promising features without asking us."

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator points out that the team cannot stop another team's behavior. The group rewrites the item as something it controls: "Start a short weekly slot where sales can bring feature requests to the product manager before promising dates."

The original concern is also kept as a request: the product manager will raise the issue with the sales lead and report back at the next session. Both items are specific, and each has an owner who can act on it.
