# FAQ: Writing Effective Start Stop Continue Feedback

## What makes start stop continue feedback actionable?

An actionable item names a specific behavior, the situation where it happens, and its effect, and it is something the group could act on before the next session. If a reader who was not in the room could understand it and start on it without asking questions, it is actionable.

## How do I write behavior-focused feedback?

Describe what someone did or what the team does, in the situation where it happens, instead of labelling a trait. The Center for Creative Leadership's Situation-Behavior-Impact model is a simple guide: situation, behavior, impact. Replace words like "rude" or "sloppy" with the observable action.

## Should I name people in my items?

In a team retrospective, generally no, except for praise. Describe the practice and raise individual feedback privately. In a 1-on-1, the feedback is about the person, so describe their behavior specifically and kindly.

## How many items should I write?

Enough to cover what you noticed, one idea per note. Quality matters more than volume, because the group will group and vote on everything posted. A few well-written items per column is usually more useful than many vague ones.

## What are some start stop continue tips for Continue items?

Be as specific as you would be for Stop. Name the practice and why it helps, such as "continue writing a one-paragraph summary at the top of each design document, which lets reviewers start quickly." Specific Continue items tell the team what to protect when pressure rises.
