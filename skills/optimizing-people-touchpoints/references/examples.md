# Examples: People in the Marketing Mix

## An IT support firm writes standards for its help desk

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small managed IT firm positions itself as the calm expert for busy offices. Client feedback praises the technical fixes but complains that nobody explains what is happening.

**Walkthrough:**

The team maps the human touchpoints: the first call, remote sessions, on-site visits and the monthly review. For each, it writes a short standard. On every ticket, the technician confirms the problem in plain language, gives an expected time, and sends a two-line summary when it is fixed.

Hiring interviews add a role play where the candidate explains a fix to a non-technical manager. New technicians shadow a senior colleague for their first weeks. The firm adds a single question about clarity to its post-ticket survey and reviews the answers in weekly coaching.

## A restaurant group gives staff room to recover mistakes

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a restaurant group has good food reviews, but complaints about mistakes escalate because servers must find a manager before offering anything.

**Walkthrough:**

The group decides servers can replace a dish or remove a drink from the bill without approval, and records these in a simple log. Training covers how to apologize, what to offer and when to call a manager.

Incentives change too. Servers had been rewarded mainly on table turnover, which pushed them to rush. The group adds guest feedback to the monthly recognition. After a few months, managers review the log to spot recurring kitchen problems behind the recoveries.

## A software company includes back-office roles

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a subscription software company trains its support and success teams well, yet many complaints involve invoices and account changes.

**Walkthrough:**

Mapping the journey shows that the billing team handles every plan change and refund, but it had never been included in customer training or standards. Its emails were written in accounting language and it had no view of customer history.

The company writes a short standard for billing contacts, gives the team access to the customer record, and rewrites the standard email templates with the support team. Billing staff join the monthly review of customer feedback, so they see the effect of their work directly.
