# Examples: Running Inspect and Adapt Workshops

## Business value gap traced to late integration

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a train of seven teams planned 70 business value points across its PI objectives and achieved 48, with most of the shortfall in objectives that spanned several teams.

**Walkthrough:**

During the system demo, two cross-team features could not be shown end to end because their components had only been combined in the final iteration. The measurement review showed single-team objectives scored close to plan, while multi-team objectives scored far below it. The room voted this as the top problem. The workshop group traced the cause to teams integrating only at the end of the PI rather than continuously.

The committed improvement was an integration checkpoint every iteration for shared features, owned by the system architect, with the next I&A set to compare multi-team objective scores.

## Escaped defects rising over three PIs

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a train's escaped defect count rose from 12 to 19 to 27 over three consecutive PIs, although features completed stayed roughly flat.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator put the trend chart first in the measurement review instead of the business value chart, because the trend was the clearest signal. Teams initially attributed it to one difficult release, but the three-PI view made that explanation hard to sustain. The workshop group found that test environments were shared and frequently broken, so teams skipped integrated testing late in iterations. They proposed a dedicated environment health owner and an agreement to stop the line when the environment failed.

Both items went into the next PI Planning with sizes and owners.

## An improvement list that finally got done

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a train had produced 15 or more improvement ideas at each of its last two I&A events, and almost none had been completed.

**Walkthrough:**

The new RTE opened the next I&A by listing the previous PI's items and their status, which showed that nearly all were untouched. The room agreed the problem was volume and lack of ownership rather than bad ideas. For this event the workshop was capped at two problems and the train committed to three improvement items, each with a named owner and a size. Product management placed them in the next PI's backlog alongside features.

At the following I&A, all three were done and the facilitator could show the numbers they had been meant to move.
