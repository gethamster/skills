# FAQ: Iterating from Evidence

## How do I know whether to go back to Define or just fix the prototype?

Ask whether anyone can name the specific flaw in the artifact that caused the failure. If they can, fix the prototype. If the same failure appears across different concepts, or users succeed at the task but say it does not matter to them, the framing is the problem. Go back to Define and rewrite the point of view.

## Is the design thinking iteration process supposed to follow the five modes in order?

No. The five modes are commonly taught in sequence, but the d.school [cautions against treating design thinking as one fixed, linear process](https://dschool.stanford.edu/stories/lets-stop-talking-about-the-design-process). Movement between modes should be driven by what each test shows. The order of the first pass matters far less than the quality of each loop-back decision.

## How many iterations should we plan for?

There is no fixed number, so plan for an exit condition rather than a count. For example, a team might require a concept to hold for the defined user across two consecutive rounds with no new framing failures. Budget time for at least one loop beyond the first test, since the first round almost always changes something.

## Why do design sessions so often fail to ship?

A [2026 practitioner survey](https://designsprintx.com/articles/is-design-thinking-dead-in-2026) found only 12% of teams reliably ship what their design sessions produce, which points to a handoff gap. Sessions often end with outputs but no owner and no delivery milestone. Treating the handoff as the final iteration decision, with a named owner who watched the tests, closes much of that gap.

## What should a test session capture to support good loop-back decisions?

Capture what users did and said separately from what the team thinks it means. Include tasks or questions that probe whether the underlying need is real, not only whether the prototype is usable. Without that, you can only diagnose surface problems and will miss framing failures.
