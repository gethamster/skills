# Examples: Iterating from Evidence

## Onboarding flow that kept failing

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A product team tested three versions of an onboarding checklist with new users of an internal reporting tool. Each version tested poorly, and the team kept adjusting copy and layout between rounds.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator laid out observations from all three rounds side by side. Users understood every checklist and completed the steps, yet most said they only needed one report and did not want to set up anything else. Nobody could name a specific prototype flaw, so the failure moved past execution and past concept, because three different concepts failed the same way. The team diagnosed a framing failure and returned to Define.

The new point of view focused on a user who needs one report fast, not on a user learning the whole tool. The next test used a single-report shortcut and targeted only whether users recognized that need as their own.

## Service role-play with a confusing handoff

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A clinic team role-played a new check-in service with patients. Patients hesitated at the moment they moved from the front desk to the waiting area, and several asked what to do next.

**Walkthrough:**

The team separated what patients did (paused, asked questions, looked for signs) from its interpretation that the concept was unwanted. When asked, patients said the faster check-in was what they wanted. A team member pointed to the exact flaw: the role-play had no cue telling patients where to go after the desk. That qualified as an execution failure, so the team returned to Prototype, added a simple handoff cue, and reran only that segment.

The hesitation disappeared, and the concept moved on to its next open question.

## From promising sessions to a shipped feature

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A startup ran several design sessions on a scheduling feature. Tests were positive, but months later nothing had shipped because each session ended with a readout and no follow-up.

**Walkthrough:**

The team adopted an exit condition: the concept had to work for the defined user in two consecutive rounds with no new framing failures. It then invited the engineer who would build the feature to observe the final two tests. After the second passing round, the engineer became the shipping owner and wrote a first milestone directly from the decision log. Remaining questions were tagged as delivery questions, not design questions, so they went into the build plan instead of triggering another workshop.
