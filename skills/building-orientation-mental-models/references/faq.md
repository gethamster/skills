# FAQ: Building Mental Models for Rapid Orientation

## What are the inputs to orientation in the OODA Loop?

Boyd described orientation as drawing on new observations plus genetic heritage, cultural traditions and previous experience. In team terms, heritage and culture show up as training, professional norms and organizational doctrine. Only the observations change quickly; the rest are slow lenses. Naming all of them is what lets you see when a lens rather than the evidence is driving the conclusion.

## What is the difference between analysis and synthesis in orientation?

Analysis breaks observations and assumptions into separate elements that can each be checked. Synthesis recombines those elements into a coherent picture of the situation. Analysis frees you from the story a situation arrived in, and synthesis lets a new explanation emerge. Skipping analysis usually means synthesis just restates the original narrative.

## How often should a team revise its orientation model?

Revise it every time you go around the loop, and immediately when a predefined trigger signal appears. Orientation is a continuing process, not a state you reach once. A practical habit is to date each model and review it whenever the related decision is revisited. If nobody can remember when the model was last challenged, it is overdue.

## Is past experience a bad input for orientation?

No. Previous experience is one of the core inputs to orientation and often the fastest route to a good model. The problem is relying on it exclusively, which forces new information into an outdated frame. Use past cases as analogies to test, and look explicitly for where the current situation differs.

## How is this different from detecting cognitive biases?

This skill covers constructing and revising the model: inputs, analysis, synthesis and revision triggers. Detecting biases such as confirmation bias or anchoring is a related control applied to that model and has its own page. In practice you build the model with this skill, then stress-test it with bias checks before handing options to Decide.
