# Examples: Synthesizing Six Forces into Strategic Recommendations

## When buyer power dominates

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company sells scheduling software to hospital groups. Its force ratings are: rivalry medium, new entrants low, substitutes low, buyer power high, supplier power low, complements medium. The leadership team asks whether to expand into a second hospital segment.

**Walkthrough:**

The synthesis team resists calling the industry "moderately attractive." Buyer power is the dominant force: a small number of hospital groups account for most of the revenue, and procurement runs formal tenders every few years. The second dominant factor is complements, because the software depends on an integration with electronic health record systems controlled by two vendors.

Mapping the interaction shows that the integration raises switching costs, which is one of the few things limiting buyer power. The team drafts three options: deepen the health record integration to raise switching costs further, enter the smaller-clinic segment where buyers are less concentrated, or partner with one health record vendor. Stress-testing shows the partnership option fails if that vendor builds its own scheduling module, so the team recommends the first two and lists the vendor's roadmap as a trigger to watch.

## Complements as the deciding force

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a startup makes a smart home sensor. Ratings show high rivalry, medium entrants, medium substitutes, low buyer power for individual consumers, medium supplier power and a complements force that is weak for the startup and strong for its two largest rivals.

**Walkthrough:**

The team ranks complements first: rivals work with the major voice assistants and home platforms, and the startup works with none. The asymmetry explains why rivalry feels so intense for the startup and less so for rivals. The options focus on closing the complement gap: certify with one major platform, or target a professional installer channel where installers act as the complement. The recommendation includes the effort each option requires and names the platform's certification policy as the main risk.

## Synthesis with thin evidence

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team finishes ratings for a new market, but two forces, substitutes and complements, rest on only a few interviews.

**Walkthrough:**

The synthesis states that plainly. The team identifies supplier power as the likely dominant force, since two suppliers control a critical input, and presents options that make sense regardless of the uncertain forces, such as qualifying a second supplier. For the uncertain forces, the recommendation is research: a short round of interviews on substitutes before committing budget. The deliverable is honest about confidence, which keeps the decision proportionate.
