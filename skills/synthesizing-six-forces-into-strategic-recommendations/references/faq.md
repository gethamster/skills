# FAQ: Synthesizing Six Forces into Strategic Recommendations

## Why not average the six ratings into one score?

An average hides the force that dominates. An industry with low rivalry and very high buyer power is shaped by its buyers, and a "medium" average would mislead anyone who reads it. Rank the forces by how much profit each takes or protects, and name the two or three that matter most.

## How many strategic options should we present?

A few, usually enough to show a real choice without overwhelming the audience. Each option should address at least one dominant force and state what it requires and what could go wrong. Options that do not address a dominant force can be evaluated separately.

## Where does the complementary products force fit in synthesis?

Treat complements both as a force and as an influence on the others. Porter sees complements as a factor acting on the five forces, as the [five forces article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter%27s_five_forces_analysis) reports, and that view helps in synthesis: look at how complements change switching costs, supplier power and substitutes. Complements also open cooperative options, such as partnerships.

## What is a positioning view?

It is a one-page chart or table that shows each force, its rating and direction, and where your company stands relative to rivals on each. It helps the audience see the asymmetries that the options respond to. Keep the format consistent across analyses so readers can compare them.

## When should the synthesis be revisited?

Set triggers when you present it: events such as a major acquisition, a new entrant, a regulation change or a shift in a key complement. Revisit when a trigger fires and on a regular schedule as well. Record the decision taken and its reasons so the next review can check them.
