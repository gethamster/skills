# FAQ: Conducting User-Centered Evaluation

## How is user-centered evaluation different from QA testing?

QA testing checks whether the product works as specified, such as whether a button triggers the right action. User-centered evaluation checks whether real people can achieve their goals with it, which a fully bug-free product can still fail. The two find different problems. You need both, and the error log is where they meet, since technical errors surface in user sessions too.

## How many participants do I need for a usability test?

It depends on the question and how varied your users are. For finding problems in a qualitative think-aloud round, a small group per distinct user type is often enough to surface the major issues, for example five or six people. For comparing measures between designs, you need more. Running several small rounds usually teaches you more than one large one.

## When should I use heuristic review instead of testing with users?

Use heuristic or expert review when the design is early, budget is tight, or you want to clear obvious problems before sessions. It is fast and needs no recruiting. It cannot replace testing with users, because experts miss problems rooted in users' real goals, vocabulary and context. Treat it as a filter that makes user sessions more productive.

## Do I always need to test in the real use environment?

Not every round, but at least one before release. Controlled sessions are good for isolating interface problems, while the real environment reveals interruptions, device constraints and workarounds a lab hides. Products used in demanding settings, such as clinics, factories or vehicles, need field evaluation earlier and more often.

## What should an evaluation report contain?

Keep it short and decision-focused. State the question the round answered, the methods and participants, and a ranked list of problems with severity, frequency, error type and supporting evidence such as quotes or clips. End with the agreed changes and their owners. A long narrative report that nobody reads is a sign the evaluation was not tied to a decision.
