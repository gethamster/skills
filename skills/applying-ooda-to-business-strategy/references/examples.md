# Examples: Applying the OODA Loop to Business and Product Strategy

## Responding to a rival's pricing move

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a software company with a team of 6 in its pricing cell learns that its main rival has launched a cheaper entry plan aimed at small customers.

**Walkthrough:**

The charter already states that the small-customer segment may be used for pricing tests but enterprise contracts may not be touched. Observe: within 2 days the cell sees trial sign-ups drop and three sales calls mention the rival plan. Orient: finance, product and sales agree the rival is buying market share, and the named dissenter argues it may be a short promotion. Decide: a pre-agreed rule says that if trial sign-ups fall for 2 consecutive weeks, the team extends the free trial for small customers.

Act: the growth team ships the extension without escalation because it sits inside guardrails, and the next weekly loop checks whether sign-ups recover.

## Entering a regulated market

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product group of 10 people is deciding whether to launch in a new country where a data rule is under consultation.

**Walkthrough:**

The strategic question is whether to launch this year or wait for the rule to settle. The group lists signals: consultation milestones, statements from two local competitors, and early customer interest from 5 pilot accounts. Legal and risk join the orientation cell, which records the assumption that the rule will be finalized within 6 months. In the design step they prepare two response options, a limited launch without the affected feature and a full launch after the rule passes.

When the consultation is delayed, the escalation rule sends the choice to leadership, which approves the limited launch and the team communicates it to sales and support the same week.

## Running operating and strategy loops together

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a mid-sized retailer runs a weekly operating loop for promotions and a quarterly strategy loop for its product range, involving 8 managers.

**Walkthrough:**

Each weekly loop logs which promotions fired under the if-then rules and what customers did in response. Over one quarter, the log shows 4 promotions in a category failing while search interest in a related category rises. At the quarterly review, the strategy loop orients on that pattern and concludes customer demand is shifting. The team updates the charter to allow more experimentation in the rising category and tightens guardrails in the weak one.

The next weekly loops inherit the new boundaries, so frontline managers act on the updated strategy without waiting for approval.
