# FAQ: Applying the OODA Loop to Business and Product Strategy

## Is the OODA Loop really suitable for business, or only for the military?

It can work in business, but as an analogy rather than a direct transfer. The loop began as a tactical air-combat concept, and applications to higher-level decisions are looser. Businesses that benefit tend to face moving competitors or fast-changing customers. For stable problems, conventional planning is usually a better fit.

## How is the OODA Loop different from a regular strategy review?

A strategy review usually happens on a fixed schedule and looks backward at results. An OODA-style loop is triggered by signals, emphasizes leading indicators, and uses pre-agreed responses so the team can act between reviews. The two can coexist, with the review acting as the slow strategy loop. The difference is that decisions no longer wait for the calendar.

## Who should own the loop in an organization?

One person should own the charter and the cadence, but interpretation should sit with a cross-functional group. The original model shows a single decision-maker, which is why businesses need to add explicit collaboration. A good owner is someone with authority to change the rules when they stop working. Ownership by a reporting function alone tends to turn the loop into a dashboard.

## What does risk appetite look like in an OODA charter?

It is a written statement of which moves the team may make without escalation and which it may not. The most usable versions list concrete examples, such as which customer segments can be exposed to experiments. It also names the constraints that no fast decision may break. Without it, guardrails and escalation rules have nothing to anchor to.

## How do I adapt the loop for long-range strategy rather than daily operations?

Add a design step where the team creates response options before they are needed, and a dissemination step where decisions are communicated and monitored across teams. One strategy-level adaptation names these stages discovery, design, decide and disseminate/monitor. Run this slower loop alongside a faster operating loop and let each feed the other.
