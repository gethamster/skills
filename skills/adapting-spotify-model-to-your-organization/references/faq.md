# FAQ: Adapting the Spotify Model to Your Organization

## Should we copy the Spotify model exactly?

No. Spotify's own 2016 presentation said the approach [helps you understand how Spotify works but should not be copied](https://infoq.com/news/2016/10/no-spotify-model), because it changed constantly. Take the elements that solve your problems and adapt them. Expect your version to keep changing too.

## Is there evidence that the Spotify model works?

Evidence is limited. A practitioner review notes it has not been academically evaluated and rests on an informal report. A [2023 exploratory study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/02683962231164428) documented a transformation with significant problems. Treat adoption as an experiment with its own measures.

## What should we measure during a Spotify-style transformation?

Combine flow measures such as cycle time with quality, technical debt, customer outcomes and team health. The [2023 study found flow metrics alone narrowed success to short-term performance](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/02683962231164428). Agree the measures with each stakeholder group before starting, since groups otherwise judge results differently.

## Do we need tribes, chapters and guilds, or can we adopt only squads?

You can adopt only what addresses a real problem. Squads suit handoff-heavy delivery, chapters suit specialists who lack coaching, and guilds suit siloed knowledge. Tribes usually matter only once you have many squads in related areas. Leaving an element out is a reasonable design choice when it solves nothing on your list.

## How big should the first pilot be?

Keep it small enough to learn from and reverse, for example two or three fairly independent squads for one quarter. Prefer volunteers and give them the authority the design promises. Fix the review date at the start so the pilot does not linger indefinitely.
