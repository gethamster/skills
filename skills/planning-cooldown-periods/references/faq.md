# FAQ: Planning the Shape Up Cooldown Period

## How long is the Shape Up cooldown period?

Basecamp scheduled two weeks of cool-down after each six-week cycle. The book's glossary defines cool-down as a two-week break between cycles to do ad-hoc tasks, fix bugs and hold a betting table. Teams with different cycle lengths adjust it, but it needs to be long enough for the betting table and some self-directed work.

## What should builders work on during cool-down?

Whatever they choose. The book says programmers and designers are free to work on whatever they want, and names fixing bugs, exploring new ideas and trying new technical possibilities as typical uses. The time is deliberately unscheduled.

## Is cool-down the same as a retrospective?

No. The book does not prescribe a retrospective meeting. Cool-down is a period of unscheduled time plus the betting table. A team can hold a short review of the last cycle if it finds that useful, but that is a team choice and not part of the method as written.

## Can a team use cool-down to finish its project?

Occasionally. The book says the two-week cool-down usually gives a team with a few too many must-haves enough slack to ship before the next cycle. It also warns that this should not become a habit, because running into cool-down points to a shaping or team performance problem.

## When is the betting table held?

During cool-down. The pitches considered there are ones shaped during the previous cycle, or an older pitch someone deliberately revived. Holding it in cool-down keeps planning away from the busy end of a cycle, when everyone is focused on shipping.

## Does every team need a cool-down?

No. The book says a team of two or three people can drop cycles, cool-downs, formal pitches and the betting table, and simply alternate between shaping and building. The structure becomes useful once the team is large enough that coordination starts to slip.
