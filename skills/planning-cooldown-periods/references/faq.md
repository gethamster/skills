# FAQ: Planning Cool-Down Periods

## How long should a shape up cooldown period be relative to the cycle length?

The standard ratio is roughly one week of cool-down per three weeks of cycle. A six-week cycle gets a two-week cool-down. A three-week cycle gets one week. If your cool-downs are consistently consumed entirely by bug fixes with no time for exploration or shaping, that is a signal the cool-down is too short relative to the complexity of your cycles.

You can experiment with extending by a few days, but address the root cause first: cycles may be shipping too rough, or technical debt may be accumulating faster than cool-downs can absorb.

## Should I plan cool-down activities in a project management tool like I plan cycle work?

No. The whole point of cool-down is that it is self-directed and unstructured. If you create tickets, assign them, and track velocity during cool-down, you have just created another sprint under a different name. The only items that should appear in your tracker are critical production bugs that need formal assignment and tracking.

Everything else lives on the cool-down plan document as a menu of options. Use the archive step at the end to record what was accomplished, but do not manage it during the period.

## What if the team has no shaped pitches ready by the end of cool-down?

If the betting table arrives with no strong pitches, you have two options: extend the cool-down by a few days to finish shaping, or run a shorter next cycle to buy more shaping time. Do not start a cycle without well-shaped work just to maintain the calendar cadence. An under-shaped pitch leads to a cycle full of scope discovery, rabbit holes, and missed deadlines, which is far more expensive than a few extra days of cool-down. If this happens repeatedly, shaping needs to start earlier, possibly during the build cycle itself, with a dedicated shaper working in parallel.

## How do I handle cool-down when team members want to take vacation?

Cool-down is an excellent time for vacation because there are no assigned projects with deadlines. Encourage it. The only constraint is ensuring that critical bug coverage is maintained and that at least one person involved in shaping is available to prepare pitches for the betting table. If multiple people want time off during the same cool-down, coordinate so that coverage is staggered.

A cool-down where half the team is refreshed from actual vacation is more productive than one where everyone is present but exhausted.

## Should exploration projects during cool-down have any structure or deliverables?

Exploration projects should not have formal deliverables, but they benefit from a lightweight "show and tell" at the end of cool-down. Ask anyone who explored something to spend five minutes sharing what they found, whether it is a working prototype, a failed experiment, or an interesting data finding. This makes exploration visible (which protects it politically) and surfaces insights that might feed into future shaping. Do not require a slide deck or a formal presentation.

A screen share and a verbal explanation is sufficient.

## Can shaping work happen during the build cycle instead of during cool-down?

Yes, and for many teams it should. Shaping is senior product work that runs on a different track from building. If your shaper is not on a build team, they can shape during the cycle and have pitches ready before cool-down even starts. This is the ideal scenario because it means cool-down is not bottlenecked on shaping completion.

The cool-down then becomes review and refinement time for pitches rather than creation time. However, if your shaper is also a builder on a cycle team, shaping must happen during cool-down because they will not have bandwidth during the build cycle.

## How do I prevent cool-down from becoming 'just another sprint' over time?

Watch for three warning signs: the team starts having daily standups during cool-down, managers begin assigning specific cool-down work to individuals, or the exploration lane disappears because 'there is too much real work to do.' If you see any of these, explicitly reset expectations at the start of the next cool-down. Restate the four-lane model, emphasize self-direction, and cancel any recurring meetings that crept in. The cool-down plan document helps here because it codifies the expectation of autonomy. If the cultural drift keeps happening, raise it in the cycle retrospective and discuss what structural change would protect the cool-down's character.
