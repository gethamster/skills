# FAQ: Applying JTBD Insights to Product Strategy

## Can I build a roadmap from interviews alone?

Yes, with less confidence. Use the struggles and forces from interviews to form themes, and be explicit that their order is a judgment rather than a measurement. Plan a survey when the choices become expensive enough to justify it.

## How do I choose between growth strategies?

Look at whether the target segment is underserved or overserved. Strategyn's framework links underserved customers to strategies that do the job better, and overserved customers or non-consumers to disruptive ones that do it more cheaply ([Strategyn growth strategies](https://strategyn.com/5-business-growth-strategies-jobs-to-be-done/)). Write the choice down before debating features.

## How do I handle a stakeholder whose favorite feature scores low?

Show which outcomes the feature serves and how those outcomes scored, with the caveats. Ask whether there is evidence the survey missed, such as a strategic partner or a compliance need. If there is, record it as a separate reason on the roadmap. If not, the declined-requests list shows the reasoning.

## How do JTBD themes relate to OKRs?

An underserved outcome statement translates directly into a key result, since it already names a direction and a metric. Roadmap items are then the concepts meant to move it. This keeps objectives tied to the customer's job rather than to the list of features.

## Why does the roadmap drift back to feature requests?

Usually because roadmap items stop naming their outcomes and success is measured by shipping. Require every item to list the outcomes it targets, measure those outcomes after release and review the evidence at a set point.
