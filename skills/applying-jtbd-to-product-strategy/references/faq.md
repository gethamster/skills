# FAQ: Applying JTBD Insights to Product Strategy and Roadmaps

## How do I build a product manager roadmap from JTBD data when I only have qualitative interview data, not quantitative scores?

You can still build a directional roadmap, but you lose the ability to defend precise sequencing. From qualitative interviews, count outcome frequency (how many interviewees mentioned each struggle) and intensity (how emotionally charged the language was). Use frequency as a proxy for importance and intensity as a proxy for dissatisfaction. Group the most frequent and intense outcomes into themes and sequence by your best judgment.

Acknowledge to stakeholders that this is a hypothesis-stage roadmap and plan a quantitative survey within 60 days to validate or adjust. See [conducting JTBD customer interviews](https://tryhamster.com/skills/conducting-jtbd-customer-interviews) for how to extract outcome language from interviews.

## How many outcome opportunities should a product manager roadmap address per quarter?

For most teams, 1-2 themes covering 3-8 outcomes is the right scope per quarter. A single theme typically requires 4-10 weeks of focused execution. If you try to address three or more themes simultaneously, each theme gets diluted and you ship partial solutions that do not move satisfaction scores meaningfully. The goal is to fully address a theme so that when you re-score those outcomes, satisfaction has increased by 2+ points.

## Should I apply JTBD insights to the product manager roadmap before or after segmenting customers by unmet needs?

After segmentation, if possible. Aggregate-level opportunity scores can mask critical segment-level differences, leading you to build for an average customer that does not exist. If you have already completed [segmenting customers by unmet needs](https://tryhamster.com/skills/segmenting-customers-by-unmet-needs), run the opportunity sort per segment and compare the top-5 lists. If they align, your aggregate roadmap is fine.

If they diverge, you need either segment-specific roadmap lanes or an explicit decision about which segment to prioritize. If you have not yet segmented, build the roadmap on aggregate data and plan segmentation as a next step.

## How do I handle stakeholder pushback when the JTBD opportunity data contradicts their preferred features?

## Why does my product manager roadmap keep drifting from the original JTBD-based priorities?

Drift usually comes from one of three sources. First, the roadmap was set once and never refreshed, so new requests accumulated without being scored against outcomes. Fix this by scheduling quarterly re-scoring. Second, urgent requests bypass the prioritization process entirely, which signals a governance problem, not a data problem.

Fix this by requiring every unplanned request to reference an outcome statement and its score before it enters the backlog. Third, the original outcome data was too old or too narrow in sample size, making the team lose confidence in it. Fix this by running a larger survey or refreshing the data, then rebuilding trust in the numbers.

## How do I use a JTBD-based product manager roadmap to align marketing and sales messaging?

The positioning briefs from Step 7 are the primary alignment mechanism. Each brief contains the customer segment profile, the outcome statements in customer language, the capabilities being built, the current alternatives and their shortcomings, and a draft positioning statement. Hand these to marketing as inputs for campaign messaging, landing pages, and sales collateral. The key principle is that messaging should use the language of the job and the outcome, not the language of the feature.

Instead of "We built a new dashboard," the message should be "Spot project blockers before they delay your deadline," which mirrors the outcome statement. Sales teams can use the outcome language in discovery calls to test whether a prospect shares the same unmet need the product was designed to address.

## Can I apply this skill if my company uses OKRs or another planning framework alongside JTBD?

Yes, and the two are complementary. OKRs define what the business wants to achieve (objectives) and how to measure it (key results). JTBD themes and outcome scores tell you which customer problems to solve to achieve those objectives. The mapping is straightforward: each OKR's key results should connect to specific outcome statements whose satisfaction scores you intend to move.

For example, an OKR key result of "Increase NPS from 35 to 50" connects to the JTBD themes that address the highest-opportunity outcomes for your largest customer segment. The JTBD roadmap feeds the OKR planning process by providing evidence for which initiatives will most likely move the key results.
