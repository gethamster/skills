# FAQ: Disrupting an Opponent's Decision Cycle

## What does it mean to get inside an opponent's OODA loop?

It means completing your observe, orient, decide and act cycle before the rival completes theirs, so their decisions are based on a situation that has already changed. [Boyd's briefings](https://coljohnboyd.com/static/documents/1986-12__Boyd_John_R__Patterns_of_Conflict__PPT-PDF.pdf) describe this faster cycling as a source of confusion and advantage. In practice it depends on knowing roughly how long their cycle takes, which is why modelling comes first.

## Is disrupting a competitor's loop the same as deceiving them?

Not necessarily. Most of the practical leverage comes from moving where they are not looking, varying your pattern, and timing moves against their slow decision points. Those are legitimate competitive choices. Set clear lines in advance about what you will not do, particularly anything that misleads customers, because that damages you in the wider environment.

## Does this work outside military settings?

The loop was originally a tactical concept derived from air-to-air combat, as the [analysis of Boyd on competition and conflict](https://tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01495933.2022.2057733) notes. Critics summarised by [Brehmer](https://ooda.de/media/berndt_brehmer_-_the_dynamic_ooda_loop.pdf) argue it does not apply outside aviation, while others use it widely for business competition. Treat it as a useful lens for structuring thinking about rivals, not a proven formula.

## Is there evidence that out-cycling a competitor improves results?

Evidence is thin. One [2024 paper](https://thinkmind.org/articles/achi_2024_3_30_20026.pdf) cites a study reporting faster and more accurate decisions with an OODA sequence, but it gives no sample size or effect sizes. The comparative command-and-control study lists several gaps in the model. Measure your own results by checking predictions against actual rival responses.

## How is this different from shortening my own feedback loop?

Shortening your own loop is about internal structure: signals, decision rules and delegated authority, covered in [Shortening Feedback Loop Cycles](https://tryhamster.com/skills/shortening-feedback-loop-cycles). Disrupting an opponent's loop uses that speed against a specific rival by aiming at their weakest phase. A fast loop with no model of the rival is only half of the skill.
