# Examples: Implementing a Six Pillar SEO Strategy End to End

## Example: B2B SaaS startup with 200 pages and limited resources

**Scenario:**

A 15-person B2B SaaS company selling project management software has a marketing team of two (a content marketer and a growth marketer). They have about 200 pages on their site: 30 product/feature pages, 120 blog posts, and 50 help docs. Organic traffic has plateaued at 8,000 monthly sessions. Budget for external help is minimal, so most execution must be done in-house.

**Walkthrough:**

The team runs the six-pillar baseline audit over three days. Technical scores a 5/10: crawl errors are manageable but Core Web Vitals are failing on mobile due to unoptimized images and a heavy JavaScript bundle. Content scores a 4/10: the 120 blog posts cover many topics shallowly with no cluster structure. On-page scores a 6/10: titles are mostly unique but internal linking is sparse and schema is absent.

Off-page scores a 3/10: only 45 referring domains, mostly from directory listings. UX scores a 5/10: mobile experience is poor and bounce rate on blog posts averages 78%. Analytics scores a 7/10: Google Analytics and Search Console are set up correctly with conversion tracking. The bottleneck is off-page authority at 3/10, but the dependency rule says to fix technical (5/10) and content (4/10) first because links to poorly crawled, thin content will not move the needle.

The team allocates Q1 as follows: 50% effort to content (reorganizing existing posts into three clusters and filling gaps with five new supporting articles), 30% to technical (image optimization, lazy loading, Core Web Vitals fixes), and 20% to on-page (adding internal links and FAQ schema to the top 20 posts). By end of Q1, technical rises to 7/10, content to 6/10, and on-page to 7/10. In Q2 they shift focus to off-page authority, now the clear bottleneck, by creating an original research report from anonymized product data and using it as the centerpiece of outreach to industry publications. Organic traffic grows 35% over two quarters.

## Example: E-commerce brand expanding into programmatic SEO

**Scenario:**

A direct-to-consumer home goods brand has 500 product pages and 40 blog posts. They want to create 2,000 programmatic category and comparison pages targeting long-tail queries like 'best [material] [product type] for [room]'. Their engineering team can build templates, but they are not sure how to integrate this with their existing SEO strategy.

**Walkthrough:**

The baseline audit reveals: Technical 7/10 (clean site built on Shopify with good page speed), Content 3/10 (40 blog posts with no structure and product pages with thin descriptions), On-page 5/10 (product pages have basic schema but blog posts lack it entirely), Off-page 6/10 (decent brand authority from press coverage but no intentional link building), UX 7/10 (solid mobile experience, good navigation), Analytics 6/10 (GA4 in place but no content-level attribution). Content is the clear bottleneck. Before launching 2,000 programmatic pages, the team applies the content pillar systematically. They define 8 topic clusters around their core product categories, write pillar pages for each, and create a programmatic template that generates unique value per page by combining product data, customer review excerpts, care instructions, and room-specific recommendations.

Each programmatic page includes at least 3 internal links to related products and the parent cluster pillar page. They add Product and ItemList schema to every programmatic page. They launch 200 pages initially as a test batch, monitor indexation rate (targeting 95%+), and check for thin content signals in Search Console. The test batch indexes at 97% and begins ranking for long-tail queries within 6 weeks.

They scale to the full 2,000 pages over the next quarter, running the on-page checklist against every template variation before launch. Organic traffic from long-tail queries increases by 180% over two quarters.

## Example: Content publisher pivoting to AI search visibility

**Scenario:**

A B2B content publisher with 3,000 articles and strong domain authority (DR 72) notices that organic traffic has declined 15% year-over-year despite maintaining the same publishing cadence. They suspect AI Overviews are cannibalizing clicks. They want to adapt their six-pillar strategy to account for AI search.

**Walkthrough:**

The baseline audit shows: Technical 8/10 (well-maintained infrastructure), Content 7/10 (deep content library but many articles are 2-3 years old without updates), On-page 6/10 (inconsistent schema, many articles lack FAQ markup and author attribution), Off-page 8/10 (strong backlink profile), UX 7/10 (good experience but no 'answer above the fold' pattern), Analytics 5/10 (no AI visibility tracking, no monitoring of citation in AI answers). The bottleneck has shifted from traditional SEO to AI search readiness. The team adds an AI visibility layer to each pillar. For content, they prioritize updating the top 200 articles with current statistics, source citations, and 'last updated' dates.

For analytics, they set up monthly manual checks across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews for their top 50 queries, tracking citation rate and competitors cited.
