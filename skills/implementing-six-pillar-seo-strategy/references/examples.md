# Examples: Six Pillar SEO Strategy: Implementing End to End

## A full pass after a change in direction

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company that sold one analytics product has launched a second product for a different buyer. The content plan still reflects the first product, and the team wants a strategy for the coming year.

**Walkthrough:**

The pre-read answers show a split. Half the team assumes search will keep rewarding long guides; the other half fears that answer panels in search results will absorb most informational traffic. Mapping confirms the site has strong coverage for the first product and almost none for the second. Anticipation adds a new data privacy rule that will change how the second product's buyers search.

Timing shows the new product's category is rising steadily with no strong season. The deepening session finds that buyers of the new product describe their problem as "flying blind", a phrase that never appeared in the team's keyword lists. Scenarios are built around two uncertainties: how much informational traffic stays on websites, and how fast the new category grows.

The preferred position is to be the reference on the new category's core problem. The backcast lists one pillar page, a first cluster built around the buyers' own language, and an original research piece. Content that pays off in every scenario, such as the core how-to pages, goes first. A large glossary that only pays off if informational traffic holds is kept on the list and delayed.

## Running one pillar inside a quarterly review

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team finished a full six pillars pass last year. In the quarterly review, one scenario indicator has moved: a major competitor has started publishing heavily on the team's main topic.

**Walkthrough:**

The team does not rerun the whole sequence. It refreshes the current-reality map for that topic, then runs a short alternatives session on how the competitor's move could play out. The preferred position still holds, but the backcast changes: two planned cluster pages move up, and a refresh of the pillar page with new original data is added.

The roadmap is updated, and each change is noted against the scenario that justified it.

## A small team running the pillars lightly

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a two-person content team at a nonprofit wants a strategy but cannot spend weeks in workshops.

**Walkthrough:**

They answer the six questions themselves and ask three colleagues from programs to answer too. They build a simple current-reality map in a spreadsheet, list three emerging issues, and write one paragraph per topic on timing. They spend most of their time on one deepening session with two volunteers, which changes how they describe their main cause.

They build two scenarios instead of four and choose a preferred position on one topic. The backcast gives them a short, ordered list of pages. They agree to review every quarter and to run a fuller pass once the first topic is complete.
