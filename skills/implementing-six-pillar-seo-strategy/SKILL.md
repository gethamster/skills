## Overview

A six pillar SEO strategy runs Sohail Inayatullah's six pillars of futures thinking (mapping, anticipating, timing, deepening, creating alternatives and transforming) over a search and content program from start to finish. The [Six Pillars Framework](../../methods/six-pillars-framework/METHOD.md) page explains each pillar and its origin. The other skills in this method cover single pillars or single outputs in depth. This skill is the conductor: it sets up the program, runs the pillars in order, passes each pillar's output to the next, and turns the result into one comprehensive SEO plan.

Inayatullah writes that the pillars can be used in a workshop "in a linear sequential sense", from mapping with the futures triangle to transforming with visioning and backcasting, or by focusing on a single pillar ([Inayatullah, Futures Studies: Theories and Methods](https://www.metafuture.org/library1/FuturesStudies/Futures-Studies-theories-and-methods-published-version-2013-with-pics.pdf)). This skill uses the sequential form. It fits when a team is setting direction for a year or more: after a reorganization, before a large content investment, or when the market has shifted enough that the current plan no longer makes sense.

The pillars work as an SEO strategy framework because each one answers a question that ordinary SEO planning skips. Mapping asks where the site really stands and what history weighs on it. Anticipation asks what could change the queries that matter. Timing asks where each topic is in its cycle. Deepening asks why people search the way they do. Creating alternatives asks how the plan holds up if search changes. Transforming asks which position the site should hold and works back to today.

Each pillar ends with a short document. The chain of documents is the strategy, and the roadmap at the end is traceable back through them. When someone asks why a pillar page is on the plan, the answer points to a scenario, an emerging issue or a gap on the current-reality map.

## How It Works

The program runs in three phases.

The first phase frames the work. Inayatullah's paper poses six questions that guide futures thinking, summarized by [Insight & Foresight](https://www.insightandforesight.com.au/blog-foresights/knowledge-base-six-pillars-futures-thinking-for-transforming-by-sohail-inayatullah): "What do you think the future will be like?", "Which future are you afraid of?", "What are the hidden assumptions of your predicted future?", "What are some alternatives to your predicted or feared future?", "What is your preferred future?" and "How might you get there?" Send these to the team before the kickoff, framed around the site's search visibility. The answers show which futures people already assume, which they fear, and where they disagree. That tells you which pillars will need the most time.

The second phase runs the pillars. Mapping produces the current-reality map, built with the [pillar one skill](../mapping-current-reality-with-pillar-one/SKILL.md). Anticipation produces a short list of emerging issues, each with a futures wheel of first and second-order effects on search. Timing produces a note per topic on its demand pattern, built with the [timing skill](../timing-content-interventions-strategically/SKILL.md). Deepening produces a causal layered analysis of the one or two most important topics. Creating alternatives produces a set of scenarios with indicators, built with the [scenario planning skill](../scenario-planning-for-alternative-futures/SKILL.md). Transforming produces the preferred position and a backcast.

The third phase converts the backcast into build work. The preferred position names the topics the site should own. The backcast lists what must exist, working back from that position to today. That list becomes keyword maps, cluster blueprints and a publishing sequence, using the keyword mapping, architecture and topical authority skills. Items that only make sense in one scenario are held until that scenario's indicators appear.

The program then becomes a cycle. Each quarter the team refreshes the map, the emerging issues and the scenario indicators. Google's Search Console keeps [16 months of performance data](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2018/01/introducing-new-search-console), which is enough for year-over-year comparison in the review. About once a year, or when an indicator fires, the team revisits the preferred position.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Frame the program and send the six questions

Name an owner, the people who will take part, and the period the strategy should cover. Send the six guiding questions as a pre-read, adapted to the site's search visibility and content. Collect the answers and group them into assumed futures, feared futures and preferred futures. Note disagreements, because the transforming pillar will need to resolve them. Set a date for each pillar's output.

### Step 2: Map the current reality

Run the pillar one skill to produce the current-reality map: the site's shared history, the futures triangle, the content inventory by topic, the competitors and the technical constraints. Review it with the whole group until everyone accepts it as accurate. Disagreement at this stage is useful, because it exposes assumptions before they reach the plan. File the map as the first document in the strategy.

### Step 3: Anticipate emerging issues

Collect emerging issues from practitioner communities, product and sales teams, regulators and changes in how search engines present results. Keep only those that could change what your audience searches for or how they get answers. For each, sketch a futures wheel of its first and second-order effects on your topics. Record how much evidence supports each issue, since issues with little data are often wrong. The output is a short list with owners who watch each issue.

### Step 4: Time the topics

Run the timing skill for the site's main topics. Classify each by its demand pattern: seasonal, rising, declining, or swinging between framings. Mark which topics warrant permanent pillar pages and which suit timely content. Note windows where publishing or refreshing would matter most. The output is a timing note per topic.

### Step 5: Deepen the core topics

Hold a causal layered analysis session on the one or two topics that matter most. Move from the litany of common questions and headlines to systemic causes, then worldview, then the metaphor the audience uses. Bring in customers or outsiders, because Inayatullah notes that outsiders are better at seeing the deeper levels. Record the new angles and vocabulary the analysis produces. These feed the keyword maps and the pillar page outlines.

### Step 6: Build alternative futures

Run the scenario planning skill with the two largest uncertainties from the map, the emerging issues and the deepening session. Write short narratives and attach indicators to each scenario. For each planned content investment, note which scenarios it serves. Investments that serve every scenario move to the front of the plan.

### Step 7: Choose the preferred position and backcast

Agree on the position the site should hold at the end of the period: the topics it owns and the framing it wants the market to use. If the group holds competing visions, look for a combination that serves both before settling for a compromise. Work backward from that position to today and list the pillar pages, clusters, research and links required. Put the list in order and convert it into keyword maps, cluster blueprints and a publishing sequence.

### Step 8: Run the cycle

Publish in sequence and track visibility by topic as well as by page. Each quarter, refresh the map, the emerging issues and the scenario indicators, and adjust the sequence. Revisit the preferred position once a year or when an indicator fires. Keep all pillar documents together so the reasoning behind the plan stays visible.

## Best Practices

- **Write one document per pillar.** A short written output for each pillar makes the reasoning traceable and lets new team members catch up quickly.
- **Mix inside and outside views.** Include people from sales, product and customers in mapping and deepening. Insiders see the litany; outsiders help reveal the worldview and metaphor levels.
- **Keep the emerging issues list short.** Watching a few issues well beats tracking many badly. Drop issues that show no movement after a few reviews.
- **Tie every roadmap item to a pillar output.** If an item cannot be traced to the map, an issue, a timing note or a scenario, question whether it belongs.
- **Hold scenario-specific bets until indicators move.** Build what serves every scenario first. Keep the rest ready and start it when the signals appear.
- **Review on a schedule.** A quarterly review keeps the plan current without rebuilding it each time.

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating the pillars as a checklist**: Running each pillar as a box to tick produces documents no one uses. Make sure each output changes a decision in the next pillar.
- **Skipping mapping because everyone knows the site**: Shared assumptions are exactly what mapping tests. Build the map and review it together even when the team feels sure.
- **Stopping at scenarios**: Scenarios without a preferred position and a backcast leave the team informed but without a plan. Always finish with transforming.
- **Letting the roadmap drift from the reasoning**: Over time, requests get added to the roadmap without passing through the pillars. Check new items against the pillar documents before adding them.
- **Running the full sequence too often**: A full pass takes weeks. Use quarterly reviews for updates and save the full sequence for real changes in direction.
