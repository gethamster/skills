# FAQ: Creating Circular Consumer Journey Maps

## Why make the map circular instead of linear?

Because the purchase is not the end. The postpurchase experience shapes the next decision, and loyal customers skip straight back to buying. A circular map shows those return paths and the brands added during evaluation, which a linear funnel leaves out.

## How much research do I need before drawing the map?

Enough to fill each phase with evidence rather than guesses. A set of interviews with recent buyers, including some who chose competitors, plus existing analytics and support data, is a practical start. Mark the gaps so they can be filled in the next round.

## Should one map cover all our customers?

No. Use one actor and one scenario per map. Different buyer types take different paths, and merging them produces a map that matches nobody. Build a few focused maps instead.

## Who should be involved?

Everyone who owns a part of the loop: marketing, sales, product, support and customer success. Bring them in to review and interpret the evidence, and give each of them ownership of specific opportunities on the map.

## How often should the map be updated?

On a fixed schedule and after major changes to the product, pricing or competitive landscape. Keep earlier versions so you can see how the journey is shifting over time.
