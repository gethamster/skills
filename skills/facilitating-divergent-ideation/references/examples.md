# Examples: Facilitating Divergent Ideation in the Double Diamond

## Ideation for a hospital discharge process

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: Define ended with the problem "Patients leaving a ward do not know which medicines to take or who to call, because discharge information arrives in a rush on the last morning." A facilitator has two half-day sessions with nurses, a pharmacist, two former patients and the design team.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator opens with the question "How might we make sure patients leave knowing what to take and who to call?" and posts the problem statement and three key insights on the wall.

Round one is silent: everyone writes ideas alone for a few minutes, then reads them out without debate. The former patients propose ideas the staff had not considered, such as a phone call two days after discharge.

Round two uses analogies. The group asks how airlines prepare passengers before a flight and how pharmacies handle repeat prescriptions. This produces ideas about starting discharge preparation days earlier.

Round three is a timed sketching exercise. The pharmacist sketches a single-page medicine calendar. A nurse draws a bedside checklist that fills in over the stay.

After a night's gap, the second session clusters the notes and picks four concepts that take different approaches: earlier preparation on the ward, a follow-up call, a printed calendar, and a named contact card. Each gets a rough prototype and a note of its riskiest assumption.

## Remote ideation for a distributed product team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team spread across several time zones needs ideas for helping new users set up their first project. Everyone joins a shared online whiteboard.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator splits the work into two short live sessions and one asynchronous day in between, so no one works late.

In the first session, people add ideas silently to the board for a timed period, then each person walks through theirs in a minute. The facilitator asks the most senior product manager to present last.

During the asynchronous day, people add ideas and build on others by leaving linked notes. A support engineer adds several ideas based on the questions new users ask most.

In the second session, the group clusters the board and picks three concepts that differ in approach: a guided template, a sample project to explore, and an optional call with a specialist. Each is turned into a paper-style clickable sketch for Deliver.

## Rescuing a session that converged too early

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: twenty minutes into a brainstorm, a department head says "the answer is obviously a mobile app," and the room starts designing the app.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator thanks them, writes "mobile app" on a note and posts it, then restates the rule that judgement and selection come later.

They switch technique immediately to a reversal: "How might we make this problem worse?" The absurd answers break the group's focus on the app and lead to new ideas when inverted.

The facilitator then runs a silent round asking for ideas that involve no new software at all. That constraint produces several service and policy ideas.

At the end, the app is one concept among five, and it goes into Deliver alongside the others to be tested on equal terms.
