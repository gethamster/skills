# Examples: Diagnosing Growth Stalls with the Four Fits

## More ad spend, flat revenue

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B software company sees new revenue flatten over two quarters. Marketing has raised paid social spend to compensate, and new signups are up, yet new revenue is not.

**Walkthrough:**

The team puts indicators for each fit on one timeline. The first thing to move was cost per paying customer in paid social, which began rising a few months before the stall, after the platform changed its targeting options. Retention for older cohorts is stable. Newer cohorts from paid social retain worse than referral cohorts.

The chain runs from the channel to the model: weaker targeting brought less qualified users, which raised effective CAC and stretched payback. The product was not the cause. The team reduces paid social to the level where payback holds, invests in the referral path that still works, and sets recovery metrics for CAC and new-cohort retention.

## Strong retention, slowing growth

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a consumer app for a hobby community keeps its users well, and engagement is steady. New user growth has slowed every month for a year despite stable channel costs.

**Walkthrough:**

Retention and channel indicators are healthy, so the team looks at Model Market Fit. Counting the people who match the market definition shows the app already reaches a large share of the active hobby community. New users are harder to find because few remain.

The diagnosis is a market ceiling. The team considers adjacent hobbies with similar needs, runs interviews to test whether the problem carries over, and treats the expansion as a new Market Product Fit question rather than a marketing push.

## A stall after a pricing change

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a self-serve tool introduces a new mid-priced plan to raise revenue per customer. Within a few months, signups from its main organic channel convert to paid less often, and growth slows.

**Walkthrough:**

The timeline shows the pricing change came first, followed by a drop in trial-to-paid conversion, while traffic stayed stable. The new plan sits in the middle of the ARPU to CAC spectrum: buyers hesitate to pay it without talking to someone, and the company has no sales motion.

The team traces the chain from model to channel and chooses a correction that restores the channel's fit: a lower self-serve entry price with paid add-ons. They check the change against Model Market Fit, confirm the arithmetic still works with the wider market, and set conversion as the recovery metric.
