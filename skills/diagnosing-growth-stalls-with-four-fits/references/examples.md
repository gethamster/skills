# Examples: Diagnosing Growth Stalls Using Four Fits Analysis

## Example: SaaS Product Experiencing Flattening MRR Despite Increasing Ad Spend

**Scenario:**

A B2B SaaS company selling project management tools has seen MRR plateau for three consecutive months despite increasing paid acquisition spend by 40%. The team is debating whether to increase spend further, lower pricing, or invest in new features.

**Walkthrough:**

Following the Four Fits diagnostic process, the team first documents the stall precisely: MRR growth dropped from 8% monthly to 1.5% starting in January. They pull leading indicators for each fit.

**Market-Product Fit check:** Day-30 retention has declined from 35% to 24% over the last four months, starting in November—before the MRR stall became visible. NPS dropped from 42 to 31. This is a Red signal.

**Product-Channel Fit check:** Organic referral rates dropped 30% in December. Paid channel conversion rates are stable. This is Yellow—partially explained by the retention decline reducing word-of-mouth.

**Channel-Model Fit check:** CAC increased 25% since January because the team compensated for lower organic with more paid spend. LTV:CAC ratio dropped from 4:1 to 2.5:1. This is Yellow-Red.

**Model-Market Fit check:** ARPU is stable. Pricing page conversion unchanged. This is Green.

The temporal analysis reveals Market-Product Fit degraded first (November), followed by Product-Channel Fit (December), then Channel-Model Fit (January). The root cause is Market-Product Fit.

Digging deeper, the team discovers that a major competitor launched AI-powered task automation in October. Their core market segment—mid-market teams—has shifted expectations. The product no longer delivers a 'must-have' experience for the most valuable segment.

The causal chain: weakened Market-Product Fit → reduced organic referrals (Product-Channel Fit) → increased reliance on paid channels (Channel-Model Fit degradation). The fix: prioritize shipping AI automation features to restore Market-Product Fit for the core segment, rather than increasing ad spend (which treats a downstream symptom) or cutting prices (which addresses a fit that isn't broken).

## Example: Consumer App With Strong Retention But Declining Growth Rate

**Scenario:**

A consumer fitness app has excellent 60-day retention (45%) and strong NPS (55), but weekly new user growth has slowed from 12% to 3% over six months. The team assumes they need to spend more on Instagram ads.

**Walkthrough:**

The team applies Four Fits diagnostic analysis. Market-Product Fit indicators are all Green—retention is strong, users love the product. This rules out the most common root cause.

**Product-Channel Fit check:** The app grew primarily through Instagram and TikTok organic content. However, algorithm changes on both platforms in Q2 reduced organic reach for fitness content by approximately 50% industry-wide. Their viral loop (user shares workout completion screenshots) generates 60% fewer impressions per share than six months ago. This is Red.

**Channel-Model Fit check:** Because the app relies on a freemium model with $9.99/month premium, they need high-volume, low-cost acquisition. Paid Instagram CPMs have risen 35% in their category. CAC for paid has risen to $8.50 against an LTV of $22—workable but tight. This is Yellow.

The root cause is Product-Channel Fit: the product's natural sharing mechanics no longer align with how the primary channels distribute content. The causal chain is straightforward—channel algorithm shifts broke the fit.

Corrective actions: (1) Redesign shareable content for short-form video (Reels/TikTok clips of workouts rather than static screenshots) to re-align with current algorithm preferences, (2) Explore a secondary channel (YouTube Shorts, community challenges) that better fits the product's natural sharing behavior, (3) Only then consider increasing paid spend once organic channels are re-aligned, which will also improve blended CAC.
