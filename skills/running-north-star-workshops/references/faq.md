# FAQ: Running a North Star Framework Workshop

## How long does a North Star workshop take?

Amplitude's workshop guide sets out a typical agenda of about two hours, and its playbook says many successful workshops have run in less than two hours. That first session produces candidates for the metric and inputs. Plan for one or two shorter refinement sessions soon after it, where the group pulls data and defines the metric precisely.

## Who should attend?

Invite the smallest group that still covers each critical discipline, such as engineering, design, product, analytics, finance, marketing, sales and customer success. Include people who talk to customers often and a few senior decision-makers. Add other stakeholders in later rounds instead of crowding the first session.

## Who should facilitate?

Someone unbiased, ideally with enough positional authority to keep the session moving and make calls on time. The facilitator should read the playbook and rehearse the exercises beforehand. If every candidate facilitator has a stake in the outcome, pair two people from different teams.

## What if the group cannot agree on a North Star by the end?

That is a normal result for a first session. Record the strongest candidates, what you eliminated, and what information you still need, and give each missing piece an owner. Use the follow-up sessions to test the candidates against data and customer research.

## Can the workshop run remotely?

Yes. Use a shared online whiteboard for the worksheets and the candidate board, and keep the silent drafting step, since it works just as well on a screen. Keep the group smaller than you would in person, and use breakout rooms for the pair step.
