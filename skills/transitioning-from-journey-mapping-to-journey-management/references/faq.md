# FAQ: From Journey Mapping to Continuous Journey Management

## What is the difference between journey mapping and journey management?

Journey mapping produces a picture of an experience at one point in time. Journey management is the ongoing practice of owning that journey, measuring it, reviewing it, and improving it through the teams that deliver it. Mapping is an input to management. A managed journey has an owner, live data, a review rhythm, and a backlog.

## Do we need a journey management platform to start?

No. Early journey management depends more on habits than tools: ownership, reviews, and links to delivery can all run in shared documents and existing backlogs. Platforms help as the number of managed journeys grows, because keeping maps, data, and backlogs connected by hand becomes tedious. Get the process working first.

## How many journeys should we manage at first?

Start with a small number, for example one to three, chosen for business importance and the availability of data and a willing owner. Starting small lets the team learn the practice and show results before asking for more resources. Add journeys as capacity and evidence of value grow.

## How often should managed journeys be reviewed?

Pick a rhythm that matches how fast the journey changes. Quarterly is common, and fast-moving digital journeys may need monthly reviews. Add triggers for launches, major research findings, and sharp metric changes. Tie reviews to existing planning forums so decisions reach the backlog.

## How do we show that journey management is worth it?

Record a baseline for each managed journey's metrics before changes ship, then report what moved after each release. Include decisions made in reviews as well as metric changes. Connect results to outcomes leadership already tracks, such as retention, revenue, or cost to serve. Evidence from a few managed journeys is the strongest case for expanding the practice.
