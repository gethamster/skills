# Examples: From Journey Mapping to Continuous Journey Management

## A retailer revives two maps that had gone stale

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a retailer ran a large journey mapping project a year ago. The maps were well received, but nobody has opened them since, and store and online teams have both changed their processes.

**Walkthrough:**

The assessment shows that none of the maps has an owner, data, a review, or a backlog link. The CX lead picks two journeys that match this year's goals, "Return an item" and "Collect an online order in store," and gets a named owner for each.

Each owner connects their map to existing data such as return volumes and collection wait times, and schedules a review inside the monthly operations meeting. The first reviews produce backlog items for store operations and the e-commerce team, and the maps are updated to show what changed.

## A software company proves the practice with one journey

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a software company's leadership is sceptical that journey management is worth dedicated time. The CX team has permission to try it on one journey.

**Walkthrough:**

The team chooses onboarding, where drop-off data already exists and a product manager is willing to own it. The owner records a baseline, links the map's stages to product analytics, and joins the journey review to the product team's planning cycle.

Over a few cycles, several onboarding fixes ship and the owner reports what moved and what did not. Leadership approves owners for two more journeys, and the team reuses the same review agenda and metric set to start them faster.

## A public service connects its map to delivery

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a government agency has a detailed map of how residents apply for a permit, but findings from it never reach the teams that build the online service.

**Walkthrough:**

The agency names a service owner for the permit journey and sets up a route for pain points into the digital team's backlog, each with an expected effect and a metric. Reviews now happen alongside the digital team's planning.

The first backlog items address confusing eligibility questions. After release, the owner checks completion rates and support calls against the baseline, updates the map, and records the result for the next review.
