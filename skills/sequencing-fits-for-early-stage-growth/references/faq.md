# FAQ: Sequencing the Four Fits for Early-Stage Growth

## Why does the market come first?

Because every other fit depends on knowing who the customer is and what problem they have. Balfour renames product-market fit as Market Product Fit to make this point. Starting from the product risks building a solution looking for a problem.

## Should I wait for strong Market Product Fit before thinking about channels?

Wait before investing heavily in channels, but think about them early. A rough check during stage one can reveal that no affordable channel could carry the product, which is much cheaper to learn before building. Heavy channel investment should wait until the product shows pull in a segment.

## Why are channel and model worked together?

Each constrains the other. The price determines which channels can pay back, and the channel determines what buyers will accept as a price. Choosing one without the other is how products end up in the middle of the ARPU to CAC spectrum.

## What if the market turns out too small late in the process?

Go back to stage one for the wider or different market, and re-run the later stages for it. The earlier work is not wasted if the new market shares the problem, but it must be re-validated. A rough size check at the start reduces the chance of this happening late.

## How do I know when to move to the next stage?

Set the exit criterion before starting each stage. For Market Product Fit that might be flattening retention in a named segment; for channel and model, a primary channel that brings activated users at a cost you can recover. Moving on when the criterion is met keeps the decision tied to evidence.
