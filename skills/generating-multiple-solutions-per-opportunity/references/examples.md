# Examples: Generating Multiple Solutions per Opportunity

## A local news site generates ideas for sharing

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a trio at a local news site owns the outcome "more new readers." Its target opportunity, heard in several interviews, is "I know someone who should read this article."

**Walkthrough:**

Each person spends a quiet stretch writing ideas alone: share buttons for social media, emailing the full text, texting a link, a "gift this article" option for subscribers, a weekly digest readers can forward, printing a clean version to hand over. After sharing, a second round adds ideas sparked by others, such as letting the recipient read the article without a paywall.

The trio clusters duplicates and dot-votes. It chooses three different approaches: social share buttons, emailing the full article, and a subscriber gift link. Each rests on different assumptions about how readers share and how recipients respond, so assumption tests can tell them apart.

## Bringing in a stakeholder's idea

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a head of sales insists the product needs a chatbot. The trio's current target opportunity is "I can't tell which plan is right for my team."

**Walkthrough:**

The product manager invites the head of sales to the ideation session for that opportunity. Her chatbot idea goes on the board with everyone else's: a plan comparison table, a short quiz, a trial of the higher plan, and a sales call booking link.

During convergence the group keeps the chatbot as one of three finalists because it is genuinely different from the other two. The head of sales now sees her idea being tested fairly against alternatives for a real customer need, and the decision will rest on test results.

## Getting unstuck

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team at an expense app stalls after a handful of ideas for "I lose receipts before I can submit them."

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator switches to prompts. For analogous products, someone points out how parcel tracking apps prompt people at the moment of delivery. For extreme users, the team imagines someone who travels every week with dozens of receipts. For wild ideas, someone suggests the company pays for anything under a small amount without a receipt.

These prompts add several new ideas, including prompting a photo when a card payment is made and letting managers approve small amounts without receipts. The final three include one idea from each prompt.
