# Examples: GO Product Roadmap Template

## A spreadsheet template for a small product team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of one product manager, four engineers, and a designer builds an invoicing tool for freelancers. They have never kept a roadmap. The founder wants something to share at monthly investor updates, and the team already lives in a shared spreadsheet.

**Walkthrough:**

The product manager creates a new sheet. Column A holds the five row labels in Pichler's order, each with its one-line definition in smaller text beneath: date, name, goal, features, metrics. Columns B to E are four goal columns. The goal row gets a thicker border and larger font. A notes column on the far right carries the checklist prompts, one per row, and is hidden when the sheet is shown to investors.

She fills column B as the worked example: "Next quarter", no release name, the goal "Help freelancers get paid faster and grow our paying base", three features, and two metrics with a note on when each can be read. A second sheet tab, set to pull from the first, shows the external view with the date row replaced by "This half" and "Later". A footer credits Roman Pichler, links the source, and names the licence. An engineer who was not involved fills in column C without asking questions, which the team takes as the sign the template is ready.

## Consolidating three squads onto one template

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a consumer app has three squads, each with its own roadmap page in a different format. The head of product wants one view for the quarterly business review and one template all squads use.

**Walkthrough:**

The head of product builds a master slide with the five rows and one column per squad goal, and a matching whiteboard board for planning workshops. Each squad gets the same grid. The first attempt includes an owner row and a status row, and within a week the squads are using the roadmap as a task tracker.

At the next review the extra rows are removed, and status moves to each squad's backlog. The feature cells are resized so they fit about five short bullets, and a prompt in each cell's comment asks whether every feature is needed for the goal. The consolidated view becomes a sequence of squad goals, which makes overlaps visible: two squads had written goals that both targeted activation, and they merge them into one.

## An internal and a public view from one master

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a software company publishes a public roadmap for customers and keeps an internal one for sales, support, and engineering. The two were maintained separately and contradicted each other twice last year.

**Walkthrough:**

The product lead rebuilds both from one master template. The internal view shows target months in the date row and full metrics. The public view is generated from the same source: it replaces dates with coarse periods, hides the metrics row, and rewrites goals in customer language while keeping their meaning.

A short usage note at the top of the master explains which view goes to whom and who may edit it. When a goal slips a month internally, the public view still reads "this half", so it does not need to change. Customers stop receiving promises the team cannot keep, and sales stops quoting dates from the old public page.
