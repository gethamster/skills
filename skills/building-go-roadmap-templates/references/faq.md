# FAQ: GO Product Roadmap Template

## Where do I get the official GO product roadmap template?

Roman Pichler offers it as a free download on his website, together with a checklist. The template is a single page with the five rows date, name, goal, features, and metrics and a few empty columns. It is published under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike licence, so you can adapt it as long as you credit the source and share alike.

## Should I use a spreadsheet, slides, or a roadmap tool?

Use whatever your stakeholders already open. A spreadsheet is easy to share and version, slides suit leadership presentations, and a whiteboard tool suits collaborative workshops. Several roadmap and whiteboard tools offer versions credited to Pichler, which are fine if your team already uses those tools. What matters more is keeping one master copy and deriving other views from it.

## How many columns should the template have?

Pichler's PDF has four empty columns, which suits a roadmap of about a year with quarterly goals. You can add or remove columns as long as each one holds a single goal. If you find yourself adding many columns, check whether your goals are too small or you are trying to look further ahead than you realistically can.

## Can I add rows for owners, status, or dependencies?

It is better not to. Those details describe execution, and Pichler keeps execution detail in the product backlog and release plans. Extra rows make the roadmap harder to read and invite people to use it as a tracker. Link to the backlog or release plan instead.

## How do I stop people from listing too many features?

Size the feature cell for three to five short bullets and put the checklist prompt next to it. Pichler's checklist asks that each feature is required to meet the goal, is coarse-grained, and that features are limited to three to five per outcome. When someone needs more, treat it as a sign the goal is too big and split it.
