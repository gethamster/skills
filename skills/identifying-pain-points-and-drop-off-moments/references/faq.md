# FAQ: Identifying Customer Journey Pain Points and Drop-Offs

## What is the difference between a pain point and a drop-off?

A drop-off is where customers leave, and it shows up as a fall in the number of people reaching the next step. A pain point is where customers struggle, and it shows up in complaints, repeated attempts and workarounds. Many drop-offs are caused by pain points, but some pain points never cause visible drop-off until much later. Track both.

## How many customer interviews do I need?

Enough to see the same reasons repeat. For a single drop, a handful of interviews with customers who stopped at that step often reveals the main causes. Combine them with tickets and recordings so the interviews confirm rather than carry the whole finding. If every interview says something different, keep going.

## How do I find drop-offs after purchase?

Use cohort retention, product usage and cancellation data. Look for points where usage falls, downgrades cluster or cancellations spike, such as after a trial or a price change. Treat satisfied customers who never refer anyone as an advocacy drop-off too.

## How do I rank findings when everything looks important?

Estimate how many customers each finding affects and how much it moves a stage metric, then weigh that by how confident you are in the cause. Work on high-impact, well-evidenced findings first. Keep the rest on a research list rather than fixing them on instinct.

## What if the data and the customer feedback disagree?

Look for a segment where both are true. Feedback often comes from a vocal group whose problem is real but small, while the data reflects everyone. Segmenting the numbers usually resolves the disagreement. If it does not, gather more evidence before acting.
