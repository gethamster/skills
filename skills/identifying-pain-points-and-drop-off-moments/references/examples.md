# Examples: Identifying Pain Points and Drop-Off Moments in the Journey

## Example: B2C E-Commerce with High Cart Abandonment

**Scenario:**

A direct-to-consumer skincare brand with 150,000 monthly website visitors and a 68% cart abandonment rate. The team has Google Analytics, Hotjar session recordings, and a Zendesk support inbox. Average order value is $55. The brand sells primarily through its own website with some Amazon presence.

**Walkthrough:**

The team builds a five-stage funnel and finds the largest absolute drop-off at the consideration-to-purchase transition: 42,000 users add items to cart monthly, but only 13,440 complete checkout. 57 million in monthly unrealized revenue. They decompose the checkout micro-funnel into: cart page, shipping info, payment info, and order confirmation. Analytics show 38% of users who reach the cart page never proceed to shipping info.

Session recordings of 20 users on the cart page reveal a pattern: users scroll down past the product summary, see the estimated shipping cost appear only after they click 'proceed,' and 14 of 20 recorded users visibly hesitate or abandon at that moment. ' The pain point is confirmed through triangulation: quantitative drop at cart-to-shipping, qualitative evidence of shipping cost surprise. 57M. Root cause hypothesis: unexpected shipping costs revealed too late in the checkout flow.

Recommended action: display shipping estimate on the cart page before users click proceed, and test a free shipping threshold. This entry goes to the top of the register.

## Example: B2B SaaS with Free Trial to Paid Conversion Problem

**Scenario:**

A project management SaaS tool with 2,000 new free trial signups per month and a 4% trial-to-paid conversion rate. The team has Mixpanel product analytics, Intercom chat logs, and conducts monthly churn interviews. Average annual contract value is $3,600. The product serves teams of 5-50 people.

**Walkthrough:**

The stage-to-stage funnel shows the steepest drop at purchase/decision-to-retention: of the 80 users who convert to paid monthly, only 52 are still active after 90 days, a 35% early churn rate. But the largest absolute volume loss is at consideration-to-purchase: 1,920 trial users per month do not convert. The team investigates both. For trial conversion, Mixpanel data reveals that users who do not invite a teammate within the first 3 days have a 2% conversion rate, while those who do invite a teammate convert at 18%.

The micro-funnel analysis shows that the 'invite teammate' prompt appears only on the settings page, which 73% of trial users never visit. Intercom chat logs contain 34 messages in 90 days from trial users asking how to add team members. ' Pain point confirmed: the team invitation flow is buried, preventing users from experiencing the collaborative value that drives conversion. 91M annual opportunity.

The register's top entry recommends surfacing the teammate invitation prompt during onboarding setup, before the user reaches the main dashboard.

## Example: B2B Professional Services Firm with Long Sales Cycles

**Scenario:**

A management consulting firm generating 300 qualified leads per quarter through content marketing and conference speaking. Engagement-to-proposal conversion is 12%. Average engagement value is $120,000. The firm has CRM data, call recordings from sales conversations, and post-proposal surveys sent to prospects who did not sign.

**Walkthrough:**

The funnel analysis reveals the steepest drop between consideration and purchase/decision. Of 300 qualified leads per quarter, 180 have initial discovery calls (60% conversion), but only 36 receive proposals (20% of discovery calls leading to proposals). The team decomposes the discovery-to-proposal micro-funnel and identifies a specific bottleneck: after discovery calls, prospects are asked to complete a 14-field 'engagement readiness assessment' form before the firm will draft a proposal. CRM data shows 62% of prospects never complete this form.

Sales call recordings reveal a pattern: consultants explain the form as 'standard process' without connecting it to client value. ' Triangulation is strong across CRM completion data, call recording tone analysis, and survey feedback. 18M in quarterly unrealized revenue. Root cause: the readiness assessment form creates friction disproportionate to the prospect's commitment level at that stage.

The register recommends replacing the full form with a 10-minute phone-based intake conducted by the lead consultant during the follow-up call.

## Example: Small Team SaaS Startup Diagnosing Awareness-Stage Leakage

**Scenario:**

A 6-person startup offering an email marketing tool for creators. Monthly website traffic is 18,000 visitors, primarily from organic search. The team has Google Analytics, Google Search Console, and a basic customer feedback form on the pricing page. The product is $19/month with a 14-day free trial.

**Walkthrough:**

With limited resources, the team focuses on the highest-volume transition: awareness to consideration. Of 18,000 monthly visitors, only 1,260 (7%) visit any product or pricing page. Google Analytics shows the top 5 landing pages by traffic are all blog posts, and 4 of the 5 have exit rates above 85%. The team does not have session recording tools, so they use Google Search Console to analyze search queries driving traffic to these pages and compare intent.

They discover that their highest-traffic blog post ('How to Write a Newsletter Subject Line') attracts 4,200 monthly visitors, but fewer than 80 click through to any product page. The post has no CTA, no product mention, and no internal links to feature pages. ' Triangulation is limited but directional: high exit rates on blog content combined with user feedback about product awareness. Severity: 15,120 visitors per month who never see the product, multiplied by an estimated 2% would-convert rate and $228 annual value = roughly $69,000 in annual unrealized revenue.

The register recommends adding contextual CTAs and product feature callouts to the top 5 blog posts, starting with the highest-traffic post, and measuring the impact on product page visits over 30 days.
