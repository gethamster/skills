# Examples: Identifying Customer Journey Pain Points and Drop-Offs

## A checkout drop with a specific cause

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an online homeware shop sees a large drop between the shipping step and the payment step of checkout.

**Walkthrough:**

Segmenting the drop shows it is much larger for orders below the free-shipping threshold. Session recordings show shoppers reaching the shipping step, pausing, then leaving. Chat logs include several questions about why shipping costs so much. The finding is written as: shipping cost appears for the first time at the shipping step, and shoppers below the threshold leave when they see it.

The fix shows an estimated shipping cost on the product and cart pages and a note about the free-shipping threshold. The team measures the same step conversion afterward to see whether the drop narrowed.

## Trials that never activate

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a reporting tool has many trial signups, but a large share never create a first report.

**Walkthrough:**

The team splits the drop by signup source and finds it concentrated among users who arrived from a comparison site. Interviews with a few of them show they expected a template gallery and instead found an empty workspace. Support tickets from the same group ask how to connect data before seeing anything.

The finding goes to the onboarding owner: users from comparison sites expect templates and stall at an empty workspace. A sample-data workspace is tested for that segment, and activation is measured the same way before and after.

## A quiet retention drop

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a subscription coffee service has steady cancellations that nobody has examined, because the acquisition funnel looks healthy.

**Walkthrough:**

Cohort analysis shows that many cancellations happen right after the first price change following an introductory discount. Cancellation reasons mention the price, and several reviews say the full price was not clear at signup. The team also finds that customers who changed their delivery frequency before the price change were less likely to cancel.

Two findings go to owners: make the full price clear at signup, and prompt customers to adjust frequency before the introductory period ends. Both are measured against the next cohorts.
