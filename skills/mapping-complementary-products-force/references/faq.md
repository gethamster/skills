# FAQ: Mapping the Complementary Products Force

## What is the complementary products force?

It is the sixth force in the six forces model: the effect of products and services that make yours more valuable when customers use them together. [Brandenburger and Nalebuff](https://www.strategy-business.com/article/12669) call a complementor someone who makes your products and services more rather than less valuable.

## Is the complementary products force really a separate force?

Opinions differ. The six forces model treats it as one. Porter sees complements as a factor that influences the other five forces, according to the [six forces model entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_forces_model). Mapping works either way if you record how each complement affects the other forces.

## How is a complementor different from a partner?

A complementor's product adds value to yours for a shared customer. Some partners, such as resellers, add distribution instead, which belongs under buyer power or channels. Keep the map to products and services customers use together with yours.

## Can a complementor become a threat?

Yes. A complementor that controls something customers cannot do without can act like a powerful supplier, and one that expands its product can become a rival or a substitute. Rate power and incentives for every critical complement.

## What if the most important complement does not exist yet?

Record it as a gap. Missing complements can stall adoption, as Adner's [HDTV example](https://hbr.org/2006/04/match-your-innovation-strategy-to-your-innovation-ecosystem) shows. Options include partnering, co-investing or building the complement yourself.
