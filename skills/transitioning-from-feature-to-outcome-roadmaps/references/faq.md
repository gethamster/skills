# FAQ: Transitioning from Feature to Outcome-Based Roadmaps

## What is the difference between product roadmap outcomes and features?

A feature is something the team builds. An outcome is a measurable change in customer behavior or a business result the feature is meant to cause. An outcome roadmap puts the outcomes at the top level and lists features under them as bets, so a feature can be replaced if it does not move the outcome.

## How long does the transition take?

Plan on at least one pilot period before expanding. Roman Pichler's approach starts with a single outcome for three months, reviews the result, and repeats the pilot if support is still weak before moving to a six to twelve month outcome roadmap. Organizational habits usually take longer to change than the document.

## Is there an outcome based roadmap template to start from?

Several. Pichler's GO product roadmap keeps a date or timeframe on each goal and lists a few features and a metric under it, which suits teams moving away from dated plans. ProdPad's Now-Next-Later format drops dates in favor of time horizons. Choose the one your stakeholders can read without training.

## What happens to features customers were promised?

Keep them, with their dates, as commitments. Map each to the goal it serves, even if that goal is the contract itself. Removing real promises to make the roadmap look outcome-driven damages trust quickly.

## What if leadership still judges the team on features shipped?

Address that before or during the pilot. Marty Cagan describes outcome techniques as a cultural mismatch in companies that still dictate roadmaps of features with release dates. Ask your sponsor to judge the pilot on the outcome and the learning, and report delivery alongside it so nothing is hidden.
