# Examples: How to Emerge from Technician to Entrepreneur

## A photographer hands off editing, then shooting

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a wedding photographer runs a studio with two associate photographers and an editor. She still shoots most weddings and personally edits every gallery, and she has no time to develop the corporate events service she wants to launch.

**Walkthrough:**

She sorts her work. Gallery delivery and album ordering are routine and go to the editor at once. Editing needs a documented style guide, so she writes one with before-and-after examples and has the editor apply it to two galleries under full review. Shooting is judgment-heavy, so she splits it: associates take ceremonies and receptions, while she keeps the first client consultation.

Over the next months she moves the editor from "recommend, then take resulting action" to "act, but advise at once" as galleries meet the standard. Client reviews and the editor's revision count become her feedback. The freed time goes into two Entrepreneur blocks a week, used to design the corporate package and meet event planners.

## An engineer-founder stops being the release gatekeeper

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the founder of a small software company has a team of developers but approves and deploys every release himself. Releases wait for him, and he has no time for customers.

**Walkthrough:**

He writes the release process down, including the checks he runs in his head, and turns them into an automated checklist plus a short manual review. The most experienced developer runs the next releases while he watches, then runs them alone while he reviews the results the next morning.

He keeps one thing: the decision on anything that changes pricing or data retention. Everything else moves to the developer at the highest degree of initiative. He uses the freed hours for customer interviews, and within a few weeks the interviews lead to a change in the product roadmap.

## A carpenter learns to price without being on site

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a carpentry business owner does every site visit and quote because only he knows how to price. He works on site during the day and quotes at night.

**Walkthrough:**

He splits the quoting task into measuring, which a trained crew lead can do with a checklist and photos, and pricing, which he keeps for now. He builds a pricing sheet for the common job types and has the crew lead prepare draft quotes that he checks.

After several months of drafts that need few changes, the crew lead quotes common jobs alone and sends unusual ones to the owner. The owner uses his evenings back to plan the move into fitted kitchens, a line of work that his primary aim and strategic objective point toward.
