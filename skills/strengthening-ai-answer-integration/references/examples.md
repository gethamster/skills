# Examples: Strengthening AI Answer Integration

## Software comparison page

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a project management vendor's comparison page is retrieved for a prompt about the best tools for remote teams, but answers quote competitors and mention the vendor only in a trailing list.

**Walkthrough:**

The editor inventories the page and finds claims like 'teams love our timeline view' with no support. She replaces it with a cited finding from a named independent review and a one-sentence quote from a customer's operations lead describing a specific workflow. A vague line about 'faster onboarding' becomes a specific figure from the vendor's documented onboarding survey, with the sample and date stated. She deletes a paragraph that repeated 'best project management tool for remote teams' four times.

After re-running the same prompts three times on each engine, the page appears earlier in most answers, though one engine shows no change, so she logs that as unresolved.

## Clinic health explainer

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a physiotherapy clinic's article on recovering from a knee sprain is cited occasionally, but answers summarise it in one line near the end.

**Walkthrough:**

The team lists the claims that answer common recovery questions, such as typical timelines and warning signs. Each timeline claim gets a citation to a published clinical guideline rather than a general health blog. A senior physiotherapist provides a short quote explaining when to seek imaging, attributed by name and role. The team adds no new statistics they cannot source, leaving two claims as clearly labelled clinical opinion.

On re-measurement, the clinic's guidance on warning signs is quoted directly in several answers, which the team confirms across repeated runs before keeping the changes.

## Removing legacy SEO copy

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an accounting firm's service page was written years ago for classic search and contains a keyword block listing city names and service phrases.

**Walkthrough:**

The firm's marketer first confirms the page is retrieved for prompts about small-business tax filing in its region. He removes the keyword block and rewrites three padded paragraphs into two plain explanations of the filing process. He keeps the core service terms in the headings so the page stays topically relevant. He then adds a citation to the official tax authority's deadline page next to each deadline the page mentions.

Because he changed several things at once, he treats the improved visibility as a combined effect and plans a single-change test for the next page.
