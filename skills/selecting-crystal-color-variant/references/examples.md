# Examples: Selecting the Right Crystal Color Variant for Your Team

## A small internal tools team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of five developers and a product manager share one room and build scheduling tools for an operations department. A defect means staff re-enter data by hand for an afternoon.

**Walkthrough:**

The lead counts six people who coordinate daily, one team and one location. Criticality is rated as inconvenience, well below the life-critical screen. One team in one office maps directly to Crystal Clear, which is built for that structure. The gap list notes only that Clear would stop fitting if a second site joined.

The team records the decision, sets a review trigger for any new location, and moves on to picking a delivery rhythm.

## A growing payments product

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a fintech product has grown to about thirty people in three squads, all in one building. Defects can cause incorrect charges and regulatory complaints.

**Walkthrough:**

The lead counts three teams and one location, and rates criticality as significant financial loss but not harm to people. The project is well beyond Clear and too large for a modest stretch, so Crystal Orange is the starting point. The gap list records that Orange lacks sub-team structure and design and code verification activities. The lead assigns one person to define squad boundaries and interfaces, and another to introduce design reviews and code verification for payment logic.

The decision note sets a review for when head count approaches the documented upper range for Orange.

## A medical device project that screens out

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of eight is asked to build firmware and a companion app for an infusion pump. A firmware defect could deliver the wrong dose.

**Walkthrough:**

The lead completes the size count, which on its own would suggest Crystal Clear. The criticality rating comes back as potential harm to patients, which triggers the life-critical screen. Because Crystal is documented as not covering life-critical projects, the lead records that the firmware needs a method with formal system validation. The team separates the companion app, whose failures only inconvenience clinicians, and considers Clear for that part alone.

The decision note explains the split so nobody later applies Clear's lighter practices to the firmware.
