# FAQ: Selecting the Right Crystal Color Variant for Your Team

## Which Crystal variants exist?

Cockburn's book contents discuss Crystal Clear, Crystal Orange, Crystal Orange Web, and stretching Crystal Clear to Crystal Yellow. Clear, Yellow and Orange are the ones most teams choose between. The published material in this library is detailed for Clear and Orange and thin for Yellow, so document your reasoning when you pick Yellow.

## How big can a Crystal Orange team be?

One review describes Crystal Orange as suitable for projects involving up to 40 people. The same source notes that Orange lacks sub-team structure and design and code verification activities. If you run Orange near that size, you will need to design those pieces yourself.

## Can a distributed team use Crystal?

It can, but with a known gap. The family is reported as most useful for co-located teams, and Crystal Clear in particular assumes one team in one office. A distributed team should list lost ambient communication as a gap and decide deliberately how to replace it.

## Why is life-critical work excluded?

The Crystal family is documented as not covering life-critical projects because it lacks sufficient system-validation elements. Similar concerns are raised about agile methods in general, with critics arguing they [do not work for systems with criticality, reliability and safety requirements](https://cs.umd.edu/~mvz/pub/agile.pdf). For such systems, choose a method with formal validation.

## How often should we revisit the variant choice?

Revisit it whenever head count, the number of teams, locations or criticality change. Practitioner guidance places the check inside normal operation, where teams [inspect and adjust their way of working through reflection workshops](https://projectmanagementformula.com/crystal-agile-methodology). A written trigger in your decision note makes the review happen on time.
