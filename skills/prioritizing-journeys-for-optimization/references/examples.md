# Examples: Prioritizing Customer Journeys for Optimization

## A subscription business scores its journeys

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a subscription software company has an inventory of about twenty L2 journeys and budget to improve three this year. The leadership team's stated priority is reducing churn.

**Walkthrough:**

The CX lead proposes the four criteria and a one-to-five scale with written anchors. Because of the churn priority, leadership agrees to weight customer pain and retention impact more heavily than feasibility. Four people from product, support, finance, and success score every journey independently from a shared evidence pack.

Illustrative scenario: three journeys after calibration, scored one to five.

| Journey | Business impact | Customer pain | Strategic fit | Feasibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Renew my subscription | 5 | 4 | 5 | 3 |
| Get started | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Update payment details | 3 | 3 | 3 | 5 |

Calibration focuses on "Update payment details," where scores varied most. Support data shows it drives many contacts, while finance had scored it low. The group raises its pain score and adds it to the top tier as a quick win alongside the two longer efforts.

## A hospital system uses a two-by-two matrix

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a hospital system's patient experience team has little time for a full scoring exercise. It needs a defensible way to pick where to start.

**Walkthrough:**

The team plots its L2 journeys on a matrix of patient impact against organizational impact, using complaint themes and operational data for each placement. Three journeys land clearly in the high-high quadrant, including scheduling a specialist appointment.

Two journeys in that quadrant depend on a scheduling system replacement already planned for next year. The team starts with the third and schedules the other two for after the system change, recording the reasoning next to the matrix.

## Missing evidence changes the plan

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a bank's scoring session reaches a journey for small business owners opening an account. Nobody has recent data on it, and scores range from lowest to highest.

**Walkthrough:**

Rather than averaging the scores, the group records the journey as "unscored, evidence missing." The disagreement shows that some scorers assumed the journey was mostly digital and others assumed it ran through branches.

The bank moves the journey into the "research next" tier and funds a short study. At the next scoring cycle, the journey is scored from the new evidence and enters the ranking.
