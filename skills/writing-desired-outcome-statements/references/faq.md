# FAQ: Writing Desired Outcome Statements

## How many statements per job step?

Strategyn's guidance is 5 to 10 per step, adding up to 100 or more across a job and its related categories ([needs framework](https://strategyn.com/customer-needs-through-a-jobs-to-be-done-lens/)). Treat that as a guide to coverage rather than a quota. A step with very few statements usually means the evidence for it is thin.

## Why is the structure so strict?

Because customers will rate every statement for importance and satisfaction, and inconsistent wording changes how they rate. The whitepaper warns that differences in structure, terminology and syntax introduce variability into the ratings ([ODI whitepaper](https://innovationroundtable.com/summit/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Strategyn_what_is_Outcome_Driven_Innovation.pdf)). Using one structure lets the survey compare needs rather than phrasing.

## How is an outcome statement different from a user story?

A user story describes functionality to build for a user. An outcome statement describes how the job executor measures success at a step, with no functionality in it. Many user stories can serve one outcome, and the outcome is what tells you whether they worked.

## What do I do with outcomes that repeat across steps?

Check whether they really are the same. "Minimize the time it takes" at two different steps is usually two different needs, because the object of control differs. If the object is identical, keep the statement at the step where customers described the struggle.

## Can I write outcomes without interviews?

You can draft them, but treat the draft as a hypothesis. Statements written only by the team tend to describe the team's view of the job, including its product. Test them with job executors before they go into a survey.
