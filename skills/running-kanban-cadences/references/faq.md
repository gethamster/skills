# FAQ: Running Kanban Cadences and Feedback Loops

## How do I decide which Kanban cadences to start with?

Start with the daily Kanban meeting and the replenishment meeting. These two cover the essential feedback loops: unblocking current work and deciding what enters the system next. If you coordinate releases to customers, add the delivery planning meeting as your third. Add the service delivery review after 6-8 weeks, once you have enough flow metric data to make the discussion evidence-based rather than opinion-based.

Organizational cadences like the operations review and strategy review are only needed when multiple teams or services need coordination.

## How long should each Kanban cadence take?

The daily standup should take 10-15 minutes for teams of 4-8 people. If it consistently exceeds 15 minutes, you are discussing too many items in depth rather than triaging. The replenishment meeting typically takes 30-60 minutes depending on backlog size and complexity. The delivery planning meeting takes 15-30 minutes if you have clear done criteria.

The service delivery review takes 60-90 minutes because it involves data analysis and improvement planning. Strict timeboxing is more important than getting the exact duration right.

## Should I run Kanban cadences before or after setting WIP limits?

Set WIP limits first, or at least concurrently. Cadences like the replenishment meeting depend on WIP limits to function correctly: you cannot make disciplined pull decisions if there is no defined capacity constraint. The daily standup also benefits from WIP limits because exceeding limits becomes a visible trigger for discussion. See [setting WIP limits](https://tryhamster.com/skills/setting-wip-limits) for guidance on establishing your initial limits.

Once both are in place, the cadences enforce the limits through social accountability.

## What do I do when nobody has anything to say during the daily standup?

This usually means the board is not revealing enough information, or people are interpreting the meeting as a status report where 'nothing new' is a valid response. Switch to walking the board right to left and asking about each in-progress item rather than asking each person to speak. If every item is flowing normally with no blockers and no aging concerns, the meeting should be short, and that is perfectly fine. A 3-minute standup where everything is flowing is a sign of health, not a problem.

## How do I keep the service delivery review from becoming a blame session?

Anchor the discussion on the system, not on individuals. Present data as 'items in this stage averaged 5 days, up from 3 days last month' rather than 'the review step is slow because reviewers are not prioritizing it.' Use cumulative flow diagrams and cycle time charts as neutral artifacts that describe what the system did, not what people did. When identifying causes, focus on process constraints, policies, and capacity rather than individual performance. If blame patterns emerge, the facilitator should explicitly redirect: 'We are looking for system changes, not individual changes.'

## Why does my cadence attendance keep dropping after the first month?

Attendance drops when participants do not see decisions or outcomes resulting from the meeting. The most common cause is producing action items that are never followed up on, which signals that the meeting is performative. Fix this by starting every cadence with a review of the previous session's actions and their outcomes. If attendance is dropping specifically for the daily standup, it may be too long or too focused on reporting rather than flow management.

Ask absent members directly what would make the meeting worth their time, and be willing to restructure based on their feedback.

## Can I combine Kanban cadences with Scrum ceremonies if my organization uses both?

Yes, but be deliberate about which meeting serves which purpose. A sprint planning meeting can incorporate the replenishment function if you are explicit about capacity checks and pull decisions. A sprint retrospective can substitute for the service delivery review if it is data-driven and examines flow metrics rather than just team sentiment. The risk is conflation: if sprint planning becomes pure replenishment without a commitment to a sprint goal, or if the retrospective becomes pure sentiment without data, you lose the benefits of both methods.

Map each meeting to a specific cadence purpose and verify that purpose is being served. See [comparing Kanban and Scrum](https://tryhamster.com/skills/comparing-kanban-and-scrum) for more on integrating the two approaches.
