# FAQ: Kanban Cadences

## Which kanban meetings should we start with?

Start with the daily Kanban meeting and replenishment. The first keeps work flowing and the second controls what enters the system. Add a service delivery review once you have a few weeks of flow data, and leave the operations, risk and strategy reviews until several teams or services need coordinating.

## How long should each cadence take?

Kanban University advises choosing frequency and duration from your own context. The daily meeting should be short enough to hold standing up; reviews take longer because they involve data and decisions. If a meeting routinely runs over, it is probably trying to do another cadence's job.

## What if nobody has anything to say at the daily meeting?

That can be a good sign, if the board is current and nothing is blocked or aging. Walk the board anyway and look at the oldest items, which often reveals quiet problems. If the meeting still adds nothing, shorten it or hold it less often.

## How do we keep the service delivery review from becoming a blame session?

Focus on the system: the flow data, the policies and the SLE. Ask what about the workflow let an item miss its expectation, and avoid asking who was slow. End with an experiment that changes the system.

## Can Kanban cadences run alongside Scrum events?

Yes. Some teams use the Daily Scrum as their board walk and treat Sprint Planning as a form of replenishment. The Kanban Guide says Kanban can be used to augment other delivery approaches. Avoid duplicating meetings: map each cadence's purpose onto an existing event where one fits.
