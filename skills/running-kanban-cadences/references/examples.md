# Examples: Kanban Cadences

## A startup replaces its status meeting

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a five-person startup holds a daily meeting where each person lists what they did yesterday. It runs long, and blocked work is often mentioned only in passing.

**Walkthrough:**

The team turns the daily meeting into a Kanban meeting at the board. The facilitator starts at the Released column and walks left, asking of each card what is keeping it from flowing and who can help. People with nothing to raise stay quiet.

Their Monday planning meeting becomes replenishment: they count open slots under the WIP limits, pick the top options with the product lead and confirm each meets the entry criteria. After a month, they add a short service delivery review every other Friday, using the cycle time chart and the list of aging items. Total meeting time goes down.

## A company with three delivery teams

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a mid-size software company has three teams that each run a daily Kanban meeting. Cross-team dependencies keep causing delays that no single team can see.

**Walkthrough:**

Each team keeps its daily meeting and replenishment. The engineering managers start a monthly operations review that looks at the three boards together, with a focus on items blocked by another team and on shared specialists who are overloaded.

The first review shows that all three teams depend on one database specialist. The group sets a WIP limit on requests to that person and plans to train a second specialist. Findings that affect the product roadmap go to the quarterly strategy review with the leadership team.

## A marketing team adds delivery planning

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a marketing team runs campaign work on a kanban board. Assets are finished on time, but launches slip because nobody coordinates what goes out together.

**Walkthrough:**

The team already has a daily meeting and replenishment. They add delivery planning before each campaign launch, where they decide which finished assets ship together, confirm dates with sales and plan the release order.

Keeping this separate from replenishment helps: replenishment still decides what to start, while delivery planning decides what to release and when. Launch slips become rare, and the service delivery review starts tracking time from asset completion to launch.
