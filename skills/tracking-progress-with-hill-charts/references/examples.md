# Examples: Shape Up Hill Charts

## A dot that stops moving

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a designer and two programmers are building shared saved searches in a six-week cycle. By the middle of the second week they have four scopes on the hill: Save Search, Share, Search List and Permissions.

**Walkthrough:**

Save Search and Search List cross the peak within a few days. Share moves steadily. Permissions sits a third of the way up for three updates in a row, and its note keeps saying the approach is being worked out.

The product lead reads the chart and asks the programmer one question: what is still unknown about Permissions? The answer is that nobody is sure whether shared searches should respect folder access rules, which the pitch did not cover. The lead and the shaper settle it the same afternoon by deciding that shared searches only show results the viewer can already see. The programmer builds a first version the next day and moves the dot past the peak. Without the chart, the question would likely have surfaced in the last week.

## A scope that needs splitting

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team adding reminders to a task app has a scope called Reminders that the programmer places near the peak and the designer places near the bottom.

**Walkthrough:**

The disagreement is the signal. When they talk it through, the programmer is thinking about the scheduling job, which is working, and the designer is thinking about the reminder settings screen, which has no agreed design yet. They split the scope into Reminder Delivery and Reminder Settings.

Delivery goes straight to the downhill side. Settings starts low on the left, and the team now sees that the real open question is on the screen. The designer builds a rough version with plain controls and wires it up within two days. From then on, each dot moves on its own, and the chart shows progress more often.

## Backsliding after a confident update

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team is adding a CSV import to a reporting tool. Early in the cycle, the programmer moves the Parse File scope to the peak after reading the parsing library's documentation.

**Walkthrough:**

A few days later the programmer tries real customer files and finds date formats the library does not handle. The dot slides back to the middle of the uphill side. At the next check-in, the team names the pattern: the scope was moved uphill on the strength of a plan instead of built work.

They agree on a rule for the rest of the project. A dot only enters the final third of the climb once the team has built something that runs against real inputs. Parse File crosses the peak again after the programmer handles the formats in the sample files, and this time it stays there.
