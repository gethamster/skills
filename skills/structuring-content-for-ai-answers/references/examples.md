# Examples: Structuring Content to Appear in AI-Generated Answers

## Restructuring a software features page

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a project management software company has a features page with marketing headings such as "Supercharge your workflow" and long narrative paragraphs. The team wants the page to be used when people ask an assistant which tools suit remote teams, and the page is never cited for that question today.

**Walkthrough:**

The team starts by writing the page's primary question, "Which project management features matter for remote teams?", and four follow-ups: time zones, async updates, integrations and pricing. Each becomes an H2 phrased the way a buyer would ask it.

Under each heading, the first sentence now answers the question with the product named, for example "The product supports async status updates through a daily digest that each team member can schedule in their own time zone." The feature list that was spread across paragraphs moves into a table with columns for the feature, what it does and who it suits.

The pricing section keeps a plain sentence stating the current plans and links to the pricing page. After publishing, the team adds the page's questions to its tracking sheet and checks over the following weeks which passages, if any, show up in answers.

## Turning a clever blog post into answerable sections

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a marketing agency has a well-read blog post on improving email open rates. Its section headings are jokes, the tips are buried in stories, and the post ranks in classic search but does not appear in AI answers to questions about open rates.

**Walkthrough:**

The editor rewrites each joke heading as the question the section actually answers, such as "How should I write subject lines?" and "When should I send marketing emails?". The first sentence of each section now states the advice directly, and the story that used to lead the section moves below it as an example.

Where the old post quoted a statistic without a source, the editor either finds the original study and links it in the same sentence or deletes the figure. A comparison of subject line styles that was written as prose becomes a short table.

The post keeps its voice in the supporting paragraphs. The change is in where the answer sits and how each section announces what it covers.

## Writing a template for a documentation site

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a developer tools company has a few hundred help articles written by different people over several years. Some start with the answer, many start with background, and headings are inconsistent.

**Walkthrough:**

Instead of editing every article by hand at once, the documentation lead writes a template: the title states the task, the first paragraph says what the reader will achieve and any prerequisite, steps go in a numbered list, and a closing section covers common errors with each error message as a heading.

The team applies the template first to the articles that support and sales link to most often, since those answer the questions customers ask. Each rewritten article is checked by reading its first paragraph alone to confirm it makes sense out of context.

The lead tracks a sample of rewritten and untouched articles side by side in the team's AI visibility sheet, so the effect of the template can be judged against a comparison group rather than assumed.
