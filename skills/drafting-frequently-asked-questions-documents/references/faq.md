# FAQ: Drafting the FAQ Section of a PR/FAQ

## How long should the FAQ be?

Colin Bryar and Bill Carr say the FAQ should be five pages or less. Within that limit, spend the space on the questions a decision maker most needs answered, which are usually in the internal FAQ. If you cannot fit the essentials, the product may need to be split into more than one PR/FAQ.

## What is the difference between the external and internal FAQ?

The external FAQ answers what customers and the press would ask, in plain language, such as price, how it works and where to get help. The internal FAQ answers what leaders and other departments would ask: market size, costs, dependencies, legal issues, hard problems and the reasons the product might fail. The internal section is usually longer and does most of the analytical work.

## What if I do not know the answer to a question?

Say so, and say how and when you will find out. An FAQ that marks its unknowns lets reviewers focus on them, and unresolved questions can become conditions on the decision. Guessing and presenting the guess as fact is the one response that makes the FAQ worse.

## Should I name competitors?

Yes, when customers use them today. The FAQ should say what customers currently do about the problem and why they would switch, and that often means naming specific products. Keep the comparison factual and focused on the customer's problem.

## Who should help write the internal FAQ?

The author writes it, but the answers in each department's area should be checked by someone from that department before review. Finance can check the economics, engineering the build questions, legal the regulatory ones. This catches weak answers early and means those reviewers arrive at the meeting already familiar with the idea.
