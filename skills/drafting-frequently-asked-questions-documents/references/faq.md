# FAQ: Drafting the FAQ Section of a PR/FAQ Document

## How many questions should the FAQ section contain in total?

Aim for 20-50 questions total, split roughly evenly between external and internal. Fewer than 20 usually means you have not interrogated the press release deeply enough. More than 50 often means questions are too granular and should be combined. The right number depends on the complexity of the product: a simple feature addition might need 20, while a new product line might need 40-50.

## Should I draft the FAQ before or after writing the press release?

Always write the press release first. The FAQ exists to interrogate the claims and promises in the press release. Without a stable press release, you are generating questions in a vacuum. That said, the press release does not need to be perfect.

Write a complete first draft of the press release, then draft the FAQ. Expect the FAQ process to reveal weaknesses that send you back to revise the press release, which is exactly how the Working Backwards process is designed to work.

## How do I use FAQ-drafting skills to prepare for product manager interview questions?

The FAQ-drafting discipline trains you to decompose any product concept into customer risks and business risks, which is exactly what interviewers test. When given a case study or product critique question, mentally split your analysis into external questions (customer experience, value proposition, competitive positioning) and internal questions (feasibility, cost, measurement, risk). This gives you a structured, comprehensive response instead of a scattered list of observations. Interviewers consistently rate candidates higher when they demonstrate this kind of systematic risk identification.

## What if I genuinely do not know the answer to a question I have written?

Include the question and write an honest answer that states what you do not know, what you would need to find out, and how you plan to find it. For example: 'We do not yet know the expected latency for this integration. Our next step is to build a proof-of-concept with the vendor API by March 15 to measure actual response times under load.' An unanswered question with a clear investigation plan is far more valuable than a fabricated answer. Reviewers respect intellectual honesty and lose trust in authors who bluff.

## How long should each FAQ answer be?

Target 2-6 sentences per answer. Shorter than 2 sentences usually means the question is too simple or the answer is too vague. Longer than 6 sentences usually means the answer bundles multiple claims together and should be split into separate Q&A pairs. The exception is pre-mortem or risk-related answers, which sometimes need 7-8 sentences to describe the risk scenario, the mitigation plan, and the trigger conditions for escalation.

## Should the FAQ section include questions about competitors by name?

Yes, for internal FAQs. Naming competitors directly in the internal FAQ is essential for honest analysis. Questions like 'How does our pricing compare to Competitor X?' or 'What happens if Competitor Y launches this feature first?' force you to do real competitive analysis. For external FAQs, use customer-natural phrasing like 'How is this different from what I already use?' which addresses the competitive question without naming specific competitors, since the press release is written as a public-facing document.
