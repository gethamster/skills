# FAQ: Product Backlog Management and Refinement

## How much time should backlog refinement take?

There is no fixed rule, and the Agile Alliance's definition does not set one. Most teams hold one regular session per sprint, sized so the top of the backlog stays ready for the next sprint or two. If sprint planning keeps running long, refine more.

## Who can add items to the product backlog?

Anyone can propose items, but the product owner decides whether they go in and where. Keeping one accountable owner prevents the backlog from reflecting whoever asked last. Many teams use a simple intake queue that the product owner reviews regularly.

## Do all backlog items have to be user stories?

No. User stories work well for user-facing features, but bugs, technical work and experiments can be written as short outcome statements. The test is whether the item says who benefits and how the team will know it is done.

## How far ahead should the backlog be detailed?

Detail only what the team is likely to work on in the next sprint or two. Items further down can stay as one-line ideas or epics. Detail written too early is often wrong by the time the work starts.

## How do we handle stakeholders who disagree about priority?

Make the ordering criteria visible, bring the disagreement to the product owner, and let them decide and explain. A shared scoring method such as cost of delay can help structure the discussion.
