# Examples: Product Backlog Management and Refinement

## Cutting an overgrown backlog down to size

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product owner inherits a backlog of about 400 items built up over several years. Sprint planning takes most of a day because nobody trusts the order, and stakeholders keep messaging developers directly.

**Walkthrough:**

The product owner sorts items by last update and archives everything untouched for over a year that no stakeholder claims when asked, which removes about half. Duplicates are merged. The remaining items are grouped under a handful of product themes, and only the top two sprints' worth are refined in detail.

The product owner shares the new top of the backlog at the next sprint review and explains the order. Direct requests to developers drop because stakeholders can see where their items sit.

## Splitting an epic into sprint-sized stories

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the top of the backlog holds one item, "Customers can manage their subscription." The team estimates it at several sprints of work.

**Walkthrough:**

In refinement, the team splits by user-visible path: view current plan, upgrade, downgrade, cancel, and update payment details. Each becomes a story with acceptance criteria. Upgrade is ordered first because it earns revenue; cancel is ordered second because support handles it manually today.

The team estimates the five stories with Planning Poker. A wide spread on "downgrade" reveals an unresolved question about refunds, which the product owner takes to finance before the next session.

## Making technical work visible

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: developers keep a private list of technical improvements they never get time for, while the product owner orders only feature work.

**Walkthrough:**

The team adds its top three technical items to the product backlog, each written to say who benefits: for example, "Developers can deploy without a manual database step, so releases stop needing an evening window." The product owner can now weigh them against features.

One item moves near the top once the product owner sees that manual releases delay every feature. The private list is retired.
