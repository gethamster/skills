# Examples: Building Guilds for Cross-Tribe Knowledge Sharing

## A web guild started from repeated questions

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: web developers in four tribes keep asking the same questions in chat about build tooling and accessibility checks. Each tribe has solved some of them differently.

**Walkthrough:**

A senior web developer volunteers to coordinate, and her manager agrees she can spend a small part of each week on it. Three others from different tribes join as seed members. The charter says the guild shares practices and tools for web development, is open to anyone, and makes shared decisions by written proposal.

The first session compares how three tribes run accessibility checks and ends with a draft shared checklist. The guild's channel becomes the default place for web questions, and answers are collected into a short guide. After a few months, most members never attend a session but use the guide and the channel daily, which the coordinator counts as a success.

## A guild that splits by region

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a testing guild has members in two offices several time zones apart. Meetings suit one office and exclude the other, and attendance from the second office has fallen to almost nobody.

**Walkthrough:**

The guild forms two regional sub-guilds, each with its own coordinator and sessions at a sensible local time. They share one channel and one space for guides. Once a quarter, a cross-site session is held at a time that works for both, recorded for anyone who cannot join.

A yearly in-person unconference, funded by a sponsor, brings the whole guild together to agree its priorities for the coming year. Engagement from the second office recovers, and both regions now contribute to the shared guide.

## Closing a guild that has done its job

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a guild formed to migrate every team to a new deployment tool. The migration is finished, and sessions have had no topic for two months.

**Walkthrough:**

The coordinator asks members whether they would miss the guild. Most say the migration guide was valuable but the meetings no longer are. The guild archives its guide and decision records, points the channel to the platform team's support channel, and closes.

The closing note says what the guild produced and who used it, and it invites anyone to restart a guild if a new shared problem appears. Leadership thanks the coordinator in the next company update.
