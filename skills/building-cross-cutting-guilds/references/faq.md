# FAQ: Building Guilds for Cross-Tribe Knowledge Sharing

## How is a guild different from a chapter?

A chapter lives inside one tribe, groups people with the same skill, and has a lead with defined responsibilities, often including line management. A guild spans the whole company, is open to anyone with an interest, and has a coordinator rather than a manager. Use a chapter for craft and growth within a tribe, and a guild for sharing knowledge across tribes.

## How many guilds should we have?

Start with the few that have obvious pull and let more form when people ask for them. Each guild needs a coordinator with some time, and too many guilds spread that time thin. Close guilds that have lost their purpose so the list stays meaningful.

## What if only a few people come to meetings?

That alone does not mean the guild is failing. Research on Spotify's guilds found that most members follow rather than attend, and still report benefits. Check whether people use the guild's channel and artifacts, and offer more than one way to take part.

## Should guild work count as real work?

Yes, if you want guilds to last. Members of Spotify's guilds reported lack of dedicated time and lack of recognition as major barriers. Agree time for coordinators, and ask managers to include guild contributions in performance conversations.

## Can guilds make decisions?

They can, if the charter says how. Some Spotify guilds used written requests for comments, where any member proposes a technical change and the whole guild reviews it asynchronously. Be clear which decisions are binding on squads and which are recommendations.
