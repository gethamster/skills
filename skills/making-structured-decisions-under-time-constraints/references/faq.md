# FAQ: Making Structured Decisions Under Time Constraints

## Who should be the Decider in a design sprint?

The Decider is the person with final authority over the direction the team will prototype. Choose someone who already owns the product or budget decision in normal work, so their call carries weight after the sprint ends. Appoint them before the sprint begins rather than during the decision session. If they cannot attend, they should formally delegate to someone who can.

## Why are the Heat Map and Straw Poll silent?

Silence lets individual judgement register before group dynamics kick in. If people talk while voting, the first confident opinion tends to steer everyone else. Silent dots make shared interest visible without starting a debate, and a simultaneous silent poll shows genuine leanings. Discussion still happens, but only inside the timeboxed critique.

## Can the Decider combine parts of different sketches?

Yes. The supervotes can go on one solution or be spread across several, and the output can be a combination of compatible elements. The key word is compatible: the chosen parts need to form one coherent customer journey. If they conflict, the Decider should pick one and park the other.

## What if the Decider ignores the Straw Poll?

That is allowed and sometimes correct, because the poll is nonbinding by design. The Decider may weigh risk, strategy or the sprint question differently from the room. What matters is that they explain the reasoning aloud so the team understands the choice. Recorded critique notes make that explanation easier and more credible.

## How long should the decision sequence take?

It depends mainly on how many sketches are on the wall, since the critique is timeboxed per sketch. Budget time for the silent read, the critiques, the poll and a quiet moment for the Decider. Protect the timeboxes rather than the total, because overruns almost always come from one critique turning into a debate. Leave a few minutes at the end to write the prototype brief.
