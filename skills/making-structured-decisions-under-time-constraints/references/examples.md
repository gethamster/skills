# Examples: Making Structured Decisions Under Time Constraints

## Onboarding flow for a budgeting app

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of six has six sketches for a new onboarding flow, and the head of product is the Decider.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator hangs all six sketches and runs a silent Heat Map. Dots cluster on a progress bar in sketch 2 and a bank-connection screen in sketch 5, while sketch 4, the head of engineering's favourite, gets few dots. Speed Critiques stick to those clusters, and the scribe notes a concern that sketch 5 asks for bank access too early. In the Straw Poll, four people pick sketch 2.

The Decider puts two supervotes on sketch 2 and one on the bank screen from sketch 5, with a note to move it later in the flow. The prototype brief combines both elements into one journey.

## When the Decider overrides the room

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B team's Straw Poll strongly favours a polished dashboard concept, but the sprint question is whether buyers will trust automated recommendations.

**Walkthrough:**

The Heat Map shows interest spread across the dashboard sketch and a rougher sketch built around an explanation panel. Critique notes record a repeated objection that the dashboard hides how recommendations are made. In the Straw Poll, most participants choose the dashboard and explain it looks most shippable. The Decider places all three supervotes on the explanation-panel sketch and says aloud that the test must answer the trust question, which the dashboard avoids.

Nobody reopens the debate, and the parked dashboard ideas are listed for later. The team prototypes the riskier concept because it teaches them more.

## Recovering a stalled session

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a facilitator notices that critique of the first sketch has run far past its timebox while two senior people argue.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator stops the discussion, writes both positions on sticky notes, and places them above the sketch. They restart with a visible timer and move to the next sketch when it sounds. The remaining critiques run on time, and each ends with the author's check. The Straw Poll shows a split between two sketches, which matches the argument earlier.

The Decider reads both recorded positions, chooses one direction, and borrows a single element from the other. The session ends on schedule with a written brief.
