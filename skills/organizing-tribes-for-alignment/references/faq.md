# FAQ: Organizing Squads into Tribes for Alignment

## How many squads should a tribe have?

Size the tribe by people rather than squads. The original Spotify paper designed tribes to be smaller than about a hundred people, based on the Dunbar number. Within that cap, a tribe needs enough squads to justify a tribe lead and shared rituals, and few enough that its gathering stays useful.

## What does a tribe lead do day to day?

They keep squads aligned with the tribe's mission, clear obstacles squads cannot clear alone, and represent the tribe to the rest of the company. In the original description, the tribe lead is responsible for giving squads the best possible habitat. They do not assign tasks to squads or run their planning.

## Should squads come before tribes?

Usually yes. Tribe boundaries are best drawn from how squads actually depend on each other, and that data only exists once squads are working. If you are reorganizing everything at once, sketch the tribes from product areas first and plan to redraw them after the first dependency survey.

## When should a tribe be split?

Split when it passes your size cap, when its gathering no longer serves most attendees, or when the dependency survey shows two groups inside it with few links between them. Split along the weakest dependency line and give each new tribe its own mission and lead.

## How do chapters and guilds relate to tribes?

In the original model, a chapter lives inside one tribe and groups people with the same skill across its squads. A guild spans the whole company and is open to anyone. Tribes, chapters and guilds overlap on purpose, so that people have a delivery home, a craft home and optional communities of interest.
