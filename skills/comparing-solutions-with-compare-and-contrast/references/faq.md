# FAQ: Comparing Solutions with Compare-and-Contrast Decisions

## How many solutions should I compare, and is there a maximum?

Compare at least three, because two creates a false binary that usually favors whichever idea was proposed first. Torres suggests [choosing three solutions to explore further](https://producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees) after brainstorming, which keeps the table manageable. If you have many candidates, do a quick screen to drop clearly non-viable options before the formal comparison.

## How long should a compare-and-contrast session take?

Plan for roughly an hour to an hour and a half: a short review of the opportunity and criteria, independent scoring, then discussion and decision. If sessions regularly run longer, your solutions aren't defined crisply enough or you have too many criteria. Remove criteria where every solution scores the same, since they add noise without helping the decision.

## Should I run a product solution comparison framework before or after assumption testing?

Run the comparison first, then use it to build your assumption testing backlog. Testing before comparing risks spending effort on a solution you would have rejected against alternatives. The exception is when one data point would separate two otherwise equal solutions, in which case run a single targeted test to break the tie.

## What if the team can't agree on a solution after completing the comparison?

Persistent disagreement usually points to a contested assumption, where two people hold different beliefs about a factual question such as whether customers will pay. Identify that assumption and design a fast test to resolve it rather than forcing consensus by authority or vote. If the disagreement is about values, such as speed versus quality, the product leader needs to clarify the strategy.

## How do I present the comparison to stakeholders who weren't in the session?

Lead with the decision, not the process. Summarize the opportunity, the solutions you evaluated, which you chose and the strongest reasons, what you rejected and what would change your mind, and the top assumptions you're testing next. Attach the full comparison table for anyone who wants the detail.

## Why does my comparison keep producing the same winner as whatever solution was discussed first?

This is almost always anchoring. The usual causes are discussing solutions in detail before defining criteria, and scoring as a group instead of independently. Define criteria first, require hidden independent scores revealed at once, and vary the order in which solutions appear in the table.
