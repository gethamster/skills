# Examples: Building Topical Authority Maps for Niche Domination

## B2B payroll software narrowing its territory

**Scenario:**

A payroll software company's blog covers payroll, general HR, recruiting tips and office culture, and no category feels complete.

**Walkthrough:**

The team writes an entity statement naming its audience as finance leads at small and mid-sized employers. Testing each existing subject against that statement, they keep payroll processing, payroll compliance and contractor payments, and move recruiting and office culture to the out-of-scope list. Under payroll compliance they list subcategories by situation, such as hiring in a new region, and write the concepts each page must explain. Coverage marks show most compliance subcategories are only partly covered, so the next quarter's work shifts toward expanding existing pages.

The recruiting posts stay live but stop receiving new investment.

## Independent physiotherapy clinic planning local content

**Scenario:**

A physiotherapy clinic wants to publish helpful content but has been writing whatever patients ask about that week.

**Walkthrough:**

The owner drafts the entity statement: a clinic that treats sports and desk-work injuries for people in its city. From searches and results pages, recurring themes group into categories for common injuries, recovery and rehab, and what to expect from treatment. Each injury subcategory gets a concept list covering causes, self-care, warning signs and when to book, rather than a length target. General fitness programming goes on the out-of-scope list because it does not tie back to treatment.

The resulting map gives the part-time writer a clear brief for each piece.

## Developer tools startup finding room for original research

**Scenario:**

A startup selling an API monitoring tool has a map-less blog competing against large, established documentation sites.

**Walkthrough:**

The team builds categories around monitoring setup, incident response and performance tuning, each defined in one sentence referencing the engineers they serve. While breaking categories into subcategories, they flag the ones where their own anonymised product data could support original findings. Those flagged subcategories are prioritised over ones where they could only restate existing tutorials. Coverage marks reveal that incident response has no pages at all, which becomes the first handoff to the keyword clustering work.

The map is reviewed when the product adds a new capability, not when keyword suggestions change.
