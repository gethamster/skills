# FAQ: Building Topical Authority Maps for Niche Domination

## What is the difference between a topical authority map and a keyword list?

A keyword list records demand; a topical authority map records decisions. The map states what you will own, how that subject is divided into categories and subcategories, and what each subcategory must explain. Keywords are used as evidence when drawing category boundaries, but they do not define them. That is why the map stays stable while keyword data shifts.

## How detailed should the subcategories be?

Split until each subcategory represents a distinct problem, use case or comparison with its own set of concepts to explain. Stop when a further split would leave a subcategory holding a single thin question. Detail beyond that belongs in the keyword clustering and page briefs that follow. A useful check is whether a writer could be handed one subcategory and produce a complete piece from its concept list.

## Should I look at competitors before building the map?

Look at search results for evidence, but define your own scope before running a formal competitor comparison. The SEO Handbook treats your own coverage model and competitor gaps as [two separate levels of analysis](https://seohandbook.co.uk/content-strategy/content-gap-analysis). Building the map first gives the competitor audit something to measure against. Otherwise you risk copying another site's territory.

## Why use concepts instead of word counts in briefs?

Concepts give an objective test of completeness, while word counts reward padding. Majestic's [SEO in 2023 Preview](https://blog.majestic.com/training/seo-in-2023-preview) makes the case for covering the concepts and questions behind keywords rather than hitting a fixed length. With a concept list, editors can check whether each idea is explained. Length then follows from what the topic genuinely requires.

## How often should the map change?

Change it when the business changes: a new audience, product line or service area. Routine keyword movement should update coverage marks and briefs, not the scope boundary. Keep a dated change log so teams understand why categories were added or removed. A map that is rewritten every month is usually tracking tool output rather than strategy.
