# FAQ: Sketching Competing Solutions Individually

## Do I need to be good at drawing to sketch a solution?

No. GV describes the sketch as a process that [emphasizes critical thinking over artistic ability](https://gv.com/sprint). Boxes, stick figures and clear words are enough. What matters is that someone else can read the idea and understand how it works.

## Why sketch alone instead of brainstorming as a group?

Group brainstorming tends to converge on the first confident idea or a compromise. The sprint instead gives each person responsibility for an independent solution, and practitioner guides put it bluntly: [no group brainstorming, no whiteboard consensus](https://designelite.co/en/blog/design-sprint). Working alone together produces several competing concepts for the team to compare later.

## How long should the Solution Sketch take?

Sources differ. One summary allows [30-90 minutes](https://pulserevops.com/sales-book-summaries/bs0194), while a university sprint team reports [finishing its detailed solution in a shorter window](https://ideacenter.nd.edu/news-and-events/news/the-power-of-the-google-ventures-design-sprint). Choose a time box that fits your agenda and announce it before the step begins.

## Should everyone on the sprint team sketch?

Yes. Even if the problem is divided up, one book summary notes that [everyone should sketch a solution for the problem](https://parkerklein.com/notes/sprint). People outside design often bring the domain knowledge that makes a concept realistic. Leaving someone out also removes one competing option from the wall.

## What happens to the sketches once they are finished?

They are titled, kept anonymous and posted side by side. The team [reads through each sketch](https://voltagecontrol.com/blog/austin-workshop-w-jake-knapp) before any discussion, then moves into a structured decision sequence to pick what to prototype. That sequence is covered in [making structured decisions under time constraints](https://tryhamster.com/skills/making-structured-decisions-under-time-constraints).
