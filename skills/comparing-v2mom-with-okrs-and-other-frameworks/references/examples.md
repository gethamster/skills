# Examples: V2MOM vs OKRs and Other Goal-Setting Frameworks

## A growing company with a direction problem

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company of about one hundred and fifty people has run OKRs for a year. Teams hit most of their key results, but product and sales keep pulling in different directions, and leaders argue about priorities at every planning meeting.

**Walkthrough:**

Interviews show the problem is direction, not execution. Teams are good at hitting targets they set, but the targets do not add up to a shared strategy, and nobody knows which priority wins in a conflict. The leadership team adds an annual company V2MOM with ranked values and methods. OKRs stay, but each quarterly objective now has to cite the V2MOM method it serves. After the first quarter, several team OKRs that served no method are dropped.

## A team that needed execution focus, not more direction

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a department has a clear, well-understood V2MOM, but at mid-year reviews nobody can say whether the methods are working, because the measures section says things like "improve partner engagement."

**Walkthrough:**

The diagnosis points to execution focus. Rather than replacing V2MOM, the department adds quarterly OKRs for its top three methods, each with measurable key results reviewed monthly. The V2MOM keeps its job of setting direction and ranking priorities. The department also rewrites its V2MOM measures with baselines, targets and dates so the two documents agree.

## A small team that did not need a framework yet

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a founding team of six is considering both V2MOM and OKRs after reading about how larger companies plan.

**Walkthrough:**

The diagnosis finds no alignment problem: everyone sits together, hears every decision and agrees on priorities. The founders write a single one-page company V2MOM to force themselves to rank priorities and name risks, and skip the cascade and OKRs entirely. They set a review point: revisit the choice when the team adds a second product group or when new hires start asking what the priorities are.
