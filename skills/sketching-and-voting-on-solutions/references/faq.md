# FAQ: Design Sprint Exercises: Sketching and Voting

## What is Crazy 8s in a design sprint?

Crazy 8s is a fast sketching exercise in which each person folds a sheet into eight frames and sketches eight variations in eight minutes. Google's Design Sprint Kit calls it a core method and says its goal is to push beyond the first idea, which is often the least innovative ([Design Sprint Kit](https://designsprintkit.withgoogle.com/methodology/phase3-sketch/crazy-8s)). In the published sprint it is the third step of the four-step sketch, between rough ideas and the solution sketch.

## How does dot voting work in a design sprint?

There are two kinds of dots. Small dots make the heat map: everyone silently places one to three beside every part of any sketch they like. Large dots are used for the straw poll, where each person places one on a favorite at the same moment, and for the Decider's supervote ([Design Sprint guide](https://www.character.vc/guide/design-sprint)). The small dots show interest; only the supervote decides.

## Why are the solution sketches anonymous?

Anonymity keeps the critique focused on the idea. When authors are known, people defer to seniority or pitch their own work, and Jake Knapp found that consensus built that way tended to compromise ([Knapp, 2012](https://jakek.medium.com/the-product-design-sprint-a-five-day-recipe-for-startups-84fde3e97d79)). The sketcher is invited to add anything missed only at the end of the critique of their sketch.

## What if the Decider picks an idea the team did not vote for?

That is allowed, and the checklist says the team prototypes what the Decider chooses. The heat map, critique notes and straw poll are there so the Decider sees the team's view before choosing. If the Decider consistently ignores that view, raise it at a break, since it can undermine trust in the rest of the week.

## What is a Rumble?

A Rumble is a test of two or three competing prototypes when the winning ideas conflict and cannot fit into one. Each prototype gets a fake brand name, chosen with a quick note-and-vote, so customers can talk about them separately on Friday. Use it when the Decider's supervotes point to different answers to the same question.
