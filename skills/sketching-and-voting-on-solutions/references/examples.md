# Examples: Design Sprint Exercises: Sketching and Voting

## A team with no designers

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a sprint team at a logistics company is made up of an operations manager, two engineers, a customer support lead, a salesperson and the Decider, the head of product. Nobody has sketched a user interface before, and two people say they cannot draw.

**Walkthrough:**

Before the four-step sketch, the Facilitator shows an example solution sketch made of boxes, arrows and headlines, and says the sketches only need to communicate. During Crazy 8s, the support lead fills all eight frames with variations on one idea: a delivery status page that explains delays in plain language. Her solution sketch uses three sticky notes with a short headline on each and a title, "Tell me why it's late."

On Wednesday the heat map shows a thick cluster of dots on her middle panel, and on an engineer's idea of sending a text before a delay happens. The Decider puts two supervotes on the status page and one on the proactive text, and the team combines them into one prototype, since the two ideas fit the same flow.

## Splitting the vote between two directions

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a subscription meal kit team is sprinting on the moment a customer decides whether to skip a week. The solution sketches fall into two camps: one offers a discount to stay, and the other lets customers swap recipes without leaving the page.

**Walkthrough:**

The straw poll is split almost evenly between the two camps. The Decider's supervotes also land on both. The Facilitator asks whether the two ideas fit one prototype, and the team agrees that showing a discount and a recipe swap at the same moment would make it impossible to tell which one worked. They choose a Rumble: two prototypes of the same screen, each with a different approach and a different fake brand name. Friday's test will show customers both and compare their reactions.

## A critique that keeps drifting

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: during a speed critique, the most senior engineer starts explaining why each idea would be hard to build, and the discussion on the second sketch runs well past its three minutes.

**Walkthrough:**

The Facilitator thanks him, writes "build effort" on a sticky note above the sketch as an objection, and restarts the timer for the next sketch. At the break, the Facilitator asks the engineer to write feasibility concerns on sticky notes during the rest of the critique, where the Decider will see them before the supervote. The critique finishes on time, and the Decider takes the concerns into account when choosing.
