# FAQ: Selecting the Right MVP Type for Your Idea

## How do I choose the right type of MVP when multiple risks feel equally important?

Force-rank by asking: 'If this assumption is wrong, does anything else matter?' Demand risk almost always outranks other risks, because a perfectly built product that nobody wants is worthless. If two risks genuinely feel tied, test the cheaper one first. The goal is to eliminate risks sequentially, not to find the one perfect experiment that resolves everything. Run the faster, cheaper test, learn from it, and then address the next risk with a follow-up MVP.

## Can I combine multiple types of MVP into one experiment?

You can run separate MVP experiments in parallel if they test different assumptions, but do not merge two types into a single experiment. For example, you could run a landing page MVP to test demand while simultaneously running a concierge test with a different group to validate the workflow. The key rule is that each experiment should have one success metric and test one assumption. Hybrid experiments produce ambiguous results because you cannot attribute the outcome to either format.

## How long should MVP type selection take before I start building?

The selection process described in this skill should take 1-2 hours for a small team and up to half a day if you need to align stakeholders. If you are spending days debating the format, you are likely disagreeing about the riskiest assumption, not the MVP type. Resolve the assumption disagreement first by reviewing customer evidence together. Once the riskiest assumption is clear, the format usually follows obviously from the risk-to-type mapping.

## Should I select the MVP type before or after conducting customer discovery interviews?

After. Customer discovery interviews, covered in [conducting customer discovery interviews](https://tryhamster.com/skills/conducting-customer-discovery-interviews), provide the evidence you need to identify and rank your riskiest assumptions. Without that evidence, MVP type selection becomes a guessing game. If you have not done customer discovery yet, your first step is not an MVP.

It is a conversation with potential customers. The exception is if you have strong demand signals from other sources, like existing waitlists, inbound requests, or market data.

## Why does my MVP keep growing beyond its original scope?

Scope creep in MVPs usually happens for one of three reasons. First, the scope boundary was not written down explicitly before the build started, so there is no shared reference to point to when someone suggests an addition. Second, the team is emotionally attached to the idea and unconsciously inflates the MVP to match their vision of the final product. Third, early feedback from test users introduces new feature requests that feel urgent.

The fix is structural: write the scope boundary in Step 6, share it with all stakeholders, and enforce a rule that any proposed addition requires removing something of equal effort. This creates a real cost for scope expansion.

## When is a piecemeal MVP a better choice than a concierge MVP?

Use a piecemeal MVP when you want to test the end-to-end customer experience at moderate scale without dedicating a person to manual delivery. A concierge MVP requires a human to serve each customer individually, which limits you to 5-15 customers and produces qualitative insight. A piecemeal MVP built from tools like Zapier, Airtable, and Stripe can serve 50-100 customers with minimal ongoing effort, producing quantitative data on conversion and retention. Choose concierge when you need deep qualitative understanding of the workflow.

Choose piecemeal when you need broader quantitative validation.

## How do types of MVP relate to the build-measure-learn cycle?

MVP type selection is the critical decision that determines the shape of the entire build-measure-learn cycle. The type you choose dictates what you build (a landing page, a manual service, a front-end shell, a coded feature, or a tool integration), what you measure (conversion, satisfaction, completion, retention, or cost), and what you learn (whether people want it, whether it works, or whether you can deliver it). Every cycle through the loop in the [Lean Startup](https://tryhamster.com/methods/lean-startup) framework should begin with a conscious choice about MVP type, because the same idea may need different formats at different stages of validation.
