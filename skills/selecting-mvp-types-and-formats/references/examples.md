# Examples: Types of MVP: How to Choose the Right Format

## Choosing a landing page for a demand question

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team believes independent bookshops would pay for a service that runs their online events, such as author talks. They do not know whether bookshop owners see online events as worth paying for.

**Walkthrough:**

The question is about demand, so the shortlist is a landing page, a short explainer video and direct outreach. The team picks a landing page with a clear price and a "book a call" button, since a booked call is a stronger signal than an email address. They rule out the video because the service is easy to describe in words.

They drive traffic from bookseller newsletters and forums. Enough owners book calls to pass the mark, and the calls reveal that owners care most about ticket sales. The team records the decision and plans a concierge MVP next, running events by hand for a few shops.

## Concierge before Wizard of Oz for a tutoring service

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a founder wants to match parents with tutors based on a child's learning style. The riskiest assumption is that the matching improves outcomes enough for parents to pay a premium.

**Walkthrough:**

A landing page would only test interest in the promise. The founder needs to know whether the matching itself works, so the shortlist is concierge and Wizard of Oz. She chooses concierge: she interviews each family, picks a tutor by hand and checks in after each session. Parents know she is doing the matching, which lets her ask why each match worked or not.

After a few months, the matches that worked share clear patterns she can write down as rules. Only then does she plan a Wizard of Oz MVP, a questionnaire that appears to produce an automatic match while she still makes the final choice using those rules.

## Piecemeal versus single-feature for a booking tool

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team has validated demand for a booking and payment tool for yoga teachers who rent studio space by the hour. They now need to know whether teachers will run their classes through it week after week.

**Walkthrough:**

The question is sustained use, so the candidates are piecemeal and single-feature MVPs. A piecemeal version would combine a scheduling tool, a payment link and a shared spreadsheet. A single-feature version would build the booking page properly and leave payments for later.

The team picks piecemeal, because payment is part of the habit they want to test and existing tools can handle it. They rule out the single-feature version for now and note it as the likely next step if teachers keep using the stitched-together service.
