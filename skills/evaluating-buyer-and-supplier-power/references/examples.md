# Examples: Evaluating Buyer and Supplier Bargaining Power

## Two buyer segments, two very different ratings

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company sells packaging to food producers. It has 5 large national customers and about 200 small regional ones. Leadership believes "buyers are powerful" and wants to know whether that holds across the business.

**Walkthrough:**

The team segments buyers first. For the national customers, concentration is high, each runs formal tenders, switching is easy because packaging specifications are standard, and price information is complete. Interviews with two procurement managers describe competitive tenders every contract cycle. Buyer power for this segment is rated high.

For the regional customers, orders are small, packaging is a minor cost for them, and interviews show they value delivery reliability and rarely compare prices. Buyer power there is rated low. Because the national segment brings in most of the revenue, the overall buyer power rating is high, but the assessment names the regional segment as a place where margins are protected.

## A single critical supplier

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a hardware startup depends on a specialized sensor chip available from 2 manufacturers, one of which supplies most of the market.

**Walkthrough:**

The supplier power evaluation looks at concentration (two suppliers), input criticality (the product cannot work without the chip), substitute inputs (none suitable today) and switching costs (a redesign and recertification). Supplier interviews and the engineering team confirm that moving to the second manufacturer would take months. Supplier power is rated high, with a note that the second manufacturer is expanding capacity, which could lower it over the analysis horizon. The assessment flags qualifying the second supplier as an option for synthesis.

## Buyers who could build it themselves

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a data tools vendor sells to large retailers, several of which have in-house engineering teams.

**Walkthrough:**

Interviews reveal that two customers have built internal prototypes of similar tools and use them in negotiations. The threat of backward integration becomes the main driver of buyer power for the large-retailer segment, even though neither customer has replaced the product. The team records the prototypes as evidence and rates buyer power for that segment as high, with the direction marked as rising.
