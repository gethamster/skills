# FAQ: Evaluating Buyer and Supplier Bargaining Power

## What is the difference between buyer power and supplier power?

Buyer power is the ability of customers to push prices down or demand more for the same price. Supplier power is the ability of input providers to raise prices or limit quality and availability. They are separate forces with different drivers, so rate them separately.

## What drives buyer bargaining power?

Common factors include how concentrated buyers are relative to sellers, their switching costs, how much price information they have, the availability of substitutes and how price-sensitive they are. The ability to make the product themselves also gives buyers bargaining power. The [five forces article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter%27s_five_forces_analysis) lists the standard factors.

## What drives supplier bargaining power?

Supplier concentration, how differentiated and critical the inputs are, whether substitute inputs exist, what it costs you to switch suppliers and whether suppliers could integrate forward into your business. A single supplier of a critical input is the classic high-power case.

## Why segment buyers and suppliers?

Because power varies across groups. A few large accounts may negotiate hard while many small customers accept list prices. One rating for all buyers averages these into a number that describes no real segment, so segment first and weight each segment by its share of revenue or cost.

## What if customers will not agree to interviews?

Use other evidence: your own sales and procurement records, win-loss notes, public tender documents, and conversations with channel partners who see many customers. Mark the resulting rating with lower confidence and note what evidence is missing.
