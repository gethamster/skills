# FAQ: Building a Product Idea Bank for the GIST Ideas Layer

## How is an idea bank different from a backlog?

A backlog usually implies the items will be built, in order. An idea bank holds hypotheses, and most will be parked or fail a test. Gilad lists idea banks rather than product backlogs as one of GIST's principles. Items leave the bank for the board only when evidence says they are worth testing.

## Who can add ideas?

Anyone. Gilad's guidance is that all ideas are welcome, whether they come from research, customers, management, the team or analytics. What changes is that every idea goes through the same triage and scoring, so the source does not buy it a place on the plan.

## How big should the bank get?

It can be as large as it needs to be. Gilad writes that an idea bank can hold hundreds of ideas indefinitely. The candidate list is what needs to stay small, and he describes it as typically no more than a few dozen.

## Should we ever delete ideas?

Rarely. Park ideas instead, with the reason and any evidence. When the idea comes back, the history saves the team from repeating the same debate or test. Removing true duplicates and ideas tied to features that no longer exist is fine.

## How much time should triage take?

Only a few minutes per idea. Gilad recommends shallow assessment for new ideas: rough estimates, quick calculations and easy data lookups. Deeper evaluation happens only for candidates that are close to being tested.

## Do we need a special tool?

No. A shared spreadsheet or a simple database works, and Gilad names both. Choose something everyone can reach and add to quickly. The discipline of weekly intake matters more than the tool.
