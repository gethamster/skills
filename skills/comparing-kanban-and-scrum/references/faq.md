# FAQ: Kanban or Scrum

## How do we compare Kanban and Scrum if the team has used neither?

Start from the work. Look at how requests arrive, how far ahead you can plan and what stakeholders need. Then read the Scrum Guide and the Kanban Guide, which are both short, and match their practices to what you found.

## Is Scrumban a real method or just a compromise?

Scrumban has a specific origin: the Agile Alliance glossary credits Corey Ladas's book Scrumban with describing it as a way to move from Scrum toward a pull-based kanban system. In practice, teams use the name for many combinations. Whatever you call it, write down which rules you follow so the hybrid stays intentional.

## Can we use Kanban flow metrics if we choose Scrum?

Yes. Cycle time, throughput and work item age work inside Sprints and can help decide how much to select at Sprint Planning. The Kanban Guide describes Kanban as something that can augment other delivery approaches.

## What if half the team wants Kanban and half wants Scrum?

Look for the reason behind each preference; it often reflects different kinds of work within the team. If the work really splits, consider separate lanes or separate setups for each kind. Agree on a trial with shared signals and review it together.

## What if our organization mandates Scrum but our work fits Kanban?

Present the evidence from your work history and propose a trial that keeps what the organization needs, such as regular reviews and reporting. Many Kanban practices, such as WIP limits and flow metrics, fit inside Scrum. The goal is a setup that works for the team and still meets the organization's reporting needs.
