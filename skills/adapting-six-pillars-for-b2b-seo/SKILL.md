# B2B SEO Six Pillars: Adapting the Framework

> Adapt the B2B SEO six pillars approach: map pillar pages and clusters to buying jobs, buying groups and niche queries with low volume and high intent.

## Overview

B2B SEO with the six pillars means running Inayatullah's six pillars (mapping, anticipating, timing, deepening, creating alternatives and transforming) against a market where purchases are made by groups, take a long time, and start from searches that few people make. The [Six Pillars Framework](../../methods/six-pillars-framework/METHOD.md) covers the pillars themselves. This skill covers what changes when the audience is a buying group instead of a single consumer, and how that shapes your pillar pages and clusters.

Three things change. The first is the unit of research. Gartner describes B2B buying as a nonlinear process in which customers loop across six "buying jobs": problem identification, solution exploration, requirements building, supplier selection, validation and consensus creation ([Gartner, The B2B buying journey](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey)). Each job produces different searches, so a B2B pillar content strategy maps content to jobs instead of to a simple funnel. The second is the audience inside one account. Gartner also notes that buying groups have many members with different goals, each doing their own research. The technical evaluator, the budget holder and the daily user may all reach the same pillar page, and each needs a different cluster page.

The third change is how you judge a keyword. B2B queries often have little measurable volume, but the people behind them may be the exact buyers you want. Search volume alone will push a B2B team toward broad, educational topics that attract students and job seekers. This skill replaces volume as the main filter with fit to a buying job and to the problems your customers describe.

The deepening pillar matters most here. Buyers in a committee test claims against each other, and thin content that repeats what every vendor says does not help them build consensus. Google's guidance on [helpful, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) asks whether a page provides "original information, reporting, research, or analysis", and B2B pillar pages are where a company's own data, implementation experience and customer evidence belong.

The output is a B2B pillar worksheet: each pillar page with its buying job, the roles it serves, its cluster pages, the next step each page offers, and the internal links that move a reader from one job to the next.

## How It Works

Start from the buying jobs. For each job, collect the questions buyers ask while doing it. "We need to do something" produces problem and cost-of-inaction searches. Solution exploration produces category and approach searches. Requirements building produces checklists, integration questions and security reviews. Supplier selection produces comparisons and alternatives. Validation produces proof: case studies, references, implementation detail. Consensus creation produces material one buyer can forward to another, such as a business case or a summary for finance.

Next, decide which jobs deserve a pillar page. A pillar page works when a job has enough distinct questions to support a set of cluster pages. HubSpot's topic cluster report suggests starting by mapping "five to ten core problems" that your buyer persona has, grouping them into broad topics and building each out with keyword research ([HubSpot, Topic Clusters](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/53/assets/hubspot.com/research/reports/Topic%20Clusters%20SEO%20Report.pdf)). In B2B, those problems should come from sales calls, support tickets and lost-deal reviews as well as from keyword tools, because the tools under-report niche professional queries.

Then segment clusters by role. One pillar page can hold clusters for different members of the buying group: an integration guide for the technical evaluator, a cost and risk page for the budget holder, a workflow walkthrough for the daily user. The pillar page links to all of them and says plainly who each one is for. Buyers do their own research, so each page should answer its question completely without requiring a sales call.

Because buyers loop back across jobs, the internal links should run in both directions. A reader on a supplier comparison page may return to requirements, and a reader on a validation page may need the consensus material. Google's [link best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable) ask for descriptive anchor text and a link to every page you care about from at least one other page, which in B2B also serves as navigation for readers who arrive mid-journey.

Finally, run the other pillars at B2B speed. Anticipation watches for regulation, platform changes and new categories that will generate new buying jobs. Timing follows budget cycles and industry events. Alternatives cover how the buying group itself could change.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Collect buyer questions by buying job

Interview the sales and customer success teams and read recent call notes, support tickets and lost-deal reviews. Sort every question you find under one of Gartner's six buying jobs. Note which role in the buying group asked it and the words they used. Buyer language often differs from the category terms marketing uses, and those differences are keyword opportunities. The output is a question matrix with buying jobs as columns and roles as rows.

### Step 2: Map existing content to the matrix

Take the current-reality map from the [pillar one skill](../mapping-current-reality-with-pillar-one/SKILL.md) and place each existing page in a cell of the matrix. Many B2B sites cluster heavily in problem identification and supplier selection and leave requirements, validation and consensus nearly empty. Mark cells where several pages compete for the same question. Mark empty cells that sales says come up in every deal. These gaps are usually the strongest candidates for new cluster pages.

### Step 3: Choose pillar topics by buying-job fit

Select pillar topics where one buying job, or two adjacent jobs, has enough distinct questions to support a cluster. Check keyword data for each candidate, but do not drop a topic because measured volume is low if sales hears the question often. Prefer topics that connect directly to the problem your product solves over broad educational topics. Write one sentence per pillar stating which job it serves and which roles will read it. If you cannot write that sentence, the topic is not ready to be a pillar.

### Step 4: Build role-segmented cluster maps

For each pillar, list the cluster pages needed for each role in the buying group. Run keyword research on each cluster and group keywords that share search results so each group gets one page, as described in the [semantic keyword mapping skill](../creating-semantic-keyword-maps/SKILL.md). Add vertical or use-case modifiers where your buyers search that way. Give each cluster page one clear question to answer. Record the role, the buying job and the primary query for every page.

### Step 5: Deepen each pillar with your own evidence

Run a causal layered analysis on each pillar topic. List the common claims at the litany level, the systemic reasons buyers struggle, the worldview behind how they frame the purchase, and the metaphor they use for it. Add what only your company knows: implementation data, patterns across customers, failure modes you have seen. This is the material competitors cannot copy and that validation and consensus pages need. Get subject matter experts to review each pillar page before it goes live.

### Step 6: Design next steps and loop-back links

Give each page one next step that fits its buying job: a checklist for requirements, a comparison worksheet for selection, a business case template for consensus. Link each cluster page to its pillar page and to the pages for the jobs before and after it. Use anchor text that names the destination's topic. Check that a reader who lands on any page can reach the consensus material in two clicks.

### Step 7: Measure by buying job and review quarterly

Track organic visibility and engagement per pillar and per buying job, and ask sales which pages prospects mention or share. Treat low traffic on a validation or consensus page as normal if the pages are used in active deals. Each quarter, update the question matrix with new questions from sales, retire pages that no longer match how buyers talk, and check the emerging issues list for new buying jobs.

## Best Practices

- **Source questions from the field.** Sales calls, support tickets and lost-deal reviews show the exact questions buying groups ask. Keyword tools miss many niche professional queries, so treat them as a second source.
- **Write for the whole buying group.** Label cluster pages by role on the pillar page so each member finds their page quickly. A technical evaluator should not have to read a finance summary to find the integration details.
- **Cover the later buying jobs.** Requirements, validation and consensus content is where B2B sites are usually thinnest. These pages get less traffic but are used in real decisions.
- **Put original evidence on pillar pages.** Your implementation data and customer patterns meet Google's question about original analysis and give buyers something to test. Generic category overviews rarely help a committee decide.
- **Link for loops.** Buyers revisit earlier jobs, so link backward as well as forward. Descriptive anchors help both readers and search engines understand the path.
- **Keep one page per question.** When two pages answer the same buyer question, merge them. Overlapping pages compete in search and confuse readers.

## Common Mistakes

- **Choosing pillars by search volume alone**: High-volume B2B topics often attract students and researchers. Check each pillar against the buyer question matrix and keep topics sales hears in every deal, even when measured volume is low.
- **Writing for a single persona**: A pillar page written only for the champion leaves the budget holder and technical evaluator without answers. Build role-specific cluster pages and link them from the pillar.
- **Stopping at supplier comparisons**: Comparison pages get attention, but buyers still need validation and consensus material after the shortlist. Map every buying job and fill the empty cells.
- **Gating the answers buyers need to research alone**: Buyers research on their own before they talk to sales. If the requirements and validation answers sit behind forms, many buyers leave to find them elsewhere. Offer the answer openly and make the next step optional.
- **Measuring every page by traffic**: A consensus page with modest traffic can still be the page that closes a deal. Measure later-job pages by use in deals as well as by visits.
