# Examples: B2B SEO Six Pillars: Adapting the Framework

## A software company with a blog full of awareness posts

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company sells scheduling software to manufacturers. Its blog has about forty posts, almost all general explainers, and sales says prospects never mention them. The buying group is usually an operations lead, an IT lead and someone from finance.

**Walkthrough:**

The team interviews four sales reps and reads the notes from ten recent deals. Sorting the questions by buying job shows that the blog covers problem identification well and has nothing for requirements building, validation or consensus creation. IT asks the same integration questions in almost every deal, and finance asks for a way to compare the cost of the current process with the new one.

They choose two pillar pages. One covers scheduling software for manufacturing and serves solution exploration and requirements. The other covers moving from spreadsheets to a scheduling system and serves validation and consensus. Each pillar gets cluster pages by role: integration guides for IT, a cost comparison worksheet for finance, and workflow walkthroughs for operations. Old explainer posts that answer the same question are merged into the new cluster pages.

The team agrees to judge the new pages by whether sales sees prospects using them, and to check traffic per buying job each quarter.

## A consultancy with very low measured search volume

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small security consultancy serves banks. Keyword tools show almost no volume for the phrases its clients use, and the partners doubt SEO is worth doing.

**Walkthrough:**

The team runs the deepening pillar first. At the litany level, clients talk about audit deadlines. At the systemic level, the pain is that security, compliance and engineering teams hold different parts of the evidence. At the worldview level, clients see compliance as a yearly event. The metaphor they use is "fire drill".

That analysis gives one pillar page about preparing continuous audit evidence, with cluster pages for the compliance lead, the engineering lead and the executive who signs off. Each page draws on the consultancy's own engagement experience, reviewed by a partner. The team accepts that traffic will be small and tracks which pages clients forward inside their organizations.

## A platform whose buying group changed

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a data platform used to sell to engineering teams. Over the past year, sales reports that security and legal now join most evaluations and ask questions the site does not answer.

**Walkthrough:**

The team treats this as an emerging issue that has already arrived. They add security and legal as rows in the question matrix and find that validation and consensus cells are empty for both. Two new cluster pages go under the existing pillar: a security review guide and a data processing summary written for legal readers.

The pillar page gets a short section that tells each role which page to read. Internal links now run from the comparison pages back to the security guide, because sales says evaluations stall there. The next quarterly review checks whether the new pages shorten that stage in sales conversations.
