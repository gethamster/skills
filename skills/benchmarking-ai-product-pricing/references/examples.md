# Examples: Benchmarking AI Product Pricing Against Competitors

## Seed-Stage Writing Assistant

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small team sells an AI writing assistant to marketing teams. Prospects keep saying a general-purpose chat assistant is "good enough", and the founders want to know whether their price is the problem.

**Walkthrough:**

The team builds its competitive set from sales notes: two specialist writing tools, a general-purpose assistant subscription, and a freelance writer as the do-nothing option. It normalizes each to cost per finished draft at light, typical and heavy use. The general assistant is cheapest per draft at every level, but the feature map shows it lacks brand voice controls and team review, which buyers named as essential. The team keeps its price, rewrites its pricing page around those features, and adds a lower tier for single users who only need drafting.

## Document Processing Platform Moving Upmarket

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a document processing company wants to sell to larger enterprises. Its benchmark from a year ago showed it priced near the market, but sales now reports frequent pushback.

**Walkthrough:**

The team refreshes the benchmark and finds that two competitors cut per-page prices and one moved to credits. Normalizing at enterprise volumes shows the company is now priced above the market for high-volume buyers, while still competitive for smaller ones. The feature map shows its governance and audit features are stronger than those of the cheaper vendors. The team adds volume pricing for large commitments and moves audit features into the enterprise tier rather than cutting its list price.

## Developer API Against Model Vendors

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company sells a specialized extraction API built on top of general models. Developers can also call the underlying model vendors directly and build extraction themselves.

**Walkthrough:**

The team treats the do-it-yourself path as the main competitor. It estimates the cost per extraction of calling a model directly, using the vendor's current rates, and adds an honest estimate of the engineering time to build and maintain the prompts and validation. Its own price sits above raw model cost but well below the build-it-yourself total for typical teams. The team publishes a comparison that makes the build cost visible and prices a free tier low enough to let developers test before building.
