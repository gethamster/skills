# Examples: Rotating and Managing Advisory Board Membership

## Staggering terms after a burst of signups

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a startup recruits eight members in its first month, all from the founders' networks. Their six-month terms would all end in the same month.

**Walkthrough:**

The founders realize that when the first group finishes, they will have no members in early calls and nobody to supply fresh first-call data. They keep recruiting through the following months, aiming for a few new members each month, mostly through posts and individual invitations to people who engaged with them.

By the fourth month, members are spread across every stage of the term. When the first group finishes, a later group is already in its prioritization calls, and newer members are in discovery. Synthesis never runs short of new material.

## Being honest when there is nothing to show

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: by the third call, members of a TAB for a voice-agent testing tool are asking what the startup has built. The team has shifted focus to a problem members raised and has little to show yet.

**Walkthrough:**

Instead of filling the call with a demo of unrelated features, the founder tells each member directly: the team heard that testing conversations end to end is their biggest problem, it is working on that, and nothing will be ready for a while. She asks whether she can reconnect when there is something concrete to discuss.

Most members agree and pause their remaining calls. The founder records the pause and the reason in each member's record. Months later, with an early version ready, she writes to each paused member, refers to what they described, and several resume.

## Closing a term and reconnecting later

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an SRE finishes his six months on the TAB of a startup working on incident tooling.

**Walkthrough:**

At the final call, the founder thanks him for his account of an outage where alerts fired but nobody knew who owned the service, and tells him that story changed the team's first release. She sends a t-shirt, asks whether he wants to hear from the team again, and he says yes, when there is something to try.

She marks him as alumni with a closing note. When a beta addressing service ownership is ready, she writes to him, mentions the outage story, and asks whether he would try it. He agrees and introduces a colleague on another team, who joins the TAB as a new member.
