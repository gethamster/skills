# FAQ: Lean Startup Hypothesis Template: Testable Hypotheses

## What is a good lean startup hypothesis template?

Strategyzer's Test Card is a common choice: "We believe that... To verify that, we will... And measure... We are right if..." Jeff Gothelf's Lean UX format, "We believe [this statement is true]. We will know we're [right/wrong] when we see the following feedback from the market," works too. Either is fine as long as every hypothesis names a customer, an observable action, a metric and a threshold.

## What makes a hypothesis falsifiable?

It predicts a specific, observable result that could fail to happen. If the team cannot say in advance which result would count as wrong, the hypothesis is not falsifiable. Adding a named segment, a measurable action, a timeframe and a fail mark usually fixes it.

## What are the value and growth hypotheses?

They are the two main hypotheses Ries distinguishes for a new product. The value hypothesis is about whether the product creates enough value for customers that they use and pay for it. The growth hypothesis is about how new customers will discover and adopt it. Most detailed hypotheses are parts of one or the other.

## How many hypotheses should we test at once?

One per experiment, and only a few at a time across the team. Testing several assumptions in one experiment makes the result impossible to attribute. Rank the list and work from the riskiest assumption down.

## How do I choose the pass mark?

Work back from the business model. Ask what result, if it held at scale, would make the business viable, and set the pass mark near it. Then ask what result would make it clearly unviable and set the fail mark there. Record both before the test starts.
