# Examples: Lean Startup Hypothesis Template: Testable Hypotheses

## From a vague belief to a testable value hypothesis

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a founder says "landlords want an easier way to collect rent." The team wants to start building a rent collection app.

**Walkthrough:**

They start by naming the subject: independent landlords with three to ten units who collect rent by bank transfer and chase late payments by text. Then the action: these landlords will connect their bank account and invite their tenants. Then the measure: the share of recruited landlords who invite at least one tenant within a week.

The Test Card reads: we believe independent landlords with a handful of units will invite their tenants to a rent collection tool. To verify that, we will offer a simple sign-up and invite flow to landlords recruited from local landlord groups. And measure the share who invite a tenant within a week. We are right if at least half do, and wrong if fewer than one in five do. The vague belief is now something a week of evidence can settle.

## Splitting a compound hypothesis

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team writes "Hospital nurses will use our shift-swap app and managers will approve swaps through it."

**Walkthrough:**

A reviewer points out that this is two assumptions about two different people. If the test fails, the team will not know whether nurses did not post swaps or managers did not approve them.

They split it into a value hypothesis for nurses, measured by swaps posted per nurse, and a separate hypothesis for managers, measured by the share of swaps approved in the app instead of by phone. They rank the manager hypothesis higher, because without approvals the app is useless to nurses, and test it first with a concierge version where a coordinator relays approvals.

## Rewriting a growth hypothesis

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team building a tool for student societies believes "the product will spread by word of mouth."

**Walkthrough:**

The team asks what word of mouth would look like as behavior. The answer is that a society treasurer who uses the tool invites the treasurer of another society. They rewrite the hypothesis: treasurers who complete their first month will invite at least one treasurer from another society, and invited treasurers will sign up at a rate high enough that each cohort brings in a new one.

They set a pass mark tied to the growth they need and a fail mark below which they would switch to direct outreach to student unions. Both marks are recorded before the invite feature ships.
