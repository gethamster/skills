# How to decide whether to pivot or persevere

> Decide whether to pivot or persevere by comparing measured customer behavior against criteria you wrote down before the experiment ran.

## At a Glance

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Time to Learn | 1-2 hours to set criteria before a test, plus a short review session after results arrive |
| Outcome | A recorded decision to persevere, pivot, or retest, justified against pre-set criteria and linked to the next experiment. |
| Prerequisites | A written, falsifiable hypothesis about customer behavior, An experiment or MVP that has run with real target customers, Behavioral metrics tied to the hypothesis rather than cumulative totals, Agreement on who owns the final decision |
| Part of | [Lean Startup Framework](../../methods/lean-startup-framework/METHOD.md) |

## Overview

The pivot-or-persevere decision is the output of every Build-Measure-Learn cycle. Eric Ries describes the fundamental activity of a startup as turning ideas into products, measuring how customers respond, and then learning [whether to pivot or persevere](https://alumni.lincolncollege.ac.uk/files/2016/11/The-Lean-Startup-by-Eric-Ries-Book-Summary.pdf). Persevering means continuing the current strategy because the evidence backs it. Pivoting means [changing strategy while retaining what the experiment taught you](https://tryhamster.com/methods/lean-startup). For the history and definition of the wider method, see the [Lean Startup Framework](https://tryhamster.com/methods/lean-startup-framework) page; this page is about making the call itself.

The decision sounds simple, but it is where many teams quietly fail. Results rarely arrive clean, the team has invested weeks in the current direction, and every stakeholder can find a number that supports their preferred story. Without criteria agreed in advance, teams tend to rationalize weak results and postpone a change they need, a failure mode highlighted in [analyses of how the loop breaks down](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/lean-startup-ries-build-measure-learn-mvp). A [2019 review of Lean Startup's limits](https://gavinpublishers.com/article/view/the-limits-to-lean-startup-for-opportunity-identification-and-new-venture-creation) lists pivoting alongside hypothesis design and MVP scoping as areas where implementations often go wrong.

Doing it well matters. A study comparing business planning with lean practices found that pivoting based on customer feedback was one of the lean startup activities [correlated with venture performance](https://digitalcommons.sacredheart.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1416&context=neje). That is a correlation, not proof that pivoting causes success, and the quality of any pivot depends entirely on the evidence feeding it.

This skill covers four things: setting pivot criteria before the test runs, reading the evidence against the original hypothesis, handling results that neither confirm nor refute it, and spotting the rationalizations that keep teams on a failing path. The inputs are a written hypothesis, a success threshold, a pivot trigger, and behavioral data from the intended customers. The output is a short written decision that names the action and the next experiment.

The decision is only as good as what comes before it. If your hypothesis is vague, start with [designing validated learning experiments](https://tryhamster.com/skills/designing-validated-learning-experiments). If your dashboard is full of cumulative totals, work through [choosing actionable over vanity metrics](https://tryhamster.com/skills/choosing-actionable-over-vanity-metrics) first, because a decision built on a vanity metric will look confident and still be wrong.

## How It Works

The decision is a comparison between three things: the hypothesis you started with, the threshold you set for it, and the behavior you observed. In the learning stage, the input is [measured evidence compared with the original hypothesis and goal](https://tryhamster.com/methods/lean-startup), and the output is a choice to persevere, change direction, or run another test. The comparison only works if the threshold existed before the data did. Setting the success metric and decision threshold before launch lets the team [judge observed behavior against the original hypothesis instead of shifting the standard afterward](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/organization-frameworks/lean-startup-build-measure-learn-loop).

A pivot is not a tweak. Ries frames it as a [fundamental change to the strategy](https://alumni.lincolncollege.ac.uk/files/2016/11/The-Lean-Startup-by-Eric-Ries-Book-Summary.pdf), such as a different customer segment, problem, channel, or revenue model, made because the evidence says the current one will not work. Adjusting a button colour or rewriting onboarding copy is iteration inside a persevere decision. Keeping that distinction clear stops teams from calling every small change a pivot and from avoiding real pivots by making endless small changes.

Evidence lands in one of three buckets, and each maps to an action:

| Evidence outcome | What it looks like | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Supports | Behavior meets or beats the pre-set threshold | Persevere and test the next riskiest assumption |
| Contradicts | Behavior falls at or below the pivot trigger | Pivot strategy, keep what was learned |
| Inconclusive | Result sits between threshold and trigger, or data is too thin | Revise the test and run another cycle |

When evidence supports the hypothesis, the team can [persevere and keep developing the strategy](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/lean-startup-ries-build-measure-learn-mvp). When it contradicts the hypothesis, the team pivots, carrying forward what the experiment revealed about customers. When the result is inconclusive, practitioners [fold the new information into a revised test and repeat the cycle](https://leanstartup.co/resources/articles/lean-startup-method), for example by narrowing the segment, fixing an instrumentation gap, or running longer.

The gap between the success threshold and the pivot trigger is deliberate. It creates an honest middle zone for ambiguous results so that a borderline number cannot be spun into either a win or a loss. How wide to make that zone is a judgment call; a wider zone means more retests, a narrower one means more decisive but riskier calls.

The decision must actually change something. Each cycle should produce knowledge that [changes the next product or experiment decision](https://realgrowthmatters.com/learn/frameworks/lean-startup-build-measure-learn), and collecting data without interpreting it or deciding what to do next is not learning. A useful check: if the next sprint looks the same regardless of which bucket the result landed in, the team never really made a decision.

Finally, the evidence must be behavioral and come from the right people. Stated intentions, praise from friends, and cumulative signup counts all feel like support but say little about whether the strategy works. The decision rests on what the intended customers did.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Write the success threshold before launch

Before the experiment starts, state the metric that tests the hypothesis and the value that would count as support. Tie it to a behavior, such as repeat purchase or weekly active use, not to reach or awareness. Setting this in advance lets you [compare results against the original hypothesis rather than moving the standard afterward](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/organization-frameworks/lean-startup-build-measure-learn-loop). Record it somewhere the whole team can see and cannot quietly edit.

> **Pro tip:** Phrase it as a sentence a skeptic could check: for example, at least 20% of trial accounts create a second project within 14 days.

### Step 2: Set the pivot trigger

Separately from the success threshold, write the result that would force a strategic change. Failing to set pivot criteria up front is how teams end up [rationalizing weak results and delaying a necessary change](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/lean-startup-ries-build-measure-learn-mvp). Leave a gap between the trigger and the success threshold to hold inconclusive results. Also agree on the minimum sample or time window before anyone looks at the numbers.

> **Pro tip:** Ask each team member privately what result would make them give up on this direction, then reconcile the answers before launch.

### Step 3: Collect behavioral evidence from the target customers

Run the experiment with the customers named in the hypothesis, not colleagues or friendly early fans. Capture what they do, since the loop is meant to [measure real behavior and learn what is true](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/lean-startup-ries-build-measure-learn-mvp). Watch for instrumentation failures during the run so you do not discover at review time that key events were never logged. Keep qualitative notes too, as they help explain the numbers later.

### Step 4: Compare results against the written rule

At review, put the pre-set threshold and trigger next to the observed number before any discussion starts. The learning step compares [measured evidence with the original hypothesis and goal](https://tryhamster.com/methods/lean-startup), not with how the team hoped it would go. Check segment and cohort breakdowns only if they were planned; new slices invented after the fact are where rationalization begins. Note any data quality problems openly.

> **Pro tip:** Have someone who did not build the experiment read the result aloud against the rule. It lowers the pull of sunk cost.

### Step 5: Classify the outcome

Place the result in one of three buckets: supports, contradicts, or inconclusive. Supports means the threshold was met, contradicts means the trigger was hit, and anything between them or with too little data is inconclusive. Resist upgrading an inconclusive result to support because it trends in the right direction. If the classification is disputed, the dispute usually reveals a vague criterion to fix next time.

### Step 6: Choose and record the action

Map the bucket to its action: persevere, pivot, or retest. For a pivot, name what changes (segment, problem, channel, or model) and what learning carries forward, since a pivot [retains what was learned from the experiment](https://tryhamster.com/methods/lean-startup). For a retest, name what specifically is different this time so it is not a rerun of the same ambiguous test. Write the decision, the evidence, and the owner in one short record.

> **Pro tip:** Cap retests on the same hypothesis, for example at two, after which an unresolved result is treated as a failure to find support.

### Step 7: Feed the decision into the next cycle

Turn the decision into the next hypothesis and experiment immediately. Each cycle should produce knowledge that [changes the next product or experiment decision](https://realgrowthmatters.com/learn/frameworks/lean-startup-build-measure-learn). If persevering, move to the next riskiest assumption rather than re-proving the one just supported. Review the decision log periodically to see whether your criteria were well calibrated.

## Best Practices

- Write the success threshold and the pivot trigger as two separate numbers before launch. A single threshold forces every borderline result into a win or a loss, while two numbers create an honest zone for inconclusive evidence.
- Base the decision on what customers did, not what they said. Asking customers what they would do and treating stated intent as behavior is a [recognized mistake in running the loop](https://tessl.io/registry/skills/github/wondelai/skills/lean-startup), because people are poor predictors of their own actions.
- Keep the decision owner separate from the experiment builder where possible. The builder has the most sunk cost and the strongest pull toward a generous reading of the results.
- Define what a pivot would change before you need one. A short list of candidate pivots (new segment, new channel, new pricing) makes a contradicting result actionable instead of demoralizing.
- Treat a persevere decision as a prompt to test the next riskiest assumption. Supporting one hypothesis does not validate the business, it just earns the right to test the next thing that could kill it.
- Log every decision with its evidence and criteria. Over time the log shows whether your thresholds are too lenient or too harsh and makes later rationalization harder to hide.

## Common Mistakes

- **Deciding what success looks like after seeing the data.** — Commit the threshold before launch, as the loop requires [metrics and decision criteria tied to the test](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/organization-frameworks/lean-startup-build-measure-learn-loop). If you must change criteria, document why and apply the new criteria only to the next test.
- **Rationalizing a weak result with newly invented segments or explanations.** — Only analyze slices that were planned in advance. Teams without pre-set pivot criteria are the ones that [talk themselves past weak results](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/lean-startup-ries-build-measure-learn-mvp), so treat any unplanned slice as a hypothesis for a new test, not as rescue evidence.
- **Collecting data and never making the call.** — Measuring without completing the learning step is [a common failure of the loop](https://realgrowthmatters.com/learn/frameworks/lean-startup-build-measure-learn). Schedule the review before the experiment starts and end it only when one of the three actions is recorded.
- **Retesting indefinitely because results keep landing in the middle.** — Each retest must change something specific, such as sample, segment or instrumentation. Cap the number of retests per hypothesis so persistent ambiguity is eventually read as absence of support.
- **Calling a small iteration a pivot, or making a pivot too timid to test anything new.** — A pivot is a [fundamental change to the strategy](https://alumni.lincolncollege.ac.uk/files/2016/11/The-Lean-Startup-by-Eric-Ries-Book-Summary.pdf). If the new plan still rests on the hypothesis that just failed, it is not a pivot, and the next test will likely fail the same way.
