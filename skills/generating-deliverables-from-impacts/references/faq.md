# FAQ: Generating and Prioritizing Deliverables from Impacts

## What counts as a deliverable on an impact map?

Anything the organization or delivery team can do to support an impact. The impactmapping.org drawing page describes deliverables as software features and organisational activities. That includes product changes, but also process changes, training, campaigns and manual services. Gojko Adzic notes that some impacts can be supported without building software at all.

## How many options should we generate per impact?

Enough to have a real choice. There is no fixed number in Adzic's guidance, but a single option per impact defeats the purpose of the level, which is comparing alternatives. Aim for a mix of different kinds of options before evaluating any of them.

## How detailed should deliverables be?

High level. Adzic advises listing only high-level deliverables and breaking them down later into user stories or similar items, which can be added as further levels of the map. Detail is worth adding only to the deliverable the team has chosen to start with.

## How do we choose between deliverables?

Weigh how likely each option is to change the actor's behavior, how much it costs, and how much it would teach the team. Prefer a smaller slice that produces some observable change over a larger piece that produces nothing until it is complete. Adzic's Votito summary describes slicing by targeting a subgroup of actors or a smaller amount of the impact.

## What happens to the deliverables we did not choose?

Keep them on the map under their impact, with a note on why they were not chosen first. If the first deliverable does not produce the behavior change, the alternatives are ready to try. If the impact is achieved, the remaining options can be dropped for this goal.
