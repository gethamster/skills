# FAQ: Generating and Prioritizing Deliverables from Impacts

## How many deliverables should I generate per impact on an impact map?

Aim for 8-12 candidate deliverables per impact during brainstorming. You'll typically prioritize 2-3 for immediate action. The larger initial set ensures you've explored creative options beyond the obvious first idea.

## How do I prioritize deliverables for a product manager roadmap when data is limited?

Use relative scoring on contribution, confidence, effort, and risk rather than precise metrics. Where confidence is low, design a lightweight experiment to test the assumption before committing to build. Prioritize high-confidence, high-contribution items first and treat low-confidence items as experiments.

## What's the difference between deliverables and user stories in impact mapping?

Deliverables in an impact map are strategic options—candidate features, content, or activities hypothesized to cause a behavior change. User stories are implementation-level descriptions of functionality. A single impact map deliverable may break down into multiple user stories during sprint planning.

## Can a single deliverable serve multiple impacts on an impact map?

Yes, and these are often high-value items. However, be cautious—multi-impact deliverables can become bloated. Score the deliverable's contribution to each impact separately and ensure it genuinely serves both rather than being a compromise that underserves each.

## How often should I revisit and re-prioritize deliverables on my product manager roadmap?

Re-prioritize after every significant learning event: experiment results, major customer feedback, competitive shifts, or quarterly goal reviews. Impact maps are living documents. A deliverable that was Tier 1 last month may drop to Tier 4 after new data invalidates its underlying assumption.

## Should I include 'do nothing' or 'remove a feature' as a deliverable option?

Absolutely. 'Stop doing X' or 'remove feature Y' are valid deliverables that can cause behavior change. Including them prevents feature bloat and forces the team to consider whether subtraction might achieve the impact more efficiently than addition.
