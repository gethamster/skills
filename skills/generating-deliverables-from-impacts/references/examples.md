# Examples: Generating and Prioritizing Deliverables from Impacts

## Finding a non-software option first

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B software company's chosen impact is "new customer admins complete account setup in their first week". The backlog already contains a large setup wizard project.

**Walkthrough:**

The group generates options before discussing the wizard. The list includes the wizard, a short setup checklist emailed on day one, a scheduled onboarding call with a support specialist, pre-filled configuration for the most common customer type, and a video walkthrough.

Engineers point out that the pre-filled configuration is cheap and could be shipped quickly. The support lead offers to run onboarding calls for new accounts for the next few weeks as a manual test. The team chooses the calls and the pre-filled configuration as the first slice, keeps the wizard on the map as an option, and agrees to review setup completion before committing to it.

## Slicing by subgroup

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a food delivery platform wants restaurants to update their menus themselves instead of emailing changes to staff. The full solution, a menu editor supporting every restaurant type, looks like a large project.

**Walkthrough:**

The team asks which subgroup of restaurants could get most of the value from a smaller slice. Independent restaurants with simple menus send most of the change requests, while chains with complex menus send few and already have account managers.

The first deliverable becomes a basic editor for simple menus only, offered to independent restaurants in one city. The team will measure whether those restaurants stop emailing changes. Chains and complex menus stay on the map as later options, and the team keeps the full editor under the same impact in case the basic version proves too limited.

## Pruning pet features from the backlog

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team inherits a backlog of several dozen feature requests from different stakeholders. Its impact map has a goal of reducing churn among small-business customers and four ranked impacts.

**Walkthrough:**

The team places each backlog item under the impact it would support. Most fit under one of the four impacts. A handful fit under an actor or impact that was deliberately parked, and several fit nowhere on the map.

Items that fit no impact go back to their requesters with a short note explaining that they do not support the current goal, and an offer to revisit them when the goal changes. The remaining items become candidate deliverables, and the team picks one per impact to start. The stakeholders can see why each item was kept or set aside, which shortens the next backlog review.
