# FAQ: Shaping Product Pitches with a Shape Up Pitch Template

## What goes into a Shape Up pitch?

Five ingredients: the problem, the appetite, the solution, rabbit holes and no-gos. The problem is ideally one specific story showing why the status quo fails. The appetite says how much time the work is worth. The solution shows the core elements with sketches, rabbit holes record details already decided, and no-gos list what is deliberately left out.

## How long should a pitch be?

The book does not set a length. It should be long enough that someone who was not in the shaping sessions can understand and judge the bet, and short enough to read before the betting table. Basecamp posted pitches as messages with embedded sketches.

## Who should shape and write pitches?

The book describes shaping as design work that also needs technical literacy and strategic judgement, done by a generalist or a small group. Ryan Singer's later advice is stronger on the technical side: involve senior engineers who know the code, since he calls undershaped work the number one failure mode of Shape Up adoptions.

## Should a pitch include wireframes?

No. Wireframes over-specify the design, box in the designers and pull the discussion toward layout and color. Use breadboards and fat marker sketches, add labels so readers can follow them, and note where designers have latitude.

## What is the difference between a rabbit hole and a no-go?

A rabbit hole is a part of the solution that could trap the team, which the shaper resolves in advance with a decision, such as how URLs will be created. A no-go is something deliberately excluded from the project, such as rich editing of a form, to keep the work inside the appetite. Both appear in the pitch so the team does not reopen them.

## What happens if the pitch is not chosen?

It is let go. There is no backlog of old pitches. The shaper can keep it on their own list and bring it back to a later table, ideally with a new reason to raise it or a reshaped version that answers the table's concerns.
