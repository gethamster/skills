# Examples: RACE Planning Template

## A small consultancy writes its first 90-day plan

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a three-person consultancy wins most work through referrals and has never written a marketing plan. The founders want more inbound leads without hiring.

**Walkthrough:**

They build a spreadsheet template with a Plan tab and one tab per RACE stage, all with the same columns. The Plan tab records one key issue: almost no one outside their network knows the firm. The objectives follow from it: grow newsletter subscribers in Act and booked discovery calls in Convert.

Filling in the stage tabs shows that Reach has one tactic (a monthly article) and Engage has none. They add a short case study series for Reach and a check-in email to past clients for Engage, each with an owner and a date. The summary tab fits on one screen, and they review it at the end of every month.

## A retailer merges four channel plans

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a mid-sized retailer has separate plans for search, social, email and stores, each owned by a different manager. Budgets are set per channel.

**Walkthrough:**

The marketing lead moves every tactic from the four plans into one RACE template. Each tactic must name the stage it serves and the KPI it moves. Several social tactics turn out to serve no stage objective and are dropped. The email plan contains both Act tactics, such as welcome emails, and Engage tactics, such as loyalty offers, and is split accordingly.

With budgets totaled by stage for the first time, the team sees that almost nothing supports Convert in stores. They add click-and-collect promotion and in-store follow-up as Convert tactics and assign the store manager as owner.

## A software company reuses the template for a launch

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a software company already runs its annual plan in a RACE template and is preparing a new product launch.

**Walkthrough:**

The team copies the template and scopes it to the launch. The Plan section summarizes the target segment and the launch objective. Each stage section gets launch-specific tactics: announcement content in Reach, a product tour and webinar in Act, a trial-to-paid sequence in Convert, and an onboarding program in Engage.

Because the columns match the annual plan, the launch budget and KPIs roll up into the annual summary without rework. At the first monthly review, the Act numbers are strong but trial-to-paid conversion is behind target, so the Convert owner brings a fix to the next review.
