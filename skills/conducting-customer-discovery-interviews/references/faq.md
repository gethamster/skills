# FAQ: Customer Discovery Interview Questions and Technique

## What are good customer discovery interview questions?

Questions that ask for a story about the past. Examples are "When did this last happen?", "What did you do about it?", "What have you tried?", "What did that cost you?" and "Who else was involved?" Follow-up questions on the answers usually produce the most useful detail. Avoid questions about your idea or about what the person would do in future.

## How many customer discovery interviews should I do?

Enough that new interviews stop changing your conclusions. Steve Blank's classes set a target of talking to a hundred customers over a term, which shows the scale he expects. For a single, narrow segment, patterns often appear sooner. Keep going until the evidence map for each risky assumption is stable.

## Should I show my product or prototype in a discovery interview?

Not in the problem part of the interview. Once you show a solution, the conversation turns into feedback on it, and politeness takes over. After you have validated the problem, a separate solution interview or an MVP is the right way to test the solution.

## How do I know when someone has a real problem?

Look for evidence of effort or spending. People who have built workarounds, paid for partial solutions or spend regular time on the problem are showing you it matters. People who only agree that it sounds frustrating are not. A willingness to commit to a next step, such as an introduction, is another strong sign.

## Can customer discovery interviews be done remotely?

Yes. Blank added the principle that in-person interviews are not the only way to test hypotheses when classes moved online. Video calls work well for most business and consumer problems. Where the problem happens in a physical setting, a site visit or a screen share of the actual workflow still adds detail a call can miss.
