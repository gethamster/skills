# Examples: Conducting Customer Discovery Interviews

## Scheduling tool for independent physiotherapists

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a two-person team believes independent physiotherapists lose significant time managing cancellations and wants to validate the problem before building a booking tool.

**Walkthrough:**

The team names the assumption: practitioners running solo clinics spend meaningful time each week refilling cancelled slots. Their screener asks when the candidate last had a same-day cancellation and what they did about it; out of an invented pool of 30 applicants from a professional forum, 11 pass. Interviews walk through the most recent cancellation step by step, and 8 of the 11 describe calling a waiting list by hand, with two keeping a paper list by the phone. None are shown a product.

At the close, the team asks each person to share their current waiting-list method for a follow-up session, and 6 agree. Synthesis scores 8 interviews as supporting the assumption and 3 as contradicting it, noting that the contradicting three all use a clinic-management system with built-in reminders, which narrows the segment for the next test.

## Internal expense tool at a mid-size company

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product manager inside a company wants to know whether field sales staff struggle with expense reporting enough to justify a mobile-first redesign.

**Walkthrough:**

The PM's first instinct is to interview managers who have complained about late expense reports, but that would capture the managers' problem, not the field staff's. Instead the screener targets reps who submitted a report in the past two weeks. The guide asks each rep to reconstruct their last report: when they did it, where they were, which receipts went missing and how long it took. Several describe photographing receipts and typing them up later at home, which reveals the delay comes from re-entry, not the submission form the team had planned to redesign. The PM asks each rep for a copy of their receipt workaround and gets several screenshots of personal note apps.

The conclusion reframes the hypothesis around receipt capture, which the team then tests with a simple prototype.

## Recovering from a biased first round

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a founder ran 15 interviews for a budgeting app, heard strong enthusiasm, but saw almost no signups after launching a landing page.

**Walkthrough:**

Reviewing the notes, the founder finds that 12 of the 15 interviewees came from their own social circle and that most questions were of the form "Would you use an app that...". Nearly every positive score rested on a stated intention, and none on a described behavior such as an existing budgeting habit or workaround. The founder writes a screener requiring candidates to have tried and abandoned a budgeting method in the last three months, and recruits through a personal-finance community. The new guide asks what they tried, why they stopped and what they do now.

This round shows most people stop budgeting because categorizing transactions is tedious, not because they lack an app. The founder rewrites the core hypothesis around automated categorization and plans a behavioral test before building further.
