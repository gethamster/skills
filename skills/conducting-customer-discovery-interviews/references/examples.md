# Examples: Customer Discovery Interview Questions and Technique

## Interviewing clinic managers about scheduling

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a two-person team believes small physiotherapy clinics lose revenue to missed appointments and would adopt a reminder tool. They have not built anything and plan a first round of interviews with clinic managers.

**Walkthrough:**

The question guide avoids the idea completely. It asks when a patient last missed an appointment, what the manager did about it, how they fill gaps in the diary today and how much time that takes each week. In early interviews, managers describe missed appointments as a nuisance, but most already send text reminders through their booking software.

A different problem keeps coming up unprompted: last-minute cancellations that leave slots empty, which managers fill by phoning a waiting list by hand. Several managers show the paper lists they keep. The team marks the reminder assumption as contradicted, writes a new assumption about filling cancelled slots, and asks each manager for an introduction to another clinic to test it.

## Catching a leading question in the notes

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a founder reviewing her notes from a week of interviews about expense reporting sees that almost everyone said they would use an automatic receipt scanner.

**Walkthrough:**

She checks the transcripts and finds that she described the scanner early in each call and then asked whether it would help. The positive answers are responses to her pitch. She rewrites the guide so the first half asks only about the last expense report each person filed: how long it took, what went wrong and what they did about missing receipts.

In the next round, the stories are different. Most people lose time chasing approvals, not scanning receipts. She moves the approval problem to the top of her assumption list and drops the scanner answers from her evidence, noting why.

## Recruiting outside the founder's network

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team building a tool for warehouse shift planning has interviewed only people introduced by their investors, and every interview has gone well.

**Walkthrough:**

The team notices that all of the interviewees work at companies that already use modern planning software. They recruit a second group through an industry forum and by contacting smaller warehouses directly, screening for anyone who planned shifts in the last month.

The second group plans shifts on whiteboards and spreadsheets and describes very different pain: last-minute absences, not forecasting. The team splits its evidence map by segment and decides the smaller warehouses are the better first market to test, because they have the problem and no tool for it.
