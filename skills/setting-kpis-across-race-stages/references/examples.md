# Examples: RACE Framework KPIs

## An online store sets its first RACE KPI table

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small online homewares store has been reporting sessions and revenue only. The owner wants to know why revenue is flat when traffic is growing.

**Walkthrough:**

The team writes one objective per stage and picks a short set of KPIs for each. Reach gets visits by channel and new users. Act gets product views and email sign-ups, both marked as key events in analytics. Convert gets orders and conversion rate. Engage gets the share of customers who order again within the period.

The first month of baselines shows the problem. Reach is growing, but most of the new visits come from a social channel whose visitors rarely view a product. Act is flat, so Convert is flat. The team keeps the Reach target but adds a quality check, product views per visit by channel, and moves budget toward the channels whose visitors interact.

## A B2B software company adds offline KPIs to Convert

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B software company sells through demos run by a sales team. Its marketing dashboard stops at "demo requested", so marketing cannot see what happens next.

**Walkthrough:**

The team defines Act as content downloads and demo requests, and Convert as qualified opportunities and closed deals recorded in the CRM. Each Convert KPI gets a written definition naming the CRM stage that counts, so marketing and sales calculate it the same way.

In the first review, demo requests are above target but qualified opportunities are below it. The gaming test explains why: a campaign rewarded any demo request, including from students and competitors. The team adds a quality filter to the Act KPI and sets the Act owner's target on qualified requests only.

## A subscription service defines Engage properly

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a meal-kit subscription tracks new subscribers closely but measures Engage only through an annual survey.

**Walkthrough:**

The team picks Engage KPIs it can see every month: active subscribers at month end, the share who skip no deliveries, and a recommendation score from a short survey sent after the third box. Each gets a baseline from the last full month.

The monthly review now shows that new subscribers from a discount campaign leave much faster than others. The Reach and Convert numbers had looked strong. The team changes the campaign's success measure to subscribers still active after the third box, which ties Reach spending to Engage.
