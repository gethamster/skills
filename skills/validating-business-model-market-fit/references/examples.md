# Examples: Validating Model Market Fit

## A niche tool that runs out of market

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a startup sells scheduling software to independent physical therapy clinics at a modest monthly price. Retention is strong and customers refer each other, so the founders plan to raise a large round.

**Walkthrough:**

The team counts clinics that match its market definition using a professional registry and multiplies by the annual revenue per clinic. Even at a generous capture share, the result lands far below the goal the investors expect.

Following the adjustment order, they first test a wider market: other small outpatient practices with the same scheduling problem. Interviews show the problem and motivation carry over for some specialties and not for others, so they add only those. Next they test price, and find clinics would pay more for a bundled billing feature. The revised model reaches the goal on paper, and the team records that both changes need fresh Market Product Fit evidence before they are treated as real.

## A price increase that breaks the channel

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a self-serve design tool grows through sharing and free signups. Finance proposes tripling the entry price to improve the Model Market Fit arithmetic.

**Walkthrough:**

The new price does make the calculation pass. But the team places the new price on the ARPU to CAC spectrum and sees it would sit in the middle: too high for users to pay without a trial conversation, too low to fund a sales team.

Rather than adopt the middle price, they compare two options. One keeps the low price and widens the market to adjacent roles who share files with designers. The other adds a separate team plan sold through a small sales motion. They model both, choose the wider market for now, and keep the team plan as a later test.

## An enterprise product with too few buyers

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a compliance product sells to a narrow set of regulated institutions at a high annual contract value. The founders believe the price leaves plenty of room.

**Walkthrough:**

Counting buyers bottom-up shows only a few hundred institutions fit the definition, and several already use a competitor. The model needs a capture share well above the rule of thumb to reach the goal.

The team works through the order. The market can widen to a second regulated industry with the same reporting problem, which interviews support. Price has little room, since buyers already benchmark against the competitor. The revised model depends on winning the second industry, so the team treats that expansion as the main risk in its plan and sets a checkpoint to test it early.
