# Article Anatomy — Method Announcement Format

The skill-launch skeleton (from dexhorthy's show-me piece) with the method spine. Eight sections. Lowercase casual headers. The article shows a system, so the sequence visual carries more weight than in the other formats.

## Section-by-section

### 1. tl;dr block

One sentence stating what the method replaces or makes unnecessary, then the get-it block (real tryhamster.com method page or "add it in Hamster"; never an invented command).

Shape: "tl;dr [the old loop] is gone. [n] skills, [n] routines, one sequence."

`[IMG-01 — wide: the method flow diagram. The whole shape at a glance: components as nodes, the sequence as arrows, the human's position marked. This is the hero asset and likely needs designing, not capturing.]`

### 2. the pain: the whole loop leaks

H2 naming the workflow-level problem. 2-4 embedded posts of people describing the broken loop across steps, not a single task: handoffs, context lost between stages, redoing alignment. Prefer fewer, heavier posts from credible operators over many small complaints. One-line attribution each.

Prior art beat: the piecemeal fixes people run today (a skill here, a script there, a doc nobody updates). If those fixes include known community skills, credit their creators here; the method will include or extend some of them.

Zero words about the method yet.

### 3. the personal stake

Short H2. Three or four lines tracing the workflow consequence chain: the leak between stages, where it lands (the person, the stage), why patching one step never fixed it. This is the section that justifies a method instead of another skill: "we kept fixing steps. the loop stayed broken."

`[IMG-02 — the leak happening in your own workflow: the handoff moment, the stale doc, the re-explained context]`

### 4. the proposal: the whole sequence

H2. Contents in order:

- One or two sentences announcing the method by name
- Get-it block, second appearance
- The flow, in words this time: component A feeds B, routine C keeps D true, the human decides at E. Three sentences max; the diagram already did the heavy lifting.
- Intellectual grounding if the method descends from a known framework or idea: link it, one blockquote, credit the originator. Methods more than skills tend to have lineage; honor it.

`[IMG-03 — the flow diagram again if the article runs long, or a mid-sequence screenshot showing two components chained]`

### 5. what's inside

H2, then a lowercase sub-heading per component, in running order (the order they fire in the sequence, not alphabetical, not by importance). Per component:

- 1-2 sentences: its job in the sequence and what it hands to the next step
- One visual: the component doing its job
- Link to its deep-dive article if one exists ("full article on this one here")
- Creator credit at point of use if third-party, with their post embedded where possible
- If it's a routine, name its trigger in passing ("runs every monday" / "fires when a brief is approved"); the deep-dive holds the spec

Component sections are teasers, not tours. If a component needs more than 2 sentences here, that's a sign it needs its own article, which goes in the gaps list.

### 6. other inspiration

Optional. The frameworks, essays, or people the method learned from beyond the direct lineage. One or two, with visuals where they exist.

### 7. go try it

H2. Get-it block third appearance. Then:

- **Where to start**: name the one component that pays off standalone on day one, and the natural second. Explicitly permission piecewise adoption ("you don't need the whole thing. start with [X].")
- Two blockquoted starting invocations, verbatim and pasteable, drawn from the starter components
- CTA: tag @usehamster or @eyaltoledano with what you adopted or changed, let's riff
- `[IMG-FINAL — wide: GIF or recording of the sequence running end to end, even sped up]`

### 8. (third-party mode only) whose shoulders

When the method bundles or extends community skills: a short section before the closer naming every borrowed component and its creator in one place, on top of the point-of-use credits. Two or three sentences. The method is the packaging; the parts have authors; say so twice.

## Format conventions

- Get-it block 3x: tl;dr, proposal, closer.
- The flow diagram appears at minimum once (hero); twice if the article is long enough that the reader loses the shape.
- Visual density: one visual per component plus the diagram, the stake shot, and the closer, typically ~10-16 visuals depending on component count.
- Prose ceiling: 3 sentences between visuals; 2 sentences per component in the tour.
- Deep-dive links inline where the component is discussed, never in a link dump at the end.
- Embedded posts: X only embeds X; Reddit finds quoted with link.
- Headers lowercase, casual, short.

## Voice (compact floor; full rules in hamster-content)

- No em dashes. No "not X but Y". No staccato preachy rhythm. No corporate filler. Lowercase on X.
- No "nobody talks about" openers/closers. No "here's the truth about."
- Never invent components, mechanics, commands, or sequence steps. Never fabricate quotes or numbers. No exact follower counts.
- Consequence chain lands on a person and a stage, in 3 sentences.
- Anti-ceremony framing where true: the method removes coordination, it does not add process. Never describe the method with process-manager language ("stages", "gates", "cadences" as virtues).
- Credit generously, at point of use AND in the shoulders section for third-party parts.

## Pre-delivery checklist

1. Does the pain section prove the workflow-level leak through real, credible posts?
2. Is every component verified against its actual SKILL.md/spec, and is the sequence confirmed by Eyal, not assumed?
3. Get-it block 3x, real path or flagged placeholder?
4. Flow diagram in the shot list, flagged design-needed?
5. Components in running order, 2 sentences max each, one visual each, deep-dive links where articles exist?
6. Third-party creators credited at point of use and in the shoulders section?
7. "Where to start" names a concrete first component with piecewise permission?
8. Two pasteable blockquoted invocations in the closer?
9. Voice pass clean, including the anti-ceremony check?
10. Shot list numbered; gaps list includes missing deep-dive articles as pipeline candidates?
