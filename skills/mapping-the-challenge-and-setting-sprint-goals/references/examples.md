# Examples: Mapping the Challenge and Setting Sprint Goals

## Onboarding drop-off for a B2B tool

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team building project software wants to fix poor activation among new trial accounts and has a folder of analytics and support tickets.

**Walkthrough:**

Before the sprint, the facilitator confirms the data shows where trials stall, so the evidence check passes. The team sets the goal "new teams get real work done in their first week" and lists questions such as whether admins will invite colleagues before seeing value. The map runs from the admin signing up, through setup and inviting teammates, to the first completed project. A support lead explains that many admins stall at importing existing work, which becomes a new box on the map and several HMW notes.

The decider targets the admin at the import step, and Tuesday's sketches all attack that moment.

## Pausing a sprint for missing data

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a founder wants a sprint on a new subscription offering but has no customer interviews and no usage data for the idea.

**Walkthrough:**

During the preparation check, the facilitator lists the available evidence and finds it is all internal opinion. Rather than proceed, the team spends a week on a handful of customer calls to learn who would pay and why. The calls reveal that the assumed buyer is not the person who feels the pain, which changes the actors on the eventual map. The sprint runs a fortnight later with a clearer goal and sharper questions.

Monday takes less time because the debate about who the customer is has already been settled by evidence.

## Narrowing a sprawling service journey

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a clinic network wants to improve how patients book, attend and follow up on appointments, and the first map covers every channel and exception.

**Walkthrough:**

By mid-morning the board is full of branches for phone, web, walk-in and referrals, and nobody can agree where to focus. The facilitator collapses the map to four steps per patient type and asks experts to mark where complaints concentrate. HMW notes cluster heavily around rescheduling. The decider targets returning patients at the rescheduling step and parks the referral flow for a later sprint.

The team leaves with one circled target and a list of deferred questions, rather than a map too large to sketch against.
