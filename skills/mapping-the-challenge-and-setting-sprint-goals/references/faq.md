# FAQ: Mapping the Challenge and Setting Sprint Goals

## How much data is enough before running a design sprint?

There is no fixed threshold, but you should be able to name who the customer is, where they struggle and what evidence shows it. Practitioners report sprints are [not worth the effort without enough data on the problem](https://geyfman.org/dossiers/gv-design-sprint-deep-research-report). If your understanding rests mainly on internal opinion, gather customer evidence first. A useful check is whether the team could draw a rough map before Monday.

## What is the difference between the long-term goal and the sprint questions?

The long-term goal is the optimistic outcome you want well after the sprint. Sprint questions are the pessimistic view: the specific risks that could stop you reaching that goal. The goal orients the whole week, while the questions shape what Friday's test tries to learn. You need both, because a goal without questions gives no test plan.

## Who should the experts be on Monday?

Choose people who see the problem from different angles, such as someone close to customers, someone who understands the technology, and someone who owns the business constraints. They do not need to join the whole sprint. Their job is to correct the map and expose risks the core team missed. Ask each one where they would worry most.

## Why use How Might We notes instead of ordinary notes?

Phrasing each observation as a question turns a problem into an opportunity someone can sketch against. Summaries of the book describe each person [writing HMW in the corner of their own notes](https://paulminors.com/blog/sprint-jake-knapp-book-summary-pdf) and converting interesting points as they hear them. The format also makes notes easy to cluster and vote on. Ordinary notes tend to become a list of complaints that is hard to act on.

## Can problem mapping replace user research?

No. Monday organizes and sharpens what the team already knows, but its compressed schedule can [limit opportunities for deep understanding](https://cris.fbk.eu/retrieve/883a8fe4-957f-44fa-af9e-6159cfa53bd4/1-s2.0-S0166497225000719-main.pdf). Expert interviews are secondhand evidence. If the map rests on assumptions nobody has checked with customers, treat Friday's results as a prompt for more research rather than a verdict.
