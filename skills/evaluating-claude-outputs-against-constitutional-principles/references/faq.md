# FAQ: Claude Content Optimizer: Evaluate Against the Constitution

## Can Claude review its own drafts?

Yes, and Anthropic's constitution lists drafting, critiquing as an expert evaluator and revising among the things Claude can do to produce a better response. A separate critique pass with a written rubric and the source material works well as a first review. A person should still make the final decision on anything published.

## Which honesty properties matter most for content?

Truthful, calibrated and non-deceptive do most of the work. Truthful catches false statements, calibrated catches claims stated more confidently than the evidence allows, and non-deceptive catches accurate sentences that add up to a misleading impression. Forthright is useful too: it asks whether the page leaves out something the reader would clearly want to know.

## How does this relate to Google's guidance?

Google's helpful content guidance asks whether content is original, accurate, made for people and written with real expertise. Those questions cover usefulness and search quality, while the constitution's honesty properties cover how claims are made. A rubric that combines them checks both.

## Should I score drafts on a numeric scale?

Pass or fail items with written definitions are simpler to apply and make reviewer disagreements easy to trace to a definition. If you need a single number for reporting, count the items passed. Avoid scores for keyword density or word count, which say little about quality.

## What should happen when many drafts fail the same item?

Look for the cause in the prompt or the source material. Repeated failures usually trace to a missing source, a goal that rewards exaggeration, or a constraint that forces padding. Fix the template and rerun a small batch to confirm the pattern is gone.
