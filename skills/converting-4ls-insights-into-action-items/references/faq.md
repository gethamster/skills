# FAQ: Turning 4Ls Retrospective Insights into Action Items

## How many actions should a retrospective produce?

As few as the team can actually finish before the next retrospective, usually one or two. A short list that gets done builds trust in the retrospective. A long list that slips teaches the team that commitments are optional.

## Why one owner instead of the team?

Because shared ownership usually means nobody follows up. The owner does not have to do all the work; they make sure it happens and report on it. Several people can help, but one name goes on the action.

## Where should actions live?

In the same backlog or tracker as the team's other work. The Scrum Guide allows the most impactful improvements to go into the next Sprint Backlog. A separate retrospective list is easy to forget.

## What if nobody volunteers?

Treat it as information. If nobody will own an action, it may not matter enough, or it may be too big. Shrink it to a first step or drop it deliberately.

## What happens to themes we do not act on?

They stay on the record. Review them at the next retrospective, and if they keep coming back, they become candidates for a bigger action or an escalation.
