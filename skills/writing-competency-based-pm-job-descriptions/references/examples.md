# Examples: Writing Competency-Based PM Job Descriptions

## Hiring into a strategic market gap

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a B2B analytics company's team chart shows the strategic Users and Market boxes greyed out. The head of product opens a senior PM role.

**Walkthrough:**

The role's home region is upper-left. The core boxes are Market Positioning, Opportunity Discovery, Competitive Gap Analysis and Jobs to be Done. Responsibilities read, for example, "Own positioning for the analytics suite against the two main competitors and turn it into roadmap proposals."

Requirements are written as evidence: "You have led discovery for a new segment and can show how it changed a roadmap." Shared work with design and engineering gets one paragraph. The secondary list names only Business Case. The interview loop then assigns one interviewer to each core box.

## A junior internal tools PM

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an operations platform team needs a junior PM to handle requests from internal teams. The first draft copies a generic PM template.

**Walkthrough:**

The hiring manager places the role lower-right. The core boxes become Internal Tools Requirements, Business Analysis, System Analysis and Data Queries. Product vision and market positioning lines are removed, since a junior internal tools role does not own them.

Requirements ask for evidence such as having written requirements for an internal system and having used data queries to answer an operational question. The ad is shorter and attracts candidates from operations and analyst backgrounds.

## Cutting an unfocused draft

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a draft PM description has many responsibility lines. When mapped to the grid, they cover boxes in all five categories.

**Walkthrough:**

The hiring manager asks which boxes the person must own in the first months. The answer points to Core Product and Market. All lines outside those columns are cut or moved to a short nice-to-have list.

The revised draft states the team gap in the summary and lists a handful of responsibilities. Candidates in the first round say they understood the role before the first call.
