# FAQ: Writing Competency-Based PM Job Descriptions

## How many competencies should a PM job description name?

A handful of core boxes from the role's home region, plus a short list of secondary ones if they would change a hiring decision. Shared PM work can be covered in one paragraph. A longer list describes a generalist nobody can match.

## Should I include domain knowledge as a requirement?

Only if the role truly cannot succeed without it. Cagan deliberately leaves domain knowledge out of his sample product manager job description. Adding it narrows the candidate pool, so keep it only where the role depends on it.

## How do I write requirements that are not vague?

Phrase each requirement as something the candidate has done and can discuss. Replace trait words such as strategic or data-driven with a specific piece of work, like leading positioning for a product. Interviewers can then check the evidence.

## How does the job description connect to interviews?

The core boxes become the interview plan. Each box gets at least one interviewer and a rubric describing strong and weak answers. Keep the two documents together so a change in one is reflected in the other.

## What if the team has no clear gap?

Then the hire may be a backfill or a capacity hire. Use the role profile of the person or role being replaced to set the core boxes. It is still worth checking the team chart, since a departure often turns a covered box grey.
