# Examples: Collecting Six Forces Model Data

## Evidence file for a regional logistics software market

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product strategy team at a small software company is preparing a six forces analysis of route-planning software for regional delivery firms in one country. They have two weeks, a shared spreadsheet and access to a handful of customers.

**Walkthrough:**

The team writes research questions for each force first. For rivalry they ask how many vendors sell to regional delivery firms and whether prices have moved in the past year. For complements they ask which telematics devices and accounting tools customers connect to route planning. Their inventory shows plenty of material on rivals from win-loss notes, and nothing on substitutes or complements.

Secondary research fills the rivalry and entrant questions: annual reports of the two listed competitors, a patent search that turns up filings by a telematics hardware maker, and government business counts for delivery firms in the target regions. Primary research targets the empty forces. The team interviews six customers and four former customers, asking what they used before and what they connected the product to. Two former customers moved back to spreadsheets plus a mapping app, which goes into the file as a substitute.

The finished file has entries for all six forces, each with source, date and confidence. The gap list names one open question: whether the telematics maker plans to bundle routing, which could turn a complement into a rival.

## Refreshing an evidence file after an acquisition

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a year after a first analysis, a strategy lead learns that two mid-sized competitors in her market have merged. She has the old evidence file and a week to update it.

**Walkthrough:**

She does not restart from scratch. She marks every entry that the merger could affect: rivalry concentration, buyer alternatives and the partner ecosystem, since both companies had integration partners. For each, she checks whether the source is still current and adds new evidence dated this year.

The merged company's announcement and filings give its combined customer base and plans. Three interviews with shared customers show that some are worried about fewer choices, which feeds buyer power. One partner says it will keep supporting both products for now. The old entries stay in the file, marked as superseded, so the rating step can see what changed and why.

## Pre-entry research with no customers yet

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a founder is considering a product for independent veterinary clinics and wants six forces evidence before building anything. She has no customers and a small budget.

**Walkthrough:**

She leans on secondary sources: business counts for veterinary practices from government statistics, filings of listed companies that sell software to clinics, and trade association material on how clinics buy. For primary research she interviews five clinic managers about the software they use, what it connects to and what they would never give up.

The interviews show that clinics depend heavily on a lab-results integration controlled by two large lab companies. She files that under complements with a note on power. Her gap list is long, and she marks every force as low confidence. The file is still useful: it shows that the complement and supplier questions, not rivalry, will decide whether a new product can work.
