# FAQ: Collecting Six Forces Model Data

## How much data is enough for a six forces analysis?

Enough to rate each force with evidence a colleague could check, and to say where the evidence is thin. There is no fixed quantity. A force with two solid, independent sources and a few interviews is often enough for a first rating. What matters more is that every force has something and that gaps are written down.

## Should we buy an industry report?

Only if it answers a question that public sources and interviews cannot. Reports are useful for market shares and private-company estimates, but their methods vary, so record the report as the source and grade its confidence. Start with free sources such as filings, patent search and government statistics, and buy only to fill a named gap.

## Who should we interview?

Choose people by the force you need to understand. Customers and former customers explain buyer power and substitutes. Partners explain complements. Procurement staff and suppliers explain supplier power. Your sales team sees rivalry up close, but balance its view with customers, since salespeople see one side of every deal.

## How do we keep interview evidence reliable?

Ask about specific past events, such as the last time the customer compared vendors, rather than hypothetical choices. Avoid leading questions, and keep raw notes separate from your interpretation. Nielsen Norman Group's [interview guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-interviews/) covers both points in detail.

## How often should the evidence file be refreshed?

Refresh it on a schedule that matches how fast the industry changes, and immediately after a trigger event such as a merger, a new entrant or a regulation change. Keep old entries and mark them as superseded so the next analysis can show what moved.
