# Examples: Running a Shape Up Betting Table Session

## Choosing between three pitches

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team has two build teams for the next cycle. Three pitches were shaped during the current cycle: bulk editing for invoices (six weeks), a faster onboarding checklist (two weeks) and a new reporting page (six weeks). The betting group of four has read all three.

**Walkthrough:**

The group starts with the problems. Bulk editing comes from repeated support complaints from the largest customers, and everyone agrees it matters now. Reporting is appealing, but the last two cycles were both spent in the reporting area, and the team that would build it is tired of that part of the code. The group passes on it because of timing; the pitch itself is sound.

Bulk editing goes to the big batch team. The onboarding checklist goes to the small batch team along with a small bug pitch a programmer shaped. The reporting pitch is let go; its shaper notes it on their own list and may bring it back later. The kick-off message goes out the same afternoon.

## Negotiating the appetite

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a shaper pitches a six-week redesign of the notification settings screen. The CTO says he is not interested in spending six weeks on it.

**Walkthrough:**

The shaper asks how the CTO would feel if it were two weeks. The CTO says the time is not really the issue: the pitch adds a new dependency to a fragile part of the app, and he does not want that at any size.

With the real objection on the table, the group passes on the pitch as written. The shaper goes back to the shaping track to find an approach that avoids the dependency. A smaller version, without the dependency, comes back to a later table and is bet on as a two-week small batch project.

## A table that turned into grooming

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company new to Shape Up holds its first betting table and brings a spreadsheet of forty feature ideas along with two shaped pitches.

**Walkthrough:**

The meeting runs for three hours and ends without a decision, because most of the time goes into debating ideas that were never shaped. The product lead resets the process for the next cycle: only shaped pitches are allowed on the table, and anyone who wants an idea considered has to shape it or find someone to shape it first.

The spreadsheet is not deleted, but it stops being an input to the table; its owners treat it as their own list. The next table reviews three pitches in under an hour and ends with two bets and named teams.
