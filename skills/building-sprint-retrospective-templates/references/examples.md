# Examples: Building a Reusable Sprint Retrospective Template

## A standard one-hour sprint template

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of six rotates the facilitator role each sprint. Retrospectives vary a lot depending on who runs them, and newer facilitators often skip the insight phase.

**Walkthrough:**

The Scrum Master writes a standard template. The check-in is a one-word round. The data activity is a timeline seeded with the sprint's key dates and the previous actions. Insights use a 5 Whys on the top theme, decisions use dot voting and a control check, and the close is a read-back of actions and a quick ROTI.

For each phase she writes an opening line, the instructions and a transition sentence, plus a note on what to do if the phase runs long. The timings add up to one hour with a small buffer. After two sprints, facilitators report the data phase keeps overrunning, so she shortens the timeline instructions and moves the buffer there.

## A short template for a busy week

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of five sometimes has only half an hour for a retrospective at the end of a release week. Under pressure they tend to cancel it.

**Walkthrough:**

The team builds a short template that keeps all five phases with lighter activities. Data is collected on a shared board the day before, so the session starts with notes in place. The check-in is a single typed word, the insight phase is one quick 5 Whys, and the decision is limited to one action.

The template's header says it is for short weeks and should not be used two sprints in a row. After using it, the team finds it keeps the habit going without replacing the full retrospective.

## A remote template for a distributed team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of eight works across two time zones. Its in-person template does not work well on video calls.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator adapts the template for remote use. Data gathering moves to an asynchronous board opened two days before, with a reminder the day before. The live session keeps set the stage, a short review of the data, insights, decisions and close, with a short break in the middle.

The template lists the digital board layout, how to enable anonymous notes, and how to hide votes until the end. It also names who posts the summary for people in the other time zone. The team tags it as the remote version of the standard template and reviews it after the first two uses.
