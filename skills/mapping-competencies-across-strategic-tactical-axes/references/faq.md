# FAQ: Mapping PM Competencies on Strategic and Tactical Axes

## Should we change Cabage's categories to fit our company?

Keep the five categories and both axes, and adapt through definitions and relevance marks instead. The seniority and product-type readings of the chart depend on its layout. If real work has no home, add a clearly marked local box rather than reshaping the grid.

## Why is Product-Market Fit on the tactical side?

That is where Cabage places it on his diagram, under Market and below the band, next to conversion optimization and A/B testing. His page does not explain individual placements, so treat it as his choice and describe what the box means at your company in its definition. Moving it would make your map harder to compare with the original.

## How detailed should each definition be?

One sentence naming the output and one example from your products is usually enough. The test is whether two reviewers reading the definition would agree that a given piece of work belongs in the box. Longer definitions tend to drift into level expectations, which belong in a separate leveling document.

## What if a box does not apply to us?

Mark it as not applicable and write down why. Cabage's framework does not expect every PM to cover every box, and an irrelevant box should never appear as a gap in an assessment. Revisit the mark if your products change.

## Where do leadership and people skills go?

Cabage's grid does not include a people management category. Strategic boxes such as Product Council and Portfolio Planning cover some leadership work, but managing PMs is outside the chart. Many companies handle it on a separate management track.
