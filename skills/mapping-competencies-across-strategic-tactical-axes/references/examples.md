# Examples: Mapping PM Competencies on Strategic and Tactical Axes

## A consumer app team adapts the grid

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small consumer subscription app has four PMs and no shared definition of the PM role. The head of product wants one page the team can use for reviews and hiring.

**Walkthrough:**

She copies Cabage's grid unchanged and writes a definition for each box with an example from the app, such as "Conversion Optimization: changes to the trial and paywall flow measured against a control." She marks the Users and Market boxes as core, most Core Product boxes as core, and several Business and Technical boxes as occasional. Product Council and Internal Tools Requirements are marked not applicable, because the company has neither.

In review, two PMs place their paywall experiments in different boxes. The group agrees the work belongs under Market, in A/B Testing and Conversion Optimization, and tightens both definitions so the split is clear. The published page records that consumer work sits on the left of the chart and that senior PMs are expected above the band.

## A platform group finds homeless work

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an internal platform group of three PMs serves other engineering teams. Its lead adapts the grid for a new leveling process.

**Walkthrough:**

The lead marks the Technical and Business boxes as core and most Users boxes as occasional, following Cabage's note that technical PMs on internal tools and platforms work on the right side. While listing recurring work, the team finds that writing API deprecation plans has no clear box. They check Technical and Core Product first and decide it fits the existing Product Requirements box once the definition mentions API changes.

No box is added. The lead notes the decision on the page so later readers see why deprecation work counts as requirements.

## A merged team reconciles two maps

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: after an acquisition, two product teams each bring their own skills list. The combined leadership group wants one map.

**Walkthrough:**

Instead of merging the lists, the group translates every item on both lists into a box on Cabage's grid. Items such as "customer empathy" and "user research" both land in User Interviews and Jobs to be Done. Items such as "people management" have no box, and the group records them as out of scope for the PM map and covered by the management track.

The result is one grid with shared definitions. Both teams can see where their old terms went, which makes the switch easier to accept.
