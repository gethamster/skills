# Examples: Pivot or Persevere: When to Pivot a Startup

## Persevering while the engine is still tuning

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team selling a scheduling product to dental practices holds its regular decision meeting. Trial-to-paid conversion started low, and the last three experiments each targeted a different step of onboarding.

**Walkthrough:**

The evidence pack shows each experiment moved the conversion rate for its cohort, and the latest cohort converts better than any before it. Retention among paying practices is steady. The core assumptions about the customer and the problem are supported by interviews.

The group agrees the strategy is working and that tuning is still paying off. They persevere, pick the next weakest step in the funnel to test, and keep the next meeting on the calendar.

## A zoom-in pivot from one popular feature

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a project management tool for small agencies has spent several cycles trying to raise weekly use. Usage is flat across cohorts, but the time-tracking screen is used far more than anything else.

**Walkthrough:**

The team reviews its assumptions. The problem assumption, that agencies struggle to coordinate projects, has weak support: most already use other tools. Interviews show that agencies do struggle to bill accurately, and the time-tracking screen is how they have been working around it.

The group decides the evidence supports a zoom-in pivot. Time tracking and billing become the whole product. What stays: the customer segment, the channel and the relationships with existing users. The team writes a hypothesis that agencies will pay for accurate client billing and plans a concierge test with a handful of current users before rebuilding anything.

## Stopping when no pivot is credible

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a founder has run a consumer meal-planning service through two pivots, first to a new segment and then to a new channel. Money for roughly one more cycle remains.

**Walkthrough:**

At the meeting, the founder and two advisers go through the assumptions. Each pivot improved one metric briefly, then the numbers returned to the baseline. None of the remaining pivot types has evidence behind it, and interviews show customers treat meal planning as a nice extra.

The group chooses to stop. The founder records what was learned about the segment and the channel, returns what remains of the money, and writes up the evidence so it can inform the next idea.
