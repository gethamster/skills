# FAQ: Pivot or Persevere: When to Pivot a Startup

## How do I know when to pivot a startup?

Look at the trend across experiments. If repeated attempts to tune the product have not moved the key actionable metrics away from their baseline, the strategy is probably wrong, and more tuning is unlikely to fix it. A pivot is also worth considering when interviews and experiments keep contradicting one core assumption. A single bad result is not enough on its own.

## What is the difference between a pivot and starting over?

A pivot changes one part of the strategy and keeps the rest, along with what the team has learned. Starting over changes everything at once. Ries warns against jumping to a completely new vision because it throws away the validated learning about customers that came before.

## How often should we hold a pivot or persevere meeting?

Ries suggests that less than a few weeks between meetings is too often and more than a few months too infrequent, and that each startup should find its own pace. A good rule is to meet once enough experiments have run to show a trend. Keep the date even when things look fine.

## What types of pivot are there?

Ries's book lists ten, including zoom-in, zoom-out, customer segment, customer need, platform, business architecture, value capture, engine of growth, channel and technology pivots. Each changes one part of the business model. Researchers who studied software startups found some additional types, such as complete and side project pivots.

## Who should be in the decision meeting?

The people who can change the strategy, the people who ran the experiments and, if possible, someone who did not run them. Founders and sponsors are needed for the decision to stick. An outside reader helps the group judge the evidence without defending a favorite idea.
