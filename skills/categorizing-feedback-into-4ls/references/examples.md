# Examples: Sorting Team Feedback into the 4Ls Categories

## Lacked and Longed For describing the same gap

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: after silent writing, a board has "no realistic test data" under Lacked from one person and "a seeded test database per branch" under Longed For from another.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator asks both writers what they meant. Both describe the same problem, one looking back and one looking ahead. Instead of moving either note, the facilitator links them into a single theme named "realistic test data", visible across both columns.

The theme is voted on once. During action planning, the Lacked note supplies the evidence and the Longed For note supplies the proposal, and the team agrees on a small spike to seed data for one service.

## A note that could be Liked or Learned

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a developer writes "pairing on the migration caught bugs early" and posts it under Learned. Another teammate thinks it belongs in Liked.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator applies the action-type test and asks the writer what the team should do with it. The writer says they want the team to keep pairing on risky changes. That makes it a Liked item, a practice to repeat, and the writer moves it.

A second note from the same person, "migrations need a dry run on a copy of production", stays under Learned because it is new knowledge the team can write down.

## A crowded not-sure area

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: in a team's first 4Ls retrospective, several notes end up in the not-sure area because people were unsure of the categories.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator takes the notes one by one and asks each writer what they want the team to do about it: keep, teach, fix or propose. Most notes find a place within a minute. One note is really a question for the product manager, so it goes to the parking area.

Before the next retrospective, the facilitator adds a one-line definition and a concrete example to each column, and the not-sure area stays nearly empty.
