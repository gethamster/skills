# FAQ: Running PR/FAQ Review Meetings

## How long should the silent reading take?

Colin Bryar and Bill Carr describe 15-20 minutes of silent reading and note-taking, followed by the remaining time in a one-hour meeting for discussion. The right length is whatever it takes for everyone to finish, which is one reason to keep the document within its length limits.

## Why not send the document in advance?

People who are asked to read in advance often skim or skip it, and the discussion then starts from different levels of understanding. Reading in the room means everyone discusses the same version with full attention. Sending it early does no harm, as long as the meeting still starts with reading.

## What if the most senior person dominates?

Ask them before the meeting to speak last, and explain why: the meeting is meant to collect independent reactions. In the book, Bryar and Carr describe senior attendees tending to speak last to avoid influencing others. A facilitator can also call on quieter people by name during the detailed discussion.

## Is the review meeting where the decision is made?

Usually not. Bryar and Carr describe the meeting as focused on truth-seeking and improving the idea, with the go or no-go decision coming once the document is complete enough. A final review with the decision maker may end in a decision, but early reviews should end in revisions.

## Can the review be done asynchronously?

Parts of it can. Comments can be gathered in the document over a few days, but the live debate is where hard questions get followed up and where disagreement surfaces. If the team is spread out, keep a short live discussion even if the reading happens separately.
