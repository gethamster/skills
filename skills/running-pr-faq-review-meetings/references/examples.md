# Examples: Running PR/FAQ Review Meetings

## A first review that finds the wrong customer

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product manager at a mid-size software company brings a PR/FAQ for a reporting add-on to its first review. Six people attend: her manager, an engineering lead, a designer, a support lead, a salesperson and a finance partner.

**Walkthrough:**

Everyone reads the document in the room and comments inline. In the round of general reactions, the support lead and the salesperson independently say the customer in the press release, the team administrator, is not the person who asks for reports; department heads are. The manager, speaking last, agrees that this is the main issue.

The page-by-page discussion is short, because most detailed comments depend on who the customer is. The note-taker records the customer question as the top item, with the product manager as owner. Minutes go out that afternoon, and the next draft is rewritten around department heads.

## An executive review with a dominant voice

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a PR/FAQ for a new pricing tier has passed two small-group reviews and goes to the head of the business unit. In earlier meetings with this leader, discussions have followed whatever the leader said first.

**Walkthrough:**

Before the meeting, the author asks the leader privately to hold comments until others have spoken, and the leader agrees. After the reading, the finance partner raises a concern about existing customers moving down to the cheaper tier. Two others add detail from their own areas.

When the leader speaks last, the comments build on the concern instead of replacing it. The meeting ends with an open question on migration risk, owned by finance, and a decision to revisit once the numbers are in.

## A distributed team reviewing across time zones

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team spread across two continents cannot find an hour that suits everyone for a live review of a PR/FAQ.

**Walkthrough:**

The author holds two short live sessions, one for each region, each starting with silent reading and inline comments in the same shared document. The second group reads the first group's comments after finishing their own reading, so their first reactions stay independent.

The author merges both sets of notes into one set of minutes and marks the points where the two groups disagreed. The disagreements become the agenda for a short joint call with the decision maker a week later.
