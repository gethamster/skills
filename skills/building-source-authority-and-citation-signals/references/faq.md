# FAQ: Building Source Authority and Citation Signals

## Why do AI search engines favor third-party sources over my own website?

A 2025 ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919)) study of AI search services found a systematic bias toward earned media, meaning third-party authoritative sources, over brand-owned and social content. That differs from Google's more balanced mix of sources. The practical implication is that coverage about you on sites engines trust often matters more than further polishing your own pages.

## Can a small or niche organization compete with big brands in AI answers?

It is harder, because the same research names big-brand bias as a limitation for niche organizations. Its recommended responses are earned-media coverage, engine-specific strategies and language-aware optimization. In practice, niche organizations do best by targeting specialist outlets and narrower prompts where their expertise is distinctive, rather than broad category queries dominated by incumbents.

## Is it worse to be cited inaccurately or not cited at all?

Inaccurate citations can be more damaging than absence, particularly in regulated industries where outdated sources can spread errors quickly. An engine repeating a withdrawn claim or old price puts wrong information in front of buyers under your name. That is why this skill includes tracing each wrong claim to its source and getting the source corrected.

## Which signals besides press coverage matter for AI citations?

Practitioner guidance frames this work as building an entity footprint that includes author credentials, consistent business facts, off-site mentions and AI crawler access. Each one gives engines either more evidence that you are credible or a clearer, less contradictory picture of who you are. Neglecting crawler access in particular can undo the rest, because engines cannot use pages they cannot reach.

## How long before authority work shows up in AI answers?

There is no reliable timeline in the evidence, and results are noisy. A 2026 ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14035)) review reports substantial run-to-run variability and low source overlap in commercial engine audits. Judge progress on trends across repeated monitoring runs over months, and check each engine and language separately rather than relying on one snapshot.
