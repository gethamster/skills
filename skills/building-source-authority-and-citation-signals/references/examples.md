# Examples: Building Source Authority and Citation Signals

## Niche software vendor against category leaders

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A small vendor of scheduling software for veterinary clinics is never mentioned when buyers ask AI tools for the best clinic scheduling software, while two large practice-management suites always are.

**Walkthrough:**

The team runs its target prompts and finds the engines cite a handful of veterinary trade publications and a comparison page on an industry association site. Instead of broad tech press, they pitch a practical staffing-shortage guide to one trade publication and ask the association to review their product for its comparison page. They align their product description, pricing model and founder bios with a new canonical fact sheet before any coverage goes live. They also narrow their target prompts to clinic-specific questions where their expertise is distinctive rather than generic scheduling queries.

Over several monitoring passes, they record which engines begin citing the trade article and keep the prompts that stay stubborn on a watch list.

## Outdated regulatory claim in a health product answer

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A medical device company notices an AI engine describing its product with an indication that was withdrawn in a recent label update.

**Walkthrough:**

The monitoring log traces the claim to an old distributor listing and a years-old news article that engines still cite. Compliance reviews the finding the same day because an inaccurate indication is a higher risk than simply not being mentioned. The team updates the distributor listing through its partner contact and requests a correction note from the publisher of the article. They also publish a clearly dated product page with the current indications in plain text and confirm AI crawlers can reach it.

Follow-up runs over the next few weeks show whether the engine stops repeating the withdrawn claim, and any engine that still does is escalated again.

## Consultancy building expert authority

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: A mid-sized data consultancy publishes useful research, but AI answers credit the findings to a larger firm that wrote about the same topic later.

**Walkthrough:**

The audit shows the consultancy's reports carry only a company byline and no author pages, while the larger firm names its researchers prominently. The consultancy adds named authors with author pages describing their experience and uses one approved bio for each expert across its site and guest articles. It pitches its lead researchers as sources to the two publications engines cite most for its topic, making sure titles and names match the canonical versions. It also fixes inconsistent company descriptions across three directory listings.

Monitoring then tracks whether answers begin attributing findings to the consultancy and its named experts.
