# Examples: Role-Based Time Allocation for Business Owners

## A landscape contractor protects one morning

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the owner of a landscaping business with two crews spends every weekday on job sites and does quotes and scheduling at night. His audit shows almost no Entrepreneur time and Manager work squeezed into late evenings.

**Walkthrough:**

He sets a modest target for the quarter: keep four site days, move Manager work into daylight, and add one Entrepreneur block. Monday morning becomes a protected office block for planning, with the first agenda item being whether to add a maintenance contract service. Quotes and crew scheduling move to Friday afternoon.

His crew leads get a rule: call during the Monday block only for injuries or equipment failures, and text anything else for the Friday window. After three weeks the review shows the Monday block survived twice and was broken once by a supplier issue, so he names a crew lead as the supplier contact.

## A marketing consultant separates making from meeting

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a solo marketing consultant with one assistant writes client strategies in fragments between calls. Her days are split into small pieces and she finishes deep work late at night.

**Walkthrough:**

She keeps a large Technician share, since she is the practitioner, but changes where it sits. Tuesday and Thursday become writing days with no calls. Client calls cluster on Monday and Wednesday afternoons, and her assistant books only into those windows. Friday morning is a short Manager block for invoicing, templates and reviewing her assistant's work.

She adds a monthly Entrepreneur half-day to review which services are most profitable. The biweekly review shows clients adapt to the call windows quickly, and the writing days hold.

## A software founder rebalances after a hire

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a founder has just hired an experienced engineer who can lead delivery. His calendar still looks like it did when he was the main developer.

**Walkthrough:**

He resets the target to shift Technician time down and Manager and Entrepreneur time up. Two coding days become one, with the other turned into a Manager day for hiring, one-to-ones and documenting the release process. He adds a weekly Entrepreneur block for customer interviews and pricing decisions.

The first review shows him drifting back into code during the Manager day whenever a bug appears. He writes an escalation rule with the new engineer so bugs go to her first, and the next review shows the Manager day holding.
