# Examples: Story Mapping Facilitation: How to Run a Workshop

## An in-person workshop for a new feature

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team wants to add group bookings to a travel app. The facilitator invites a product manager, a designer, two developers, a tester, a customer support lead who handles group requests today, and the business owner for the feature.

**Walkthrough:**

The session opens with the brief: groups of friends planning a trip together want to book one itinerary and split the cost. The support lead narrates how groups book today, over email and phone, and the group writes each step on cards. The backbone settles into: plan the trip, invite the group, agree the itinerary, pay, travel.

During exploration, the developers flag "split the cost" as expensive and uncertain, so it gets a risk marker. When arranging, a card for "group chat" moves down and back up twice. The facilitator stops and asks why, and it turns out the designer sees chat as necessary for agreeing the itinerary while the business owner sees it as a nice-to-have. They agree on a simpler task above the line, "comment on an itinerary option," and move chat below. The business owner draws the first release line and writes its outcome on a card to the left.

## A remote session across time zones

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a distributed team maps an onboarding flow on a shared whiteboard. Half the group joins early in their day and half late in theirs, so the session has to be short.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator sets up the board in advance with labelled rows for activities, tasks and releases, and sends the brief and a user sketch the day before. In the session, everyone writes tasks silently for the big picture, then the facilitator reads them in order and the group arranges them. Exploration is split by column, with each pair owning one activity. The group stops before slicing and agrees to meet again for the release line, with the business owner reviewing the map in between.

## Recovering a workshop that went too deep

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: halfway through a session, a team has written detailed stories and acceptance criteria for the first activity, and the other activities have no cards at all.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator calls a pause, walks the map aloud, and points out that the story stops after its first chapter. The detailed cards are moved down the first column, and the group returns to a wide, shallow pass across the rest of the journey. By the end, every activity has at least its high-level tasks, and the detailed cards are recorded as input for later refinement.
