# Examples: Writing V2MOM Vision and Values Statements

## A support team turns a slogan into a checkable vision

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a customer support team of eight people is writing its first V2MOM under a company V2MOM whose second method is "cut the time new customers take to reach their first successful project." The team's first draft vision reads "Deliver world-class support."

**Walkthrough:**

The lead runs the check test and asks two team members what "world-class" would look like at year end. One describes faster replies and the other describes fewer tickets, so the draft means different things to different readers. The team goes back to the parent method and rewrites the vision as "New customers get unblocked in their first month without waiting on us." Both readers now describe the same outcome, and the vision clearly serves the company method. Faster reply times and a better onboarding guide become candidate methods instead of competing interpretations of the vision.

## A product team ranks speed against reliability

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team lists four values: speed, reliability, customer empathy and craft. Everyone agrees with all four, and nobody wants to rank them.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator picks a real decision from last quarter: a feature was ready on schedule but had a known bug affecting a small group of users. The team discusses whether speed or reliability should have won and agrees that shipping with a known data bug was wrong. Reliability goes above speed. A second scenario, a week spent polishing animations while a customer waited on a fix, puts customer empathy above craft. The final order is reliability, customer empathy, speed, craft, and each value gets a behavior sentence, for example "Reliability: we hold a release rather than ship a known defect that touches customer data."

## A founder separates the mission from this year's vision

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the founder of a small startup drafts a company vision that reads like a mission: "Make planning effortless for every team in the world."

**Walkthrough:**

The leadership group agrees the sentence is a good long-term purpose but fails the check test for a single year. They keep it as the company mission and write a separate V2MOM vision for the year: "Mid-sized software teams run their quarterly planning in our product end to end." The new vision names a customer and a behavior the team can observe. The values ranking that follows is sharper, because the group is now deciding tradeoffs for a specific destination instead of an open-ended ideal.
