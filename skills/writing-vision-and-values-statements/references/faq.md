# FAQ: Writing V2MOM Vision and Values Statements

## How long should a V2MOM vision be?

One sentence is the target, two at most. The vision has to fit in someone's head so they can check a decision against it without opening the document. If it needs a paragraph, it probably mixes the destination with the methods for reaching it. Move the how into Methods and keep only the destination.

## How many values should a V2MOM have?

Three to five is a practical range. Fewer than three rarely covers the real tradeoffs a team faces, and more than five is hard to rank and hard to remember. If the list keeps growing, check whether some items are methods ("run weekly customer calls") or preferences that never conflict with anything.

## Should team values repeat the company values?

A team can inherit the company values, reinterpret them for its context or add one or two of its own. What it should not do is rank them in a way that contradicts the company ranking without a conversation. If a team believes its work needs a different order, raise it with the owner of the parent V2MOM, because that disagreement is useful information.

## What if leadership refuses to rank the values?

Start with scenarios instead of the abstract question. Ask which value won in two or three real decisions from the past year, and write the order that the decisions imply. Leaders often find it easier to accept a ranking that describes what they already did. If they still refuse, note that the V2MOM will not help settle conflicts, which is one of its main purposes.

## How is a V2MOM vision different from a mission statement?

A mission statement describes a lasting purpose and rarely changes. A V2MOM vision describes what you want to achieve in the period the document covers, usually a year, and should be checkable at the end of it. Keeping them separate lets the vision change each cycle while the mission stays stable.
