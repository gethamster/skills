# Examples: Sprint User Testing: Running Design Sprint Day 5

## A clear answer on the main question

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team at a travel booking startup has prototyped a trip planner that builds a full itinerary from a few questions. The sprint questions are "Will travelers trust an itinerary they did not build?" and "Will they understand what is included in the price?"

**Walkthrough:**

The scorecard has two rows and five columns. Four of the five customers move through the planner smoothly and say they would book the suggested hotel, so the first row reads mostly yes. The second row reads mostly no: three customers scroll back and forth looking for whether flights are included, and two ask the Interviewer directly.

At the end of the day the Decider marks the trust question yes and the price question no. The team's hot takes converge on the same next step: keep the planner and redesign the price summary. The Decider schedules a short follow-up sprint focused on the price screen.

## A split result that points to two customer types

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team testing a scheduling tool for small clinics recruited five clinic managers. The key question is whether managers will let the tool book appointments automatically.

**Walkthrough:**

Two managers from single-doctor practices are enthusiastic; three from larger clinics hesitate and say they would need to approve every booking. The row for the key question splits. Reading the sticky notes, the team sees the split lines up with clinic size, a variable the screener did not control. The Decider records the question as unanswered for larger clinics and asks the recruiter to plan a follow-up test with managers from larger clinics only.

## Handling a no-show

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the third customer of the day cancels an hour before the session. The team had recruited one backup.

**Walkthrough:**

The recruiter calls the backup, who can come in the last slot of the day. The Interviewer uses the free hour to review notes with the observers and check that the prototype still works on the test device. The team still completes five interviews, and the scorecard has a full set of columns by the end of the afternoon. Without the backup, the team would have made its call on four customers.
