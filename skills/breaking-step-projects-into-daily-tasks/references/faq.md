# FAQ: Breaking Step-Projects into Daily Tasks

## Does GIST change how we run sprints?

Not at the task level. Itamar Gilad writes that tasks are well covered by agile planning tools and Kanban boards, and nothing needs to change there. What changes is that the tasks in a sprint now serve step-projects, which test ideas tied to goals.

## How small should tasks be?

Small enough to finish in about a day. The Scrum Guide notes that developers often break work into items of one day or less. Small tasks make daily progress visible, which matters when a step-project has a fixed end date.

## Who breaks the step into tasks?

The people who will do the work, with the step's owner. The product manager or step owner brings the question and the threshold, and engineers and designers bring the knowledge of what building and measuring actually involve.

## What if the tasks do not fit the step's time box?

Cut scope back to what the question needs, or split the step into two steps that test different assumptions. Do not quietly extend the step, because a step that grows loses its value as a cheap test.

## How do we keep tasks linked to goals?

Tag each task with its step-project, and keep the step linked to its idea and goal on the GIST board. That chain lets anyone see why a task exists and lets the team clean up quickly when a step ends.
