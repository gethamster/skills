# Examples: Running a Start Stop Continue Icebreaker

## A newly formed product team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: five people from three different teams have just been brought together to build a new feature. Their first planning meeting is in an hour, and the lead wants them comfortable disagreeing before real decisions come up.

**Walkthrough:**

The lead picks the topic "how our old teams ran planning meetings." Everyone writes one item per column in two minutes. The lead goes first with a Stop item about their own habit of running over time.

Each person shares one item. Several Continue items mention a written agenda sent the day before. Two Stop items mention decisions made in side chats. The debrief is short: the lead points out that everyone named something to change.

The group agrees to two practices for its own planning meetings: an agenda the day before, and decisions recorded in the meeting notes. These become the first lines of the team's working agreements.

## A workshop warm-up with people who have not met

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a facilitator opens a half-day workshop on customer onboarding with twelve people from support, sales and product. Most of them know each other only by name.

**Walkthrough:**

The topic is "how our company runs internal workshops." People write on cards silently and then pair up to share one item each with a partner, which is less exposing than speaking to the whole room.

Each pair then reports one item to the room. A common Stop item is workshops that end without clear next steps. The facilitator writes it on a flip chart and promises that this workshop will end with named owners.

The warm-up takes a few minutes, and the facilitator refers back to the flip chart at the end of the day when the group assigns owners.

## A remote team welcoming new members

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a distributed engineering team has added three new members in the same month. The team lead wants the newcomers to feel able to question existing habits.

**Walkthrough:**

At the start of the weekly call, the lead shares a board with the topic "how we use our team chat channel." Notes are anonymous and hidden until everyone is done.

When the notes are revealed, a newcomer's anonymous Stop item points out that important decisions get buried in long threads. Several longer-serving members agree. The team decides to start pinning a weekly summary of decisions, and one of the newcomers volunteers to draft the first one.

At the next call, the lead mentions the pinned summary and thanks the newcomer by name, which shows the new members that their input changed something.
