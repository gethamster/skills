# FAQ: Claude Topic Clusters with Constitutional Alignment

## What is a topic cluster?

It is a group of pages built around one broad pillar page, with narrower cluster pages each covering a subtopic and linking back to the pillar. The structure helps readers move from an overview to the detail they need. It also shows search engines how the pages relate.

## Why involve Claude in topic cluster planning?

Claude is good at breaking a subject into reader questions, spotting when two candidates are the same question in different words, and drafting briefs and linking plans. It is weak at knowing search demand, which you supply from your own data. The combination produces a plan faster than either alone.

## How many pages should a cluster have?

As many as there are distinct reader questions your site can answer well, and no more. The number depends on the topic and your expertise. If you find yourself adding pages to reach a target count, the extra pages are likely to overlap or add little.

## How is this different from generating a page for every keyword?

A page-per-keyword approach creates near-duplicates that compete with each other and can resemble what Google calls doorway or scaled content abuse. A cluster groups variants under the page that answers their shared question. Each page earns its place by serving a different reader need.

## Does the constitution say anything about SEO or topic clusters?

No. It describes Claude's values and how it should help people. The relevant parts here are its emphasis on a person's final goals and on being forthright, which is why stating the real goal in your prompt leads Claude to flag weak or duplicate pages.
