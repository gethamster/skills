# Examples: Building Interdisciplinary FDE Skills

## A strong engineer with a communication gap

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a backend engineer moving into an FDE role rates herself strong on software engineering, solid on data, and "some" on stakeholder communication, with no evidence at all for running a workshop. Her next engagement is with an operations-heavy logistics customer.

**Walkthrough:**

Her lead reviews the ratings and agrees, adding that her written updates are clear but she has never presented to executives. They pick two gaps: running stakeholder sessions, and learning the logistics domain. For the first, she co-leads the discovery workshop at the new customer with a senior FDE, then leads the second one herself with the senior FDE observing and giving feedback afterward. For the second, she spends her first days on site sitting with dispatchers. At the end of the engagement she rerates both areas with the workshops and the customer's feedback as evidence.

## Building a domain-learning routine

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an FDE has worked in retail and is assigned to an energy utility, a domain he knows nothing about.

**Walkthrough:**

He writes a short routine for his first days: read how the utility earns revenue and what regulators it answers to, list the terms he hears in meetings and ask someone to explain each one, sit with a control room operator for a shift, and ask two experts to walk him through a recent decision they made. He keeps the notes in a shared document. The routine later becomes a template the whole FDE team uses when entering a new industry.

## Running an FDE competency assessment for a team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a lead managing a new team of four FDEs wants a forward deployed engineer training plan before the team takes on several customers at once.

**Walkthrough:**

Each FDE lists sub-skills in the four areas and rates them on evidence; the lead reviews every sheet one to one. Laid side by side, the ratings show that the team is strong on engineering and weak on data quality work and executive communication. The lead pairs each FDE with a colleague strong in their gap, assigns data quality ownership on the next pipeline to the person who needs it most, and schedules a practice session where FDEs present a technical tradeoff to a mock executive audience. The team reassesses after the first round of engagements.
