# Examples: Running Sprint Retrospectives for Continuous Improvement

## After a release that broke an integration

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of five shipped a release that broke a partner integration for most of a day. Emotions are high, and the last retrospective, weeks ago, turned into finger-pointing.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator opens by reading the Prime Directive and reviewing the two actions from the previous retrospective: one done, one never assigned. The team uses a timeline format to reconstruct the release day, writing silently first. Clusters form around an undocumented deploy process and missing integration tests.

Asking why the deploy process is undocumented reveals that each person deploys differently. Two actions come out of it: one developer writes a deploy checklist by mid-sprint, and another adds tests for the broken endpoint before the next release. Both go on the sprint board, and both are confirmed done at the next retrospective.

## A quiet team giving surface answers

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a cross-functional team of ten has run the same Start-Stop-Continue format for months. Attendance is slipping and most notes say "sprint went fine."

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator switches to a Sailboat format and adds an anonymous one-question check at the start: how safe do people feel raising concerns here? The low result is shared without names, and the team discusses what would make it higher.

With silent writing and anonymous notes, far more observations appear than in recent sessions. The top cluster is late design handoffs. The team agrees one action: designers show work in progress mid-sprint, owned by the design lead. Attendance recovers over the following retrospectives.

## A calm sprint with a light format

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a sprint went smoothly, the goal was met, and the team suggests skipping the retrospective.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator keeps the slot but shortens it and uses the 4Ls format. The team notices that the smooth sprint coincided with fewer meetings and a product owner who was available every afternoon.

The single action is to protect those afternoon hours as a standing arrangement, owned by the product owner. The retrospective takes half its usual time and keeps the habit intact.
