# FAQ: Running Sprint Retrospectives for Continuous Improvement

## How long should a sprint retrospective take?

The Scrum Guide sets a maximum of three hours for a one-month sprint, and shorter sprints usually need less. Teams on short sprints can usually finish well within that maximum. Protect enough time to reach specific actions, since that is the phase that gets cut when sessions run short.

## Should the manager attend?

The product owner is part of the Scrum team and usually attends. A line manager who evaluates team members can make people less willing to speak openly, so many teams invite managers only when the team asks. Share the agreed actions with the manager afterward if they help remove obstacles.

## What if the same issues come up every time?

Recurring issues usually mean earlier actions were too big, too vague or never tracked. Review completion of past actions, dig further into causes, and scope the next action small enough for one person to finish within the sprint. If the cause sits outside the team, make escalating it the action.

## How do I stop one person from dominating?

Silent writing before discussion gives everyone a voice. During discussion, go around the group for each topic and invite people who have not spoken. If one person still dominates, talk to them privately afterward about the facilitation concern.

## Do Kanban teams need retrospectives?

Kanban does not prescribe them, but regular reflection is an agile principle and flow teams benefit from it. Many Kanban teams hold a retrospective on a fixed cadence or after a set amount of finished work.
