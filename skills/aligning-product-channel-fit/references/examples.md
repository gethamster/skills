# Examples: Aligning Product-Channel Fit

## A collaboration tool that never built the loop

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small team sells a shared planning board. They believe it should spread by word of mouth because people use it together, yet almost all new users arrive through paid search.

**Walkthrough:**

The team lists the product's distribution traits and confirms it is used in groups, with value arriving quickly once a second person joins. Virality should fit. When they look at the product, though, inviting a colleague is buried in settings, and shared boards show nothing to a viewer who has not signed up.

They choose virality as the primary channel and list the product changes: an invite prompt inside the first board, a read-only view for invited people, and a clear path from that view to signup. After shipping those changes they measure new users brought in per existing user for a fixed period, alongside activation for invited users, and decide whether to keep paid search as a secondary channel.

## An analytics product pointed at the wrong channel

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a startup sells a reporting product to finance leaders at mid-sized companies, priced as an annual contract. The founders have been trying to grow through a self-serve free trial and social ads.

**Walkthrough:**

Listing traits shows a committee purchase, a setup that needs data connections, and a time to value measured in weeks. None of that suits self-serve ads. Checking the price against channel cost points to inside sales fed by content.

The team chooses inside sales as the primary channel. The product changes are a guided pilot that a salesperson can run with the customer, a data connection checklist, and a summary report the buyer can share internally. They validate by tracking how many qualified conversations become paid pilots and how many pilots convert.

## A marketplace that depends on search

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a local services marketplace gets most of its traffic from search. A search engine update reduces the visibility of thin listing pages, and traffic drops.

**Walkthrough:**

Because search is the primary channel, the team treats the update as a product problem. They review what the platform now rewards and find that pages with genuine reviews and specific details perform better.

The product changes are review prompts after each completed job, richer provider profiles, and removing listing pages with no content. The team also starts a short list of candidate secondary channels in case search keeps weakening, and records the rule change in their channel notes for the next audit.
