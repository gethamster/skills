# FAQ: Aligning Product-Channel Fit

## Why build the product for the channel instead of choosing a channel that fits the product?

You do both, in order. First you pick a channel that matches the product's traits and price. Then you change the product to work well inside that channel, because the channel's rules are outside your control. The second half is the part teams skip.

## Can a product have more than one primary channel?

Over time, yes, and larger companies often add channels as they grow. At any given stage, though, growth usually comes mostly from one channel, and splitting product attention early tends to weaken all of them. Add a second primary channel once the first is working and understood.

## How long should a channel validation run?

Long enough to see activation and early retention for the users it brings, not just signups. That depends on your product's time to value. Set the period and the success metric before you start so the result is not reinterpreted afterwards.

## What should I do when a platform changes its rules?

Treat it as a product question first. Look at what the platform now rewards, decide whether the product can adapt, and list the changes needed. If the channel can no longer carry the business, re-run this skill to choose the next one.

## How does product-channel fit relate to pricing?

Price limits which channels you can afford. A channel that suits the product but costs more to acquire a customer than that customer pays back will fail at the channel and model check. Keep revenue per customer in view while choosing channels, then confirm the economics in the matching skill.
