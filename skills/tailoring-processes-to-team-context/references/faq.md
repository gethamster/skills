# FAQ: Tailoring Agile Processes to Your Specific Team Context

## Is it still Crystal if we drop most of the techniques?

Yes, as long as the core properties hold. Cockburn describes Crystal as a family with a common genetic code rather than a fixed set of ceremonies, and the techniques in the book are a menu. What would make it no longer Crystal is abandoning the properties themselves, such as frequent delivery or reflective improvement.

## Who should own the tailored process?

A lead usually facilitates and keeps the document current, but the whole team should shape and challenge it. The people doing the work notice friction first. Ownership by one person tends to produce a process that looks tidy on paper and diverges from what actually happens.

## How often should we change our conventions?

Review them at every reflection workshop, and make small changes rather than large ones. Changing one or two things per cycle lets you see what each change did. Revisit the context itself whenever team size, location or project stakes shift noticeably.

## What if a stakeholder insists on a practice the team thinks is useless?

Ask which need the practice serves for the stakeholder, then look for the cheapest way to meet that need. Often a report or meeting exists because someone lacks visibility, and a lighter channel solves it. Record the agreed approach and its reason so the discussion does not restart every month.

## Can we tailor Crystal for a distributed team?

You can shape practices for any team, but Crystal's communication properties assume people are close together, and some sources describe it as most useful for co-located teams. A distributed team should treat close communication as a property needing extra techniques rather than assume remote channels replace it. Test it honestly with your property questions and adjust.
