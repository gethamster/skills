# Examples: Tailoring Agile Processes to Your Specific Team Context

## Trimming ceremony on a small co-located team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team of five sitting in one room inherited a heavy sprint process from a larger department and spends a large share of each week in meetings.

**Walkthrough:**

The lead inventories the practices and asks which property each serves. The formal planning meeting and a weekly status report both serve communication, which already holds because everyone overhears each other. The team drops the status report as a one-cycle experiment and replaces the planning meeting with a shorter planning session. Reflective improvement was missing, so they add a monthly reflection workshop.

At the first workshop nobody misses the status report, so the drop becomes permanent and is recorded with its reason.

## Restoring a weak property after growth

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team grew from six to eleven people over a few months, and questions that used to be answered in minutes now wait days.

**Walkthrough:**

At the reflection workshop the team re-runs its property questions and marks close communication as weak. Their context section still described a small team, so they update it first. They add a brief daily check-in and move the two new members next to the people they most often need. They write the reason for both changes into the agreement.

After one cycle, answers are faster again, and the team also opens a separate discussion on whether a different Crystal variant now fits better.

## Replacing an unused technique

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team adopted a structured estimation technique early on, but estimates are rarely used and the sessions feel like a chore.

**Walkthrough:**

The facilitator asks which property estimation serves and who uses the output. The only consumer is the product manager, who needs rough sizing for release conversations, not detailed numbers. The team drops the formal sessions and agrees the product manager will ask for a quick size when needed. They note the change and set a check: if release planning becomes unreliable, they will revisit it.

Two workshops later, planning is fine, and the drop stays.
