# How to Run Spotify Model Chapters and Chapter Leads

> Set up discipline-based chapters and chapter leads so specialists get craft management and shared standards without losing squad autonomy.

## At a Glance

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Time to Learn | 2-4 weeks to set up, then ongoing |
| Outcome | Every specialist has a chapter lead who understands their craft, a peer group that meets regularly, and a written remit that separates discipline management from squad delivery. |
| Prerequisites | Squads already formed and grouped into tribes, A list of the disciplines present in each squad, Agreement from HR and senior leadership on who holds line-management authority |
| Part of | [Spotify Squads](../../methods/spotify-squads/METHOD.md) |

## Overview

A chapter is the home for people who share a discipline but sit in different squads. In the structure documented in the Spotify scaling paper, [a chapter groups specialists with the same skill across the squads of one tribe](https://atlassian.com/agile/agile-at-scale/spotify), such as backend engineers, testers or iOS engineers. For the model's background and how chapters sit alongside squads, tribes and guilds, see the [Spotify Squads method page](https://tryhamster.com/methods/spotify-squads). This page covers the practical work of running chapters.

Chapters solve a problem that squad autonomy creates. When every squad is cross-functional, specialists are spread thin: the only designer in a squad has nobody nearby who can judge their craft, and several squads can end up solving the same technical problem in incompatible ways. A chapter gives each specialist a manager who understands their work and a peer group facing the same problems. [At Spotify, every squad member is assigned to a chapter, with a chapter lead who serves, in effect, as a line manager](https://cisr.mit.edu/publication/2017_1201_DigitalDesignAtSpotify_BaiyereRossSebastian).

The design is a matrix with a clear primary allegiance. [The squad is the member's primary team for delivery, while the chapter lead remains their disciplinary supervisor](https://usu.com/en/blog/the-spotify-model-magic-bullet-or-overrated). The chapter lead does not decide what the squad builds. According to the [original Spotify paper](https://storage.ghost.io/c/73/a9/73a90ce4-1663-4169-a7cb-efdf906b6b25/content/files/2022/12/Scaling-Agile-@-Spotify-with-Tribes--Squads--Chapters---Guilds-Henrik-Kniberg---Anders-Ivarsson-Oct-2012.pdf), the lead carries traditional people-management duties such as developing people and setting salaries, while remaining part of a squad and involved in its day-to-day work.

Running chapters well comes down to a handful of decisions: which disciplines get a chapter, who leads each one, what the chapter is responsible for, how often it meets, and how many people one lead can realistically manage. Practitioner guides suggest the remit should be written down, commonly covering [coding standards, interview loops, onboarding and technical direction](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/spotify-model).

You can tell a chapter is working when members get regular one-to-ones from someone who can assess their craft, standards converge across squads without anyone taking delivery decisions away from those squads, and hiring in the discipline feels consistent regardless of which squad a candidate joins. You can tell it is failing when the lead has no time for coaching, when chapter meetings turn into status reports, or when the chapter starts issuing instructions that override squad priorities. Treat the arrangement as something to adapt, not copy: the creator material presents chapters as part of Spotify's own context-specific design, and later guides describe adaptations rather than a fixed template.

## How It Works

Chapters run on two lines of accountability that should not overlap. The squad decides what to build and how to deliver it day to day. The chapter lead owns the person: their growth, pay, performance and craft standards. [Umbrex's organization guide](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/organization-frameworks/spotify-model-squads-tribes-chapters-guilds) describes the chapter lead as the line manager for members of the discipline, responsible for coaching, performance, hiring and standards, and [Umbrex's delivery guide](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/project-management-frameworks/spotify-model) frames the chapter's output as shared technical practices and consistency across squads without centralizing delivery work.

| Area | Squad | Chapter lead | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily delivery | Owns priorities and work | Contributes as a squad member | [Spotify paper](https://storage.ghost.io/c/73/a9/73a90ce4-1663-4169-a7cb-efdf906b6b25/content/files/2022/12/Scaling-Agile-@-Spotify-with-Tribes--Squads--Chapters---Guilds-Henrik-Kniberg---Anders-Ivarsson-Oct-2012.pdf) |
| Hiring | Flags capacity needs | Runs interview loops | [Rework guide](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/spotify-model) |
| Salary and performance | Gives input | Decides | [Spotify paper](https://storage.ghost.io/c/73/a9/73a90ce4-1663-4169-a7cb-efdf906b6b25/content/files/2022/12/Scaling-Agile-@-Spotify-with-Tribes--Squads--Chapters---Guilds-Henrik-Kniberg---Anders-Ivarsson-Oct-2012.pdf) |
| Coaching | Daily feedback | One-to-ones, career growth | [Umbrex delivery guide](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/project-management-frameworks/spotify-model) |
| Standards | Applies them | Sets them with the chapter | [Umbrex org guide](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/organization-frameworks/spotify-model-squads-tribes-chapters-guilds) |

The squad column above is a recommended division, not something the sources prescribe. The point is that each row has one decision owner, so a member never gets conflicting instructions about the same thing.

The chapter lead stays inside a squad. The [Spotify paper](https://storage.ghost.io/c/73/a9/73a90ce4-1663-4169-a7cb-efdf906b6b25/content/files/2022/12/Scaling-Agile-@-Spotify-with-Tribes--Squads--Chapters---Guilds-Henrik-Kniberg---Anders-Ivarsson-Oct-2012.pdf) keeps the lead involved in day-to-day work rather than making the role purely managerial. That keeps the lead connected to the conditions their reports actually work in, which makes performance conversations and standards credible.

Chapter meetings are the chapter's shared working time. Specialists use them to [discuss their own technical topics, share experiences and challenges, and coordinate technologies, tools, processes and methods](https://usu.com/en/blog/the-spotify-model-magic-bullet-or-overrated), and to [keep each other up to date and share solutions to common problems](https://productschool.com/blog/product-fundamentals/spotify-model-scaling-agile). A problem one squad has solved should reach the others through this channel instead of being rediscovered.

Size decides whether the lead can do the job at all. One practitioner guide puts a manageable chapter at [5-15 people](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/spotify-model), while another describes chapters as [typically 5-20 people](https://mooncamp.com/glossary/spotify-model). The sources disagree, so treat these as a starting range rather than a rule, and set the real limit by how many people the lead can coach while still doing squad work. Henrik Kniberg has described what happens when the limit is ignored: [chapter leads had so many direct reports that finding time for one-to-ones, active mentoring and coaching became difficult](https://infoq.com/jp/news/2013/04/scaling-agile-spotify-kniberg). When a chapter grows past what one lead can coach, split it, typically by sub-specialty or by squad cluster.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Map disciplines across the tribe

List every squad in the tribe and the disciplines each one contains, such as backend, frontend, design, testing or data. Chapters should be built around [the disciplines that cut across squads in a tribe](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/spotify-model), not formed as extra delivery teams. A discipline that appears in several squads is a chapter candidate. A discipline held by a single person in one squad usually is not, and that person may need a neighbouring chapter or a guild instead.

> **Pro tip:** Build the map as a squads-by-disciplines grid; columns with many filled cells are your chapters.

### Step 2: Choose the chapter lead

Pick someone who is a credible practitioner in the discipline and who can take on line management. The [Spotify paper](https://storage.ghost.io/c/73/a9/73a90ce4-1663-4169-a7cb-efdf906b6b25/content/files/2022/12/Scaling-Agile-@-Spotify-with-Tribes--Squads--Chapters---Guilds-Henrik-Kniberg---Anders-Ivarsson-Oct-2012.pdf) gives the lead traditional people-management duties, including developing people and setting salaries, while keeping them in a squad. That combination rules out both a pure manager with no current craft practice and a strong specialist with no interest in people. Confirm the lead's squad understands their reduced delivery capacity before announcing the role.

> **Pro tip:** Ask candidates how they would handle a performance problem in someone from a squad they do not sit in; the answer shows whether they grasp the matrix.

### Step 3: Write the chapter remit

Put in writing what the chapter owns and what it does not. Practitioner guidance commonly includes [coding standards, interview loops, onboarding and technical direction](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/spotify-model), and the lead's own output covers [coaching, performance, hiring and standards](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/organization-frameworks/spotify-model-squads-tribes-chapters-guilds). Name explicitly what stays with squads, especially delivery priorities and release decisions. Share the remit with squad product owners so they know which questions to route to the chapter.

> **Pro tip:** Add a short 'not our job' list; it prevents more boundary disputes than the ownership list does.

### Step 4: Set chapter size and reporting load

Decide how many members each lead can manage while still contributing to a squad. Guides differ, citing [5-15 people](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/spotify-model) and [5-20 people](https://mooncamp.com/glossary/spotify-model), so use them as bounds to reason from. The real test is whether every member gets regular one-to-ones and coaching. Kniberg noted that [too many direct reports left chapter leads unable to find time for one-to-ones, mentoring and coaching](https://infoq.com/jp/news/2013/04/scaling-agile-spotify-kniberg), so plan the split point before you reach it.

### Step 5: Run regular chapter meetings

Set a recurring meeting where the chapter works on its own discipline rather than squad status. Use it to [share experiences and challenges and coordinate tools, processes and methods](https://usu.com/en/blog/the-spotify-model-magic-bullet-or-overrated), and to [share solutions to common problems](https://productschool.com/blog/product-fundamentals/spotify-model-scaling-agile) across squads. Keep a running list of decisions on standards so members can apply them in their squads. If attendance drops, check whether the agenda has drifted toward updates nobody needs.

> **Pro tip:** Open each meeting with one problem a member hit in their squad this cycle; it keeps the agenda grounded in real work.

### Step 6: Run people management through the chapter

Move performance reviews, salary input, career conversations and hiring decisions for the discipline to the chapter lead. The chapter is the setting for [people development, coaching and standards](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/project-management-frameworks/spotify-model) while members work day to day in their squads. Collect input from the member's squad product owner and teammates, because the lead only sees part of the member's work. Record the process so members know who evaluates them and on what basis.

> **Pro tip:** Schedule a short check-in between the chapter lead and each squad product owner before every review cycle.

### Step 7: Review the squad boundary

Every few months, look for places where the chapter has started making delivery decisions or squads have ignored agreed standards. The [Umbrex delivery guide](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/project-management-frameworks/spotify-model) describes the chapter's value as consistency without centralizing delivery work, so any drift toward assigning squad tasks is a warning sign. Ask members whether they ever receive conflicting instructions from lead and squad. Adjust the remit when the answer is yes.

## Best Practices

- Keep the chapter lead in a squad. The [Spotify paper](https://storage.ghost.io/c/73/a9/73a90ce4-1663-4169-a7cb-efdf906b6b25/content/files/2022/12/Scaling-Agile-@-Spotify-with-Tribes--Squads--Chapters---Guilds-Henrik-Kniberg---Anders-Ivarsson-Oct-2012.pdf) keeps leads involved in day-to-day work, which keeps their judgment of members' work grounded in current conditions.
- Give each decision one owner. Squads own what gets built and when; the chapter lead owns pay, performance and craft standards. Members who get conflicting instructions on the same topic are a sign the split is unclear.
- Write the remit down and share it with squads. Listing items such as [coding standards, interview loops and onboarding](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/spotify-model) lets product owners know which questions belong to the chapter.
- Size chapters for coaching, not for org-chart neatness. The practitioner ranges of [5-15](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/spotify-model) and [5-20](https://mooncamp.com/glossary/spotify-model) people are starting points; the lead's actual coaching capacity sets the limit.
- Use chapter meetings to spread solutions, not report status. The chapter exists partly so specialists can [share solutions to common problems](https://productschool.com/blog/product-fundamentals/spotify-model-scaling-agile) that recur across squads.
- Treat the setup as an adaptation. The original material describes chapters inside Spotify's own organization, and guides such as [Umbrex's](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/organization-frameworks/spotify-model-squads-tribes-chapters-guilds) describe variations, so fit remit and size to your own disciplines.

## Common Mistakes

- **Making the chapter lead a detached manager with no squad role.** — Keep the lead part of a squad, as the [Spotify paper](https://storage.ghost.io/c/73/a9/73a90ce4-1663-4169-a7cb-efdf906b6b25/content/files/2022/12/Scaling-Agile-@-Spotify-with-Tribes--Squads--Chapters---Guilds-Henrik-Kniberg---Anders-Ivarsson-Oct-2012.pdf) describes. Reduce their delivery load rather than removing it.
- **Letting one lead accumulate so many reports that coaching stops.** — Kniberg described leads with [too many direct reports to find time for one-to-ones, mentoring and coaching](https://infoq.com/jp/news/2013/04/scaling-agile-spotify-kniberg). Set a split point in advance and act on it when the chapter reaches it.
- **Turning the chapter into a second delivery team that assigns squad work.** — Keep delivery with squads. The chapter supplies management, capability development and technical alignment, and [the squad remains the member's primary team for delivery](https://usu.com/en/blog/the-spotify-model-magic-bullet-or-overrated).
- **Leaving the remit implicit.** — Write down what the chapter owns, such as standards, interviews and onboarding, and what stays with squads. Without it, every boundary question becomes a negotiation.
- **Running chapter meetings as status updates.** — Focus meetings on the discipline: shared problems, tools, methods and standards, as [practitioner guidance describes](https://usu.com/en/blog/the-spotify-model-magic-bullet-or-overrated). Squad status belongs in squad forums.
