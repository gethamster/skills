# Examples: Planning a Design Sprint Agenda and Schedule

## A first sprint on the full five-day schedule

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team at a mid-sized software company wants to test a new self-serve onboarding flow. Nobody on the team has run a sprint before, and the head of product has agreed to be the Decider.

**Walkthrough:**

The Facilitator chooses the five-day format, since it is the team's first sprint. They write a one-page brief with the challenge, the Decider and the dates, invite six core participants and book three experts for short slots on Monday afternoon. Recruiting starts two weeks ahead with a screener aimed at small-business owners who signed up for a trial but never finished setup. By the Thursday before the sprint, five customers and one backup are confirmed.

The Facilitator copies the published checklist into a shared schedule, marks the Decider's required moments in bold, and buys a supply kit of sticky notes, pens, dots and timers. A short meeting with the Decider the week before settles how tie-breaks will work and who will act on the result.

## Choosing four days for an experienced team

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team that has run two five-day sprints needs to test a pricing page redesign, but its Decider, a vice president, can only give two days.

**Walkthrough:**

The Facilitator proposes a four-day format in which the Decider attends the first two days, when the team maps, sketches, decides and storyboards, and checks in briefly during prototyping and at the end of testing. Experts are booked only for the first morning. The team keeps every phase and accepts shorter expert interviews. Recruiting still starts early, with five customers and a backup booked for the fourth day.

## A sprint rescheduled for lack of a Decider

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: two weeks before a planned sprint, the product director who agreed to be Decider is pulled onto an urgent project and cannot attend or name a delegate.

**Walkthrough:**

The Facilitator reviews the brief and concludes that without a Decider the team would end the week with a recommendation that still needed approval. They move the sprint by three weeks, keep the recruited customers informed, and use the extra time to gather existing research for Monday's expert interviews. The director confirms the new dates and names a delegate in case of another conflict.
