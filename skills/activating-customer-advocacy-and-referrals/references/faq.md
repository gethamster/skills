# FAQ: Activating Customer Advocacy and Referral Programs

## When is a business ready for an advocacy program?

When the retention stage is healthy: customers stay, get value and would say so if asked. Check recent satisfaction responses, churn and reviews before building anything. If those are weak, an advocacy program will surface complaints rather than referrals. Fix retention first.

## Should referral incentives be cash, credit or something else?

Choose something the referrer is comfortable accepting and that does not make the recommendation feel bought. Account credit or a discount on the product is common because it keeps value inside the relationship. Two-sided rewards, where the new customer also benefits, reduce awkwardness. Keep the amount modest enough that it does not attract people who only want the reward.

## How do I identify advocates without a Net Promoter survey?

Use behavior. Customers who use the product heavily, renew without negotiation, praise you in support conversations or attend your events are likely advocates. You can also ask account managers who their happiest customers are. A survey helps, but it is not required to start.

## Is it acceptable to ask only happy customers for reviews?

Asking only customers you expect to be positive can break the rules of some review platforms and misleads buyers. Ask customers broadly at a moment of value, and respond openly to every review, including negative ones. Honest reviews with visible responses build more trust than a wall of perfect scores.

## How do I know if the program is working?

Compare the customers the program brings in with similar customers from other channels. Look at conversion, retention and value over time, not only at the number of advocates who signed up. If referred customers are no better than others, revisit who you ask and what you offer.
