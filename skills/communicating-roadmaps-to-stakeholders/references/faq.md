# FAQ: Communicating Now Next Later Roadmaps to Stakeholders

## How do I answer "when will it ship?"

Answer with the objective it serves, where it sits on the horizon and what would need to be true for it to move to Now. If the item has a real external date, give that date. If it is in Now and the team has estimated it, you can give a range. For items in Next and Later, explain that a date would be a guess and offer to report progress against the objective instead.

## What if executives insist on a dated roadmap?

Start by asking what they need the dates for, since the underlying need is often a target or a coordination point. Offer objectives with time-bound key results, plus dates on the few items that have real deadlines. If they still need a dated view, provide one for the Now column only and keep the rest in horizons.

## Should sales see the roadmap?

Yes, in a version designed for them. Show problem statements and horizons with approved language, leave out candidate solutions, and give them a route to request a real commitment when a deal depends on one. Hiding the roadmap from sales usually leads to informal promises based on hearsay.

## How often should stakeholders get roadmap updates?

After every reprioritization review, as a short note on what moved and why. People affected by a specific move deserve a direct message. Regular, brief updates do more for trust than occasional long presentations.

## Can we share the roadmap publicly?

Many teams do. Use a customer view built from the same source, showing problems rather than solutions, no dates and a list of recently launched work. Leave off anything sensitive. A public view also gives you feedback on the direction before you build.
