# FAQ: Applying the E-Myth Framework to Agencies

## Why does the E-Myth fit agencies so well?

Agencies are usually started by practitioners who were good at the craft and began taking clients, which is the Technician founder Gerber describes. The founder's skill becomes the product, so the agency's capacity is tied to the founder's hours. The framework gives a way to separate the service from the person who currently delivers it.

## Can creative work really be systematized?

The routine parts can, and they are larger than most founders expect: scoping, kickoff, file handling, review steps, handover and billing. The creative core needs judgment, but the standard it must meet can often be written as criteria. Systems then protect creative time by removing avoidable chaos around it.

## What if clients hired us because of the founder?

Keep the founder visible at the moments clients value most, such as kickoff and major reviews, and introduce an account lead for everything else. Explain the change directly. Clients mostly stay when the quality holds and someone responsive owns their work.

## Should we productize our services?

Consider it for work you deliver repeatedly with a similar scope. A fixed-scope offer is easier to document, price and deliver without the founder. Keep custom work where it creates real value, and use a productized first phase to structure it.

## Where should the founder's time go after the shift?

Into Manager work, such as playbooks, hiring and delivery reviews, and Entrepreneur work, such as choosing services and clients, pricing and partnerships. Plan these blocks before the time is freed. Otherwise the founder tends to take on new client work and recreate the dependency.
