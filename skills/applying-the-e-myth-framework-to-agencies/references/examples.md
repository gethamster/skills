# Examples: Applying the E-Myth Framework to Agencies

## A branding agency where every logo goes through the founder

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a branding agency with six designers has a founder who reviews every concept before it goes to a client. Projects queue for his review, and he works evenings to keep up.

**Walkthrough:**

The dependency map shows three founder-only steps: pricing proposals, creative direction at kickoff and final concept review. He writes a playbook for the agency's most common project, a brand identity package, including written criteria for a concept that is ready to show: it answers the brief, works in one color and at small sizes, and has a clear rationale.

A senior designer becomes the checker at the concept gate. For two months the founder reviews the gate results and one concept per project. Once the gate catches the problems he would have caught, he stops reviewing concepts and keeps only kickoff direction for new clients, using the freed evenings to design a smaller fixed-price package.

## A two-partner accounting practice

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: two partners run an accounting practice with four staff. Every client relationship belongs personally to one partner, and the practice cannot take on more clients.

**Walkthrough:**

Their map shows that staff prepare the work but partners handle every client question, every review and every year-end meeting. They write playbooks for their two main services, annual accounts and monthly bookkeeping, with a review gate a senior staff member can run for straightforward clients.

Each partner introduces a senior staff member as day-to-day contact for their simpler clients and keeps the year-end meeting. The partners use the freed time to build an advisory service for their larger clients, a decision they reach by applying the Entrepreneur questions from the vision skill.

## A development shop that sells custom work

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small software development agency sells only custom projects, each scoped by the founder. No two projects look alike, and the team cannot quote without her.

**Walkthrough:**

The founder realizes the custom work is hard to systematize, but the discovery phase that starts every project is always similar. She productizes it as a fixed-scope discovery engagement with its own playbook, deliverables and price, delivered by a lead developer.

Discovery now runs without her, and its output gives the team a structured basis for quoting the build. She keeps final approval of build quotes for now and writes down the pricing rules she uses, so that approval can eventually move to the lead developer as well.
