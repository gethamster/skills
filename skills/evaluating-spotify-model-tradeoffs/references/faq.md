# FAQ: Evaluating Spotify Model Tradeoffs and Failure Modes

## Should we adopt the Spotify model?

Evaluate each element rather than the model as a whole. Adopt the ones that solve a problem you can show evidence for and that your architecture and culture can support. Many organizations do well with cross-functional squads and guilds and skip other parts, or run chapters without line management.

## What are the most common failure modes?

The best documented are renaming teams without changing how decisions are made, keeping the old structure and governance alongside the new one, chapter management that leaves nobody accountable for delivery, squads with no shared way to collaborate, and architectures that do not let squads release on their own. Check each against your own situation.

## How long does a Spotify model evaluation take?

For most organizations, a focused evaluation takes one or two weeks of part-time work: a few days to map the current state and survey dependencies, a few more to rate elements and review them with the people affected. Larger organizations with several product areas may evaluate each area separately.

## What if leadership has already decided?

Agree before starting that the evaluation can change the plan, including skipping or delaying elements. If that is not possible, use the evaluation to set preconditions and success measures for each element, so problems are caught early rather than after a full rollout.

## Are there alternatives to the Spotify model?

Yes. Team Topologies describes team types and interaction modes for the same problem of organizing many teams. More prescriptive scaling frameworks exist for organizations that need a shared cadence. Sometimes the right answer is simply cross-functional teams with clear ownership, without tribes or chapters.
