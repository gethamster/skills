# Examples: Running a Remote Design Sprint with Distributed Teams

## A team spread across adjacent time zones

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a product team has members on the east and west coasts of North America, with a Decider on the east coast. The in-person schedule would start the west coast members very early.

**Walkthrough:**

The Facilitator fits each day into the hours both coasts share, which is shorter than the book's day. To keep every exercise, the sprint runs over six days instead of five, with the same order of activities. A tool check the week before catches two people who cannot edit the board. On Tuesday, everyone sketches on paper during the shared hours and sends photos of their solution sketches to the Facilitator, who has the art museum laid out on the board before Wednesday's session starts.

## Keeping discussion fair on video

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: during Monday's sprint questions, two senior people dominate the call and several others stay silent with their cameras off.

**Walkthrough:**

After a break, the Facilitator asks everyone to turn cameras on and introduces Pass the Mic: everyone is muted, and the Facilitator unmutes one person at a time in a fixed order, with the option to pass. The co-facilitator messages the quieter participants privately to check they can see the board. By the afternoon's How Might We round, every person has contributed notes, and the vote draws on the whole team's input.

## Testing with customers over video

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: the target customers for a small business accounting tool are spread across several regions, so the team cannot bring them to an office.

**Walkthrough:**

The recruiter books five customers and a backup for video interviews and sends each of them joining instructions and a short consent form in advance. On Friday, the Interviewer runs the sessions while the rest of the team watches on a separate stream that the customer cannot see. Observers enter a yes or no for each sprint question in a shared spreadsheet after each interview, and the Decider records the final answer. The team reviews the completed sheet together at the end of the day.
