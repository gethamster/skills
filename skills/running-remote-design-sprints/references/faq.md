# FAQ: Running a Remote Design Sprint with Distributed Teams

## Does a remote design sprint take less time than an in-person one?

No. The Remote Design Sprint Guide, compiled by Jake Knapp, John Zeratsky and Jackie Colburn, says the community is unanimous that remote sprints take longer ([Remote Design Sprint Guide](https://www.jackiecolburn.com/remote-design-sprint-guide)). It recommends keeping close to the book's schedule and adding more breaks. If time zones shorten the shared day, spread the same activities over more days.

## What tools do we need for a remote design sprint?

A video tool, an online whiteboard, and paper and pens for sketching. The guide's picks include Zoom for video and Miro for the whiteboard, and Knapp and Zeratsky offer an official Miro template for the design sprint ([Design Sprint guide](https://www.character.vc/guide/design-sprint)). A second screen helps each participant see the video and the board at once.

## How do we run sketching remotely?

Keep it on paper. Everyone sketches with a thick pen on plain printer paper, following the same four-step process, then photographs the solution sketch and sends it to the Facilitator. The Facilitator places the photos on the board for Wednesday's art museum.

## How do we handle voting online?

Give each person draggable dots on the board for the heat map, so votes stay visible and anonymous in the same way as dot stickers. Run the straw poll so that everyone places their vote at the same moment, and give the Decider distinct large dots for the supervote. Some teams use the board's built-in voting feature instead.

## Can we test with customers remotely?

Yes. Knapp and Zeratsky report that most of their customer tests with Michael Margolis at GV were done online, and that very few changes are needed ([Remote Design Sprint Guide](https://www.jackiecolburn.com/remote-design-sprint-guide)). Send joining instructions in advance, keep the observers out of the customer's view, and record the sessions with permission.
