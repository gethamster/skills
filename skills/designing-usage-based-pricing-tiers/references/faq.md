# FAQ: Designing Usage-Based Pricing Tiers for AI Products

## How many tiers should I have?

Usually a free or trial tier, two or three paid tiers and an enterprise option. Fewer tiers make the choice easier and billing simpler. Add a tier only when the usage distribution shows a distinct group that fits none of the existing ones.

## Should I design tiers before or after calculating unit economics?

After. The margin check at maximum use needs the cost per value unit. You can sketch customer groups from usage data at the same time, but prices and included usage should wait for the cost table.

## Why do customers bunch at the lowest paid tier?

Either the next tier is too expensive relative to what it adds, or the lowest tier includes enough for most customers. Check where customers sit relative to the limit. If most are far below it, the included usage is too generous; if many are at it, the step to the next tier is too steep.

## How do I handle customers whose usage swings from month to month?

Offer a commitment measured over a longer period, rollover of unused units for a limited time, or a pooled allowance across a team. Each smooths the bill without removing the limit. Make sure the margin check still holds when a customer uses a large share of the pool in one month.

## Should usage tiers have annual discounts?

They can, if the discount applies to the base fee and included usage and the overage rate stays published. Annual commitments help forecasting on both sides. Check the margin floor with the discount applied, since a discount reduces the headroom at maximum use.
