# Examples: Defining Target Markets for Expansion Strategies

## From "healthcare" to a definable target market

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a document signing product used by real estate agencies plans to expand into healthcare. The board has approved "healthcare" as the new market, and marketing needs something it can target.

**Walkthrough:**

Interviews with clinic managers, hospital administrators and dental practice owners show the pressing job belongs to small private clinics: collecting signed patient intake and consent forms before appointments without paper. Hospitals have the job too, but they buy through long procurement cycles and already have systems in place.

The team sets the boundary as independent clinics with a small number of practitioners, in regions where the product already meets the relevant privacy rules. A count from professional registers, cut down by the share of interviewees who described the problem as pressing, gives a reachable market the team can plan around. A small outreach test to clinic managers produces enough demo bookings to meet the threshold set in advance. The one-page definition names independent clinics as the primary target and dental practices as secondary.

## A consumer brand narrows a broad audience

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a maker of lightweight hiking gear wants to expand from serious hikers to "outdoor families." Early campaigns aimed at parents in general have performed poorly.

**Walkthrough:**

Interviews reveal that the job is specific: parents planning their first multi-day trip with young children who want to carry less weight. The circumstance, a first trip, is more predictive than income or age. The team redefines the boundary around families who have recently joined hiking clubs or booked family campsites.

The team sizes the group using club membership figures and campsite booking data shared by a partner, then tests a checklist and bundle offer through the partner's newsletter. Response is well above the earlier broad campaigns. The definition is rewritten around the first-trip circumstance, and messaging shifts from product specifications to planning help.

## Evidence that disproves the first definition

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an inventory software company targets mid-sized breweries as a market development move, assuming production managers are the buyers.

**Walkthrough:**

Interviews show production managers like the product, but purchases of this size are made by finance directors who care mainly about excise tax reporting. The company's current product does not handle that reporting.

The team records this as disconfirming evidence. The definition is revised to name the finance director as the buyer and excise reporting as the triggering job, and the move is flagged for the product team, since meeting the job now needs a product change. The Ansoff map is updated to show the initiative sitting between market development and diversification until that change is scoped.
