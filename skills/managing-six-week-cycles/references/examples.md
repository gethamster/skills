# Examples: Managing Shape Up Six-Week Build Cycles

## Holding the line on interruptions

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: in the third week of a cycle, a sales lead asks to borrow one of the two programmers on a big batch team for a day to build a custom report for a prospect.

**Walkthrough:**

The cycle owner explains that the programmer is committed to the current bet for the whole cycle, and that the request is not a crisis. They offer two routes: the programmer can look at it during cool-down, or the sales lead can bring a shaped version to the next betting table if the report matters beyond one prospect.

The sales lead chooses cool-down. The team keeps its momentum, and the report request is handled a few weeks later without breaking the bet.

## The circuit breaker trips

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team is building offline mode for a field-service app. At the start of the last week, two scopes are downhill, but Sync Conflicts is still uphill: the team has not found a way to merge edits made on two devices.

**Walkthrough:**

The cycle owner checks the two conditions for an extension. The outstanding work is a must-have, but it is not downhill, because the core problem is still unsolved. An extension would mean betting more time on an unknown.

They let the circuit breaker trip. The team ships nothing from the project this cycle, which is disappointing but bounded. During cool-down, the shaper works on the conflict problem with a senior engineer and finds a simpler rule: the last edit wins, with a visible history. A new pitch built on that rule goes to the next betting table.

## Running a small batch cycle

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small batch team of one designer and one programmer has three small projects for the cycle: a new export option, a notification settings cleanup, and a faster search filter.

**Walkthrough:**

Nobody schedules the three projects for them. The team looks at the pitches, decides the search filter carries the most unknowns, and starts there. The export option ships in the second week, the filter in the fourth, and the notification cleanup in the sixth.

The notification project runs tight at the end, so they drop a nice-to-have preview and ship the core change. All three projects are deployed before the cycle ends, which is the only requirement the book places on a small batch team.
