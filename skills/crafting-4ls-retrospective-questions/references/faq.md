# FAQ: Writing 4Ls Retrospective Questions for Each Category

## Are the four words enough on their own?

For an experienced team, sometimes. The original EBG technique used the words directly. Most teams get more specific notes with one or two questions per category, especially for Learned and Longed For.

## How many questions should each category have?

One or two. A long list per column turns silent writing into a survey, and people answer only the easiest questions.

## Should the questions change every sprint?

Partly. Keep one stable question per category so answers stay comparable, and rotate one that fits the sprint's events. Stable questions help when someone later looks for trends.

## How do I avoid leading questions?

Start with what, which or when, and avoid questions that assume a conclusion. "What slowed you down during the release?" is open. "Didn't the release go badly?" tells people what to write.

## How do I keep Lacked questions from inviting blame?

Ask about missing conditions, not people. "What was missing that would have helped?" keeps the focus on information, tools, time and access. Read the question as the person most likely to feel criticized and rephrase anything that could land as an accusation.
