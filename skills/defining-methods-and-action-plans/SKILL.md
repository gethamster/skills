# Defining V2MOM Methods and Action Plans

> Write V2MOM Methods as a short, ranked list of owned initiatives that move toward the vision, then break each one into concrete actions.

## Overview

Methods are the "how do you get it?" part of a V2MOM. They are the actions and initiatives a team commits to in order to reach its vision, and they are written in priority order. Marc Benioff asks authors to rank methods just as they rank values, in [his description of the V2MOM](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/how-to-create-alignment-within-your-company/), and [Trailhead's personal V2MOM module](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/selfmotivation/get-focused-with-your-personal-v2mom) puts it plainly: the highest-priority method goes at the top. The ranking is what makes the section useful when time or budget runs short.

The section usually goes wrong in one of three ways. The list is too long, so everything looks equally important. The items are activities ("hold weekly syncs") rather than initiatives that change something. Or the methods sound strategic but nobody could say who owns them or what they will do on Monday. This skill addresses all three.

The output is a list of methods, usually three to six, each written as a short initiative with a named owner and a first handful of actions underneath. In a cascaded organization each method also names the parent method it supports, so the team's work can be traced upward. The [V2MOM Framework](https://tryhamster.com/methods/v2mom-framework) method page explains how methods fit with the other four parts; here the focus is on writing them.

Methods sit between the vision, which says where you are going, and the measures, which say how you will know you got there. A method with no measure attached cannot be checked. A measure with no method behind it is a hope. Writing methods well therefore means writing them with their measures and obstacles in mind, even though those sections have their own skills.

## How It Works

A method describes a change the team will make, stated at the level of an initiative. "Launch self-serve onboarding for small accounts" is a method. "Write the onboarding copy" is an action under it, and "grow the business" is too broad to act on. A useful test is the owner test: can you name one person who will be accountable for the method, and can they list what they would do first? If not, the method is either too abstract or really several methods.

Ranking forces the real choices. Resources are finite, and when two methods compete for the same engineers, the ranking says which one wins. Rank by asking which method contributes most to the vision, weighted by the ranked values. A method that advances the vision but conflicts with the top value should drop or be rewritten. Writing the ranking down in advance means the decision does not have to be renegotiated every time a conflict appears.

In a cascade, each team method points to a method in the parent V2MOM. [Salesforce's Trailhead module on organizational alignment](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/manage_the_sfdc_organizational_alignment_v2mom/msfw_oav2m_creating_org_alignment_v2mom) describes the corporate V2MOM cascading to functions, teams and individuals so that each person's plan lines up with the company's priorities. A simple way to make the link explicit is to number the parent methods and write the number next to each child method. A team may add one method that serves its own health, such as paying down a reliability problem, but most of its methods should trace upward.

Action plans sit beneath each method and stay short. They list the first concrete steps, the owner of each, and a rough time frame. They are there to prove the method is executable and to give the owner a starting point, and they do not replace a project plan. When a method needs detailed scheduling, link to the project plan instead of copying it into the V2MOM.

Finally, each method is checked against the rest of the document. Every method should have at least one measure that shows it is working and at least one obstacle that names what could stop it. [ClearPoint's V2MOM guide](https://www.clearpointstrategy.com/blog/v2mom) recommends tying each measure to an owner, and the same discipline applies here: a method, its measure and its owner should be readable together.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Start from the vision and the parent methods

Reread the vision and ranked values, and if you are below the company level, list the parent methods your team supports. Ask what would have to change for the vision to come true by the end of the period. Write each answer as a candidate method in a single sentence. Do not rank or edit yet, because the aim is to see the full set of options.

### Step 2: Rewrite candidates as initiatives

Go through the list and turn activities into initiatives by asking what each activity is for. Merge candidates that describe the same change, and split any that bundle two unrelated changes. Remove items that are ongoing business-as-usual work unless the team is deliberately changing how that work is done. What remains should be a list of changes, not a job description.

### Step 3: Apply the owner test

For each method, name one accountable owner and ask them to list the first three things they would do. If nobody can be named, the method is too abstract or has no home, and it needs rewriting or escalating. If the first actions are unclear, break the method down further. Record the owner next to the method.

### Step 4: Rank the methods

Order the methods by their contribution to the vision, using the ranked values to break ties. Put the highest-priority method at the top, as Salesforce's guidance recommends. Cut the list to what the team can actually resource, usually three to six methods, and move the rest to a "not this period" list so the choice is visible. Write one line explaining why the top method is first.

### Step 5: Add action plans

Under each method, list the first handful of actions with an owner and a rough time frame for each. Keep the actions concrete enough to start this month. Link to any detailed project plan rather than copying it in. Review the actions after the first few weeks and replace completed ones with the next steps.

### Step 6: Link to measures and obstacles

Check that every method has at least one measure showing it is working and at least one named obstacle. A method with no possible measure is too vague, and a method with no obstacle has usually not been thought through. Adjust the method wording if needed so the three sections read together. Then share the section with the parent V2MOM owner to confirm the links upward.

## Best Practices

- Write methods as changes the team will make, beginning with a verb and naming the thing that will be different. Activity descriptions belong in action plans.
- Keep the ranked list short enough that the order is meaningful. When a list reaches double digits, the bottom half rarely gets resources, so leaving it off is more honest.
- Keep a visible "not this period" list. It records what the team chose not to do and stops cut items from creeping back in unannounced.
- Give each method exactly one owner. Shared ownership blurs accountability when the method stalls.
- Revisit the ranking at each review. When a higher-priority method finishes or stalls, the next one should move up deliberately.

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating methods as a to-do list**: dozens of small tasks hide which changes matter. Group tasks under a few initiatives and rank the initiatives.
- **Leaving the list unranked**: without an order, every method competes for the same people and the loudest request wins. Rank them, and explain the top choice.
- **Copying the parent's methods unchanged**: a team method that repeats a company method word for word has not been translated into the team's work. Describe the specific change your team will make toward it.
- **Writing methods that nobody owns**: an initiative with no owner will not start. Name one person, or remove the method.
- **Packing the project plan into the V2MOM**: detailed schedules make the document too long to read and go stale quickly. Keep the first actions in the V2MOM and link to the plan.
