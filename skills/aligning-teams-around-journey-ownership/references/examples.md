# Examples: Aligning Teams Around Customer Journey Ownership

## Onboarding has four owners and none

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: at a software company, product owns the setup screens, success owns kickoff calls, marketing owns welcome emails, and support owns the help articles. Each team considers onboarding partly theirs, and new customers still get stuck.

**Walkthrough:**

The ownership map shows four contributing teams and no accountable owner. The executive sponsor names a senior product manager as owner of the onboarding journey, with the other three teams listed as contributors in the ownership matrix.

The owner sets up a working review with one representative from each team and a shared set of onboarding metrics. When a fix needs time on the marketing team's roadmap, the owner escalates through the stage owner rather than negotiating team by team.

## A bank starts with a small set of priority journeys

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a retail bank's CX team wants owners for every journey in its hierarchy, but only a handful of senior people have both the influence and the time to take one on.

**Walkthrough:**

The team assigns owners only to the top-ranked journeys from its prioritization, one journey per person. The remaining journeys are listed in the matrix under a portfolio caretaker in the CX team, who monitors their metrics but does not drive improvement.

After the first cycles, the owners report progress through the existing quarterly planning forum. The sponsor uses those results to argue for more owner capacity, and further journeys receive owners in the next planning round.

## Resolving a conflict between two journeys

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: at an insurer, the owner of the claims journey wants to shorten the claim form, while the owner of the fraud review journey needs more detail collected up front.

**Walkthrough:**

The two owners bring the conflict to the owner of the lifecycle stage both journeys sit under, following the written escalation path. Each presents the evidence behind their position.

The stage owner agrees a compromise: a shorter form for low-value claims and an additional step only when certain risk signals appear. The decision and its reasoning are recorded, and both journeys' metrics are reviewed at the next portfolio review to check the effect.
