# FAQ: Designing Validated Learning Experiments

## What makes an experiment validated learning?

The design is fixed before the data arrives. The hypothesis, the test, the metric and the pass and fail criteria are written down first, and the result is read against them. That makes it possible to say honestly whether an assumption held. Without that, the team is only interpreting numbers after the fact.

## What is the difference between a concierge MVP and a Wizard of Oz test?

In a concierge MVP the customer knows a person is delivering the service by hand. In a Wizard of Oz test the customer sees what looks like an automated product while people do the work behind the scenes. Use a concierge MVP to learn whether the result is valuable and what customers need. Use a Wizard of Oz test to learn how customers respond to the product experience itself.

## How big does the sample need to be?

It depends on the size of the difference you need to detect. Small differences need large samples, which is why early experiments should look for strong signals, such as whether anyone pays at all. If you cannot reach enough people for a clean comparison, choose a test that gives a stronger signal per person, such as a concierge MVP with a payment.

## Can interviews count as an experiment?

They can test assumptions about the problem and current behavior, and they are the cheapest place to start. They are weak evidence about whether people will use or pay for a solution, because they record what people say. Follow interviews with a test that asks for a real action.

## What should we do when the result is between pass and fail?

Treat it as a result and record it. Look at what customers did and said to find the likely reason, then design a sharper follow-up test aimed at the biggest remaining uncertainty. Avoid lowering the pass line after the fact.
