# Examples: Designing Validated Learning Experiments

## Pricing assumption for a scheduling tool

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a four-person team building appointment scheduling for physiotherapy clinics has assumed clinics will pay a monthly fee, but every experiment so far has tested usability.

**Walkthrough:**

The team maps a dozen assumptions and ranks willingness to pay as the most uncertain and most consequential. They write: "We believe independent clinic owners who book a demo will start a paid plan at 40 dollars a month at a rate of at least 20% within 14 days." The pass line is 20%, the fail line 8%, and anything between triggers a retest with a different price. They send the offer to 60 clinic owners from their target list.

Nine start paid plans, 15%, which lands in the inconclusive band, so the team reruns the test at a lower price point instead of declaring success.

## A/B testing an onboarding change

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a note-taking app with steady daily sign-ups believes a guided template picker will raise the share of new users who create a second note.

**Walkthrough:**

The hypothesis reads: "New users shown the template picker will create a second note within 48 hours at least 5 percentage points more often than users shown a blank page." The team splits new sign-ups evenly and fixes the test at 4,000 users per arm before looking at results. They agree in advance that a lift of 5 points or more ships the picker, a lift under 2 points drops it, and anything between gets a redesigned variant. At the end, the picker arm shows a 3-point lift.

Following the pre-committed rule, they iterate on the picker rather than shipping it or abandoning it.

## Channel assumption for a B2B data product

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a startup selling supplier-risk data assumes procurement managers will find it through LinkedIn content rather than through sales outreach.

**Walkthrough:**

The assumption map shows the whole go-to-market plan depends on this channel, so it outranks product questions. The hypothesis: "Procurement managers reached by three sponsored posts will request a sample report at a rate of at least 2% of clicks within three weeks." The team sets a fail line at 0.5% and commits in advance to test direct outreach if the result falls below it. The campaign produces a 0.3% request rate.

The team logs the result, records the pivot to outbound, and writes a new hypothesis for the outreach test.
