# Act Stage: Driving Interactions in the Consideration Stage

> Run the RACE framework Act stage: build consideration stage content, landing pages and micro-conversions that turn visits into interactions and leads.

## Overview

The Act stage of the [RACE Framework](../../methods/race-framework/METHOD.md) covers what happens after a visitor arrives and before they buy. Dave Chaffey says Act is short for Interact, and describes it as persuading site visitors or prospects to take the next step on their journey when they first reach your site or social presence: finding out more about the company, searching for a product, reading a blog post ([Chaffey: RACE marketing model](https://www.davechaffey.com/digital-marketing-glossary/race-marketing-planning-model/)). For most businesses, he writes, the main aim of Act is to generate leads that can then be nurtured toward purchase.

Act maps to the consideration stage of the buyer journey. The prospect knows you exist and is weighing options, gathering information and deciding whether you are worth more attention. Chaffey made Act a separate stage from Convert because encouraging interactions on websites and in social media is so hard in a crowded, fragmented media landscape. Treating it as its own stage gives consideration content its own goals, so it is not judged only on immediate sales.

The practical core of this skill is micro-conversions. Nielsen Norman Group defines a macro conversion as a desired action that directly contributes to the primary goals of the business, and micro conversions as actions that are indirectly related to KPIs but tend to happen more frequently, such as adding a product to a cart, subscribing to a newsletter or watching a video ([NN/g: Macro vs. Micro Conversions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/micro-conversions/)). Act KPIs are mostly micro-conversions. Chaffey's own examples of Act goals are "Viewed product", "Added to Basket", "Registered as member" and "Signed up for an e-newsletter".

Act also covers participation. Chaffey notes that it includes encouraging sharing of content on social media and customer reviews, though he adds that reviews strictly belong to Engage. Smart Insights stresses that these interactions happen across web, mobile, social and email, and that the leads they produce need separate management from final conversion ([Smart Insights: The RACE Framework](https://www.smartinsights.com/digital-marketing-strategy/race-a-practical-framework-to-improve-your-digital-marketing/)).

The output of this skill is a set of defined and tracked Act interactions, consideration stage content and landing pages mapped to the questions prospects ask, and a follow-up path for each type of lead.

## How It Works

Start with the questions prospects ask while they consider. Pull them from sales calls, support tickets, site search, reviews of competitors and interviews. Each question suggests a piece of content or a page, and each page should offer one clear next step that is also an Act interaction.

Match content formats to the question. The Smart Insights campaign guide lists formats that get audiences interacting, including infographics, written guides and PDFs, games, videos, and quizzes and interactive tests ([Smart Insights: campaign plan](https://www.smartinsights.com/digital-marketing-strategy/organize-campaign-plan-race-framework/)). Comparisons and pricing explanations answer "which option?" questions; guides and tools answer "how do I?" questions; case studies and reviews answer "will it work for someone like me?" questions.

| Consideration question | Content that answers it | Act interaction to track |
|---|---|---|
| Is this relevant to my problem? | Guide, explainer video | Scroll depth, video view, second page view |
| How does it compare? | Comparison page, product pages | Product view, add to shortlist or basket |
| Will it work for me? | Case study, interactive tool, demo | Tool completed, demo request |
| Can I stay informed? | Newsletter, webinar | Sign-up, registration |

Define and track the interactions. Pick the micro-conversions that matter for your journey and set them up as events in analytics; in Google Analytics 4 you create or identify the event and mark it as a key event ([Google Analytics Help](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/13128484)). Keep the list short enough that each one has a target and an owner.

Follow up on the interactions you capture. A newsletter sign-up needs a welcome sequence that answers the next questions. A content download needs a relevant next step. Visitors who interacted but did not continue can be re-engaged through ad platforms: Google Ads, for example, describes its "your data" segments, formerly called remarketing, as a way to re-engage people who have previously interacted with your brand ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453998)).

Measure Act by the rate of interactions per visit and by what happens next. A stage that produces many sign-ups that never convert needs better targeting or better follow-up before it needs more sign-ups.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Audit current interactions

List what visitors can do today after arriving: pages they read, forms they fill, tools they use, emails they sign up for. Pull the rates for each from analytics and note which are not tracked at all. Look at the paths visitors take from landing pages and where they leave. This shows which consideration needs you already serve and where visitors drop.

### Step 2: Collect consideration questions

Gather the questions prospects ask before buying, from sales, support, site search and interviews. Group them by the kind of doubt they express: relevance, comparison, proof, cost, risk. Rank them by how often they come up and how close to purchase they are. These questions become the content brief for the Act stage.

### Step 3: Match content formats to questions

For each high-ranked question, choose the format that answers it best, using the range of interactive formats in the [Smart Insights campaign guide](https://www.smartinsights.com/digital-marketing-strategy/organize-campaign-plan-race-framework/) as a menu. Reuse and improve existing content before creating more. Give each piece one primary next step. Assign owners and dates.

### Step 4: Build landing pages with one clear next step

For each main Reach channel, make sure visitors land on a page that matches what brought them and offers a single, obvious next action. Keep forms short and ask only for what the follow-up needs. Make the value of the next step clear on the page. Test the page on mobile, where many visitors arrive.

### Step 5: Define and track micro-conversions

Choose the Act interactions that matter, using the [NN/g distinction](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/micro-conversions/) between process milestones and secondary actions to cover both. Set each up as an event and mark the important ones as key events. Give each a baseline and a target. Check the data after launch to make sure the events fire correctly.

### Step 6: Build follow-up for each interaction type

Write a short follow-up path for each type of lead: a welcome sequence for subscribers, a relevant next resource for downloaders, a sales handoff rule for demo requests. Set up re-engagement ads for visitors who interacted but did not continue, with frequency limits. Agree with sales which interactions count as sales-ready.

### Step 7: Review and improve

Review Act KPIs with the Reach and Convert numbers each month. Look for channels whose visitors interact poorly, content with high traffic and no next step, and interactions that rarely lead to Convert. Test changes one at a time. Retire content that attracts visits but never leads to an interaction.

## Best Practices

- Treat every page as a step. Each piece of consideration content should offer one relevant next action that is also a tracked interaction.
- Answer the questions prospects actually ask. Content built from sales and support questions addresses doubts prospects really have, which content built from internal priorities often misses.
- Track micro-conversions as well as macro conversions. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/micro-conversions/) notes micro conversions happen more often, which gives you data sooner.
- Keep forms short. Ask only for what the follow-up needs; you can learn more about the prospect later.
- Separate lead follow-up from final conversion. Smart Insights recommends managing Act leads separately, through techniques such as retargeting and assisted selling ([Smart Insights](https://www.smartinsights.com/digital-marketing-strategy/race-a-practical-framework-to-improve-your-digital-marketing/)).
- Judge Act by what happens next. An interaction that never leads to Convert is a weak KPI, however often it occurs.

## Common Mistakes

- **Judging content only on sales**: Consideration content rarely closes a sale directly. Measure it by the interactions it produces and by their later conversion rate.
- **Too many calls to action**: Pages that offer every possible next step get fewer of each. Pick one primary action per page.
- **Capturing leads with no follow-up**: A sign-up that receives nothing is a lost interaction. Build follow-up before launching the capture.
- **Tracking nothing between visit and sale**: Without micro-conversion tracking, Act is invisible and cannot be improved. Set up events first.
- **Chasing sign-up volume**: Aggressive gating and pop-ups can raise sign-ups while lowering their quality. Watch the conversion rate of new leads.
