# Examples: Act Stage

## An outdoor gear shop adds a buying guide with a next step

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an online outdoor gear shop gets steady traffic to its tent category pages, but most visitors leave without viewing a single product in detail.

**Walkthrough:**

Support emails show the same consideration question again and again: which tent size and season rating a buyer needs. The team writes a short buying guide with a quiz that recommends two or three tents, and links it from the category pages.

They track three micro-conversions: quiz completed, recommended product viewed, and added to basket. After launch, product views per category visit rise, and quiz completers add to basket more often than other visitors. The guide becomes a standing Act asset with an owner who updates it each season.

## A B2B service firm fixes lead follow-up

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an IT services firm offers a downloadable security checklist and collects many downloads, but sales says almost none of them turn into conversations.

**Walkthrough:**

The team finds that downloaders receive the PDF and nothing else. They build a short follow-up sequence that answers the next questions a downloader is likely to have, with an invitation to a free review call in the last email. They also agree with sales that a review-call request, not a download, counts as a sales-ready lead.

The Act KPIs change to match: downloads stay as a supporting KPI, and review-call requests become the headline. Sales now receives fewer, better leads.

## A software company re-engages trial visitors

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a project management tool sees many visitors reach its pricing page and leave without starting a trial.

**Walkthrough:**

The team adds a lower-commitment Act interaction on the pricing page: a short product tour video, tracked as a micro-conversion. Visitors who watch the tour but do not start a trial are added to a re-engagement audience and shown ads that answer the most common pricing questions, with a frequency limit.

The monthly review compares trial starts from re-engaged visitors with the cost of the ads, and the team keeps the campaign because trial starts from that audience cost less than from new paid traffic.
