# FAQ: Act Stage

## What does Act mean in the RACE Framework?

Act is short for Interact. Dave Chaffey describes it as persuading visitors or prospects to take the next step on their journey after they first reach your site or social presence. For most businesses the main aim is to generate leads that can be nurtured toward purchase. It corresponds to the consideration stage of the buyer journey.

## Why is Act separate from Convert?

Chaffey says he made Act its own stage because winning interactions on websites and social media is so hard in a crowded media landscape. A separate stage gives consideration content its own goals and KPIs. Without it, content that builds interest gets judged only on immediate sales and tends to be cut.

## What are micro-conversions?

Nielsen Norman Group describes micro conversions as actions that are indirectly related to KPIs but tend to happen more often than the main conversion. Examples include adding a product to a cart, signing up for a newsletter, watching a video or comparing products. In RACE, these are the typical Act KPIs.

## What consideration stage content works best?

Content that answers the specific questions your prospects ask before buying. Comparison pages, buying guides, case studies, interactive tools and webinars are common formats. Choose the format by the question it answers, and give every piece one clear next step.

## How do I know if the Act stage is working?

Look at two things: the rate of Act interactions per visit, and how often those interactions lead to Convert. Rising interactions with a falling conversion rate from them usually means the interactions are too easy or the follow-up is weak. Review both monthly alongside Reach and Convert numbers.
