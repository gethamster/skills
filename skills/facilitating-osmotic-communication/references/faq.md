# FAQ: Facilitating Osmotic Communication in Agile Teams

## What is osmotic communication in agile?

It is the flow of information through overheard background conversation in a shared workspace, so people pick up context without formal meetings or handoffs. In Crystal it is one of the core properties, listed with frequent delivery, reflective improvement and easy access to expert users. The practical aim is faster answers, shared awareness and earlier discovery of problems.

## Does osmotic communication mean everyone must work in an open-plan office?

No. It means the people who need each other's answers should be within earshot and sightline, but the same Crystal guidance also calls for private space and protected quiet periods. A single team area with lowered partitions, a nearby private room and agreed quiet hours fits the practice better than a large open floor shared by many teams.

## Can remote teams practise osmotic communication?

The practitioner sources describe physical proximity and shared space as its normal basis, so remote tools should not be assumed to be equivalent. Some teams experiment with always-on audio or shared channels. If you try this, measure whether answer speed, handoffs and late surprises actually improve rather than trusting that the tool works.

## How do I stop osmotic communication from becoming constant interruption?

Set default quiet periods when questions wait or move to writing unless something is blocking, and provide a room for loud pairing sessions or personal calls. Watch for signs like all-day headphone use or people leaving the area to work. Those signals mean concentration is losing out and the balance needs adjusting.

## Who should sit closest together?

Seat the people whose work overlaps most, such as developers on the same components, testers and the developers whose code they check, and the business expert who answers requirement questions. Build this from a map of which questions currently cause the longest waits. Review the arrangement periodically, because overlaps shift as the work changes.
