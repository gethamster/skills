# Examples: Process in the Marketing Mix

## A veterinary clinic blueprints its check-in

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a veterinary clinic gets good reviews for its vets but frequent complaints about waiting at the front desk. Staff feel busy all day and cannot see where the time goes.

**Walkthrough:**

The team maps one scenario, a booked check-up, from arrival to payment. The customer line shows owners filling in the same pet details on paper that they gave when booking online. Backstage, the receptionist retypes the form into the clinic system while the queue grows. That is the main fail point and the main bottleneck.

The clinic connects the booking form to its system and replaces the paper form with a quick confirmation of details. It sets a time standard for check-in based on what owners said they would accept, and tracks it for a few weeks. The blueprint becomes part of training for new reception staff.

## A software company reduces onboarding handoffs

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a company sells analytics software to mid-sized businesses. New customers wait for their first report longer than the sales team promised, and some churn before they see any value.

**Walkthrough:**

The blueprint shows the new customer passes through sales, a solutions engineer, a data team and customer success, with a queue at each handoff. Two backstage steps wait for the same approval. The time from signature to first report far exceeds what customers said they expected.

The company merges the two approvals, gives the solutions engineer ownership until the first report is delivered, and adds a check that the customer's data connection works before the kickoff call. Sales updates its promise to match the new time standard, so promotion and process agree.

## A hair salon decides how much to standardize

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a salon group wants to open a second location and worries that service will vary between sites.

**Walkthrough:**

The team blueprints a cut and colour appointment and marks where stylists use judgement: the consultation and the styling. It decides to standardize everything around those steps, such as greeting, gowning, timing checks and payment, while keeping stylist discretion in the consultation, because the salon's positioning depends on personal advice.

The blueprint and a short procedure go to the new location as the map to copy. The owner visits both sites monthly and compares complaints and rebooking rates to see whether the process is holding.
