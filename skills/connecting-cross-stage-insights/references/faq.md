# FAQ: Connecting Cross-Stage Journey Insights

## What is a cross-stage journey insight?

It is an explanation that links a result at one stage of the journey to a cause at an earlier stage, with evidence. For example, a buying-stage loss that traces back to an expectation buyers formed during evaluation. A finding that stays within one stage is an observation, and it becomes an insight only when it is connected to another stage.

## What data do I need?

You need data for each stage that can be joined on a shared key. The best key is the same respondent answering about every stage. A segment key, such as trigger type or buyer profile, works when respondent-level links are not possible. Stated reasons and behavioral data together give the strongest evidence.

## How do I avoid mistaking correlation for causation?

Compare lost and won buyers, check that the earlier condition came before the loss, and consider other explanations. Where possible, look for behavior that confirms the mechanism buyers describe. Label insights by strength of evidence and treat weak ones as hypotheses to test.

## Where do I start if I have many possible questions?

Start with the largest losses or surprises at the later stages, such as the transition where your brand loses the most buyers. Limit the first round to a few questions and answer them well. More questions can follow once the team trusts the approach.

## Who should own cross-stage insights?

Each transition needs an owner, and the owner is often the team whose stage contains the cause rather than the team that sees the loss. A central insight or strategy lead usually runs the synthesis, and the stage teams own the actions.

## How often should the synthesis be repeated?

Repeat it when new data arrives for a stage, when a major change is made to a stage, or on a regular schedule tied to your consideration tracking. Re-test existing insights each time, since a fix at one stage can move a problem to another.
