# FAQ: Measuring FDE Success by Business Outcomes

## What counts as a business outcome for an FDE engagement?

A change in how the customer operates that their leadership cares about: lower cost, fewer errors, faster decisions, higher revenue, reduced risk. Palantir's example is a manufacturer reducing the number of defective products coming off the assembly line. Features, integrations and code are the means. If you cannot name the operational change, the mission brief needs work.

## What if the outcome takes longer to appear than the engagement lasts?

Use leading indicators during the engagement and agree with the sponsor when and how the lagging outcome will be measured after you leave. Adoption, data freshness and evaluation scores can show whether the outcome is likely. Put the follow-up measurement date in the closing review. This also gives a natural point to discuss a follow-on mission.

## How do I handle attribution when many things change at once?

Agree the method before results arrive. Options include a comparison group that did not get the system, a before-and-after window with known confounders listed, or the sponsor's recorded judgment. None is perfect, and the scorecard should say which one you used. Honest attribution protects your credibility for the next engagement.

## Should FDEs still track output metrics?

Yes, as diagnostics. Deployment frequency, pipeline errors or features shipped help explain why an outcome moved or stalled. They should sit below the outcome on the scorecard and should never be the headline. Reporting them as success is the output trap the Outcome Metrics lens warns against.

## How does this relate to how FDEs are evaluated internally?

Palantir describes its Deltas as measuring success by impact on the customer's goal. Outcomes depend on factors outside the engineer's control, so a fair review looks at the quality of scoping, execution and communication as well as the result. The scorecard and the decision log give reviewers the evidence to judge both.
