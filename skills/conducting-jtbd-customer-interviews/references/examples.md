# Examples: Conducting JTBD Customer Interviews

## Switching from spreadsheets to a project tool

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a small project management startup wants to know why teams move from spreadsheets and email to its product. It has a few dozen recent signups and budget for small thank-you gifts.

**Walkthrough:**

The product manager screens signups with three questions: what they used before, when they signed up and whether they made the decision. She interviews eight people who decided within the last couple of months. Each interview starts at sign-up and works backward.

Most stories share a first event: a missed deadline or dropped handoff that a senior person noticed. The push was embarrassment, and the pull was being able to see status without asking. The anxiety was that the team would not adopt a new tool. Several people kept the spreadsheet running in parallel for weeks out of habit.

The team had planned advanced scheduling features. Nobody mentioned them as a reason to switch, so it moves effort to a simpler status view and to onboarding that addresses the adoption worry.

## Switching from free workout videos to a paid fitness app

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a founder of an early-stage fitness app wants to understand why people pay for a program when free videos exist. She has few customers of her own, so she recruits people who recently subscribed to any paid fitness app.

**Walkthrough:**

Her first interview goes wrong: she asks what features people like, and the answers are generic. In the next one she asks about the last free workout before subscribing. A pattern appears. People had been doing free videos for months, stopped seeing progress, tried to assemble their own plan and gave up.

The first event was often a moment that made the plateau visible: a failed workout, a photo or a comment from a friend. The pull was structure and progression. The anxiety was paying for something they would stop using. She rewrites her landing page around a structured plan and adds a trial that addresses the worry about wasted money.

## Customers who cancelled

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an HR software company wants to understand why some mid-sized customers cancelled in the past quarter. The team can reach six former customers.

**Walkthrough:**

The interviews start with the cancellation and work backward. Two stories involve a new HR leader who brought a preferred tool from a previous job, a push that had nothing to do with the product. Three describe a slow build-up of workarounds for a reporting need the product never met, until a board request made the gap visible.

The team separates the two patterns. The first informs how account teams handle leadership changes. The second becomes a set of outcome statements for the reporting step of the job map.
