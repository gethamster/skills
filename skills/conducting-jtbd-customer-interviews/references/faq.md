# FAQ: Conducting JTBD Customer Interviews

## How many interviews do I need?

Keep going until new interviews stop changing the timeline patterns, the forces and the job map. With a well-defined switching event, patterns often show up after a small number of conversations. If they do not, the switching event may be too broad, or you may be mixing different kinds of customers.

## Who should I interview?

People who made the decision you are studying, recently enough to remember it. Include people who switched to competitors or workarounds and people who left your product. Avoid people who are only considering a change, since they can only guess at what would move them.

## What if the user and the buyer are different people?

Decide whose decision you are reconstructing and recruit for that role. In business products you may need separate interviews for the person who chose the product and the people who use it. Keep the notes separate so the two jobs do not blur together.

## Can I combine a JTBD interview with a usability test?

It is better not to. A usability test puts the product at the center, while a switch interview needs the customer's story to come first. The mattress interview guidance is to reconstruct the timeline before asking about the product ([the mattress interview](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/the-mattress-interview-part-one/)).

## What if I have no customers yet?

Interview people who recently switched to a competitor or built a workaround for the same job. Their stories show the forces and criteria in the market you want to enter. That is often more useful than interviewing your own early users, who may have picked you for unusual reasons.
