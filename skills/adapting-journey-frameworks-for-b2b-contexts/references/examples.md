# Examples: B2B Customer Journey Mapping: Adapting the Five Stages

## Finding the stakeholder who stalls deals

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a vendor of HR software sees deals move quickly through demos and then stall for weeks before signing. The sales team blames budget timing.

**Walkthrough:**

The team lists the roles in recent deals and interviews a few won and lost customers. A pattern appears: the IT security reviewer joins only after the champion has chosen a vendor, receives a long questionnaire and has no context. In lost deals, the security review is where momentum ended.

The map adds a late-arrival path for security: a short summary of the product's security posture, completed answers to the common questionnaire and a named contact. Marketing owns the summary, and sales introduces it at the shortlist stage rather than after the choice.

## Supporting the champion's business case

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a logistics software company learns from lost-deal interviews that champions struggled to justify the purchase to finance.

**Walkthrough:**

The internal-selling lane shows that the champion must present a business case to the budget holder, and the vendor offers nothing for it. The team builds a simple template that the champion fills with their own numbers, plus a one-page brief written for a finance reader. Account executives offer both at the consensus point where the group agrees the problem is worth budget.

The team tracks how often deals reach the next stage after the template is used and reviews the result at the next quarterly map review.

## Separating the buyer from the users

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a data platform has strong new sales but weak renewals. The account's buyer is a director, while the daily users are analysts.

**Walkthrough:**

The original map had one actor, the director, from awareness to advocacy. The adapted map adds the analysts as the main actor in retention. Their lane shows a slow start: they were not involved in the evaluation, received no onboarding of their own and kept their old tools.

Customer success designs onboarding aimed at the analysts and adds an adoption check before the renewal conversation begins. Renewal is treated as a new decision with its own consensus point.
