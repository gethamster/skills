# FAQ: B2B Customer Journey Mapping: Adapting the Five Stages

## Do the five stages still apply in B2B?

Yes, as the spine of the map. What changes is that each stage involves several people, purchase is a chain of approvals, and the users in retention are often different from the buyers. Keep the stage names and add role lanes, consensus points and internal selling. That keeps the vocabulary consistent with the rest of the company.

## How many buying roles should the map include?

Include every role that can stop a deal or that affects whether the product is adopted. That usually means the champion, the economic buyer, technical or security reviewers, procurement and the end users. Roles that appear only occasionally can be noted separately. Too many lanes make the map unreadable, so combine roles that behave the same way.

## Should I map accounts or individuals?

Map at the account level with a lane for each role. A map of a single person misses the group dynamics that decide B2B deals. A map with no individual lanes hides who needs what. The layered view keeps both.

## How do I learn about internal selling I cannot see?

Ask. Won and lost customers will usually describe what they had to do internally: who they presented to, which objections came up, what documents they had to write. Your champions in open deals can tell you too. Treat the answers as touchpoints on the map.

## How does renewal fit into the B2B journey?

Treat renewal as a new purchase decision within the retention stage. New approvers and new alternatives often appear, and the original champion may have moved on. Map who decides the renewal and what evidence of value they need, and start that work well before the contract end date.
