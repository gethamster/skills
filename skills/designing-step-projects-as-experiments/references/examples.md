# Examples: Designing Step-Projects to Validate Product Ideas

## A fake-door test before a build

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team wants to add a scheduled-reports feature to raise weekly engagement. The idea ranks well on ICE, but its Confidence rests on a few customer requests.

**Walkthrough:**

The team lists the assumptions and picks the riskiest: that many users want reports on a schedule at all. The cheapest test that could answer it is a smoke test. They add a "Schedule this report" button that opens a short note saying the feature is coming and asking for interest.

Before starting, they write the threshold: if fewer than a small share of report viewers click during two weeks, they stop; if many click, they move to a prototype with a few customers. The result lands in between, so they change the idea: they interview the users who clicked and learn that most wanted a one-off email export, which becomes a new, smaller idea in the bank.

## A sequence of steps for a new onboarding flow

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a team believes a redesigned onboarding flow will raise activation. Building and A/B testing the full flow would take most of a quarter.

**Walkthrough:**

The team plans the idea as a sequence. The first step is fact finding: analyzing where new users drop off and interviewing a handful of them. The second is a clickable prototype tested with a few new users to check they understand it. The third is a version released to internal users. Only the fourth is an A/B test of a built flow.

The first step shows that most drop-off happens at one permissions screen that the redesign did not touch. The team changes the idea to focus on that screen, and the later steps are rewritten around it. The sequence saved them from testing a full redesign aimed at the wrong problem.

## Stopping an idea early

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a sales-led company wants to add an in-product chat assistant for trial users. Leadership is enthusiastic, and Confidence is based mostly on market buzz.

**Walkthrough:**

The step-project is a concierge test: for two weeks, a product manager answers trial users' questions in a chat window by hand. The threshold is that users who chat convert to paid noticeably better than similar users who do not.

Few users open the chat, and those who do ask billing questions that the help center already answers. The team stops the idea, records the result in the idea bank, and shares it with leadership. The time saved goes to the next candidate on the board.
