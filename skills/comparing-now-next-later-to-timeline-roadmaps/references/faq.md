# FAQ: Now Next Later vs Timeline Roadmap

## Is a timeline roadmap ever the better choice?

Yes. When most of the work has fixed external dates, the solution is known up front and dates have held in practice, a timeline is an accurate picture of the plan. Fixed-scope contract work and coordinated hardware launches are common examples. The skill is about matching the format to how certain your work really is.

## What is the difference between a quarterly roadmap and a Now Next Later roadmap?

A quarterly roadmap places each item in the period it is planned for, so readers treat the column as a delivery quarter. A Now Next Later roadmap places each item by how much the team knows about it, and items move when evidence changes. The two can look similar on a slide, which is why the definitions under each column matter. The parent method page covers this comparison in more depth.

## Can we use both formats at once?

Yes, and many teams do. A common hybrid uses Now Next Later for direction, a release plan for work that is built and ready to launch, and dated objectives for the commitments the business needs. What to avoid is two competing roadmaps of the same work, one for the team and one for executives, kept in sync by hand.

## How do we convince leadership to try Now Next Later?

Bring your own delivery record rather than an argument about formats. Show how many recent dates held, what the slips cost, and which dates were real. Then propose a time-boxed parallel run with a clear review question, such as whether date renegotiations dropped. Leaders are more willing to try a change that has an exit.

## How long does a switch usually take?

The mechanics can be quick. ProdPad's guidance is that the conversion itself fits in a workshop if vision and objectives already exist. Getting stakeholders to read the new format comfortably usually takes a couple of planning cycles. Plan for the longer of the two.
