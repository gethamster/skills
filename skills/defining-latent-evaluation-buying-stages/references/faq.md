# FAQ: Planned Journey Framework Stages

## What are the latent, evaluation and buying stages?

They are the three stages of the planned journey. In the latent stage the buyer is not looking for information but is still receiving impressions passively. In the evaluation stage the buyer has formed an intention and is actively researching and comparing. In the buying stage the buyer is choosing among specific offers and completing the purchase.

## How do I know when a buyer moves from latent to evaluation?

Look for the trigger that created an intention to buy and the first active behavior that followed, such as a search, a quote request or a question to a friend. Buyer interviews usually reveal a small set of recurring triggers for a category. Turn those into observable entry signals that a survey question or a data event can detect.

## Should the stages match our sales pipeline?

Not necessarily. Pipeline stages describe what your company does with a buyer, while journey stages describe what the buyer is doing. Define the journey stages first from buyer evidence, then map the pipeline onto them and note where the two disagree, because those gaps often explain lost deals.

## Can a buyer go backward between stages?

Yes. A buyer can drop from evaluation back to latent when the trigger fades, for example when a repair turns out cheaper than expected or a budget is cut. Record these loops in the definition document so later analysis treats them as a real pattern rather than bad data.

## How many interviews do I need to define the stages?

There is no fixed number. Keep interviewing until new accounts stop adding new triggers, behaviors or exits, and make sure the sample includes buyers who chose competitors and people who stopped looking. A small, varied sample is more useful than a large sample of your own customers.

## How often should stage definitions change?

Revisit them when buyer accounts show a new trigger or information source, when a new channel appears in the category, or when downstream analysis keeps finding buyers who do not fit. Stable definitions make results comparable over time, so change them deliberately and note what changed.
