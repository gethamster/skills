# Examples: Planned Journey Framework Stages

## Defining stages for a used-car marketplace

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: an online used-car marketplace wants stage definitions before it commissions a consideration study. The insight lead has interview notes from a dozen recent buyers, a few people who bought from a dealer instead and a few who stopped looking.

**Walkthrough:**

Reading the notes for the moment each person started looking, the lead finds three recurring triggers: a repair bill that seemed too high to justify, a change in commute, and a new family member. These become the entry signals for evaluation. The latent stage is defined as owning a car that works while hearing about models from friends and seeing listings in passing.

Evaluation is defined as saving listings, comparing prices across sites and asking someone knowledgeable for advice, and it ends when the buyer arranges to see a specific car. Buying covers the inspection, financing and the handover. Two of the people who stopped looking had gone back to latent after the repair was cheaper than expected, so the document records that loop explicitly.

## Defining stages for a home insurance switch

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a home insurer wants to understand why customers of other insurers switch to it or do not. The team has survey responses from new customers and a small set of interviews with people who got a quote and stayed with their current insurer.

**Walkthrough:**

The team finds that the renewal notice is the dominant trigger, followed by a claim experience with the current insurer. Latent is defined as the period between renewals, when people see advertising but do not compare. Evaluation starts with a comparison-site search or a direct quote request after the renewal notice arrives.

The exit from evaluation is defined as holding two or three specific quotes. Buying covers checking cover details, calling to ask about exclusions and switching. The interviews with people who stayed show a buying-stage objection the team had not seen before: uncertainty about whether cover would start without a gap. That becomes a named buying-stage question in the definition document.

## Defining stages for premium laptops sold through retailers

**Scenario:**

Illustrative scenario: a laptop brand sells mostly through retailers and wants a shared stage definition for its brand and retail teams, who have been using different words for the same buyers.

**Walkthrough:**

The brand team had treated anyone who visited a product page as evaluating. Buyer interviews show that many visitors were only curious, and that the real start of evaluation was a specific need, such as a new job or a failing battery, followed by comparing named models. The team rewrites the evaluation entry signal around that behavior.

The retail team adds that most buyers decide on the brand before entering the store and choose the retailer on price and availability. The buying stage is therefore defined as choosing between specific retailers' offers for one or two models. Both teams sign off on a single one-page definition and agree to review it when the next product line launches.
